# Script format: long narrated video (VSL, long explainer)

Load this with a script task skill. Where the two differ, this wins.

A long script is judged on 2 things: at every point the viewer is still learning something that leads to the promise, and nothing in it is longer than the brand's facts can fill.

## What this surface carries

Narration over pictures that change: footage, diagrams, product pictures, animation. It shows the unshowable, controls the order, has sound and holds attention for as long as it keeps teaching. It has a believed face only where real footage of real people is cut in.

It is the format for **unaware and problem-aware** buyers: the length is the education they need. It suits scripts whose load-bearing beat is the **mechanism**, with a long **problem** in front of it.

## How the beats grow

Write for the length you were given. Don't write long and expect it to be cut down. The table spells out, for a long script, what the principles say about length (`length-and-format`); if the two ever differ, the principles are right.

| Beat | 60 s | 2 to 3 min | 5 to 10 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 1 line | 1 line | 1 line |
| Problem | 1 to 2 lines | Several lines | A scene of 2 to 3 minutes, as a story |
| Promise | 1 sentence | 1 to 2 sentences | 1 to 2 sentences per loop |
| Mechanism | 1 sentence | 2 to 4 sentences; can be most of the ad | A taught section in each loop |
| Objection | 1 line | 2 to 3 | Blocks, often inside the problem scene |
| Proof | 1 piece | 1 to 3 pieces | 5 or 6, every rung the brand has |
| Offer | Short | Short | Short |
| Risk reversal | 1 line | 1 line | 1 line after each loop |
| Call to action | 1 line | 2 to 3 lines | Several, at intervals |

The hook and the offer never grow. Narration runs at a calm pace.

## Length is earned by what the brand holds

Length comes from problems, mechanisms, objections and proof, and each of those comes from the brand's facts. Count them before writing.

- 5 to 10 minutes needs about 3 separate problems, a mechanism for each, and 5 or 6 pieces of real proof. A brand that holds 2 pieces of proof gets a 2 to 3 minute script, and the notes say what is missing.
- Never fill a length with adjectives, a repeated point, or a proof the brand does not have.

## Loops

- **60 seconds:** 1 pass through the beats. No loop.
- **2 to 3 minutes:** 1 pass with the mechanism as most of it, or 2 short loops.
- **5 to 10 minutes:** about 3 loops: problem, promise, mechanism, objection, proof, then a second problem and round again.

Each loop has its own problem and its own mechanism, and each mechanism builds on the last ("that alone isn't enough, which you know because you've tried it; what makes it work is..."). Put a light call to action after each loop, then return to proof.

A timeline is a good vehicle when the product works or changes over time, each step a loop: for something taken, "by day 7, by day 30"; for something used or worn, "the first cook, the tenth, the hundredth", "wash 1, wash 50", "year 1, year 5". Use only the time points the brand's facts support.

## How to write for it

- **Open cold.** A story or an observation, not the problem stated. The viewer shouldn't know for several seconds that there is a product.
- **Teach.** The viewer should feel they are learning how something works. Every taught point leads toward the promise.
- **Open questions.** Tell them what is coming and make them wait for it.
- **The product's arrival:** late in the first loop, as the answer the teaching has been leading to. At 60 seconds, about two thirds of the way through.
- **Person:** 2nd or 3rd person narration. 1st person only for a real person's true story, told with their say and in their words.
- **Voice:** written for 1 narrator at a steady pace. Short sentences, varied length. Read it as if aloud end to end: breath, stumbles and dull stretches show up only over the full length.

## Visual track: notes an editor can cut from

Every line gets a picture, and the picture changes every 2 to 4 seconds. Start each visual note with what kind of picture it is:

- `on file`: footage or photos the brand already holds (say which, if known).
- `product photo`: the pack, the product out of its pack, the product in use.
- `diagram`: the mechanism drawn, with its labels given as on-screen text. A diagram shows only what the line claims.
- `text card`: the words given exactly.
- `to film` or `to make`: a picture that does not exist yet, described as 1 shot.

Real people (a customer, the founder, an expert) appear only from footage on file; mark where. A proof asset is shown at the moment it is cited, with its source readable.

## What to avoid

- A stretched short script: more adjectives and repetition, no new problem or mechanism.
- Agitating until it reads as pity. The long problem is 1 person's story escalating, not a list of symptoms.
- A single call to action at minute 8.
- A timeline point, a result or a count that is not in the brand's facts.
- A narrator who speaks as a customer.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `brain.md`: `Market awareness level`, `Market sophistication` (how cold to open, how much to teach).
- `customer-avatars.md`, the avatar: `Grounded in` and the `Customer language bank` (the problem scene, in their words), `What almost stops them` (the objections), `The moment`.
- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `How it works` (proposed: the mechanism as links with sources; 1 loop needs 1), `Result over time` (proposed in this pass: what a buyer notices and by when, each with its source; it is what a timeline is built from), `Key features`, `How it is used`.
- `brand.json`: claims with sources, proof assets (count them: they set the length), guarantee, offers, forbidden claims.
- `assets/` and `brand-guidelines.md`, `Motion and sound`: `Footage on file` (proposed in this pass: the real clips the brand holds, what each shows, who is in it and whether they agreed to ads).

Blank `Result over time`: no timeline. Too little proof for the length asked: write the longest length the facts fill and say so in the notes.
