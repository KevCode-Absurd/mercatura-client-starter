# Song ad: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A micro music video. An original song carries the product's story over animated pictures, with 1 recurring stylised lead who sings on camera in some shots. The script is the lyric: each line is a lyric line, and its visual note says `SUNG` or gives the silent picture. It is judged on:

1. The hook line is seen and sung in the first 5 seconds.
2. Every picture shows what the lyric says at that moment.
3. The lead and the product look the same in every clip.
4. The picture claims nothing the lyric could not, and the lyric nothing the brand's facts do not.

## The app does not make music

The song itself is made in a music tool from the lyric and the style line, once and whole, and its real timings then set the cut. Until the song exists, shots are planned at about 2.5 sung words a second. Clips are silent picture under the song; a `SUNG` shot shows the lead singing and is matched to the song when the clips are joined.

- Copy the style line (the `Style:` line in the first visual note) and every section tag (`[Verse 1]`, `[Chorus]`) into `notes`, unchanged. They are for the music tool, not for the picture.
- Change no word of the lyric, and cut none: the song is made from all of it.

## The look

1 look (preset `default`): animated pictures with 1 recurring stylised lead. `visual_direction` opens with this wording, the palette filled in from the brand's colours:

3D animated render with the feel of an animated feature film, simple rounded shapes, soft even light, saturated [palette] colour, clean uncluttered backgrounds, shallow depth of field.

No named studio, artist or film. Not live action.

## Structure in seconds

- About 2.5 sung words a second: a clip of 10 seconds holds about 25 sung words, which is 3 lyric lines. 1 lyric line is 1 scene of about 2.2 to 3.5 seconds.
- No lyric line runs across a clip boundary. Where it can, a clip ends where a section ends.
- Clip 1 opens on the lead already singing the hook line, facing the lens.
- The last chorus is `SUNG`. The last scene holds the lead beside the product for the offer line.
- An instrumental stretch still has a picture: the lead's action goes on, at the end of the clip whose lyric leads into it.

## The lead, so it stays the same

- `character` is 1 sentence: a stylised animated woman or man, adult, or an animated object; body shape; hair as 1 shape and its colour; 2 clothing colours; 1 mark (glasses, a scarf, a hat); large expressive eyes and a mouth that can sing. Every `visual` names it by the same 2 or 3 words.
- A fictional adult by its proportions and dress, or an object. Never a child, a real person, a celebrity or a known character.
- 1 lead. Nobody else sings.
- It sings to "you" or as a character in a story. It claims no result of its own and is no customer, founder or expert.

## Where the lead sings

- A scene whose script line is marked `SUNG` starts its `visual` with "SUNG:". The lead faces the lens and sings that line, its mouth matching the words.
- In every other scene no mouth forms words. The lead acts, turned to the thing it is doing, and the picture shows what the line says.
- The lead rarely sings all the way through: the hook and the last chorus at least, the middle mostly silent picture.

## What the camera and the set do

- `setting`: the main place, which holds the things the lyric names. Simple shapes, a clean backdrop, no signs, labels or screens.
- `camera`: at the lead's eye level in a sung shot. Each shot is held steady and each cut changes how close it is; the cut does the moving.
- `props`: the product first, then the few things the lyric names, each in view when its shot opens.
- Pictures a model can animate simply: an object, the lead, 1 action. No crowds, no crane shots, no dancing troupe.

## The turn to the lens, and the 1 move

- This look's own transition is the turn: the lead turns from the action to the lens to sing, and back to the action after the line. Nothing comes in from out of view.
- 1 action to a scene, at real speed, starting on the line and finished by its end, so the next line has its own picture.
- A sung scene has no other action: the lead sings, and may sway or lift 1 hand.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The lead singing to the lens, medium (a `SUNG` shot).
- The lead closer, on the hook word.
- The thing the lyric names, close, as silent picture.
- The lead's hands doing the line's 1 action.
- The lead and the product together, wide, to close.

## The product on screen

- Its shape, colours and printing are those of its photo, lit by the scene. Its `look` gives its size against the lead.
- It comes into the frame at the lyric line that names it, at a cut, within the lead's reach or in its hand, front to the lens.
- It is named in the song once or twice, not in the hook. It is shown used only as the lessons say.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` is "No voice in the clip: silent picture under a song." The style line stays in `notes`.
- `dialogue` is the lyric line of that scene, exactly, so the cut can be matched to the song. Nothing is spoken.
- No music and no sound is made in the clip.

## First and last frame

- First: the lead mid-line, mouth open, facing the lens, a plain backdrop.
- Last: the lead beside the product, the product's front clear, the top third plain for the offer line, held. Only this last shot is a held pose.

## Avoid

- A lead who sings every line, or mouths along in a silent scene.
- A picture of something the line does not say.
- A number, a time or a health result drawn or sung that the script does not state.
- A beat-for-beat dance: the model cannot keep time.

## After the render

- The lead matches the first clip: shape, colours, the 1 mark.
- In `SUNG` scenes the mouth moved as singing; in the others it stayed shut.
- No letters anywhere, and the product's printing is unchanged.
- When the song exists: every scene still sits on its line. If not, the cut follows the song, never the plan.
