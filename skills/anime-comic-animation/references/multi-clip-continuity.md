# Multi-clip anime continuity

Use when a brand sequence crosses clip boundaries, changes object contact or needs a deliberately held composition. GHOST HAND demonstrates preparation and causal choreography: cast and place references first, difficult moments as stills, then timed video beats. Adapt the method to the brand's chosen story. Its cast, violence, franchise references, heavy VHS finish, no-music rule and 48-second runtime are project choices.

## Give every image a bounded role

Keep a per-clip reference map: actual attachment tag → identity, environment, entry composition, timed action anchor or exit pose. Tags can change between clips. A neutral turnaround supplies design, not framing. An entry frame supplies starting pose; a mid-clip anchor supplies a later composition. Do not ask an identity image to do all three jobs.

For a difficult contact shot, make the still readable first: hand orientation, grip, load-bearing support, object scale, travel direction and visible expression. Check actual route support before binding multiple images or an end frame. When a timed reference role is unsupported, use the still as a planning anchor and describe the target; do not promise exact reproduction.

## Write a state handoff

Keep this beside the sequence, then express the relevant facts in each clip's existing plan/prompt fields. This is a planning worksheet, not a new application schema.

| Clip | Entry state | Event / target moment | Exit state passed forward |
|---|---|---|---|
| [ID] | Lead position/facing; camera side; product location, grip, support and open/closed state; light/effect state | Ordered action; target image role and time; affected object; chosen hold or slow motion | Final pose/location; product state and holder; residue/effects; next opening and cut |

Avoid resetting a latch, grip or object position in the next prompt. Explicitly introduce any time jump or relocation. A camera cut can change the view while keeping world positions, gaze targets and travel consistent. Establish a new angle before an ambiguous crossing; retain a fixed spatial anchor such as a window, counter edge or lamp.

For interaction, describe who or what approaches, where contact occurs, what supports the load, when it transfers and when contact ends. Name what remains undisturbed. If several transfers, reversals and reactions cannot fit the clip, split at a stable pose rather than compressing every event.

## Direct a timed accent

An action anchor need not become the whole clip's pose. Pick one treatment:

- **Freeze:** reach the chosen pose, hold the main action for a bounded interval, then release. Specify whether secondary animation continues; a true full-frame freeze has none.
- **Slow motion:** reach the chosen composition, continue a small visible advance, then return to the selected pace. State what travels and what remains fixed.

```text
[before target] Approach: [visible motion/contact that leads into the key pose].
[target interval] [ACTUAL TAG, if supported] guides the timed composition: [framing, contact and expression]. Treatment: [freeze OR slow motion], [duration within accepted clip length]. Secondary movement: [chosen behaviour].
[after target] Release: [next action and consequence]. Exit: [state inherited by next clip].
```

Keep the product legible through the accent. An impact frame, silence or drawn speed line should emphasize a meaningful turn, not hide an incorrect grip. The source's unequal clips (15, 10, 11 and 12 seconds) show story-led pacing; they do not establish supported model durations or a required four-clip structure.

## Keep light, voice and effects causal

Specify source → nearby illumination → fade/residue. If a chosen graphic glow appears at the product reveal, it casts matching cel light from that position and recedes with the effect; it does not randomly relight every cut. Sound follows visible contact, distance and enabled audio settings. Silence and BGM remain user choices.

Carry voice register, timbre, accent and speaking rhythm as the recurring signature; let surprise, breathlessness or confidence change the delivery. Preserve exact approved spoken copy and language. Prompt direction alone cannot guarantee voice identity or separate native audio stems.

## Brand adaptation: the commuter's tidy reveal

Illustrative concept for a portable cable organizer; confirm its actual closure, capacity and use before scripting. One adult lead, one desk, one practical action. A stylized cable tangle is a comic metaphor; the real organizer's use earns the payoff. Select accepted clip lengths and allocate time for recognition, contact and reaction.

| Clip purpose | Direction and continuity |
|---|---|
| Setup | Lead sits at desk with bag left, loose cable centre, organizer closed right. Establish the window as a light/space anchor. End with right hand moving toward the organizer. |
| Preparation | Open on the same reach. Show the actual opening mechanism, then coil the cable using both hands; organizer stays supported on the desk. End with the coil beside the open organizer. |
| Payoff accent | Show placement before closure. Generate the closure/contact still first; use it as a brief chosen hold or slow-motion accent when the route permits. A restrained drawn burst sits behind the product. End closed, held in the right hand; no duplicate cable remains on the desk. |
| Reaction and exit | Begin with the same closed organizer in the same hand. Lead puts it in the bag, leaves the desk clear, and reacts with calm satisfaction. Keep the product visible before the exit; apply approved CTA in editing. |

The same structure can support a packaging reveal, kitchen tool ritual, fashion accessory handoff or comedic old-way/new-way comparison. Change the event and evidence to fit the product rather than copying the example's action genre.

## Compare the joins, then repair locally

Inspect each rendered ending beside the next opening: identity, screen/world direction, grip/support, product state, effects, light and voice. A good storyboard still does not prove motion continuity. If a hand detaches too soon, fix release timing in that beat; if an object teleports, fix the entry/exit map. Preserve the other choices. Keep only relevant current corrections and stable invariants in the next prompt, not an accumulating archive of negatives.
