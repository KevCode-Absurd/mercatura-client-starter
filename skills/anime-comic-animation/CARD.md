# Anime ad directing: production craft

The drawing fills the screen. Build one original 2D hero and a supported product-led turn, not a printed comic page or an unrelated fight. Comedy, an emotional story, reveal, routine change, comparison or surreal metaphor are choices. A muted viewer should understand the obstacle, change and product role.

## Look and identity

Use the selected preset, not a generic anime label. Lock line weight, cel shadow shapes, palette, texture and motion rhythm in `visual_direction` across clips. Looks: cel (clean line and flat fills), inked (brush outlines/spot blacks), manga (monochrome screentone and product accent), graphic-novel (muted ink texture), pop (bold primary shapes), retro-ova (painted cels, hard ink shadows, subtle analog grain/colour separation), cyberpunk (urban depth/mechanical detail and controlled neon), deadpan-comedy (simple adult face/calm reaction against one exaggerated event). No forced VHS, paper or panel artifacts. Product and expression remain legible.

`cast.character` describes one original adult hero's silhouette, build, face, hair shape/colour, outfit and distinguishing accessory; leave `cast.guest` null. Never copy a known character, costume, franchise emblem or exact artist style. The same design appears wherever that hero is named. Close-ups, product inserts and environment shots need not show the hero.

## Reference roles and geography

Identity sheet = design only. Scene master = geography/light/palette. Opening composition = entry arrangement. Action key frame = timed contact/reveal, accent hold or ending pose. Product photo = true geometry/colour/artwork. Rebind actual tags by role per clip; no assumed slots, continuation or automatic memory.

Give each input a relevant exclusion and shot scope. Keep subject designs distinct; track prop holder/support and transfers, without adding cast to the single-lead route. Remove repeated prose before contact paths, state handoffs, style anchors or approved words.

Use approved stills before motion. Define positions, distance, gaze and travel direction. Record entry state, timed target and exit state; carry grip, support, changed props/outfit and persistent effects into the next opening. Put these in existing shot `visual`, `place`, `move`, `tone` and plan fields, not new schema. Repair space before style.

Camera: name viewpoint/distance, crop/depth and one visible perspective cue. Check sightline and true product scale. Alternatives vary more than height; fixed key frames keep their composition. Lens cues serve drawn perspective, not forced photoreal optics or blur.

## Animation exceptions to generic montage guidance

Anime rhythm is purposeful anticipation, readable action and live reaction. One event and dominant camera move per timed shot. A steadier camera supports fast action; a complex background needs simpler movement. One set is valid. Strong key poses, short holds with hair/fabric/blinks, a brief impact frame or speed lines serve the story. Clear walking or a short dash is allowed with a start, path and endpoint. Split complicated choreography into manageable shots.

Contact has an order: approach → grip/contact → transfer → release → reaction. Name the affected object, hand support and what stays fixed. A bounded freeze holds a pose; slow motion advances it. Animate the approach and release around the key frame. Choose either treatment for that beat. Motivated VFX affect nearby cel lighting and fade with their source; carry remaining effects across the cut.

Do not force real-life speed, perpetual motion, photoreal skin or smooth 3D interpolation. Keep hand-drawn 2D line and cel shadows stable. Exact frame counts, fps, duration, reference tags and supported clip modes come from the actual route, not a tutorial's example. Match joins, geography and prop states across clips.

## Product, words and sound

The product earns the payoff through supported use. Draw true geometry and colours from the photo, preserve label artwork and keep effects from obscuring it. An aura is a graphic metaphor, not a changed body or fabricated efficacy. No fictional customer results. Captions, balloons and SFX words are editing overlays, not generated footage.

Preserve exact words and speech budgets. `voice_direction` identifies narrator or hero; only that speaker lip-syncs. Keep voice register/timbre/accent across clips; change emotional delivery, not casting. Planned pauses are allowed. User Audio, Scene sounds and BGM choices control audio, including silence. Sounds mark contact; music supports dialogue. Native audio is combined, not separate stems.

Check identity, palette, shadows, scale, layout, grip, product, clip joins, unintended freezing, flicker and 3D/photoreal drift. Repair one instruction in the affected beat; keep a short relevant correction list, not every historical failure. Example clip lengths, heavy VHS, violence and no-music choices are not defaults.
