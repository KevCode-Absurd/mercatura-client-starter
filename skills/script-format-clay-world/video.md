# Clay world as a video: a narrated story with a cast

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger sees a clay film, follows who it happens to, and can say what the product changed.

## How to write for it

- **Narration, not dialogue.** 1 narrator, in the brand's voice, tells the story over the pictures, in 3rd person about a lead or in 2nd person. No clay figure speaks or mouths words.
- **The look brings no story.** Use the script's own shape: a problem deepening to 1 consequence, failed fixes, a discovery, a witness. Each line is 1 picture of what the narrator is saying at that moment.
- **Use the cast.** This look shows a problem among other people: the lead and 1 other, or the lead in a crowd (a carriage, an audience, a queue). Give the lead stakes: something risked, lost or embarrassing in front of them.
- **The product's arrival:** inside the story, as what the lead found. It comes into the picture when the words reach it, in the lead's hands.
- **The mechanism,** only where the brand's facts give 1: promise first, then 1 thing acting on another, as clay changing shape.
- **One close that works:** offer and call to action as narration over the lead with the product, then 1 identity line.

## Length

30 to 60 seconds, 90 at most. Under 30 seconds: 1 place, the problem, the product, the offer.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- **Describe the lead in full, the same way every time:** adult, build, clay skin colour, hair, clothes. 1 other figure at most gets the same. A crowd is "each a distinct sculpted clay face".
- **Name the clay on each thing:** "a coiled clay towel", "clay plants with thumb-pressed leaves". Glass, liquid and metal are "sculpted as a clay imitation of" the real thing.
- **A feeling is face mechanics, never an adjective:** "her inner brows pushed up and her mouth pulled flat", "his cheek squashed against his palm, lids half lowered".
- **Bright warm light.** No dusk, night or long shadows. A cold source (an open fridge, a screen) is named, with the clay skin holding its warm colour against it.
- **Shots to write:** a tight medium on the lead (the usual one); clay hands handling the product; a macro close-up of a texture or the product; a crowd scene; a worm's-eye view from ankle height.
- A thing is already in the frame when its shot opens. A hard cut may change the view or the place. **At the turn a new place may arrive by a clay transition,** written as a line of its own: the scene melts into a swirled pool while the product stays standing; it kneads and reshapes into the next; it rolls into a clay ball and unfolds as the next; coils and pellets roll in and build the product; a clay smear wipes the frame. 1 to a clip at most, with 6 narrated words at most over it.
- 1 action per shot: 1 gesture or 1 camera move. Movement is stop-motion: stepped, small, at real speed. No running, falls or crashes.
- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip. Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.
- No words inside the picture beyond the product's own printed label, and no signs or labels on the set. Overlays are added afterward.

## What to avoid in a video

- A crowd shown as buyers, or as proof.
- 3 figures each doing something: 1 acts, the rest hold.
- "Smooth", "polished" or "glossy" in a visual note: the clay turns into a 3D render.
- 2 shots in a row from the same distance and angle.
