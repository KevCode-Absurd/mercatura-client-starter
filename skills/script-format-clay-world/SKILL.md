# Format: clay world

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

Clay world is a look, not a story: the script brings the story. It is not the claymation story format: that is 1 character on 1 tabletop set with 1 thing that changes; this is a cast in a whole world.

## What makes it a clay world

- **A whole miniature world in clay,** seen from inside like a stop-motion film: rooms, streets, a crowd.
- **A cast.** A lead and the people around them, each a sculpted clay adult. They are fiction: never a customer, a founder or an expert, a child, a real person or a known character. A narrator tells the story; nobody speaks.
- **Made by hand, and it shows:** fingerprints or tool marks on every surface, real shadows, bright warm light. Glass, liquid and metal are clay imitations.
- **1 of 4 looks per ad.** `cinematic` (the default, for most ads): detailed faces, real fabric clothes, a film's close framing. `goofy`: oversized heads, googly eyes, wonky sets, for playful brands and comic hooks. `clean`: soft rounded forms and dot eyes, for minimal brands and calm explainers. `puppet`: thread hair and fabric details in moodier film light, for story-led, premium ads.
- **The product is sculpted in clay under a real printed paper label,** at its true shape and colours, its label words exact.
- **Clay changes shape.** A scene can melt, knead or roll into a ball and unfold as the next.

## What it is good for

A story that needs other people in it (a commute, an office, a dinner), told as a small film nobody had to cast. It makes an embarrassing problem easy to watch, and shows a change as only clay does.

It is weak where the sale rests on a real face, a real result or the product's true finish: a clay pack is a likeness, and a clay figure proves nothing.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers (the problem recognised as a scene among people) and for solution-aware buyers (the mechanism as clay changing). Product-aware and most-aware buyers want the real product and the terms: another format does more.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

- Show only what the script's lines and the brand's claims state. A change sculpted into the scene is a claim made.
- A mechanism is shown only as `How it works` gives it, and no further. A real function (heat spreading, a seal closing) is shown as it is; a metaphor stays plainly a metaphor (a knot of clay loosening).
- No body before and after.
- A crowd is a setting, not proof: clay faces are not customers.
- Numbers, study results, medical results and named competitors come only from the brand's facts.

## What to avoid

- A clay figure who talks to camera or reports a result.
- A feeling written as 1 adjective: it renders a pleasant blank face.
- Dusk, night, long shadows: clay goes dead in low light.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are` (the lead), `The moment` (the place, who else is there), `What almost stops them`, `Tried before`, `Customer language bank`. Blank: ask, or mark both inferred.
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Looks like` and `Pack` (shape, colours, every label line), `How it is used`, `Visible result`, `How it works`, `Core benefit`. No label words: ask; never invent a label. No `How it works`: no mechanism.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Brand guardrails`, `Brand world`, `Color palette`, `Typography` (words on a still), `Motion and sound`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer, the guarantee. A claim that is not there is not sculpted.
