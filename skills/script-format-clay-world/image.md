# Clay world as a still image: 1 frame of a clay film

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees a still from a handmade clay film with 1 person in 1 plain moment, and reads the headline once.

## The frame

- **1 moment, 1 lead.** Unaware or problem aware: the lead caught at the worst of the problem, the feeling in the face. Solution aware: the change half done at the product, only as the brand's facts give it. Product aware: the lead's clay hands on the product, or the product alone on a dressed clay surface.
- **The face is the story.** A still has no narrator. Figures are fictional adults: no child, no real or known person, nobody shown as a customer, a founder or an expert.
- **Composition:** a tight medium view from inside the world, the lead sharp, the set soft behind and running past every edge. A second figure or a crowd is background. A calm blurred area in the top third is left for the words.
- **The product:** where the ad shows it, front to the lens, its label large enough to read. Where the ad leaves it out, the scene holds only the problem.

## Words on the picture

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer, as clean type in the brand's typeface, laid over the calm area in the top third. It says what the picture cannot: whose problem this is, or what just changed.
- 1 smaller line, or 2 or 3 chips, and a button only where there is an offer. 20 words in all.
- Type is laid over the photograph. Nothing is written in clay, and nothing on the set carries words except the product's own printed label.
- No speech bubble: a clay figure says nothing. Every number and term is the brand's own.

## For the image prompt

- Open with the look's fixed words. `cinematic` (the default): a detailed cinematic claymation scene, hand-sculpted plasticine characters with an appealing idealised build and richly sculpted features, matte clay skin with thumb-worked smoothing and fine tool striations, chunky rounded clay hands with softly tapered fingertips and no fingernails, real woven fabric clothing with stitched seams, on a physical miniature set, macro lens, shallow depth of field, strong background bokeh, warm practical light with a soft rim light.
- Every `cinematic` face in full: large almond eyes with prominent white sclera around a deeply coloured iris, a dark sculpted upper lash line, thick sculpted brows on a defined brow ridge, defined cheekbones and jaw, sculpted hair with deep comb grooves. State adult age, build and clay skin colour. A feeling is brows, lids and mouth.
- `goofy`: plasticine with fingerprints and thumb smudges, wonky geometry, oversized heads, huge round googly eyes, teal and terracotta.
- `clean`: matte modelling clay, clean rounded forms, subtle tool marks, dot eyes.
- `puppet`: clay puppets with thread-wrapped hair, fabric details and wire accents, film light with gentle shadows.
- Name the clay on every thing ("a coiled clay towel"); glass, liquid and metal are "sculpted as a professional clay imitation of" the real thing. Beyond the words above, never "smooth", "polished" or "glossy".
- Bright warm light, a named palette, real cast shadows. Camera inside the miniature world at a named angle, the scene filling the frame.
- The product is sculpted in clay at its true shape and colours with a real printed paper label: state every line of label copy exactly.
- Not a 3D render: no photographic skin, real human hands or wrinkled faces.

## What fails in a still

- A wide view of the whole cast: faces go stiff and doll-like.
- A product alone among real glass or metal: it becomes a product photograph.
- A change the brand's claims do not state.
