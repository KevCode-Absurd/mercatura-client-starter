# Claymation ad

A narrated story in clay: 1 character, 1 small set, 1 product. Clay exists in an ad to show what a camera cannot: a mechanism nobody can see, or a problem the buyer has stopped seeing, as a thing that changes shape in front of them.

## What it is judged on

1. **With the sound off, a stranger can say what changed and what changed it.**
2. **The character, the set and the product look the same in every clip.**
3. **The product is the real one:** its shape, colours and printing.
4. **The picture claims nothing the brand's facts do not.**

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, materials, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, clip lengths, how narration is written, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-claymation`: how the story and the narration are written. This skill is the clay.

## 1. Find the thing that changes

Before any scene: 1 clay object that stands for the problem or the mechanism, with 3 states (at its worst, changing, changed), and the 1 thing that acts on it.

| The product's edge | The thing that changes | What acts on it |
|---|---|---|
| A routine made simpler (1 evening drink for 4 pots) | A wobbling stack of grey pots that sinks flat, 1 pot left standing | The tub set down beside the stack |
| Even heat (a layered steel pan) | An orange clay ripple under the pan, from a hot spot in the middle to the whole base | The pan itself: a real function, shown as it is |
| Rain kept out (a waterproof jacket) | Blue clay drops that sit as beads and roll off | The jacket's cloth |

- **It changes by clay's own moves:** it shrinks, flattens, unknots, straightens, lifts, takes on colour. It never appears from nowhere, vanishes or flies in.
- **It reads without words.** If the picture needs the narration to be understood, find another picture.
- **A picture claims as much as a sentence.** Show only what the brand's claims state, in their terms. No body before and after, no organ, no symptom leaving a body. When `Visible result` is "nothing visible", show the routine, or a metaphor that is plainly a metaphor (a knot loosening), never a cure.
- **A real function is shown as it is; a metaphor is not passed off as one.**
- **The change happens at the product:** it, or what comes out of it, touches the thing; or the character turns from the thing to it.
- **When clay is the wrong format.** If the product's result can simply be filmed (`Visible result` is a change anyone can watch), a demonstration with the real product is stronger. Clay earns its place when the cause, or the problem, can't be filmed.

## 2. The character

1 clay figure, written once in 1 sentence and used unchanged everywhere:

> A clay woman, adult, about 3 heads tall, pear-shaped, warm brown clay, black hair as 1 smooth bun, mustard jumper and grey trousers, 2 black bead eyes, a short pressed-line mouth, round wire glasses.

- It stands for the avatar: the same rough age, hair and dress. It is an adult by its proportions and dress. Never a child, never a real or known person, never another brand's character.
- Simple shapes hold: 1 shape for the hair, 2 colours for the clothes, bead eyes, 1 mark (glasses, a scarf). Detail is what drifts.
- It does not talk. Its mouth changes shape as a feeling, and a narrator tells the story. Feelings are poses: shoulders drop, head tilts, arms lift.
- 1 character. A second figure doubles what can go wrong.

## 3. The set, the material and the 2 palettes

- **1 tabletop set** of a place from the avatar's `The moment`, in clay: a floor, a plain backdrop, 3 or 4 pieces. Nothing on it carries words.
- **Everything except the product is clay** (or the handmade materials of the chosen look: card, felt, wire). Water, steam, light and fire are clay shapes. 1 real-looking material breaks the world.
- **Handmade and well made:** matte surfaces, even proportions, slight tool marks. How rough (fingerprints or none) follows the brand's imagery style and is the same in every picture.
- **Soft, even studio light,** shallow focus, the look of a small model photographed close.
- **2 palettes, named in advance.** The problem: greyed, cool, 1 dull colour. The changed state: the brand's `Color palette`. Until the change, the product is the only thing in the brand's colours; when the thing changes, it takes them.
- **A tall frame has height to spare:** the thing that changes sits above or behind the character, the product at the character's side.

## 4. The product in clay

- From its own tagged photo (`Reference photos`: pack front). Its shape, colours and printing are exactly those of the photo. It is lit by the set and may sit in the clay world; no letter of it is redrawn in clay.
- **Its size against the character is decided once and written down,** large enough that its printing or its shape reads at the size of a thumb: a pack stands between waist and shoulder height on the character; a thing that lies flat is about as wide as the character. The same in every clip.
- It stands with its front to the lens. The character touches its side or its lid, never its front.
- It opens, pours, fastens or rolls only the way `How it is used`, `Moves and sounds` and the lessons say. What comes out keeps its real colour and shape.
- It is on the set from the first frame, within the character's reach, so it never has to appear and nobody has to walk to it.

## 5. Movement, camera, sound

- **The stepped cadence of stop-motion, at real speed:** pose to pose, with short holds. Not smooth, not slowed.
- **Restrained.** The character moves 1 limb or leans; the thing that changes does the large movement. No falls, avalanches, crashes or explosions.
- **1 change of state to a clip,** and few words over it, so it can be watched.
- **The camera is on a stand at the character's eye level and does not move.** By hand, with a still per scene, 1 slow move in a clip is possible (a push in, a pull back or a pan), starting and ending still.
- **Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.**
- **The narration rides in the prompt.** A line with a word the filter refuses (`models.md`) is reworded before the clip is asked for.
- **Sound:** 1 narrator, off screen (age, pace and accent from the brand's voice), and small set sounds: a soft tap of clay, a pop, a slide. Music only if the brand's `Motion and sound` says so.

## 6. Two ways to build it

**1 set, clips that continue (what the app does).** 3 to 6 clips of about 10 seconds. Clip 1 starts on the approved opening frame; each later clip continues from the last frame of the one before. Everything the story needs is on the set from that first frame. A 30-second ad: clip 1 the problem at its worst; clip 2 the product acts and the thing changes; clip 3 the changed set, the character beside the product, held for the offer.

**A still per scene (by hand).** 6 to 10 scenes of 5 to 8 seconds for 30 to 45 seconds, each from its own approved still, joined with hard cuts. Every still is made with the character picture and the product photo attached and tagged, and with the same material, light and palette lines. This allows a close view of the thing that changes and a wider view of the set. The arc: hook 1 or 2 scenes, the problem deepening 2 to 4, the product acting 1 or 2, the changed state 1 or 2, the close 1.

Templates for both: `references/prompts.md`.

## 7. Stills first

1. **The character picture:** the figure alone, front on, plain background. Further views are separate pictures, 1 view each.
2. **The opening frame:** character picture and product photo tagged with their jobs; the set; the thing that changes at its worst; the character already mid-pose. Approve it.
3. By hand, each further scene's still, approved before its clip.

## 8. The check after each clip

On top of the shared check:

1. Clay is still clay: no smooth plastic, no photographic skin, no real water.
2. The character's proportions, colours and 1 mark match the first clip.
3. The thing changed in view, by a clay move. It did not pop in or out.
4. The movement is stepped but does not flicker or strobe.
5. The character's mouth did not form the narrator's words.
6. The product's printing is unchanged; no clay letters and no words on the set.

## What is not done

- No real person, child, celebrity or known character in clay.
- No clay body shown before and after, and no result drawn that the claims do not state.
- No invented pack: the product is never re-sculpted with a made-up label.
- No list of things to leave out is sent in place of a description. The look is said as what it is ("matte clay, even proportions, soft even light"). Where a tool has a separate field for what to leave out, the list in `references/prompts.md` goes there.
