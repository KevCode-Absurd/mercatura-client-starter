# Claymation story: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narrated story about 1 clay character on 1 small handmade set. Clay is used to show what a camera cannot: the problem or the mechanism as 1 clay thing that changes. It is judged on:

1. With the sound off, a stranger can say what changed and what changed it.
2. The character, the set and the product look the same in every clip.
3. The product is the real one: its shape, colours and printing.
4. The picture claims nothing the narration could not.

## The thing that changes

- Before writing scenes, name 1 clay object that stands for the problem or the mechanism, and its 3 states: at its worst, changing, changed. It is the second of `props`, after the product, and its `look` is its first state.
- It is on the set from the first frame. It changes by clay's own moves: it shrinks, flattens, unknots, straightens, spreads, lifts, takes on colour. It never appears, vanishes or flies in.
- The change happens at the product: it, or what comes out of it as the lessons say, touches the thing; or the character turns from the thing to it.
- A picture claims as much as a sentence. Show only what the script's lines and the brand's claims state. No body before and after, no organ, no symptom leaving a body. A real function (heat spreading, water beading) is shown as it is; a metaphor stays plainly a metaphor.
- If the scene needs the narration to be understood, choose another picture and say so in `notes`.

## Structure in seconds

- Clip 1 is the problem: the thing at its worst, the character already mid-pose with it in frame 1, 1 readable movement inside the first 3 seconds.
- Middle clips: the problem deepens (in a longer ad, 1 failed fix that leaves the thing unchanged), then the product acts and the thing changes.
- The last clip is the changed set: the character upright beside the product, held for the offer.
- 1 change of state to a shot, seen close. The shot where it happens carries 8 spoken words or fewer.
- Each clip is a run of shots, as the director's rules give them: a move, the reaction close, the thing that changed. Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The whole set, wide, the character and the thing that changes both in it.
- The character's face and shoulders, close, for the feeling.
- The thing that changes, very close, as it changes.
- The clay hands at the product's side or lid.
- From above, the set laid out like a small map of the problem.

## The character, so it stays the same

- `character` is 1 sentence: a clay man or woman, adult, how many heads tall, body shape, clay colour, hair as 1 shape and its colour, 2 clothing colours, bead eyes, a pressed-line mouth, 1 mark (glasses, a scarf). Every `visual` names it by the same 2 or 3 words.
- It stands for the persona: the same rough age, hair and dress. An adult by its proportions and dress. Never a child, a real person or a known character.
- It does not talk. Its mouth changes shape as a feeling only. Feelings are poses: shoulders drop, head tilts, arms lift.
- 1 character. Nobody else is on the set.

## What the camera, the set and the material do

- `camera`: on a stand, at the character's eye level for the wide shot, the set filling the frame like a small model. Each shot is held steady; the cut does the moving.
- `setting`: 1 tabletop set of 1 place in clay: a floor, a plain backdrop, 3 or 4 pieces, none with words on them.
- Everything except the product is matte clay (or the preset's handmade materials). Water, steam, light and heat are clay shapes. The finish follows the preset and is the same throughout.
- `visual_direction` describes the look as what it is: matte clay, even proportions, soft even studio light, shallow focus. It names 2 palettes: the problem (greyed, cool, 1 dull colour) and the changed state (the brand's colours). Until the change, the product is the only thing in the brand's colours; when the thing changes, it takes them.
- Movement is stepped, pose to pose with short holds, at real speed. The character moves 1 limb or leans; the thing that changes does the large movement. No falls, avalanches, crashes or explosions.
- In a tall frame the thing that changes sits above or behind the character, and the product at the character's side.

## The product on screen

- Its shape, colours and printing are those of its photo. It is lit by the set; no letter of it is redrawn in clay.
- Its `look` gives its size against the character, large enough to read at the size of a thumb: a pack waist to shoulder high, a flat thing about as wide as the character.
- It stands with its front to the lens, within the character's reach, on the set from the shot where the words reach it.
- The character touches its side or its lid, never its front. It opens or pours only as the lessons say; what comes out keeps its real colour and shape.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" and gives age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice.
- All `dialogue` is narration. No scene has the character speaking.
- Under the narration: small set sounds (a soft tap of clay, a pop, a slide).

## First and last frame

- First: the character mid-pose, the thing at its worst and the largest shape in the frame. 1 character, 1 thing, a plain backdrop.
- Last: the thing in its changed state, the character upright beside the product, the product's front clear, the top third plain for the offer line.

## Avoid

- A new set in a later clip, or an object that appears inside a shot.
- A crowd of props: each extra clay piece is something that changes shape between clips.
- Signs, labels, screens or letters on the set.
- A character who speaks to camera or gives a testimonial.
- A metaphor that shows a body healing.

## After the render

- Clay is still clay: no smooth plastic, no photographic skin, no real water.
- The character's proportions, colours and 1 mark match the first clip.
- The thing changed in view, by a clay move; it did not pop in or out.
- Stepped movement, with no flicker.
- The character's mouth did not form the narrator's words.
- The product's printing is unchanged; no clay letters appeared.
