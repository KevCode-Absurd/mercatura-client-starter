# Directing a clip (video-director)

A clip is a short run of shots with hard cuts, covered the way a crew covers a scene: from far and near, from several sides, with a camera that travels in some shots. A viewer shown the same picture, or the same subject from the same spot, has left.

The format and owner's pace override montage defaults: cinema can hold a reaction; UGC keeps phone-supported moves.

1. **Plan the film first.** The main place and the places it cuts to, who and what is in each, and the picture nobody expects for each idea. Then the shots. A clip is 1 scene, as long as its words need, with a picture of its own: never the last clip's again.
2. **Keep it tight: the picture changes every 2 seconds or sooner, and each change is something new being done.** Never 1 small action stretched over a clip. Plan a clip as if it had half its seconds, and fill the rest with what the person does next. Shots differ in length: quick where the clip builds, longer where it lands. How many, inside the range given, is the director's to choose by what the idea needs; a made world takes the most.
3. **Start in the middle.** Shot 1 opens with the action under way and the strangest or most telling picture of the clip. No one walks in, sits down or greets.
4. **Every cut changes how close the camera is and where it stands, both.**
   - How close: wide (the place, the subject small in it), full (head to toe), medium (waist up), close-up (a face or 1 object), extreme close-up (1 named detail).
   - Where it stands: eye level; low angle (looking up: size, nerve); high angle (looking down: small, caught out); overhead (straight down: hands, a surface, a layout); over the shoulder (what they face); point of view (what they see, their own hands in frame); profile (square from the side); framed (through a doorway, a shelf, a gap).
   - A clip uses at least 3 of each. Say the exact framing: what part fills the frame, and where the camera is.
5. **The subject changes too:** a face, the whole person, hands, the product, another object, the place. Never the same thing from the same side twice running. A look, what is looked at through their eyes, then the reaction, is 3 shots.
6. **The camera travels in about half the shots, chosen by the feeling:** push in to what matters, pull back for context, pan to a target, track at the subject's pace, truck across the set, tilt, crane up for scale, orbit for a reveal. 1 move a shot: beginning, path, pace and arrival. Face close-ups use short, simple paths; an orbit travels only far enough to reveal a named detail. Very fast action gets a held camera.
7. **Each shot is 1 action that changes the picture.** Something is different when it ends: torn, open, poured, gone, arrived. Say who, which hand, how hard, where it lands ("the scoop tips and the powder slides off onto the counter", not "she spills powder"). 2 moves that run on in the same hands are 1 action. Write what moves, never what holds still. A look, a nod or a shrug is not an action alone; the shot still says the face.
8. **Give the frame depth.** Something near the lens, the subject, something behind; 1 of them moving with the place.
9. **A cut may change the place.** The counter, the car, the gym bag, the desk. Name the place in each shot that leaves the main one, and say again who is where.
10. **Use the cut to bring things in.** An object nobody has seen is already in frame when its shot opens. It never comes out of a bag or from off screen inside a shot.
11. **The product comes in when the words reach it, being used,** opened the way the brand says it opens, with only what the brand says comes out. The shot shows the object the words are about: the single serving, not the box it came in. A pack standing in every shot is furniture.
12. **The voice does not stop for the cuts.** 1 track across the shots, at the pace given for the speaker's age, each sentence straight after the last. A cutaway shows what the words just said or are about to say, and says whose hand or face it is.
13. **Keep the same world.** The same person in the same clothes, the same light, each object in the state it was left in.
14. **The last shot lands the clip's point.** Only the last shot of the whole video holds still.
15. **What still goes wrong:** more than 3 people, a reflection in a mirror or glass, someone leaving the frame and coming back in 1 shot, counting on fingers, a hand over the product's printing.
