# Format: comic

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

A comic is judged on 1 thing: read panel by panel, with no sound, a stranger can say what the problem was, what changed and which product did it.

## What makes it a comic

- **It is read,** panel by panel: the problem, then the same person in the same place, changed.
- **1 claim.** Choose the 1 claim the comic dramatises, from the brand's facts, and build every panel toward it. A second claim does not fit.
- **Few words.** A caption is 5 words or fewer, in capitals, 1 to a panel. Where the buyer's own words fit in 5, use them word for word. A feeling is drawn as a thing: 1 sweat drop, a mug gone cold, a clock with hands.
- **Flat ink, 1 accent colour.** Bold black outlines, flat colour, halftone dots, cream paper. Black and 2 muted tones until the product appears; the accent (the product's own colour, or the brand's) enters with the product and stays.
- **The drawn person** is 1 adult, described by role, look and clothes and never by age. 2 drawn people at most. No drawn customer giving a result, no drawn expert, no drawn child: the drawn person is fiction and claims nothing as fact.
- **The real product** is real: its photograph, with its real printing, beside the drawing. Drawn inside a panel it is its shape and colour only.

## What it is good for

It has no believed human face and little room for words. It carries a **problem** told as a short story, or a **mechanism** that can be drawn as a diagram. It is weak for proof that rests on a real person, for an offer with terms, and for a product whose appeal is how it looks in use.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers (the problem recognised in 1 panel) and for solution-aware buyers (the diagram). An offer rides only as the last line.

## What a drawing may show

A drawing is a claim.

- A diagram draws the product itself: its layers, its fibre, what is in it and how it is taken, with 2 or 3 labels, 6 words in all, each a thing the brand's facts say. It draws the inside of a body only when the claim is about the body, and then no further than the claim: "supports" is the product being taken, never damage mended.
- The changed panel shows the result the brand states, in the size the brand states it. A product with nothing visible to show ("helps you wind down") gets a changed evening, not a measured outcome.

## What to avoid

- A caption longer than 5 words. Dialogue between characters.
- A story with no product in it until an advert starts at the end.
- A third claim hidden in a label.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `Core benefit` and `Key features` (the 1 claim, the labels), `How it is used` (the panel where the product arrives), `Visible result` (the changed panel; "nothing visible" changes what it may show), `How it works` (proposed: the mechanism as 2 or 3 links with sources; blank means no diagram, or ask).
- `customer-avatars.md`, the avatar: `The moment` (where and when the problem is set), `Customer language bank` (captions).
- `brand.json`: claims with sources, forbidden claims, the offer for the close.

A blank field is asked about or the line is marked "inferred" in the notes. A claim is never filled in.
