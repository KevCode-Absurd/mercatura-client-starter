# Comic as a video: a printed page filmed on an artist's desk

A printed comic page lies on a desk and is read panel by panel. 1 page is 1 clip, and a page is 2 panels.

## The pages

| Page | Panel 1 | Panel 2 | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| The problem | The person in the problem, in the place and at the hour it happens | The same person, the same place, worse | Black ink and 2 muted tones |
| The mechanism | A diagram: what the product is made of or what it does, with 2 or 3 labels | The product arrives in the story, used the way it is really used | The 1 accent colour enters with the product |
| The turn | The first panel of the problem page again, changed | The second, changed | Accent throughout |

Each page but the last ends on a hand turning it. The last ends on the real product, as a photograph, on the desk.

- 30 seconds: 3 pages.
- 15 to 20 seconds: 2 pages. The problem; then the turn, with the diagram as its first panel.
- 1 page: problem in panel 1, the product in panel 2, then the real product.

## How to write the words

1 line of the script is 1 panel. A comic video has 2 kinds of words, and they go in different places on the line.

- **Lettering** goes in the on-screen text of the line: the caption of that panel, exactly as it should read. It is set in the panel's empty caption box in editing; nothing is lettered by the model.
- **Narration** is the spoken text of the line: 1 short sentence, 8 words or fewer, said over the panel as a voice-over. It says what the caption does not and makes sense without the picture. A panel may have no narration. Nobody in a panel speaks to camera.
- **A comic carries fewer words than its length.** The last 3 seconds of every page are the turn and carry none. Count about 16 spoken words a page: about 50 for 30 seconds.
- **The diagram panel's** labels are written in the on-screen text of its line with a slash between them.
- **One close that works.** The last line is the real product on the desk. Its on-screen text is the offer and the call to action, when there is an offer; its narration is empty or the call to action alone.

## Writing panels a model can render

- The visual note of a line is that panel: who, where, the 1 small thing that moves in the ink. The visual note of the first line of a page also says what the other panel shows, because the whole page is 1 picture.
- The picture cuts between the whole page from above, a close on the panel being read and the hand at the corner. The view stays above the paper and never dives into the ink.
- The desk, the hand, the paper and the product on the desk are real; what is inside the panels is flat ink. Say which a thing is when it could be either.
- No numerals and no sound-effect words drawn anywhere.
