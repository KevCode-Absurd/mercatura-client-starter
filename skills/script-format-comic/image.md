# Comic as a still image: 1 page, read in 1 look

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb it reads as a comic, and in 2 or 3 panels a stranger gets the problem and the turn.

## The frame

- **Pick the size of the story.** 1 panel: the problem at its worst moment, for an unaware or problem-aware buyer. 2 or 3 panels: the problem, then the same person and place changed, with the product between them. 4 panels is the most a feed image holds. Fewer, larger panels beat more, smaller ones.
- **The reading order is the layout:** top to bottom in a tall frame, left to right in a wide one, each panel the full width or the full height. Gutters are even and white. The first panel is the largest.
- **The turn is visible without reading:** the first panel in black and 2 muted tones, the changed panel carrying the accent colour. The same person, the same place, the same angle in both.
- **A diagram panel** may stand in for the middle: the product cut open or laid out, with 2 or 3 short labels.
- **The product:** the real pack, as a photograph, stands at the foot of the page overlapping the last panel's edge, as if set down on the paper. Where the ad leaves the product out, the page is the problem alone and ends on a caption.

## Words on the picture

- 1 caption box to a panel, in its top corner: 5 words or fewer, capitals. The captions read on their own as 1 sentence broken across the panels.
- 1 speech or thought bubble on the whole page at most, 6 words or fewer, and only for a thought the buyer really has. Nobody states a result.
- The offer and the button, when there is an offer, sit in a plain strip under the last panel, outside the drawing. 20 words on the whole image.
- Every word is given exactly, in quotes. No sound-effect words, no lettering beyond the planned captions.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: a printed comic page, seen flat and filling the frame. Bold black outlines of even weight, flat colour fills, halftone dots in the shadows, cream paper with a faint grain.
- Name the panel grid (how many, their order and proportions) and the white gutters. Describe each panel in turn: who, where, the 1 thing drawn large.
- Name the 2 muted tones and the 1 accent colour, and the panel where the accent first appears.
- Captions sit in rectangular boxes with a thin black rule; give each one's words and panel.
- **Type, the look's own:** the captions and the bubble are comic lettering, drawn by the page's own pen: upright hand-lettered capitals of even weight, black on white. The strip under the last panel (the offer, the price, the button) is outside the drawing and is set in the brand's typefaces.
- **Type, the brand's:** the captions and the bubble are the brand's body typeface redrawn in ink by the page's own pen, in capitals: its weight, width and letter shapes kept. Never the brand's small tag face for a caption. The strip under the last panel is set in the brand's typefaces as they are.
- The product is the 1 photographic thing: its real shape and printing from its photo, with a small soft shadow on the paper. Inside a panel it is drawn as shape and colour, with no printing.
- No named artist, publisher or character style. Not manga screen-tone unless asked, not a 3D render.

## What fails in a still

- 6 small panels nobody can read on a phone.
- A page that needs every caption read to make sense: the pictures alone must carry problem and turn.
- The accent colour everywhere from the first panel, so nothing turns.
- A changed panel that shows more than the brand states.
