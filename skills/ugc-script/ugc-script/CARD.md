# UGC script into clips: the card

**What it is.** A UGC script is 1 person talking to their phone. 5 beats (hook, setup, demonstration, turn, close) are 1 shape it can take and not the only one: the writer is given no order. This card is how the beats a script has are placed on screen; a script told another way is placed as it is told.

**What it is judged on.** It still sounds like a person and not an advert once it is cut into clips. The demonstration is seen, not only told. The turn is believed. The viewer knows what to do at the end.

Approved handling/evidence overrides all use, held-close and demonstration defaults below. With `handling=display`, show only a verified sealed detail/front on a support; nobody holds, opens or uses it. Do not guess scale or imply efficacy. If the approved words need unavailable use evidence, revise and approve them before generation.

## Structure: where a script has these beats, their share of the ad and what each needs on screen

| Beat | When | What is on screen | How it is said |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | The ad's first moments, a little longer when the product arrives in it | The face, mid-thought. The product only if the line points at it | As the line asks: quick, dry or quiet |
| Setup | From the hook to about a third of the way | The face, a cut closer, a cutaway to the "before". The product not yet in frame | The doubt or the problem, plainly. Most of the words are here |
| Demonstration | The middle; the most screen time of any beat | The product in use, 1 action per shot. The script's doubts are answered here, by what is seen | Few words, and easy. The hands lead |
| Turn | A short part, after the demonstration | The hands rest and the face eases, while the words carry on | Smaller and plainer than everything before it |
| Close | The ad's last part | The product in view as the script's close has it, its front readable: in the hand mid-use, set down where it is kept, going into a bag, or held up beside the face | In the same voice, offhand |

## Placing the beats in clips

- A beat starts at the start of a clip where it can. The demonstration always does, so that its opening frame shows the product ready in the hand or on the surface.
- A demonstration is 1 step per shot, in the order the product is really used. Its start and its result are both shown.
- The turn and the close may share the last clip. The hook and the setup may share the first.
- The turn is never in the same shot as an action. The face carries it, while the words carry on.
- The beats a script has belong to the whole ad: each new clip advances a different part, without restarting the hook/reveal/CTA. Budget words, breath and hand work; never pad short dialogue with new words or stretched speech.

## When the script is longer than the clips can hold

Propose cuts in this order, say what was cut, and get revised words approved before generation:

1. A second example in the setup.
2. Any sentence that repeats what the picture shows.
3. The setup, down to 1 sentence.

Never cut: the hook; the script's 1 specific number (a time, a count, a price, a dose); the demonstration; the turn; the close. Never move the number out of the spoken words into an overlay alone. Never trade the soft close for a harder one, and never add urgency the script does not have.

## The turn: the beat most often spoiled

The turn is where the script claims less than it could ("Not dramatic. Just consistent."). On screen it is small:

- the shoulders drop a little, a small shrug or a half smile, the eyes leave the lens for a moment and come back
- no grin, no wide eyes, no nodding, no product pushed at the lens
- its tone is 1 or 2 plain words: "quiet", "matter of fact"

A turn played big undoes it, and with a generated presenter it also reads as a personal result nobody had.

## The close: soft is a tone, not a missing instruction

- The words name the action and what happens next, as the script has them. They are said the way a friend passes something on.
- The presenter does not point down, tap the air or mime a button unless the script says so.
- The product is in view, its front readable, while the last words are said: held up beside the face is 1 way, never the close of every ad. Only the last shot of the ad holds still, and briefly.
- The offer's terms, where the script has them on screen, go in the overlay of this scene, word for word.

## Delivery, beat by beat

Written as what the face and body do:

- Setup: 1 free hand moving a little with the words, a small frown or a raised eyebrow at the doubt. The eyes go where the thought goes: to the lens, down at the hands, off to one side and back.
- Demonstration: eyes on the product while the hands work, back to the lens when they stop.
- A moment of doubt or surprise, where the script has one: the eyebrows up, a small shake of the head, while the words carry on.
- Nothing is acted bigger than a person alone with a phone would act it.

## The product through the beats

It comes into the frame when the words reach it, at a cut, already in the hand, and is named as the script names it. Out of frame in the setup; handled in the demonstration; still in the turn; shown to the lens in the close.

## Sound

The spoken number is said clearly, with the stress on it and no stop after it. The product's own sound belongs to the demonstration. Nothing is added under the turn.

## Avoid

- A demonstration told over a talking face.
- A turn or a number lost to make room for the setup.
- A close that is louder, faster or more pointed than the script.
- An overlay that repeats the spoken sentence in full.

## After the render, look at

1. Is the demonstration the longest thing on screen, and can it be followed with the sound off?
2. Is the 1 number said, and clear?
3. Does the turn read smaller than the hook?
4. Is the close said in the same voice as the rest?
