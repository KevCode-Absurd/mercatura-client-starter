# Script from research

The job: write an ad that could only be for this brand, from what its customers have actually said, that moves 1 belief in 1 buyer. You have full freedom over structure and words. You have none over facts.

A script is judged on 4 things: the buyer hears their own situation in the first lines; 1 belief has moved by the end; no line could be said by the product next to it on the shelf; every line has a picture that can be made.

This file says what to do, in order. A script type, when one is loaded, wins over step 5 on the opening and the beat order. A format skill adjusts steps 5 to 10. The rules of the app, sent with the job, win over everything here.

## What never changes

1. No invented customer, quote, review, count, statistic, study, award or expert. A customer's words are used word for word, with their source, and only from the quotes you were sent.
2. A speaker who is not a real customer (a generated presenter, a narrator, a character) tells the problem and shows the product. They report no result of their own.
3. A figure you need and were not sent is a placeholder in square brackets, listed. A persona's back-story is not a source for a figure. A figure the material states 2 ways is not used.
4. The rules sent with the job say whether mechanism and proof lines are held to approved claims. Where they are, a line with no approved claim behind it is not written.
5. Examples in these skills are made up. No fact, number or phrase from an example goes into a script.

## Steps

### 1. Take stock

A script needs 7 things from the brand. Find each in what you were sent before writing a line: the table at the end of this file says where each is, and what to do when it is not there. Start `notes` with `Assumed:` and 1 clause for each need that was blank or thin, saying what you did about it.

### 2. Name the belief

Write 1 sentence: "They think ___. After this they think ___." It comes from the angle and from what nearly stops this buyer. Every beat serves that move; a line that doesn't is cut. Put the sentence first in `test_plan`.

### 3. Place the buyer, and how the product is bought

Use the concept's awareness and sophistication stages, and "How to write for this buyer" where it was sent. The whole script, on-screen text and hooks included, stays at that stage.

Then say which the product is. It changes the objection, the proof, the offer and the action. Where it isn't clear (something that lasts months, a refill), take the one whose doubts the research shows and say so in the notes.

- **Bought again** (eaten, drunk, applied, used up). The doubt: will it work on me, will I keep it up, am I locked in. The proof: the first use, shown; people who came back. The offer: the first order, and what it costs after. The action: start.
- **Bought once** (worn, cooked on, carried, kept). The doubt: the price, the size or fit, whether it lasts, whether it can go back. The proof: the thing put through something; how it is made; how long people have had theirs. The offer: 1 price, against what it replaces or how long it lasts. The action: choose the right one.

Where the length gives risk reversal no beat, the offer line carries the reversal in 1 clause.

### 4. Read the offer and find the claim worth the seconds

List what the offer really has: the thing, the terms, the reversal, the reason for now, the effort. You can only write what is there. Pick 1 price frame the real numbers support. Keep a differentiator only if it is a proposition, competitors can't say it, and it would move a stranger. Cut lines that answer objections the research doesn't show.

### 5. Write the beats

Follow the beat table for the length you were given, in the script type's order where one is loaded.

- **Problem:** the accommodation, for 1 person, unresolved, entered by the control you were given (accommodation, moment or failed solution).
- **Promise, then mechanism:** 1 promise, in their words, no bigger than the proof; then why: the feature, which means the promise, which is why.
- **Objection:** the doubt from step 3 that the research shows, named in the buyer's experience.
- **Proof:** the highest rung of the proof ladder the brand really has, with its source.
- **Offer and risk reversal:** the product is named here, eased in; the terms in the price frame from step 4; the reversal with its real terms.
- **Call to action:** the action, what happens next, the effort, a real reason for now. Then, where there is room, 1 line on who the buyer is, tied to the mechanism.

Leave a beat out when the length has no room for it or the type rules it out, and say so in the notes.

### 6. Write the hook last

Write the script's own hook from the customer's word for the problem, in the hook type you were given. Then the other variants, each a different type. Read each with line 2: a hook that doesn't lead into it is replaced.

### 7. Fix the joins

Read each join, above all the product's arrival. Where the script turns into an ad in 1 line, ease it.

### 8. So what?

Ask "so what?" of each line. Where the only answer is the mechanism, the line needs who cares and why.

### 9. Voice

The brand's voice, sent with the job, comes first; then the voice rules sent with the controls. Where both are silent: 1 idea per sentence, the verb early, plain words, sentence lengths varied. Read it as if aloud and fix every line you'd trip on or be embarrassed to say.

### 10. Write the visual track

For each line: `on_screen` (the overlay exactly as shown, or nothing) and `visual_note` (who and what is in frame, what happens, any cut).

- Every visual note can be made: the product as it really looks and is really used (the lessons sent with the job are exact), an adult, a hand, a place, a simple drawing of how something works. Not a celebrity, a real person who has not been filmed, a real news or television clip, a child, or words to be read inside the picture: words go in `on_screen`. A line with no picture that can be made is rewritten.
- The product may be seen from the first frame, in use. It is named and priced after the mechanism lands, unless the script type names it sooner.

### 11. Differ from the siblings

Read "Scripts already written for this concept". Yours differs from each in how it opens, in which beat carries it and in the proof or picture it uses. New wording on the same skeleton is the same ad.

### 12. Self-audit

Check before returning; anything you couldn't fix goes in the notes.

- [ ] Every beat serves the belief, and no line could be said by the product next to it on the shelf
- [ ] The objection, proof, offer and action fit how the product is bought
- [ ] Nothing invented; every placeholder and every assumption is listed
- [ ] Every visual note can be made; each hook variant leads into line 2

### 13. Name the load-bearing beat and the test

Say which beat does most of the persuading (mechanism, proof, offer or problem). In `test_plan`, after the belief: what this script tests against its siblings, and what to make next if it wins.

## What this reads from the brand brain

The 7 needs: where each arrives, where it lives in the brain, and what to do when it is blank.

| Need | In what you are sent | In the brand brain | When it is blank |
|---|---|---|---|
| The buyer's own words | The quotes under each theme; "Their words" in the concept | `customer-avatars.md`: Grounded in, Customer language bank | Write the problem as a plain observation of what they do. Quote nobody. |
| The moment the problem bites | The persona's trigger; a theme of that kind | `customer-avatars.md`: The moment | Use the place and time the product is used. |
| The old way: what they do, own or buy now | A failed-solution theme; the brain's competitor lines | `customer-avatars.md`: Tried before; `competitors.md`: Gaps worth owning | A kind of product or habit, in general terms. No brand named, no figure about it. |
| What nearly stops them | An objection theme; quotes from 2 to 4 star reviews | `customer-avatars.md`: What almost stops them | The first doubt of this way of buying (step 3), answered with the offer's real terms only. |
| The mechanism | The product's ingredients or specs; the selling points; the main claim | `products-and-positioning.md`: Key features, How it is used, How it is made | Carry the promise with proof or a demonstration of use. Don't make up how it works. |
| The proof, with its source | Approved claims and proof assets when sent; the quotes; what the product visibly does | `brand.json`: claims, proof assets; `products-and-positioning.md`: Visible result | A demonstration of the product in use. Otherwise a placeholder. |
| The offer | "The terms" in the controls; the concept's offer; the guarantee | `offers.json`; `brand.json`: guarantee; `products-and-positioning.md`: Buying pattern | No price and no guarantee is said. The call to action names the action only. |
