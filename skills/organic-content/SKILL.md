# Organic content and creators

Written for this app from the playbook's organic pages: "Founder Content Playbook", "The Founder Story Framework", "The Micro-Influencer Creative Flywheel", "UGC Creator Community Playbook", "DTC Brand Collaboration Playbook" and "UGC Content" (under `docs/ecommerce-playbook/organic/` and `docs/ecommerce-playbook/hooks/`). In the app it is the **Organic** screen under Create, with 2 tabs, and "/" → Organic Content and Creators in the chat. The code is `src/lib/organic/`.

Everything here is a post, not an ad: no price, no offer, no countdown. The app keeps the plan and the list and writes the briefs. It posts nothing, sends nothing to a creator and changes nothing in an ad account.

## Part 1: the founder's 10 videos

When a selected brief needs a reason to care beyond an offer, borrow the playbook's [non-discount content jobs](https://nureachmedia.notion.site/3e509b1670488198beb3fd00a0253e80): explain an ingredient/material, show official use/care, answer a real question, show verified sourcing or process, tell a true founder/team moment, or help choose between actual products. Tie one job to this buyer and the selected video's purpose; do not impose a new calendar. Customer spotlights need a real account and agreed use. Review-derived unexpected uses need official compatibility checked first. Care and expectation-setting also inform future retention copy, but this app does not configure or send email/SMS flows.

A founder on a phone gets more trust than a brand's polished post, and costs nothing to film. The plan is 1 video a week for 10 weeks, in this order. Each row on the screen has 1 button, **Write the brief**: a founder brief is written for that video, for the product and buyer chosen, with what to say and what to film.

| Week | Video | It opens on | Best for | It needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The origin story | "I started [brand] because I was frustrated with [problem]..." | Awareness, top of funnel | The founder |
| 2 | Why I made this product | "Everyone asked why I made [product]. Here's the real reason..." | Launches, middle of funnel | The founder |
| 3 | The hardest lesson | "This mistake almost killed my business..." | Trust, vulnerability | A real mistake, with its real numbers |
| 4 | Behind the scenes | "Ever wonder how we make [product]? Let me show you..." | Trust, justifying the price | Somewhere real to film |
| 5 | A day in my life | "Here's what running [brand] actually looks like..." | Personal connection | A real working day |
| 6 | A common misconception | "Everyone thinks [misconception]. Here's the truth..." | Education, expertise | A truth the brand's facts support |
| 7 | A customer's story, retold | "A customer sent me this message and I had to share..." | Social proof | A real message, with the customer's say, read as they wrote it |
| 8 | An unpopular opinion | "Hot take: [an opinion about the industry]" | Engagement, debate | An opinion the founder really holds, about a kind of product |
| 9 | What I wish I knew | "If I could go back and tell myself one thing before starting..." | Relatability, credibility | The founder |
| 10 | Why we're different | "Here's why [brand] isn't like other [kind of product]..." | Differentiation | A difference that is true, never about a named competitor |

**Beats.** The playbook gives them for 2 of the 10. The origin story: the problem you lived (0 to 10 s), why what existed failed you (10 to 20), the moment it clicked (20 to 30), what you built (30 to 40), a soft close (40 to 45). The hardest lesson: the setup (0 to 10), what went wrong (10 to 25), what it cost (25 to 35), the lesson (35 to 45). For 6 of the others the app offers the beats of the founder hook nearest to it (skills/hook-bank), marked "as a guide". A day in my life and a customer's story have none: 1 thing told well, in 45 to 60 seconds.

**What is real.** The founder is real and films it. A fact the brand's material does not hold (a year, a sum, a count) is a prompt in square brackets for the founder to answer on camera, never a number written for them. A customer's story is a real message.

**Filming.** A phone, facing a window, in a quiet room with a plain background. Talk to 1 person for about 60 seconds. 3 takes, without watching between them; pick the best. Film each opening 5 ways. Look at the lens; speak a fifth faster than feels natural. 45 to 60 seconds. Imperfect and posted beats perfect and unposted.

**1 shoot, 6 pieces.** The full video (Reels and TikTok); the first 15 seconds (a story, as a teaser); a passage of the transcript (a text post); the key lines (image posts); the whole transcript (an email); the shoot itself, behind the scenes (a second video).

For an existing recording, select complete useful passages rather than assuming its first 15 seconds make a standalone teaser. Record source in/out times, what each passage teaches and a soft ending; keep necessary context, use/care conditions and disclosure attached. Captions, cover and post description promise the same thing. **Edit organic footage** opens the dedicated editor and its Organic Video Editor skill: trim and join clips at their own pace, add uploaded music or manually timed captions, and export separate copies. It does not post them or send an email. The original voice remains the timing authority.

**Ending it.** A question with 2 answers for the comments; "yay or nay?"; what next week's video is about; "save this for later"; a reply to a comment a viewer really left.

**More to post between them**, in the playbook's 5 groups: the founding story (the origin as a weekly series, day 1 against day 1,000, why we almost quit); a show (a travel series, customer spotlights, the making of); teaching (hot takes, myth busters, compare and contrast); quick wins (product hacks, packing orders, meet the team); to get replies (this or that, answering messages, reposting customers).

A founding story told as a series is 4 weekly episodes of 45 to 60 seconds: why you started; the wall you kept hitting; the moment it changed; what you are building toward. Each ends on what comes next.

What the playbook does not give, and the app does not invent: posting days and times, a mix of post types, targets for views or followers.

**Improve from an observed result.** When the user supplies retention/watch-time data, find the actual moment viewers leave and name one test: an earlier clear opening, less setup, or a cut that makes the promised point easier to follow. Preserve the real story, product-use conditions and disclosure. Compare similar audiences, durations and posting conditions; view counts alone do not establish a cause or sales. Reuse the successful content job and visual structure with this brand's own sources, rather than copying a trending video's people, words or claims. This manual planning guidance adapts the Bootcamp Social Media resources' useful measurement principle; their engagement tricks, fixed hashtag/posting counts and algorithm claims are not app rules. The Organic screen still uses its existing plan and manually entered statuses.

## Part 2: creators, a small list

The idea: send the product to many small creators with no strings, let them film what they honestly think, and put ad spend only behind the posts that already worked.

**Who.** 5,000 to 50,000 followers, picked for the fit of their audience and how they film, not for size. A creator with 5,000 followers and clear sound beats one with 100,000 and lazy content. The playbook's start is 50 to 100 a month.

**What ships with the product.** A handwritten note and half a page: what the brand makes in 1 or 2 sentences; 3 ideas, not scripts (opening it for the first time, where it fits in a morning or an evening, an honest review after a week); the 1 thing not to say; the only rule, film it however feels natural; where to tag the brand. A 3-page brief is the first mistake. The app builds this note from the brand's own facts and no model writes it.

**Do not ask for a post.** Posting is their choice. The list has no reminder.

**The disclosure is on every brief.** A gift, a commission or a payment is a connection the viewer is told about, in the post itself. It is written into the brief as part of what is delivered, and checking it is the brand's job, not the creator's.

**The tiers, by what a creator did, never by followers.**

| Tier | What happened | Next |
|---|---|---|
| A | Posted, at twice their usual views or more | Agree the use in ads, then ask to run it as a partnership ad; offer to keep working together |
| B | Posted | Keep for the next round |
| C | Did not post | Do not send again |

The app calls a creator C when 30 days have passed since sending with no post. That figure is the app's own: the playbook gives none.

**Twice their usual views.** A post is a winner at 2 times what that creator's posts usually get. The comparison is with their own average, so a small creator can win.

**Agree the use first.** What a customer posts and tags the brand in is one thing; a creator's content run as an ad needs their agreement to that use, in writing, with how long it runs. The list keeps whether it was asked and agreed, and until when; a post is not marked as a partnership ad before it is. The playbook gives no standard length or fee.

**What to expect.** Of the creators sent the product, 4 or 5 in 10 post; of those posts, 1 in 7 to 10 is good enough to pay behind. For 100 sent that is 40 to 50 posts and 4 to 7.5 to run. (The playbook's page says "10 to 15 winners per 100"; its own rates give the smaller figure, and that is the one the app shows.)

**A fuller brief, for content the brand will pay behind.** 3 kinds:

- **Problem, then the fix** (30 to 60 seconds): the problem in the first 3 seconds; show it lightly (5 to 10 seconds); bring the product in naturally; 1 or 2 things it does; the result; a close in the last 3 to 5 seconds. Send the full unedited clip, no transitions, no music, tall and wide if possible.
- **Unboxing, first impression:** the package opened on camera, the reaction first; the packaging and the quality; trying it; 1 thing that surprised them.
- **Part of their day:** starts mid-activity; the product inside their routine; what it feels like, not its features; 1 line on why this brand. No price and no discount.

**Running a post from their handle** (a partnership ad), 6 steps a person does in the ad account: the creator sends the clip to 1 shared place; pick the posts at twice their usual views; at the ad level choose "Partnership ad" under identity and enter their handle; send the request, which they approve with 1 tap; it runs from their handle on the brand's spend; test it on its own for 7 to 14 days and move what holds.

**Pay**, as the playbook lists it: gifted (product and shipping); a flat commission of 10 to 15% of sales from their link; a ladder (10%, then 15% over $500 a month, 20% over $2,000); for proven creators a retainer of $200 to $500 a month with 5 to 8%. They are its figures, not a recommendation for this brand.

## What this does not do

It does not find creators, message them, or track a post's views by itself: a person enters what they see. It does not run or change an ad. A generated person is never used in place of a creator or a founder.
