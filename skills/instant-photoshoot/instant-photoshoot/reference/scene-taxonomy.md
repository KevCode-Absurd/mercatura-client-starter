# The direction and the frames

This file decides what the pictures of an instant photoshoot are, so the set looks like 1
real shoot for this exact product and not a batch. Nothing is picked from a menu by
category. Every run you work out a direction from the product and the brand brain, then
build the frames from it.

The standard to hold: sets made this way for very different products look nothing alike. A
fragrance set and a canned-drink set and a jacket set share no color, no prop, no place and
no light. What they share is discipline: each floods 1 world across every frame.

## The method

1. **Read the product and the brand** (SKILL.md, step 2).
2. **Work out the direction** along the 5 lines below, a few lines each. That is the
   creative direction of the whole set.
3. **Build the frames:** each is 1 of the 15 kinds, bound to the direction, with a job.
4. **Hold it against the examples at the end.** Is the set as bold and as held together as
   those? If it reads generic, push the color world and the props harder.

The method decides the system. The brand brain decides the specifics, and wins any
disagreement.

Decode references by concept, camera behaviour, light, palette, composition, materials
and memorable device before adapting them. Hold the direction's constants while varying
shot scale, angle and frame job; a campaign board is not a layout to generate as a collage.
For a comparison of one concept, change one meaningful axis at a time. Keep the actual
product material and identity fixed. See `skills/production-principles/references/visual-taste.md`.

## The 5 lines of the direction

### 1. The color world (the spine of the set)

The most important decision. Choose 1 of 4 strategies, then name 1 to 3 colors by hex from
the brand's palette.

- **Monochrome flood.** The whole set lives in 1 signature color and its lighter and darker
  tones: ground, surface, props, clothes. The most held-together and the most designed.
  Suits a product with 1 strong color or 1 benefit: a single-flavor drink, a fragrance, a
  sports product, a pan sold in 1 enamel color.
- **Multicolor pop.** Each frame on a different saturated ground, blocked in flat color.
  Lively and varied. Suits a product whose variety is the message: flavors, shades,
  colorways.
- **Muted editorial.** A quiet palette taken from the product's own materials. The place
  and the styling carry the look, not color. Suits clothes, bags, shoes, watches, tools and
  hard goods.
- **Earthy organic.** Natural materials and warm daylight: clay, olive, cream, stone. Suits
  a product sold on what it is made of: botanical skincare, natural food, linen, wood.

When unsure: a monochrome flood in the brand's main color. It is the surest way to a set
that looks directed.

2 rules hold in all 4:

- **The flood never colors the product.** The product keeps the colors of its photo.
  Colored light and colored ground stop at its edge.
- **The product must stand off the ground.** A green pouch on the same green disappears.
  Use a lighter or darker tone of the color, or the palette's second color, behind it.

### 2. The kit of props

3 to 6 physical things that belong to this product, repeated across the set. They come from
what the product is really made of and really used with (`Key features`, `How it is used`),
never from the category in general.

- Something eaten or drunk: the named ingredients raw (fruit, beans, oats), the food itself
  out of its pack, the glass or bowl it is had from, ice, steam.
- Something applied: the texture itself (a drop, a swatch, a peak of cream), water, the
  named plant, a towel.
- Something worn: the cloth, the hardware, a hanger, what it is worn with.
- Something used with the hands: the food it cooks, the things it carries, the tool beside
  it, the material it is made of.
- Something placed in a room: the furniture it sits on, a book, a match, a folded throw.

A prop is never another brand's product, and never an ingredient the product does not hold.

### 3. The places

3 to 6 real places and surfaces where the product lives or is used, from the buyer's
moment (`The moment` in the avatars) and `Brand world`. A kitchen counter in the morning, a
bathroom shelf, a hallway by the door, a gym bench, a bedside table, a street. Add plain
surfaces (stone, oak, linen, plaster, concrete) for the clean frames, tinted to the color
world.

A lifestyle place has a reason to look used: a mug after breakfast, a bag near the door,
a folded towel near the sink. Give a few details a cause and keep the product easy to find.
Clean studio frames stay clean. Choose the intended capture look before adding wear,
grain or exposure imperfections; a designed campaign set need not imitate a phone photo.

The person and product share the location's light, reflections and contact shadows.
Keep product material, colour and printing true while letting the place affect how light
falls on them. For location edits, preserve approved identity, pose and product but allow
relighting. Detailed checks: `skills/production-principles/references/environment-realism.md`.

### 4. How a person meets the product

This sets how many frames have a person and what they show.

- **Wears it** (clothes, jewellery, shoes, bags, watches). Frames on a body are the
  backbone: front, back, a cropped detail, in motion. About half the set.
- **Applies it** (skincare, make-up, fragrance, hair). The product at the skin, the amount
  on a fingertip, a swatch. The texture carries the set.
- **Eats or drinks it** (food, drinks, supplements). The pour, the serving, the bite or the
  sip. The food itself, close, carries the set.
- **Uses it with the hands** (cookware, tools, luggage, bottles, devices). Mid-task, in
  the real motion. The action carries the set.
- **Places it in a room** (candles, lamps, bedding, rugs). People are few or absent. The
  room carries the set.

For every kind, 1 frame of an adult hand simply holding the product up shows its size.
Always include clean frames with nobody in them: a person is 1 way of showing the product,
not every frame. People are fictional adults. A product for children is shown without a
child, or any part of one.

### 5. The light

1 recipe across the set, from the brand's `Light and time of day` and its photography
style, with 1 or 2 frames on purpose in contrast (1 moody frame in a bright set).

- Golden, directional, deep shadow: fragrance, evening, anything rich.
- Bright, punchy, high-key: drinks, snacks, anything playful.
- Soft daylight: skincare, wellness, linen, anything calm.
- Directional and muted: clothes, hard goods.
- Crisp and clean: devices, tools.

## The 15 kinds of frame, and the job of each

Frame kind and camera shot are separate: a routine can be wide, overhead or close;
a worn-product frame can show full fit or one construction detail. Name crop boundaries,
height, angle, viewpoint and focus according to the job. Keep product identity and state
consistent across related views. `skills/production-principles/references/camera-shots.md`
gives twelve shot choices; use the needed ones rather than every type in each set.

These are building blocks, not a quota. The direction decides which are used and how many
of each. Every frame is bound to the direction's colors, props, place, person and light.

| # | Frame | What it shows | Its job, and where it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clean hero | The product alone, whole, on a plain ground in the main color, soft shadow. A thing too large or soft to stand (bedding, a jacket) is folded, hung or laid flat, as the brand's own photo shows it | The hero: first image of a product page, a catalogue ad. Always frame 1, and the test frame |
| 2 | Held | An adult hand holding it up, label to the camera, fingers on its sides. For cloth, a hand gathering a corner, to show its weight | Scale: product page, an ad |
| 3 | On a body | A worn product on a person: front, back, cropped detail, in motion | Fit and how it sits: product page, ads |
| 4 | At the skin | An applied product at the place it goes: the real amount, a swatch, a drop | In use and texture: an ad, "how to use" |
| 5 | In action | A product used with the hands, or eaten or drunk, at the moment that matters | In use: an ad; the first frame of a clip |
| 6 | Very close | The texture, the material, the label, the finish | Texture: a carousel card, an email |
| 7 | With what it is made of | The product beside its real ingredient or raw material | "What is in it": product page, an ad |
| 8 | On a surface | On stone, oak, linen or plaster, in the set's light | A calm second hero: email, social |
| 9 | In its place | Where it is really used or kept | Where it lives: email header, site banner |
| 10 | From above | Laid flat with a few of the props, or everything in 1 order | What you get, or the routine: product page, an offer ad |
| 11 | Color ground | The product on 1 bold flat color, with clear empty space | A base for an ad: type is set on it later |
| 12 | Moody | Directional light, deep shadow | The contrast frame: a launch post, a brand ad |
| 13 | Group | 2 or 3 of the same product, or its real variants, stacked or in a row | Range: a bundle or subscription ad |
| 14 | Small in space | The product small, a great deal of clean space | A banner or an email header with room for words |
| 15 | In motion | A pour, a splash, powder falling, steam, cloth in wind: only motions the product really makes | A hook: the first frame of a clip, a story |

### How many of each, by kind of product (for a set of 12)

- **Worn:** hero; on a body 5 (front, back, cropped detail, in motion, with what it is worn
  with); very close 2 (the cloth, a seam or clasp); on a surface (folded or hung); in its
  place; from above (laid flat); moody.
- **Applied:** hero; held; at the skin 2 (the amount leaving the opening, a swatch); very
  close 2 (the texture, the closure); with what it is made of; in its place (the shelf);
  from above (the routine); color ground; group; in motion (a drop falling).
- **Eaten or drunk:** hero; held; very close 2 (the food itself, the pack); with what it is
  made of; in motion (the pour); in action 2 (served, the bite or sip); in its place; from
  above; color ground; group.
- **Used with the hands:** hero; held or carried; in action 3 (the key moment, the result,
  another use); very close 2 (the working surface, a joint); from above (open, or its
  parts); in its place (stored); on a surface; moody; small in space.
- **Placed in a room:** hero; in its place 3 (the room wide, closer, in its state of use);
  very close 2 (the material, a detail); held (for size); on a surface; from above; moody
  (evening); small in space; group.

For a set of 8, keep the hero, the scale frame, 2 frames of the kind's backbone, 1 very
close, 1 in its place, 1 from above and 1 with clear space for words.

For a montage, mark which frames it will use and keep the product at about the same height
and size in those, so it holds still while the world changes behind it. A soft thing that
changes shape from frame to frame (bedding, a jacket) cannot hold still: there the color
holds the montage together, so the flood is kept strict.

## Rules for every frame

- The product is identical in every frame: the same label, color, shape and finish, from
  its own tagged photos and the approved hero.
- No words, captions, logos, marks or graphics. The only words are those on the product.
- The brand's colors, props, surfaces, people and light come from the brand brain and
  override anything here.
- No scene the brand avoids, and no result it does not claim.
- Every frame has the product in it.
- Photographed: a real lens, real light, true textures, shallow focus, fine grain. Not a
  render.
- It opens, pours, fastens and is worn the way the product facts say.

| A carbon-steel pan | Used with the hands | Monochrome flood: charcoal with 1 warm orange | An egg, butter, a wooden spoon, a linen cloth | A hand on the handle; sliding an egg to a plate | A hob, a rail of hung pans, a table | Warm, directional |

The line through all of them: choose a disciplined color world and a kit of props and
places true to the product, then flood the whole set with them.
