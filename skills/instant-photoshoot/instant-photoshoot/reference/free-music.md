# Music for the montage

The montage is always delivered silent. It is also delivered with music only when there is
a track the brand has the right to use in paid advertising. In the app the montage is
silent and the person adds music in their own editor; this file is for work by hand, and
for briefing whoever picks the track.

2 rules:

1. **The music comes from the brand, not from a fixed list.** 2 different brands get
   different music.
2. **Rights before taste.** A track whose license cannot be read and written down is not
   used, however well it fits.

## Step 1: Write a music brief from the brand brain

From `brand-guidelines.md` (`Personality`, `Tone by context`, and `Motion and sound` where
the brand has filled it), the customer avatars (age, the moment) and the mood of the set:

- **Genre:** the 1 to 3 a music supervisor would pick for this brand.
- **Mood and energy:** uplifting, dreamy, confident, gritty, calm, driving.
- **Tempo:** slow, mid or up. The montage cuts 3 or 4 times a second, so mid to up usually
  sits best, with the cuts on the beat.
- **Voice:** instrumental, or light wordless vocal. A sung lyric competes with any
  voiceover added later, and a lyric is words nobody approved.
- **Era:** current, unless the brand is deliberately retro.

A starting map. Adapt it; do not copy it.

| The brand feels | Genres to look in | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Modern beauty, wellness, lifestyle | pop, electropop, indie pop, bright | upbeat, glowing, confident |
| Luxury, fragrance, fine goods | cinematic, ambient, neoclassical, piano, downtempo | elegant, moody, refined |
| Streetwear, youth, bold | hip hop, lo-fi, bass | cool, gritty, confident |
| Clean, technical, minimal | electronic, minimal, synth | crisp, forward |
| Sport, energy, performance | electronic, drum and bass, rock | driving |
| Calm, sleep, self-care, home | ambient, acoustic, lo-fi, piano | soft, soothing |
| Food, playful, family | funk, pop, disco, hand-claps | happy, bouncy |
| Outdoors, natural, craft, workwear | folk, acoustic, indie folk | warm, earthy |

## Step 2: Where the track comes from, in this order

1. **The brand's own licensed music.** A `music` folder with the brand's files, or tracks
   from a library the brand pays for. The best fit and no legal risk. Pick the 3 that match
   the brief.
2. **A free library whose license allows paid advertising.** Read the license of the track
   itself, not the site's front page. Keep a track only if its license allows commercial
   use and allows it to be laid under a video. A license marked non-commercial is out. A
   license that forbids derivative works is out, since a track under a video can count as
   one. A license that asks for credit is fine: the credit is written down and goes in the
   ad's caption.
3. **Nothing.** Deliver the silent montage, say that it is silent and why, and tell the
   person what to supply.

Never a track from someone else's ad, a chart song, or audio lifted from a clip on a social
platform. Never a track described as "royalty free" with no license to point to.

Offer 3 options, different from each other, each laid under the montage so the person
hears it in place. Vary what is offered from run to run.

## Step 3: Listen, choose, write it down

- A person listens to every option against the picture before choosing. A model that reads
  frames cannot hear.
- The chosen version and the silent version are both kept.
- Beside the montage, a short sources note: the track's title, the artist, where it came
  from, its address, its license, and the exact credit line where the license asks for
  one. A track is never implied to be cleared when that could not be confirmed.
