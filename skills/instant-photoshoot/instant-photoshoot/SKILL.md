# Instant Photoshoot

1 product becomes a set of photographs that belong together, and a short montage that
flashes through them. No words on any picture, no end card, no logo added.

**What it is for.** A kit for a launch or a month of posting: a product page gallery, a
carousel, a grid of social posts, an email, and base pictures for ads. The montage is a
hook for a story or a reel, a launch post, or a loop at the top of a page.

**What it is judged on.**
1. The product is the real product, and identical in every frame.
2. The set reads as 1 shoot: lay the frames side by side and they share a color world, a
   light and a kit of props.
3. Every frame has a job and a place it is used.
4. The montage's first frame is the hero, and it works with the sound off.

This skill rests on `skills/production-principles` (tagged reference photos, people, words,
the check after a render, spending). How a finished image prompt is laid out is in
`skills/image-prompt-craft`. Models, tools and prices are in
`skills/production-principles/models.md` and nowhere here.

## The 2 things it can make

| | What is delivered |
|---|---|
| **Pictures only** | 8 to 15 photographs (12 is the most 1 set holds in the app) and a sheet of them side by side |
| **Pictures and the montage** | The same, and the montage: silent, and with music only where the brand has the right to a track |

All pictures are 1 size, chosen for where they will be used (9:16 for a montage on stories
and reels; 4:5 for the feed; 1:1 for a product page).

## Files in this folder

- `reference/scene-taxonomy.md`: how the color world, the props, the places and the frames
  are worked out, and the 15 kinds of frame with the job of each. Read it in full before
  planning.
- `reference/free-music.md`: how music is chosen for the montage, and the rule on rights.
- `scripts/`: 2 small scripts that cut the montage and lay a track under it, for work by
  hand. Their first lines say how each is used. In the app the montage is cut by the app
  itself.

## Rules (each can be checked)

1. **The same product in every frame, from its own photos.** Every frame attaches the
   product's real photos, each tagged with its job. The first frame made is the hero; once a
   person has approved it, it is attached to every later frame as well, beside the real
   photos and never in place of them.
2. **The direction is worked out for this product.** A serum, a jacket, a pan and a bag of
   coffee need different colors, props, places, people and light. No fixed mix of frames.
3. **The brand brain decides the specifics:** the exact colors, the surfaces, the props,
   the people, what is never shown. Where the worked-out direction and the brain disagree,
   the brain wins.
4. **1 color world, held across the set.** That discipline is what makes a set look
   directed and not random.
5. **The color world floods the set, never the product.** The product keeps the colors of
   its photo. When the product is the same color as the flood, the ground is a lighter or a
   darker tone of it so the product's edge stays clear.
6. **Every frame has the product in it, and a job.**
7. **No words on any picture.** The only words are those printed on the product.
8. **Photographed, not rendered:** a real lens, real light, true textures, fine grain.
9. **People** follow production-principles section 6: fictional adults; no generated
   children, nor any part of one (a product for children is shown without a child, or with
   the brand's own uploaded photo); no real person's face; every hand told what to do, never over the label.
10. **The brand's guardrails hold:** no scene the brand avoids, no result it does not claim.
11. **Every picture is looked at before it is called done.**

## The steps

### 1. Ask

> 2 questions before I start:
> 1. Just the photographs, or the photographs and the montage?
> 2. Which exact product, and which variant?

And where the pictures will be used, if it is not already known: that sets the size and
weights the frames.

### 2. Read the brand brain

Read, do not skim. Write down what the direction needs:

- What the product looks like (`Looks like`, `Pack`) and what it is made of.
- What kind of product it is: worn, applied, eaten or drunk, used with the hands, placed in
  a room. 1 or 2 of these.
- The brand's colors, with the role of each, and which is the signature color.
- 3 to 6 real things that belong to this product: what it is made of, what it is used with.
- Who uses it, where and at what moment.
- The brand's photography style, and what it never shows.

### 3. The photos, the truth sheet and the hero

1. Gather the product's real photos and write its truth sheet, as in
   `skills/Photoshoot Factory/Photoshoot Factory/photoshoot-factory/references/01-product-assets.md`.
   No true photo of the exact variant, no shoot.
2. Make frame 1, the hero: the product alone, whole, on a plain ground in the color
   world's main color, soft shadow. It is the test frame.
3. Check it against the truth sheet: the label's words, the shape, the colors, the
   closure. Show it to the person. Nothing else is made until it is approved.

### 4. Work out the direction, then the frames

Read `reference/scene-taxonomy.md` first.

**The direction,** a few lines each: the color world (1 of 4 strategies, and 1 to 3 colors
by hex); the kit of props (3 to 6); the places (3 to 6); how a person meets the product;
the light.

**The frames.** Each frame is 1 of the 15 kinds, bound to the direction (its colors, props,
place, person, light), with its job and where it will be used. The mix is weighted by the
kind of product, not fixed. No 2 frames share a composition. Each is written as a scene
that:

- names the exact product in the same words every time;
- sets the color, the prop, the place and the light from the direction;
- says who is in it and what each hand does, or says nobody is;
- ends with the camera (lens feel, depth, grain) and "no words, no logos, no marks".

**Show it before anything is made:**

> Here is the direction and the list of frames before I make anything.
>
> Direction: {1 paragraph}
>
> Frames:
> {numbered, 1 line each: the kind, what it shows, its job}
>
> Change the color world, the props or any frame, or tell me to go ahead.

### 5. Make the pictures

1. 2 or 3 frames first, checked, then the rest (production-principles section 10).
2. Name them in the order the montage should run: the hero is `01`.
3. Lay them side by side and check every one: the product identical and true; the color
   world held; no words; no 2 alike; each doing its job. Make any miss again, never with
   the same words.
4. Show the sheet to the person.

### 6. The montage (only if asked for)

The pictures cut together fast, with no words: about 9 seconds, a hard cut about 3 or 4
times a second.

- **The first frame is the advert:** the hero, product large and centered.
- **The product holds still while the world changes.** In the frames meant for the montage
  the product sits at about the same height and size in the frame, so the eye stays on it
  across the cuts. Plan this at step 4; it cannot be fixed afterwards. A soft thing that
  changes shape between frames (bedding, a jacket) cannot hold still: there the strict
  color world does that work.
- **No 2 neighbors alike:** a clean frame, then a hand, then a close one, then a place.
- **Neighbors are close in brightness.** A cut from a very dark picture to a very bright one
  several times a second is hard on the eye.
- **The last picture is the hero again,** or the range.
- In 9:16 the product stays inside the safe area given in `skills/image-prompt-craft`,
  section 5.
- If words or a closing card are wanted, that is a later editing step, in the brand's
  typeface. This skill adds none.

### 7. Music (only if asked for)

Read `reference/free-music.md`. The montage is delivered silent. It is also delivered with
music only when there is a track the brand has the right to use in paid advertising. A
person listens to it before it is used.

### 8. Deliver

Say where the set is, how many pictures, their size, each frame's job, and for the montage
its length and the track with its license. Generated pictures are kept apart from the
product's real reference photos and marked as generated.

## When something goes wrong

- **The product changes from frame to frame:** attach the approved hero beside the real
  photos, and say again in the scene the 2 or 3 visible facts that matter most, in the same
  words.
- **The product takes the color of the flood:** say its colors, and that the colored light
  and ground stop at its edge.
- **The set looks like a random batch:** the color world is too loose. Cut the colors to 1
  and its tones, and the props to 3.
- **The montage flickers and nothing registers:** the product moves about too much between
  frames. Reorder so neighbors hold it in the same place, or drop the frames that do not.

## What this reads from the brand brain

A blank field is a question for the brand. It is never guessed.

- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product's entry: `What it is`, `Key features` (what it
  is made of: the props), `Who buys it`, `Variants`, `Looks like`, `Pack`, `How it is
  used`, `Visible result`, `Never show`, `Reference photos`.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette` (name, hex, role: the signature color is the one
  whose role says so; if no role says so, ask); `Photography and imagery style`; `Imagery
  do's and don'ts`; `Brand world` (places, surfaces and props, light and time of day,
  people, never in frame); `Brand guardrails`; for music, `Personality` and `Motion and
  sound` (sound).
- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are`, `The moment`.
- `brain.md`: `What to avoid`, `Product notes`.
- `lessons.json`: how the product is opened, held and used.
- Not in the brain today: which of the 4 color strategies the brand's sets use, and the
  music the brand has the right to use. Both are asked for once and should be kept.
