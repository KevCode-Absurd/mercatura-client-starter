# Product placement: a real object in the scene

Adapted from the supplied “The Product Placement Mindset” and “Place Any Product Into an AI Image” lessons. The reusable method is a sound product reference, an approved scene, evidence for scale and physically coherent integration. The lessons' sample measurements and renderer recommendations are examples, not product facts or provider requirements. Apply the same method across assistants and image/video formats.

For a held bottle, surface hero, macro, garment, wearable, flat lay, beverage replacement, food or tech placement, read the applicable [Product placement technique](product-placement-techniques.md). It also covers proposing a scene when only the product reference exists. Select an operation that serves the approved plan, keeping format and product truth authoritative.

## Establish the product reference

Use a sharp, uncropped view with visible shape, edges, closure, materials and readable branding as the master product reference. A neutral background can help separate the object from its surroundings; an already useful reference does not need a cleanup generation. Keep the original as the authority and name other views by their purpose: front label, side geometry, material, in-hand scale or use.

If the supplied photograph is cluttered, improve separation only when needed for the requested placement. Preserve geometry, proportions, material, label and closure during any cleanup. Removing the background cannot recover a missing label or hidden surface reliably. Do not invent unreadable printing, treat a generated reconstruction as verified product truth, or use an inaccurate cleaned image as the new master. Obtain a better view when the missing detail is essential; otherwise choose a shot the available evidence supports.

## Decide where it belongs

The approved plan decides product presence and purpose: held, worn, supported on a surface or in verified use. Separate reference authority: the product image defines the object; the base scene defines camera, layout and light; an identity reference defines the person. Describe one coherent state and a feasible physical relationship. Keep exact copy and approved features. A wordless or product-absent concept stays that way unless the brief changes.

## Anchor scale with evidence

Use recorded physical dimensions together with a visible comparison grounded in a supplied in-hand/in-use image or another known object. Name what is measured: bottle height, watch case width, handle length. Capacity does not establish exterior dimensions; “100 ml” alone cannot justify a 10 to 12 cm bottle. Do not derive an exact object size from a person's presumed palm or wrist dimensions.

Describe the observed relationship at comparable depth: how much of the palm the object spans, where its edges sit against the wrist, or its size beside a known surface object. A closer product can appear larger than a farther face through perspective while retaining its real dimensions. Avoid universal rules such as “the hand is always larger” or “every watch is narrower than every wrist.” Product prominence comes from crop, camera distance, focus and composition, not secretly enlarging it.

If dimensions are absent, use a reliable scale photo without inventing centimetres or ratios. If neither is available and fit/size matters, record the uncertainty and seek that evidence before presenting the fit as accurate. A plausible-looking hand is not proof of measured scale.

## Integrate light, contact and focus

Describe the support and grip: which hand, supporting fingers, thumb position, legitimate occlusion, object orientation and weight. Fingers can curve behind a bottle without all five digits being visible; rigid packaging retains its shape. Put contact shadows at real contact points and keep required label areas legible without forcing an impossible grip.

Identify the actual key light and make the product receive the same direction, softness and colour as the hand and scene. Reflect nearby skin or room colours where the material warrants them; glass, metal, matte packaging and fabric respond differently. The product's own colour and printing remain fixed under illumination. Contact shadows, cast shadows and reflections serve different physical relationships and cannot substitute for each other.

Match perspective, occlusion, focus falloff, sharpness and capture texture. A product at the face's depth should not look like a separately sharpened cutout. Preserve the chosen format: phone surroundings can remain readable, a campaign can use controlled focus, and a clean studio hero can remain clean. In a scene change, allow the necessary new lighting/reflections while retaining product identity; in a local scale repair, keep the existing camera and light.

## Motion and targeted repair

Approve the integrated still before optional video. Through a lift, turn, release or cut, review scale, grip, object rigidity, label stability, support and the changing shadows/reflections. Correct endpoints alone do not establish a sound interaction.

Name one failure before retrying: oversized object, floating contact, mismatched light, wrong perspective, cutout sharpness or label drift. Change the controlling instruction and preserve other approved choices. If both physical fit and exact artwork cannot be achieved on the selected route, use supported localized editing/compositing or a simpler feasible shot; do not claim exact preservation without inspecting the result. Existing retry limits remain in force.

An optional placement clause:

```text
PRODUCT: use the attached product photo for shape, closure, material, colour and exact printing.
SCALE: [verified dimensions, if supplied] and [observed comparison to the named scale reference at comparable depth]. Keep the real dimensions; use framing for prominence.
CONTACT: [hand or surface], [support/grip and orientation], [legitimate overlap], with contact shadows at the touch points.
INTEGRATION: follow [the existing light source], material-appropriate reflections, perspective and the scene's focus/capture texture.
KEEP: [approved identity, scene, camera, styling and exact copy].
CHECK: product identity, supported size, contact, lighting/focus agreement and label readability.
```
