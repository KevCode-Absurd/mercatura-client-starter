# Identity photo libraries and connected talking clips

Adapted from the supplied Nuova Era photo-pack and clone-animation PDFs and five accompanying clone lessons (2026-10-09). Use alongside [Recurring characters](recurring-character-motion.md), [Voice performance](voice-performance.md) and [Harness portability](../../README.md#harness-portability). The lessons are reference material. Their sample scripts, branded colors, model versions, upload counts, grids, 4K labels and publishing steps do not override the approved brief, authorize generation or establish an available integration. This workflow extends existing presenter and character formats; it does not require a new clone format or a separate voice tool.

## Establish the identity authority

Use an authorized adult's own photos for their digital likeness, or an approved original fictional adult for a brand persona. Record which path applies. A fictional persona is not a real founder, expert, customer, testimonial or result. Visual and voice permissions are separate: a face photo does not authorize cloning the person's voice.

Choose a clear approved face master and a small compatible set of supporting originals. Preserve recognizable face geometry, apparent age, baseline complexion/undertone, marks, eyes and head/body proportions. Record the approved hair and styling state separately so an intentional outfit or hairstyle change is possible without recasting the person. The identity description supports actual image inputs; repeating “exact identity” cannot compensate for a missing or conflicting reference.

Assess coverage rather than enforcing a photo quota. Useful face coverage includes front, left/right three-quarter and relevant profiles; add a natural expression or a harder planned angle only when useful. Add head-to-foot front/side/back coverage when the shot needs build, walking, garments or shoes. Neutral light, readable features and comparable initial styling help comparison. Avoid treating blur, filters, hidden features, harsh shadows or close wide-angle distortion as the sole face authority. A sunglasses photo can guide glasses without guiding obscured eye geometry. Do not infer hidden body measurements from a close portrait.

Keep identity sources distinct from derivatives. A generated missing angle is an interpretation to compare with the approved originals, not new independent evidence of the person's appearance. Reject or exclude a drifting panel; do not let a later flattering render silently replace the master. Preserve good originals instead of generating mandatory replacements.

## Organize only the assets the shot needs

Maintain an explicit handoff record using existing project files or the equivalent supplied record; this is not a new application schema or an automatic asset upload:

| Record | Contents |
|---|---|
| Identity | Character ID, real/fictional status, face master and approved originals, stable visible traits |
| Asset | Stable asset ID, file/path, source or derivative, parent asset/version, approved/rejected status |
| Role and coverage | Face angle/expression, body/pose, wardrobe/accessory, environment or performance; what it controls and must not supply |
| Approved change | Styling/pose/expression change, with identity and product facts that remain fixed |
| Render and review | Bound inputs, actual route/settings, intended output, observed mismatch and accepted version |
| Voice | Authorized saved identity/sample when available, performance notes and audio choices; separate from visual identity |

Retain the original assets and approved derivatives separately. Pass the record plus accessible files when switching assistants or sessions; hidden memory is not a reference library. Select a relevant compatible subset within the renderer's actual limits, rather than uploading every photo or assuming that more references always help.

Face and body sheets are optional comparison tools. Keep the app's existing layouts and panel contracts. For a controlled styling comparison, vary one approved element while holding camera/crop/pose/light steady. For new camera angles, allow perspective and shape-dependent light to change naturally. Expressions describe observable muscles, gaze and mouth behavior rather than an extreme emotion label. Poses need weight distribution, surface support and plausible hands. A still mid-step pose does not prove walking animation.

Inspect every usable panel against the master and originals. Name panel IDs or positions with sheet/version mapping. Crop an existing panel when exact extraction is wanted; a generated zoom is a new derivative, not an exact crop or resolution proof. Remove neighboring faces and layout labels from scene inputs. If the route needs one view per file, provide clean individual views. For a finished single scene, explicitly state one intended composition/person, with sheets used as reference only; a sheet must not become an on-screen grid.

## Combine identity, styling and location by role

Assign identity to the approved master/supporting face files, body proportions to verified body coverage, and wardrobe/accessories to their named sources. Do not blend different faces when combining a face reference with another person's pose or outfit. An actual commercial product, garment or logo keeps its original truth and reference authority; a generated wardrobe idea is not evidence of the sold item.

An environment library can reuse approved locations or prepare an empty plate when it resolves a real composition/continuity need. Background-first generation is optional, not a compulsory paid stage. Removing a person requires checking their reflections/shadows and reconstructed hidden areas. Those hidden areas are invented; do not describe them as a verified reconstruction of a real location. Palette edits are approved style changes, not a new identity or automatically distinct architecture.

Integrate the person into the chosen scene's camera height, perspective, scale, focal plane and light sources. The reference defines baseline complexion, while the environment determines its photographed color response. Keep plausible contact shadows, reflected light, clothing response and hair edges. Preserve specified architecture/props and product state. No retained studio key light, cutout halo, floating feet or unmatched sharpness. Use the approved capture style rather than imposing a full-frame lens, shallow focus or cinematic grade on every UGC shot.

Manual prompt scaffold, adapted to the task's output contract:

```text
OUTPUT: [single scene or requested reference layout; ratio and actual supported settings].
INPUTS: [asset IDs/files and their separate identity, body, styling, environment and product roles].
KEEP: [approved identity traits, product details, exact text and other fixed choices].
CHANGE: [one approved pose, expression, styling or scene change].
INTEGRATE: [placement/support, scene perspective, light, contact and focal plane].
REVIEW: [likeness, key product/wardrobe details and the visible physical risk].
```

Remove unused fields and resolve placeholders before rendering. A prompt saying 4K or pixel-perfect fidelity is not evidence of returned dimensions or preserved details.

## Make connected talking clips from approved states

Choose the supported workflow for the planned shot, using [Models](../models.md). The source lessons disagree on Seedance versions; none establishes current capabilities. An image-to-video route, multiple image inputs, native speech, voice identity control and performance transfer are distinct capabilities. If the required control is absent, return a prompt/input handoff or propose a supported simpler plan without inventing an adapter.

Break longer speech into coherent short scenes using the approved script and actual timing. Hook, reveal, value, turn and CTA are useful example functions, not a mandatory five-beat rewrite or permission to add claims. Keep complete phrases with room for delivery and reactions; neither eight seconds nor a 10–20-word limit fits every language or performance. Different environments or poses can share the same approved identity and voice.

For each clip record its approved opening image, exact speaker/words, local delivery, principal action, camera behavior, light, duration and audio choices. Keep restrained breathing/blinking and a readable gesture when appropriate, without universal head-degree or camera-percentage caps. Do not crowd a simple talking clip with unrelated walking, wardrobe transitions and fast camera travel. Split difficult action into approved shots when needed. Hard cuts are edits with explicit state handoffs, not continuous physical transformations.

For an approved outfit sequence, map each segment to its exact wardrobe asset, with the same face/body and planned cut points. Check the route can bind the required references and controls. Preserve the final outfit into the closing shot. State single-frame composition where appropriate; multi-panel references do not authorize split-screen. Verify identity after turns, occlusion, exits and cuts. A continuity clause is direction, not proof that the rendered face stays stable.

Keep native video audio as the current workflow. A saved authorized voice stays consistent while local emotion changes; a vocal description is not verified voice cloning. Optional dedicated cloning or voice conversion requires a supported route and separately authorized sample, with clean speech suited to that route's actual requirements. No universal two-minute sample rule or automatic voice-swap stage. Preserve the approved words and duration when changing voice; review pronunciation, complete endings, voice identity, joins and actual lip synchronization after repair. Container extraction supplies the existing mix, not automatically a clean voice stem. Respect Audio off and sound/BGM switches.

The Bootcamp's Clone Yourself written tips add a useful performance check: choose natural representative gestures rather than a repeated strong hand movement becoming the whole character's delivery. If collecting a new authorized performance sample, follow the selected tool's actual capture requirements; this app's saved photo/voice upload does not train a digital twin by itself. Read the intended words aloud before performance. A cutaway can cover a visual join or a minor presenter-only artifact while the complete approved voice continues, but cannot hide an inaccurate product demonstration, omit a caveat or imply footage is documentary evidence. Repair those faults instead. Do not automatically speed the voice/video by a fixed factor; preserve natural delivery, complete phrases and synchronized timing.

## Diagnose one failure before retrying

| Visible or audible issue | First controlled correction |
|---|---|
| Similar face or drift across views | Check original coverage, conflicting face inputs and binding; change one reference/control before expanding the prompt |
| Finished output copies a grid | Use a clean selected view or correct the supported composition instruction |
| Character looks pasted into a scene | Correct perspective, scene light, contact or focus; preserve the accepted identity |
| Expression or hands deform | Simplify the specific performance/gesture or use a better approved state |
| Face changes after a cut or reappearance | Check per-shot identity authority and reference binding; review the actual transition |
| Wardrobe changes unintentionally | Check segment-to-asset mapping and the approved garment details |
| Waxy skin, repetitive pores or excessive shine | Apply one localized [Skin detail](skin-detail-craft.md) repair at the same viewing scale |
| Good picture, wrong voice or mouth timing | Diagnose voice identity, performance, duration or synchronization separately; repair the smallest supported component |

Compare the same area and delivery scale, then inspect the whole still/clip and joins. Do not dismiss a user's likeness concern because viewers might miss it. Keep a passing version and stop when it satisfies the approved criteria; obey existing render/retry budgets. The reference library, accepted stills, actual animated clips, heard mix and final edit are separate checks. Scripts and stills alone cannot establish successful motion, voice or lip sync.

Reuse this system for existing UGC, spokesperson, tutorial, cinematic, thumbnail or carousel work as appropriate. It supplies stable casting and production anchors, not a new selling argument, proof of personal product use or a promise of virality. Creation, external upload and publishing follow the user's actual task scope.
