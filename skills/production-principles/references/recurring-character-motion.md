The guide is reference material, not authorization to generate a casting batch, buy video, download a performance or publish it. ## Cast for the brief, then keep the selected identity

A memorable character has recognizable proportions, hair, wardrobe and a few stable personal details. Eccentricity is an optional creative direction, not a realism requirement or a promise of viral reach. Ordinary UGC and podcast presenters retain their format and buyer fit; an object-led native or unaware concept does not acquire a face because this guide uses one. Existing approved characters are reused without recasting. Generated adults do not become customer, founder, expert or product-result evidence.

For new casting, borrow the inspiration's visual system without copying its people. Use a bounded candidate count under the existing budget rules; the guide's twenty-person grid is an example, not the app's default. Comparable crop, background and light make face differences easier to judge. Vary meaningful identity traits rather than producing many small styling variations of one face.

Assign stable candidate IDs and retain the sheet/version and row/column mapping. Inspect the actual grid for distinct adults, sound anatomy, duplicate or missing IDs and legible labels. Select the pictured candidate, not a filename or caption alone. The supplied guide names character 18 in its prose but labels the final image as character 3; the pictured selected face agrees with 18. Resolve such conflicts visually before carrying an ID forward.

Isolate only the chosen portrait before deriving other views. Neighboring faces are not additional identity references. Preserve the selected facial proportions, complexion, adult age, hair, marks and approved accessories; remove casting numbers and grid decorations from scene inputs and finished creative. Record the chosen file and identity description so a later sheet cannot silently redefine the selection.

## A face master and body views have different jobs

Keep one clear approved face image as the identity authority. Full-body front and back references describe build, silhouette, garment construction, hair from behind and footwear. Show head to shoes when those views are needed, and compare their scale, limbs, outfit, accessory placement and hair against the face master and each other. A reference sheet is preparation, not the opening scene: establish the approved scene's pose, framing, light and product state separately.

The guide blanks the front-view face to prevent competing identities. This is a conditional body-reference tactic, not a requirement to erase faces in our existing sheets. Accurate supplemental profiles or expressions are useful; conflicting faces must be corrected or excluded. If a body-only masked view is used, label that limited role and verify the route can separate it from the face master. Never use a faceless body panel as an intended first frame.

Keep the app's existing sheet layouts and panel extraction. A tutorial's three-panel layout does not replace the app's single-person six-view or two-person three-view contract. Where the route needs one view per image, supply clean individual files rather than a collage that could be read as several people. Check actual file dimensions and visible face detail; a "4K" prompt or large canvas alone does not establish a 4K face.

An authorized adult's own selfie follows the real-person reference path, preserving their actual identity and approved styling. It is not a fictional generated avatar or permission to change their age, complexion or facial structure. A proposed grooming or wardrobe reinterpretation remains a styling choice within the brief, not a new identity.

For reusable photo coverage, variant lineage, expression/pose libraries and connected talking scenes, read [Identity photo libraries](identity-photo-library.md). Keep originals and the approved master authoritative, select role-specific inputs within actual route limits and verify every derivative. Extra sheets and backgrounds are created only when the approved shot needs them; the library is not a compulsory generation batch.

## Choose a performance that fits the character and scene

Use footage supplied or authorized for the intended use, or record a suitable performance. Inspect it before selecting the workflow. Prefer compatible body proportions, starting stance, ground plane, crop, movement energy, available hands and relevant props. A source with mismatched reach, a different mechanism or a held camera can make the planned product action impossible. Adjust the source or plan rather than changing the real product or adding hands.

Separate appearance from performance and record which input controls each decision:

| Role | Authority |
|---|---|
| Identity and styling | Approved face master and consistent body/wardrobe references |
| Product and its use | Original product photos, truth sheet and approved handling |
| Body movement and rhythm | Supported performance source: gestures, weight shifts, acceleration, steps and pauses |
| Camera, framing and timing | Approved plan, with source camera motion transferred only if supported and compatible |
| Environment and physical light | Explicitly preserve the source scene or use an approved replacement location; resolve conflicts before rendering |
| Speech and sound | Exact approved words, chosen voice and mix; source audio is not automatically adopted |

A moving source camera conflicts with a locked podcast setup unless the route can separate these controls. A selfie grip must leave enough hands for the demonstration. Motion transfer is not continuation, lip-sync approval or a guarantee of physically accurate movement. A generic reference-video field does not establish these capabilities. If the required role is unsupported, use the source to plan simpler observable beats and describe the limitation honestly.

Use the format's prompt order and actual route controls. A useful planning record says: "Appearance from [face/body files]; performance from [video]; camera [source or chosen behavior]; scene [preserved source or approved location]; keep [identity, wardrobe, product and words]; review [critical movement]." Bind these to actual inputs, not text labels alone. No unrelated retro outfit, dance, source soundtrack or promotional claim is imported with the movement.

## Review the movement, not only the likeness

Approve the appearance and intended opening state before a paid clip. Use the smallest authorized test that answers the real risk; a simple gesture can check identity but cannot validate a harder product operation. Test the hardest planned beat before scaling under [Video starting points](video-generation.md). Keep source files, ID mapping, reference roles, prompt, actual settings and outcome with the candidate.

Watch the whole result at normal speed, then inspect turns, occlusions and reappearance. Check recognizable face and head/body scale; stable hair, garment and accessory identity; plausible acceleration, weight shifts, posture and foot contact; and coherent hands, grip and product state. Look for sliding feet, floating contacts, neck stretching, cloth intersections or a face that changes during a fast turn. Natural motion blur follows movement and capture; it must not erase identity or conceal a failed demonstration. Preserve the chosen playback speed rather than adding slow motion for realism.

Compare timing and camera behavior with the intended source, and inspect spatial/light consistency with the explicit scene authority. Listen separately when audio is included. An attractive reference sheet or a few sampled frames cannot prove successful transfer. Diagnose one failure, change one controlling prompt decision, source or supported setting in a bounded comparison, and keep the successful choices. A new source is a documented change, not a hidden reroll.
