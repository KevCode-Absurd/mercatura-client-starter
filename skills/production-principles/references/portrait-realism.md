## Review the moving face

Watch the complete clip at normal speed first, then inspect blink closure and reopening, head adjustment and any visible mouth/cheek movement frame by frame. Compare eye shape/color, lid motion, facial proportions, hairline, visible teeth, complexion, local marks and skin detail before, during and after movement, including occlusion/reappearance. Look for texture crawling/flicker, contrast pumping, sudden smoothing or multiplied marks, an eye changing after a blink, unstable teeth, background bending or a snap back to the source pose. Distinguish unexplained changes from plausible blur, angle or light effects. A clean first and last frame cannot establish a stable performance.

| Failure during motion | One targeted correction |
|---|---|
| Face reshapes or marks migrate on a turn | Clarify the approved identity reference and reduce the unnecessary turn amplitude; compare that one change |
| Pores flicker/crawl or contrast pumps | Specify face-attached local appearance; remove conflicting sharpness/texture cues instead of adding more pores |
| Skin becomes smoother during speech | Remove unintended beauty/retouch cues and preserve the approved local detail through mouth/cheek movement |
| Exposure/color/light flickers | Clarify actual fixed sources and stable capture response; retain legitimate shape-dependent shading |
| Camera wanders or expression exaggerates | Reduce the diagnosed camera movement or mouth/cheek/eye amplitude; keep approved dialogue/action intact |

If a head adjustment causes identity drift, reduce that motion. If camera drift feels ungrounded, use a stationary shot. Change one controlling decision per bounded comparison while retaining the source and the parts that work. A still upscale is separate finishing; it cannot repair temporal face instability in an existing video. Follow [Motion review](video-generation.md) and the existing spend/retry limits.
