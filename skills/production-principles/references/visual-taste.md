# Visual taste and campaign direction

Carry the decoded reference into the reusable [direction brief](prompt-decisions.md): purpose/feeling, visible scene, view/light, input roles, fixed/open choices and success criteria. Its role determines what transfers; a proposed luxury, atmospheric or editorial treatment never silently overrides user/brand/format direction. Keep this record separate from provider-specific wording and compare each result against it before changing one diagnosed part.

## Decode before directing

Look at the actual image, not only its written analysis. Extract these decisions:

| Decision | What to record |
|---|---|
| Central idea | What is happening visually, and how it serves this frame's selling job |
| Camera | Height, angle, distance feel, crop, apparent compression or distortion, focus hierarchy |
| Light | Source, direction, hardness, fill, highlight and shadow behaviour |
| Colour | Dominant family, contrast, accent and where each appears |
| Composition | First, second and third points of attention; product, set and quiet-space proportions |
| Materials | How metal, glass, fabric, water and skin differ under the light |
| Memorable detail | The single device worth transferring, rather than a list of decorative details |

Separate observation from inference. An image may suggest macro optics or hard sunlight; it does not reveal a verified focal length, aperture, location or light rig. Use an effective camera description instead of inventing specifications.

Turn the decode into a short recipe with editable slots: `[REAL PRODUCT]`, `[APPROVED SUBJECT]`, `[BRAND WORLD]`, `[VISUAL DEVICE]`. State what transfers, what changes, and what remains fixed. A saved recipe must be useful without copying the source's brand, person, wardrobe design or wording.

If the reveal distracts from the product or cannot show its real use, choose a simpler device. Metallic, wet, Mediterranean styling is one reference language, not a category default.

## Reflections and fantasy need an explicit mode

**Literal reflection:** the reflected scene follows the viewpoint and reflective surface. Curvature changes apparent size and perspective; tint affects the reflected colours; glints may obscure small regions. The reflected subject must fit a plausible position relative to the lens and camera. Inspect for duplicate people, impossible horizons, inconsistent light and a flat rectangular picture pasted into a curved surface.

**Editorial scene within a lens:** a deliberately invented second world or duplicated subject is allowed when the brief calls for it. Name that storytelling device and render the surfaces and lighting consistently. Do not call a physically impossible scene an optically accurate reflection merely because the prompt contains “realistic.”

In either mode, the reflected scene is subordinate to the frame's job. A reveal that disappears on a phone needs a larger, simpler reflected subject, stronger separation or a different crop. Macro crops may cut a face intentionally; they must preserve sound anatomy and the selling detail. Product cropping follows the approved frame plan.

## Coherence and variety together

For translating a reference into original ideas, use [Memorable visual ideas](memorable-visual-ideas.md). A single motif can connect objects, costume, environment and action at different scales; contrast keeps the hero readable. Invent a new premise and payoff suited to the audience rather than copying the source world with another logo. Record why a viewer might wait, replay or share, and distinguish that hypothesis from measured performance. Surreal treatment is optional direction, not a substitute for product evidence or every format's default.

Choose a few constants for the set: palette relationships, light family, material treatment, product identity and level of stylisation. Change shot scale, angle, environment, action and visual device according to each frame's job. Coherence does not require identical compositions or casting; reuse an identity only where continuity calls for it.

For testing one concept, keep the brief and message fixed and vary one meaningful axis per comparison:

- Material treatment of the set or a prop, while the actual product material stays true.
- Environment or reflected world, with relighting allowed and a reason tied to the buyer.
- Photographic language: direct flash, overcast daylight, hard sun, or another approved treatment.

A controlled comparison is different from exploring a whole new concept. For broad exploration, change the buyer, argument, format or device deliberately and label that change. Do not quietly combine several changed axes and claim to know which improved the result. The number of renders follows the request and budget; the lesson's three variations are an exercise, not an automatic paid batch.

## A useful reference record

Organise by retrievable technique and purpose, rather than “beautiful” or “cinematic.” Suggested facets: reflection, macro, hard sun, direct flash, material contrast, crop, palette, product kind and frame job. Preserve the source address when supplied, provenance when known, the decode, one reusable recipe and the failure to watch for. Mark unknown or synthetic provenance; do not infer real photography from appearance.

Prefer verified photographs when learning real lighting or optics. AI art can teach an art-direction idea, but inspect hands, repeated objects, typography, reflections and depth before treating it as technical evidence. A campaign board can communicate shared direction; individual frames need their own records. Keep inspiration examples separate from the brand's authoritative product assets. Existing reference-library intake and storage rules still apply.

## Select and learn

Judge the result against the decoded concept and its intended use, not simply a pleasing face. Check product truth and legibility first, then visual hierarchy, brand fit, material rendering and reflection mode. Compare the reference, base and variations side by side: what stayed, what changed, what failed, and what to keep next time. A convincing render establishes visual quality; only measured brand results can establish advertising performance.
