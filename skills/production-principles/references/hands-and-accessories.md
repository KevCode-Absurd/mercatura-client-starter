# Hands, nails and small accessories

Adapted from the supplied *Hands, Nails & Small Accessories* lesson and three still examples: a resting manicure, a hand holding a tube and a hand gripping a metal clip. Use for visible hands in UGC, podcast, native, unaware and product imagery, plus motion review. The burgundy/nude manicure, blank tube, silver clip, female casting, phone/macro framing and no-text examples are optional studies, not defaults or permission to generate a batch. Actual product photos, approved styling, format and handling rules remain authoritative.

## Plan the interaction before describing polish

Use **pose → visible anatomy → nails → object/accessory → contact/light**. Name left/right hand, its location and action, the thumb/fingers supporting the object, and what the other hand does. For a dedicated study, one clear hand and one main action reduce overlapping geometry. Real tasks needing two hands still use two, with a camera setup that frees them; never simplify away a required grip, opening mechanism or product-use step.

- **Resting:** choose a surface, wrist direction and relaxed finger spacing, with plausible support/shadow.
- **Holding:** describe actual grip and contact points, which fingers curve behind the object, and its orientation/weight. A thumb-index pinch works for a small clip, not automatically for every product. Hand/arm proportions and product scale come from approved references.
- **Showing nails:** frame the hand/wrist and orient visible nails to the lens. A full-hand study can show four fingers and a thumb separated comfortably; natural nail orientation still follows each digit.

An unobscured complete adult hand has five digits, each connected naturally through joints/palm/wrist, with distinct fingertips and one nail per visible nail-bearing tip. In a grip, crop or perspective, some digits/nails are legitimately hidden. State that occlusion; do not add extra digits to satisfy an “all five visible” prompt. Include the whole hand/wrist when the study calls for it; a purposeful crop is valid for a scene. Avoid fused digits, twisted joints, repeated tips, disconnected wrists and object intersections.

## Nails belong to the hand

Specify only the approved nail **shape/length → color → finish**, retaining natural nail beds and subtle cuticles. Bare/short nails, glossy polish and soft matte are choices, not mandatory femininity or grooming rules. Gloss highlights follow the curved nail surface and actual light; matte polish stays even without liquid-like shine. Do not duplicate nails, melt polish across cuticles or sharpen skin into an etched pattern. Keep fine joint creases and hand texture proportional to crop/light and consistent with the approved complexion and age; see [Skin detail craft](skin-detail-craft.md).

## Give objects weight and accessories a position

For a new close study, choose one purposeful object/accessory; keep the approved count in an existing scene. State its exact position, material and scale against the hand. Rings follow the chosen finger; bracelets follow the wrist. A held clip aligns with the fingers' grip and its visible metal reflections, while a worn clip grips hair. Do not interchange held/worn states without an action.

For worn clips, piercings and skin ink, the [physical-contact section of Skin detail craft](skin-detail-craft.md) uses position → attachment → overlap → contact → surface response. Select the response for the material: a clip compresses a lock, a stud follows its approved contour, and matte tattoo lines follow skin without a raised edge or object shadow. Body-relative position/design stays fixed through angle changes while illumination responds naturally.

Contact includes correct overlap/occlusion, gentle plausible finger pressure and source-consistent contact shadows/reflections. A hard object retains its shape rather than bending to fit the hand. The example's blank lip-balm tube does not authorize stripping real product lettering, changing packaging or substituting a generic prop. Keep the label clear and the product's actual handling/scale rules. Soft side light and controlled phone flash are alternatives when the format permits; macro blur does not override UGC focus or podcast cameras.

A still clause can follow: “The [left/right] adult hand [pose/action], with [specified fingers] supporting [approved object] at [contact points]. [Other digits] curve naturally behind it; only anatomically visible digits/nails appear. Keep [approved nail shape/color/finish], natural nail beds, cuticles and joint creases at this crop. Preserve [accessory count/position/material], true object scale, overlap and contact shadows under [actual scene light].” Keep the other hand and camera support physically possible.

## Review stills and the whole movement

For a real brand product, [Product placement](product-placement.md) pairs verified dimensions with a visible scale anchor and inherited scene light/focus. A bottle's capacity is not its height; an assumed palm size is not a measurement. Preserve rigid packaging, actual use and original printing when preparing a cleaner reference or mending the grip.

At delivery size and enlarged, inspect digit connectivity/visibility, joints, nails/cuticles, wrist, grip, scale, label visibility and contact. Through a lift, rotation, release or cut, check before/during/after for fingers merging, nails changing, slipping grip, floating/intersecting objects or accessories changing count/size. Highlights may travel naturally; nail styling and object identity do not change silently. Clean endpoints alone do not prove a sound interaction.

| Failure | Targeted correction |
|---|---|
| Extra/fused digits | Clarify pose/occlusion or reduce unnecessary overlap; never force all five into a natural grip |
| Awkward crop | Include the hand/wrist boundary needed to understand the action, without changing a purposeful scene crop unnecessarily |
| Pasted/floating object | Name supporting fingers, contact points, correct overlap, scale and pressure/shadow |
| Artificial nails or liquid polish | Restore nail beds/cuticles and curved highlights; request even polish with controlled shine |
| Accessory changes size or floats | Reassert approved count, exact position, scale and attachment |

Change one diagnosed element while preserving approved identity, styling, framing and product. Compare at the same crop/light/display size and use existing retry limits. If geometry remains unstable, revise the pose/camera approach within the actual task rather than hiding a broken demonstration.
