# Audio direction, layers and selective repair

Adapted from the user-supplied *The AI Audio Landscape* lesson (2026-10-09). It supplies production principles, not a request to subscribe, clone a voice, generate music or replace an approved script. Named services, verified capabilities and local connection status belong in [Models](../models.md). No audio samples or finished mixes accompanied this lesson.

The subsequent *The complete AI audio workflow* lesson is reference material. The user's explicit current direction is to generate audio together with video. Video jobs expose Audio, Scene sounds and Background music choices, plus optional music direction. Audio off delivers silence, including dialogue; Scene sounds off keeps approved dialogue while requesting no ambience/effects; BGM is off by default and can be requested explicitly, including when a format normally omits it. Keep these preferences across clips and manual handoff prompts. They lock when clips start; scene sounds/music are prompt controls on a combined track, not separately editable stems. Listen to native output before approving it. Do not launch separate speech, cloning, effects or music generation merely because the lesson demonstrates those tools.

The user's *Our complete ai audio workflow* adds a repeatable direction/review sequence: script, voice, music, important effects, mix, final listening. Treat these as decisions to settle, not six mandatory generation calls. With the current native route, express them in one coherent video/audio brief and respect the user's audio switches. Voice carries the message, optional music shapes the intended feeling, and selected effects mark meaningful events; professional sound does not require every layer.

## Choose by the audio job, not by a platform ranking

| Job | Intended input and control | Review requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Voice generation | Approved words plus a selected voice and delivery direction | Exact words, pronunciation, identity, pacing and intended tone |
| Voice cloning | Authorized clean samples establish a reusable voice identity; performance/script direction remains separate | Recognizable voice and approved use; a clone does not establish the truth of its lines |
| Voice changing | A recorded performance supplies intended rhythm/emotion while the selected output voice supplies identity | Words, timing and delivery against the source, then any on-screen sync; preservation is not guaranteed |
| Music, ambience or effects | A separate brief describes the layer's audible job, texture, duration and placement | Fit to the scene and format, clean edit points and no masking of speech |
| Native video audio | A supported video route creates picture and requested sound together | Full listening and visible-event/lip-sync review; inspect whether separate stems or replacement controls exist |

Voice cloning changes identity availability; voice changing transforms an existing performance. Neither is the same as dubbing, automatic lip sync or putting narration under silent footage. Choose a native route for an integrated performance when its controls suffice; choose supported separate audio/editing when a specific pronunciation, delivery or layer needs independent control. A preset ID, uploaded sample or speech instruction alone does not establish an implemented route. If the needed control is unavailable, record the limitation and prepare a handoff rather than silently changing the workflow.

Use the speaker's own voice or a voice authorized for the intended project. Record source ownership/permission and relevant provider usage terms when cloning or converting a real voice; a sample being downloadable does not settle those uses. A fictional voice is not a real customer's or expert's endorsement. Check current commercial/export conditions for the chosen route when relevant to delivery, without adopting the lesson's blanket tool rankings, subscription advice or guarantees.

## Plan audio before finalizing picture timing

Start from the approved script and scene/shot plan. Choose voice identity, language/accent, pace, conversational energy and observable performance beats; mark meaningful pauses, contact sounds and any intentional quiet. A rough visual edit can establish cue positions, but approve actual speech timing before locking shots that must contain it. Narration-led work may establish the voice first; a music-led edit may use a chosen beat grid. The lesson's picture-first order is one workflow, not a reason to discover too late that dialogue cannot fit.

Record voice, room bed, incidental action cues and music/none as distinct layer decisions. Add only sounds that help the intended evidence or scene. Not every visible motion needs an effect; a camera move or small gesture can remain quiet. Phone UGC defaults to no music; a quiet podcast keeps its existing music policy unless the user explicitly changes it; a wordless satisfying hook adds no voice. Code-built motion reels retain their approved timeline and audio build method. The audio lesson does not switch a format or add a generated track automatically.

## Direct delivery while retaining approved words

For a voice profile or performance-ready script handoff, use [Voice identity and spoken performance](voice-performance.md), adapted from the supplied voice generator/scriptwriter packages. Separate stable identity from local energy, emphasis, useful pauses and phrase endings. The same record serves the assisting scriptwriter and native video direction now, and a supported dedicated speech route if chosen later. Profile/intensity suggestions are creative direction, not automatic subscriptions, tags or provider settings.

At the script-drafting stage, read the hook and body aloud: check natural phrasing, performable sentence length, pace and emotional progression. Rewrite awkward wording before approval. Once approved, delivery cues may direct that text but cannot silently rewrite it. Describe the intended listener and conversational energy instead of turning every line into an exaggerated emotional beat.

Work in performable phrases or speaker turns, preserving enough neighboring context for coherent emphasis and joins. Sentence length and punctuation can influence a read, but arbitrary fragments, repetition, capitalization or dramatic ellipses can also produce shouting or unnatural rhythm. Use the selected route's actual pause/emotion controls; unsupported tags may be spoken aloud. Keep pronunciation notes or supported phonetic aliases separate from the canonical script, captions, brand/product names and claims.

The lesson's stability/similarity/style values are provider/model-specific examples, not universal settings or controls exposed by native video. Verify a selected separate voice route's supported settings before applying numeric values or bracketed emotion tags. Choose the voice to fit the approved character, language/accent and format. Compare another take only for an identified mismatch; keep a first take that already passes rather than requiring multiple paid generations.

Describe a particular delivery: who speaks, to whom, at what energy/pace, where the useful pause falls and how the phrase ends. A dry sample of one speaker without competing voices/music is useful where the route requires an identity or performance reference. Keep intended identity references distinct from performance, words and ambient sound; do not inherit every noise or recording flaw as the desired final mix.

Changing the wording, breaking a sentence into a different claim, adding fillers or repeating a line is script revision. Proposed rewrites return to that stage before production. The lesson's rewritten example is not permission to paraphrase an approved ad. If timing fails, reduce competing action or revise the authorized timing/plan rather than truncating syllables or accelerating a natural performance to fit.

## Repair the smallest useful speech unit

Listen to the first result; keep it if it meets the brief. A refinement step does not require another paid render. Name the failure—mispronunciation, misplaced emphasis, flat delivery, identity drift, a clipped word or masked speech—and alter its controlling direction or layer within existing retry/budget rules.

Replace only the weak phrase/turn when the route supports it and the neighboring performance can remain coherent. Retain speaker, model/settings, approved text and sound character; include enough context to check the lead-in, breath, sentence ending and next turn. A mid-word splice is not a repair. If a local replacement creates voice, pace or room discontinuity, expand to the smallest coherent unit and record the change. Listen to the repaired unit inside the full edit, not just in isolation.

For an on-camera speaker, replacement voice can change syllable timing. Review the actual mouth movement again; use supported re-performance/lip-sync or revise the plan if needed. Narration over cutaways has no lip-sync obligation, but still needs the right visual cue timing. Where native audio is flattened with ambience/effects, do not claim a speech layer was isolated unless it actually was; preserve or rebuild the necessary background without leaving duplicate dialogue.

## Build a restrained sound scene

Keep separate editable assets/stems where the workflow permits. A room bed establishes acoustic continuity across cuts; avoid accidental silence, abrupt ambience changes or obvious loop seams unless the story calls for the change. Speech should belong to the depicted recording setup: close podcast microphones, a soft room, an open kitchen or outdoor distance are different decisions. Add scene response in the mix without baking unrelated music/noise into a voice identity reference.

For an effect, name its source/material, action, audible character, onset/contact/end cue, perspective and relative level: an actual cap seating with a short click is different from a generic dramatic impact. Align the audible contact with the visible operation. Sound must not imply a mechanism, result or material the product does not have; embellished effects cannot prove its performance. Silence and subtle room tone are valid choices.

If music is permitted, specify its job, mood, tempo/energy, instrument palette, vocal/instrumental choice, density, duration and edit/reveal points. Keep space for speech and reduce music/effects during important words rather than simply raising the whole mix. Do not import a source's soundtrack or use an unrelated famous voice/song as its identity. A consistent audible signature may help a recurring character or brand, but is optional approved direction, not a compulsory jingle.

Choose the musical feeling from the scene's purpose: tension, energy, nostalgia, restraint, mystery or comedy can lead to different tempo, instrumentation and density. For the current BGM control, describe an instrumental bed and its emotional job; keep that character across clips instead of starting a new track at every cut. Place effects on a reveal, contact, change or other important beat, with room for a quiet reaction; a whoosh, impact or notification is not a default for every gesture.

When editable layers actually exist, organize voice, music and SFX separately, with room ambience identifiable. During dialogue, voice has priority, meaningful effects stay below it and music supports both. An effect may lead during a speech pause; this hierarchy is about intelligibility, not fixed fader numbers. The lesson's -18 to -24 dB example is not a defined loudness/export target: identify the reference and whether a value is track gain, peak or measured loudness before using it. Balance against the actual recordings and reduce masking by ear. In native combined audio, request these relative levels in words; do not claim independently set track gains or stems.

## Review the delivered mix

Listen without picture for complete approved words, pronunciation, intended tone and recognizable speaker identity. Listen on headphones for clicks, clipping, harshness, unwanted noise and repaired-line/loop seams; check laptop speakers and especially phone speakers for intelligibility and layers that disappear or overwhelm speech. Then review the full picture and sound for lips, action contacts, cuts, cue timing and continuity. Both voices in a conversation keep distinct identities without unintended overlap.

Use the destination/format's actual export and loudness requirements where defined; file normalization or an audio-present flag is not evidence of a good mix. Measure targets when reporting them and keep the final export, source takes/stems, settings and repair notes. A transcript or sampled visual frames cannot establish sound quality. Record unperformed listening checks rather than claiming a pass.
