## Assemble the image from scene to detail

Use **scene foundation → local skin → relevant physical details → whole/local review → optional motion** as an order of decisions, not five compulsory renders. Combine compatible approved decisions in the first focused prompt; refine only a visible need within the existing render cap. Tiny detail cannot rescue wrong identity, framing, product, camera perspective or light, so establish those first.

1. **Foundation:** use the approved adult, natural expression/gaze, wardrobe and purposeful setting. Set the requested ratio/crop, real camera position/support, capture look and main light direction before microscopic cues. A handheld home selfie is one UGC choice; a propped demonstration, fixed podcast two-shot or native story moment keeps its own setup. Preserve product truth and approved text. Minimal makeup and a bedroom are not defaults.
2. **Local skin:** apply the face-zone map only at the actual distance, focus, coverage and light. Nose/inner-cheek pores may read more clearly than forehead/outer-cheek texture; fine hairs catch existing side light without becoming sharp across the whole face. A single visible cheek hair belongs to a deliberate close study when useful, not every feed portrait. Retain approved tone, age, marks and natural softness; no new freckles/blemishes or repeated dots.
3. **Relevant details:** for new fictional styling, choose at most one or two meaningful additions that fit the character and visible composition; none is also valid. For an approved look, preserve all existing details rather than deleting them to meet that count or adding the lesson's clip/piercing. Name scale, position, attachment and material response. A clip holds actual strands; a stud follows its approved contour; tattoo ink integrates with skin. Use each material's proper contact behavior, not accessory shadows on tattoo ink. A requested styling change is recorded as a new variant before it becomes a reference.
4. **Whole/local review:** first inspect the complete image at delivery size for believable capture, identity, expression, composition, light and readable product/text. Then inspect existing forehead/nose/cheek/brow detail and any visible accessories, eyes, hairline, teeth/hands as applicable at matched enlargement. Check repeated pores, blur/smoothing, crunchy hair, clipped glare, scale/overlap/contact and count/design/placement. Do not require every feature to appear through a legitimate crop or occlusion. Fix one diagnosed local fault and recheck the whole image for collateral changes; increased apparent detail is not itself improvement.
5. **Optional motion:** approve and retain the finished still as the appearance anchor before a separately authorized animation. Bind it to the supported starting/reference role, preserve only details actually present, and keep the approved action/dialogue and camera setup. Breath/blink/head movement are optional restrained supporting behavior, not three required events. Use [Portrait realism](portrait-realism.md) to review actual face-attached detail, accessory contact and natural optical changes throughout the clip. An enlarged study or a successful still edit cannot validate moving skin.

Record the chosen anchor, fixed appearance/camera/light decisions, any changed clause and the whole/local review outcome. Compare derived views against that anchor; a new angle or generated macro is separate coverage, not an exact crop, upscale or isolated repair comparison. Stop when the image works at its intended size and the required checks pass, instead of adding every course feature. For UGC/native/unaware, retain the ordinary capture and story; for podcast, retain both distinct people, their gaze, fixed cameras and room lights. Concepts without a visible person do not acquire a portrait from this workflow.

## Separate complexion, undertone, age and variation

Record **complexion depth → undertone → approved age → one or two local variations → camera/light** as separate decisions. Skin tone describes light-to-deep appearance; undertone describes warm, cool, neutral or olive cast; surface texture is a separate layer. Olive is not a required depth category, and a darker complexion does not automatically need stronger pores or shine. Start with the approved character or supplied casting brief; do not infer ethnicity or impose the reference portrait's traits.

- **Color and exposure:** preserve the intended complexion and undertone, with detail in both highlights and shadows. Light skin must not become clipped white; deep skin must not become pale, gray or flat through an unmotivated exposure/color change. Correct exposure, fill or color response before changing the person's complexion. Scene light may naturally tint highlights/shadows; do not erase the chosen lighting or freeze skin color to the screen to satisfy a color lock.
- **Age:** state the approved adult age/band independently of texture. Age alone does not specify wrinkle, freckle or pore quantity. Choose individual, expression-appropriate lines and realistic softness; neither exaggerate maturity nor smooth an older face into a younger one. Retain approved hair, face structure and age marks. The lesson's age ranges and gray hair are examples, not compulsory visual bundles.
- **Variation:** for new fictional casting, choose one or two useful local features: irregular small freckles, mild redness near nostrils/cheeks, modest tone differences across forehead/cheeks/chin, local pores, under-eye detail or gentle sheen on high points. Do not stack them all. For an approved identity, preserve existing features and their positions rather than inventing new spots, redness or freckle patterns on each shot.
- **Comparison and motion:** review the complexion at delivery size in the intended light, then inspect highlights, shadows and local detail enlarged. Compare changes under the same light/crop unless light is the tested variable. Through speech, turns and blinks, marks stay attached to the face and color changes follow actual illumination; a cut must not silently lighten, gray, age or rejuvenate the person.

A still-prompt clause can follow: “Preserve [approved complexion depth] with [approved undertone] and age [approved age/band]. Retain [one or two approved local variations] at the planned viewing scale. Under [actual scene light], keep natural skin softness, tonal depth and detail in highlights and shadows, without clipping, washing out the complexion or exaggerating age cues.” Choose only specified or approved traits; this clause does not authorize a new cast, a portrait generation or an age-transformation batch. Keep each format's camera/focus and approved text rules.

## Place texture instead of repeating it everywhere

| Zone | Useful direction | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Forehead and hairline | Small low-contrast pores, gentle local sheen, existing baby hairs | Large dark pores, uniform shine or a new hairline |
| Nose and T-zone | Slightly more visible, irregular pores at tip/sides; restrained highlights on raised areas, softer surrounding skin | Black dots, oily whole-face shine or identical pore scale everywhere |
| Cheeks | Soft pores, subtle tone variation; short fine vellus hairs visible where side light catches them; approved freckles only | Grit, dusty fuzz, repeated or invented freckles |
| Eye area | Delicate texture, faint age/expression-appropriate lines, natural lashes and source-consistent catchlights | Heavy added creases, erased texture, artificial eye whites or duplicate lashes |
| Eyebrows | Individual hairs with varied direction, spacing and length; retain the approved outline | Solid drawn-on edges or repeating hair patterns |
| Lips | Fine vertical lines, natural color variation, soft source-consistent highlight; retain shape | Smooth plastic gloss or altered proportions |
| Chin and jaw | Fine pores, subtle tone variation and approved short facial hairs | Copying nose texture uniformly onto the jaw |

Detail changes with viewing scale: ordinary portrait pores stay subtle, while a dedicated close view can reveal more. Do not make every pore equally sharp. Retain the approved person's marks and makeup; selecting a new fictional character permits choosing features, but an identity-preserving edit does not invent tattoos, freckles, piercings or years of age. Skin texture is appearance direction, never proof that a skincare product works.

## Choose makeup without erasing the skin

Specify **coverage → finish → placement → one or two surviving details → crop/light** separately. Use the approved styling first. Makeup can soften color differences and hide some marks while retaining subtle surface relief; do not demand that every pore, freckle and redness cue show equally through every coverage level.

| Coverage | Skin behavior to request |
|---|---|
| Bare | No visible foundation; approved complexion variation remains |
| Sheer tint | Gently evens color while allowing selected texture/marks to show |
| Light foundation | Thin blended layer, with local fine texture still apparent where captured |
| Medium coverage | More even complexion, but subtle relief and natural facial dimension; marks may be less visible |

Choose a finish independently: **soft matte** retains relief without flat/powdery patches; **satin** has gentle reflection on high points; **subtle dewy** limits shine to selected cheekbone/T-zone areas. These are alternatives, not three finishes layered across the whole face. Highlights follow the actual source and face shape; do not create clipped shine or oily reflections everywhere. A different approved finish is valid when requested.

Describe only the makeup that belongs to the look. Foundation follows facial contours and blends without mask-like edges. Concealer is localized under eyes/near the nose rather than erasing all under-eye texture. Blush has diffused edges on the upper cheeks; highlight is restrained at specified high points. Bare, tinted or softly glossy lips retain plausible fine lines and dimension. Individual brow hairs and natural lashes remain believable under the selected styling; do not force a no-makeup eye look onto an approved makeup brief.

Select one or two details that plausibly remain visible under the coverage: local nose/inner-cheek pores, existing freckles, short hairs catching light, modest tone variation, lip lines or under-eye texture. Keep their strength appropriate to crop, source light and coverage. Do not add new marks or exaggerate pores to compensate for foundation. A makeup change may alter visible color/contrast without changing identity, complexion, age, face shape or the locations of underlying marks.

For a still prompt: “Keep the approved person and complexion. Use [approved coverage] with [finish], placing [specified makeup] at [locations] with blended edges. Retain [one or two plausible details] at [actual crop/distance], with subtle relief and natural face dimension. Reflections follow [scene light]; no smooth mask, powdery patches, painted edges or excessive shine.” Omit the clause for concepts without a visible face. Match UGC/native capture or the podcast's fixed-camera setup; the lesson's selfie, female cast, 4:5 ratio and no-text examples are not defaults.

Across shots and actual motion, keep coverage, placement, color and finish consistent while reflections move naturally with face/light. Check concealer, blush/highlight edges, lips and local relief through speech, blinks and turns; neither reset makeup at a cut nor freeze shine to the screen. For a controlled comparison, hold identity, crop and lighting fixed and change coverage **or** finish first. The lesson's coverage-plus-finish comparison explores looks but cannot isolate which variable helped. Existing generation/retry limits still apply.

## Diagnose one visible failure

### Name, isolate, compare

1. **Name the observed symptom and area.** Use “waxy smoothing on the cheeks,” “dark repeated nose pores” or “a broad clipped forehead highlight,” rather than “not realistic.” Check the intended delivery size, then enlarge that area. Soft skin at normal camera distance, an approved makeup finish and a natural highlight are not automatically faults. A beauty/perfection cue, excessive detail wording or glossy language is a possible cause to inspect, not a diagnosis inferred from the image alone.
2. **Change one controlling instruction.** Use the latest useful image as the edit reference through the supported input role. Lock approved identity, complexion/undertone, age, marks, expression, pose, crop, camera position, light sources/direction, wardrobe and makeup. Keep product geometry/printing and approved lettering intact. Select one small local texture, pore-pattern or highlight correction; do not combine it with new framing, lighting, exposure, grain or makeup. Remove one conflicting cue before adding compensating adjectives. If lighting or exposure itself is the diagnosed cause, test that as its own named variable instead of claiming that lighting stayed fixed.
3. **Compare like with like.** Check the same facial area at the same crop and display scale, including delivery size and matched enlargement. Keep the reference, exact changed clause and result; judge both the symptom and preservation of unrelated features. Retain the best approved version and revert ineffective changes. A different zoom, crop or light cannot establish that a texture clause helped. Prompt locks guide an edit; they do not guarantee preservation, so inspect the returned image before continuing.

For the three lab symptoms, choose the smallest relevant correction:

- **Waxy or over-smoothed:** first remove an unapproved “flawless/perfect/smooth” or beauty-filter cue. If absent, recover subtle local relief already plausible for that person and coverage: fine pores or barely visible cheek hairs where the existing light catches them. Do not invent freckles, blemishes, age lines or heavy fuzz; no whole-face sharpening. A missing approved detail is recovered, not a new personal feature.
- **Strong or stamped pores:** if darkness is the fault, lower local pore contrast; if repetition is the fault, replace uniform spacing with subtle irregular distribution. Test these separately when both need work. Keep the approved zone pattern, usually clearer at nose/inner cheek and softer outward, scaled to actual distance and coverage. Do not erase all texture or stamp the nose pattern onto forehead/outer cheeks.
- **Broad or blown-out shine:** control only the excessive highlight at the named area under the existing source/direction and face shape. Keep gentle source-consistent sheen and plausible nearby relief without turning approved satin/dewy makeup into matte skin. Do not demand sharp pores inside a genuinely clipped white patch; reduce the clipping first and review the resulting detail. Retain natural facial dimension rather than replacing glare with flat shading.
