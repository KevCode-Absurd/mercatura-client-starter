# Environment and photographic realism

Adapted from the two user-supplied lessons, “What's possible?” and “Photorealism” (2026-10-09). Their examples illustrate choices, not required locations, casting, model selection or a universal aesthetic. This guidance supplements the product truth sheet and brand guardrails.

## Choose the kind of photograph

- **Everyday phone / UGC:** a buyer's plausible location, recognisable surroundings, available light, casual framing and restrained imperfections.
- **Lifestyle / campaign:** deliberate composition and styling are valid. The location, light, materials and product interaction still need to agree physically.
- **Studio / PDP hero:** controlled light and a clean set can be the right choice. Do not add clutter, noisy shadows or skin blemishes to every shot in the name of realism.
- **Illustration / designed set:** follow the approved art direction; do not force a phone-photo look.

## Build a place with a reason

Start with who uses the product, what they are doing, and why they are here. Use the brand's `Brand world` and the avatar's `The moment`; an imagined scene is creative direction, not a fact about a real customer.

Name the surface, background, two or three useful environmental anchors, and the source of light. If more props are needed, give each a purpose. A breakfast mug beside a coffee bag belongs; unrelated fruit, random luxury decor and arbitrary clutter do not. Wear should fit use: a lightly scratched desk, a folded towel, a bag waiting by the door. Leave enough quiet space for the product and any approved copy.

Consider locale, season, time, weather and materials only where they affect the picture. Avoid generic tourist scenery when the scene is meant to be an ordinary routine. Do not invent ingredients, other products, proof, credentials or results through background details.

## Make the environment affect the subject

Specify the light source and direction, softness, fill, colour temperature and exposure. Then check:

- Face, clothes and product receive the same light as their surroundings.
- Cast shadows agree in direction and hardness; feet, hands and objects have contact and weight.
- Reflective surfaces show plausible nearby light and objects; skin does not glow independently of the room.
- Camera height, perspective, scale, depth of focus, sharpness and grain agree across subject and background.
- The real product retains its material, colour and label. Environmental reflections do not replace those facts.

When moving a subject to a new place, lock identity, wardrobe, pose and product shape, but explicitly allow relighting, reflections and contact shadows to change. “Keep everything unchanged” conflicts with physical integration if the lighting changed.

## Phone capture and people

Deep focus with a readable background usually suits casual phone content. Add at most one or two appropriate imperfections: slightly uneven exposure, mild shadow noise, modest wide-angle distortion or small framing asymmetry. Exposure imperfections are optional and should leave the selling detail legible. Motion blur, compression and haze are poor choices when they obscure copy or packaging.

Describe action, expression and gaze independently. Prefer observable details over “posing naturally.” Preserve the approved person's natural texture and asymmetry; avoid gratuitous beauty smoothing or stacking cinematic quality adjectives. Do not invent scars, age changes or a new face during an identity-preserving edit.

Facial detail stays proportionate: avoid uniformly etched pores, sharpening halos and exaggerated texture added to signal realism. Inspect at delivery size and enlarged, including eyes, hairline, visible teeth and hands. For source selection, subtle portrait performance and temporal checks, read [Portrait realism](portrait-realism.md); a convincing still does not establish a stable moving face.

Choose compatible camera, skin and lighting modules instead of piling up contradictory styles. Personal details need contact: clips grip hair, jewelry has fixed placement, nails follow hand anatomy and tattoo ink follows body curvature. Use [Skin detail craft](skin-detail-craft.md) for local texture, controlled flash alternatives and troubleshooting; added flaws never replace correct photographic integration.

**Camera physics:**

- Arm-length selfie: the filming device is outside its own image; one arm holds it and only one hand is free. Gaze meets the lens. No unexplained second phone.
- Mirror selfie: the mirror edge or frame establishes the reflection; the back of the phone and camera lenses face the mirror, and gaze meets the screen or reflection.
- Propped camera: both hands may work. A visible phone needs a separate purpose; do not accidentally draw the filming device into the scene.

## Use references deliberately

Assign each attachment a separate authority: base scene/camera, approved fictional identity, wardrobe, product truth, or layout. Inspect the base photo before choosing it. A heavily filtered, retouched, HDR or poorly lit image may preserve the wrong look.

For a narrowly scoped edit, preserve the base scene's crop, pose, gaze, geometry, wardrobe and exposure unless explicitly asked to change them. An identity reference supplies identity; it does not donate its studio lighting or background. A competitor reference supplies structure or photographic direction, never the competitor's person, branding, product or copy. Existing rules for real people still apply.

Make the smallest necessary change per edit and carry forward the best approved result alongside the original product and identity references. Reinspect before using it as the next base: drift compounds. Do not turn every fresh concept into a chain of paid edits; staged edits are for preserving a useful base.

For outpainting, select the requested output ratio and continue perspective, light and texture without duplicating objects. Preserving the existing centre is suitable when requested; a different advertising layout may instead need recomposition. Exact font files and editable typography belong in the existing brand/design workflow; generated lettering still needs checking.

## Compact direction example

**Scene:** an adult buyer at a kitchen counter after breakfast; a mug by the sink and a folded tea towel explain the moment. The actual product sits beside their hand with its front visible.

**Capture:** a propped camera at counter height, recognisable background, daylight from the left, slightly darker room corners. Quiet space above the product for the approved headline.

**Integration:** window light reaches face, hand and pack; the pack casts a short soft shadow on the counter. Preserve the product's true colours and printing.

**Edit contract:** change only the room from the approved still. Keep identity, outfit, pose, product and framing; adapt light, reflections and contact shadows to the new room.

## Check the output

Does the place belong to this buyer and use? Does each prop have a reason? Is the subject grounded and lit by the same sources? Are perspective, reflections and camera texture consistent? Is identity unchanged where locked? Are the product and planned words clear? Do selfie hands and reflections make physical sense? If one detail fails, correct that detail while protecting what already works.
