# Dialogue performance and a clear voice

Adapted from the principles in the user's café dialogue lesson (2026-10-09). The sample lines, amused female delivery, visitor, rain, gaze coordinates, opening breath and café wardrobe are examples, not default copy, casting or actions. Use the approved brand script, character, tone, room and camera setup.

## Fit words and performance into the duration

Plan the actual words, conversational pace, any useful breath, clause pause and final reaction together. Give a pause a specific position in the approved line; “natural delivery” alone leaves its rhythm open. Breath and settling time are optional performance beats, not a delay inserted before every feed hook. Leave the speaker enough time to finish whole words and for a meaningful reaction to register, without padding or frozen footage.

If the line feels rushed, first simplify competing physical action, revise the timing or choose a supported longer duration within the authorized budget. If the words still need shortening, return the proposed revision to the script stage; use a newly approved version rather than silently dropping words in production. Do not accelerate playback to fit dialogue. Preserve approved copy, offer details and claims.

## Separate voice, gaze and expression

Use [Voice identity and spoken performance](voice-performance.md) to keep the approved voice stable while directing each speaker turn's intention, emphasis and energy. Name existing speakers consistently; keep delivery outside canonical words and verify supported route syntax. A changing emotion need not change voice identity, dialect or the mouth belonging to the speaker.

Specify the intended voice, pace, accent, conversational volume and tone appropriate to the approved person. Translate expression into restrained observable behavior: relaxed eyebrows, natural lips and jaw during speech, then a small smile after the final phrase when appropriate. Quiet amusement is one tone choice; it is not the persona of every presenter.

Choose a gaze target that suits the format. Direct-response UGC can meet the lens; a conversation can address a defined person just outside frame. Translate that target relative to the actual camera and preserve it through the line. Do not copy “above and right” from an unrelated scene or freeze the eyes unnaturally. Keep unnecessary head motion modest if it threatens facial stability, while allowing natural speech movement. A mostly still head does not mean a still mouth.

## Make speech the foreground

Use [Audio direction and selective repair](audio-direction.md) when choosing a separate voice/performance route or assembling layers. Cloning establishes an authorized voice identity; voice changing uses a source performance, and neither guarantees lip sync. Preserve the approved script, with pronunciation/delivery notes separate. Repair the smallest coherent weak turn where supported, then check neighboring breaths, voice/room consistency and actual mouth timing in the complete edit. Plan sound early and finalize cue positions with real speech timing; native flattened audio is not automatically a set of isolated stems.

Describe sound by priority: intelligible foreground voice, meaningful action sounds aligned to their visible beats, then a quieter continuous room bed and optional supporting music. Keep incidental conversation indistinct so it does not become a second speaker or compete with approved words. Set music or none according to the format and explicit user choice; phone UGC defaults to no music. The room's acoustic character stays physically plausible, with speech sounding recorded in that room rather than isolated from it.

Inspect the route's actual dialogue/audio capability. Speech text in a prompt does not make a silent route speak or guarantee lip sync. If speech must be created separately, use a supported voice/lip-sync and edit workflow, review the result and record that route; simply placing a voice track under unrelated mouth movement is not a successful talking shot. Keep sound, captions and performance as separately reviewed assets.

## Listen first, then inspect the face

Listen once without watching: every approved word is complete and understandable, tone fits the brief, pauses feel intentional, background stays below speech and the ending does not clip. Check voice consistency, unwanted words, room changes and accidental extra speakers. Then watch at normal speed for lip/jaw timing, audible syllables aligned to movement, gaze and the reaction beat. Inspect critical transitions frame by frame for stable visible teeth, facial proportions and eyes. A transcript alone cannot establish clarity, tone or lip sync; silent frames cannot establish audio quality.

Fix the instruction connected to the failure. Lower competing ambience for masked speech, reduce head motion for identity drift, or delay and soften a smile that overwhelms the line. Change one controlling decision per bounded comparison, retaining the source and parts that work. Record the actual audio and motion review; do not declare a full pass from selected frames or unheard audio. See [Portrait realism](portrait-realism.md) and [Motion review](video-generation.md).
