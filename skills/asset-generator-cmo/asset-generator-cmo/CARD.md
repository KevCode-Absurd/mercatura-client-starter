# Base product photographs: rules for planning them

A base picture is the real product in a real-looking scene, with no words, made so an ad, a
page or an email can be built on it. It is judged on 3 things: the product is the real
product; the picture has 1 job; something can be built on it (a quiet area for words set
later, and it holds in the sizes the brand uses).

## Read the reference in 6 parts, and take only these

- **Camera:** lens feel (wide, normal, long, macro), depth of focus, angle, distance, shape.
- **Light:** kind, direction, soft or hard, warm or cool, how the shadows fall.
- **Depth:** what is sharp, what is in front and behind, how many layers.
- **Place:** setting, surface, background, each prop, whether the place fills the frame.
- **Color:** main colors, contrast, saturation, mood.
- **Composition:** where the subject sits, how much of the frame it fills, where the empty space is, still or moving.

Never the reference's product, brand, words, logo or people. Where its palette fights the brand's, the brand's wins. A part left unread is a part the image model makes up.

## Carry it over by what kind of product it is

The recipe transfers. The subject does not.

- **Worn** (a jacket, sunglasses): on a body, sitting and hanging as its in-use photo shows.
- **Applied** (a serum, a balm): at the skin or hair, the real amount.
- **Eaten or drunk** (a sauce, a coffee): served, poured or held, 1 serving, only named ingredients beside it.
- **Used with the hands** (a pan, a bag): in the hand, mid-task, with a grip a hand can make.
- **Placed in a room** (a blanket, a candle): in place, at its true size against the furniture. It is the subject, never the background: 1 glass is context, a full table setting buries it.
- **Made for a pet:** on or beside an ordinary adult animal of the kind it is for.

Swap each of the reference's props for something from this product's real use. The reference's camera angle is a start: a flat thing wants a higher camera than a tall one.

## Each picture has a job

- **Warm and lived-in:** the product in use. For an ad.
- **Dramatic:** dark ground, 1 directional light. A hero for a launch.
- **Bright and clean:** clear space to 1 side or above. A base for an ad with a headline.
- **Very close:** texture and detail. For a carousel card.
- **A treatment:** a scroll-stopper for the feed. Never a product page hero.

5 pictures for 1 product differ on purpose: no 2 share light, distance and mood.

## The treatments (how the camera sees, not what is in frame)

- Fish-eye with flash; fish-eye from the ground in daylight: the product stays in the middle third, where the lens does not bend it.
- Warm flash film: amber cast, harsh small-camera flash, grain. A white pack stays warm white.
- Ring flash: flat frontal light, a thin shadow all round. Hot spot off the printed words.
- Hard noon sun: deep black shadows, none across the label.
- Tilt-shift: 1 sharp band, with the product inside it.
- Prism: rainbow leaks at the edges, the product in the clear middle.
- Cross-processed, colored lights, infrared: these change color. The product is lit white or kept small; never used where the product's color is the point.
- Through water: only a product the facts say goes in water, or a drink.

Describe a treatment by lens, light and color. Name no film stock, brand or photographer.

## The placements (textiles and things placed in a room)

- **Draped** off a bare surface, directional sun, hard shadows showing the weave. A hero.
- **At a table outside,** dappled low sun, 1 glass and 1 small vase. Lifestyle.
- **On a shelf,** folded, soft side light. For a product page.
- **Out of the box,** 1 adult hand lifting it from tissue. What you get.
- **Flat, from above,** the whole pattern, nothing on it. A catalogue picture.

A pattern is the product: copied from its photo, its repeat at true size, never redrawn.

## What must be true of the product

- The same object as in its photos: shape, colors, printing, finish. Only from an angle a photo shows.
- It stands in the scene's light and throws a shadow on the scene's surface. Not a cut-out laid on top.
- Whole, not cut by the frame's edge. Its real size against a hand, a body or the furniture.
- 1 product in a picture, unless more are asked for. Never an invented variant.

## People

- Fictional adults, different from anyone in the reference. Say what each hand does; no finger over the label.
- No child, and no part of one: a product for children is shown without one. Nobody is a customer, a founder or an expert.

## Avoid

- Words, logos, date stamps, borders.
- A reference prop that makes no sense for this product.
- A use the product cannot survive (under water, on a flame) unless the facts say it can.
- No quiet area: a picture nothing can be built on.

## After the render, look at

- The product beside its real photo: shape, colors, label words, finish, size.
- Does it stand in the scene, or look stuck on: light direction and shadow.
- A treatment: is the product bent, tinted, glared out or too small to read?
- A shiny product: does it reflect this scene, and nothing that is not in it?
- A pattern: is it the real pattern at its real size?
- Is there a quiet area, and does the picture do its job?
- A picture with the wrong product is rejected, not ranked.
