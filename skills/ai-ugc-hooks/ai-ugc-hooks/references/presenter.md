# The presenter

Who says the hook. The rules about people in `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md` section 6 apply in full; this is what a talking hook adds.

## Who it can be

- **A generated presenter: a fictional adult.** Not a customer, not the founder, not an expert, and never said or shown to be one. No title, no white coat, no "as a mum of 3".
- **A real person,** only from that person's own photo or footage, uploaded by the brand, with their say. A founder, a named customer or an expert appears no other way.
- **Never:** a face taken from a stranger's photo, a voice copied from someone else's ad, a child.

## Choosing a generated presenter

1. Start from the buyer: the avatar's `Who they are` and `The moment`, and `People` under `Brand world`. The presenter is someone the buyer would take for 1 of their own, or for the person they buy for.
2. Offer 3 options that really differ (age band, look, the room they are in), each in 2 lines. The brand picks 1.
3. Make 1 picture of the chosen presenter and have it approved. That picture is attached to every clip.
4. Keep the approved presenter (the picture, the description, the voice line) with the brand, so the next hook and the ad body can use the same person. 1 presenter across a hook and its body reads as 1 ad.

## A pet

A product made for an animal may have 1 in the frame. It is cast like a person: described once in fixed words (kind, size, coat, 1 or 2 markings), present in the opening still, and asked for 1 small thing (sits, looks up, eats from the bowl). The brand's own animal, if it has one, comes only from its photos. What the animal does is never offered as proof of what the product does.

## Describing the presenter

Said once, in the same words every time:

- gender, age band, build
- hair: colour, length, how it sits
- eyes; skin tone
- 2 or 3 small things that make this 1 particular person: glasses, a few freckles, a gap in the teeth, a nose stud
- wardrobe: plain everyday clothes in the brand's world, no words and no logos on them
- the voice: age, pace, 2 or 3 traits, the accent

With the approved picture attached, the prompt says only what tells this person apart. A long description fights the picture.

## The place

1 real room from `Brand world` (Places) and the avatar's `The moment`: where the buyer would be when the problem shows or the product is used. Lived-in, with the light the room would have. The same presenter in a different room reads as a different ad, which is a cheap way to vary a batch.

## Variety in a batch

- 2 or 3 hooks may share 1 presenter and 1 room. Then the approved opening still is reused and only the line and the lever change.
- When presenters differ, they differ because the avatars differ, not for decoration.
