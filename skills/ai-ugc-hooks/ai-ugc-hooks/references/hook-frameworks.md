# Hook levers

A lever is a reason to keep watching, said in 1 line by 1 person. 11 levers and 2 wildcards a generated presenter can say honestly, then 4 that only a real person can.

## Rules for every line

1. **The example is a pattern, not copy.** Rewrite it in the brand's voice, with the buyer's own nouns from the `Customer language bank`.
2. **It sells line 2.** Write it against the first line of the ad body it goes in front of. Read the 2 aloud together: if the body's line repeats the hook, or changes the subject, rewrite the hook.
3. **18 words or fewer, 1 or 2 sentences.** The pace follows the speaker's age, and the seconds follow the words. A hook whose action is the point (lever 5) has 6 to 8 words. 8 words or fewer are said while the product is being lifted or used.
4. **A cue word.** 1 word, near the end, on which the product comes up into the frame. Mark it.
5. **A generated presenter is a fictional adult.** No personal result, no personal story told as fact, no "this worked for me", no "my dad". Second person, a question, an observation, a fact, or a real customer's words given as a quote.
6. **Facts are the brand's.** A number, a price, a date or a claim only if it is in `brand.json` or `offers.json`, in the same terms. A customer's words only word for word, with the source kept.
7. **No brand name in the first 3 words, no greeting, no "so".** It starts mid-thought.

## The levers

Each gives: what it does, a pattern for a consumable and a durable, what the face and body do, and the fact it needs.

**1. Recognition.** A habit so specific that the right buyer thinks "that's me".
- "If there are 4 half-used moisturisers in your bathroom right now, look at *this*."
- "If your frying pan has a grey patch in the middle, you need to see *this*."
- Eyebrows up, a small knowing smile, eyes on the lens. Needs: the avatar's `Who they are` or the insight bank.

**2. Stop and swap.** Tells the viewer to put the usual thing down.
- "Stop. Put the cereal bar back. Read the back of *this* one."
- "Before you buy another case with 4 plastic wheels, look at *these*."
- A flat hand raised on "stop", then the lift. Needs: `Replaces`.

**3. Open question.** A question the ad body answers.
- "Why does every candle smell great in the shop and of nothing at home?"
- "Why do white trainers go yellow at the toe first?"
- Head tilted, the stress on the word that asks, the product lifted on the last word with no stop. Needs: the avatar's `What almost stops them` or a failed solution from research.

**4. Myth flip.** The opposite of what the buyer believes, only when the brand can back it.
- "Everybody says more lather means cleaner hair. It's the *opposite*."
- "Everybody says rinse the pan while it's hot. That is what *warps* it."
- A small head shake on the myth, level eyes on the turn. Needs: a mechanism in the brand's facts.

**5. Watch this.** The product's action is the hook; the line only points at it.
- "Watch what happens when *this* goes in cold water."
- "Watch how small *this* jacket folds."
- Eyes go from the lens to the product on the cue word and stay there. Needs: `Visible result`, `Moves and sounds`.

**6. Number first.** 1 fact, said plainly, then what it belongs to.
- "9 grams of protein. In something *this* size."
- "1.2 kilos. Now pick up the pan you've *got*."
- Deadpan, the stress on the number, with no stop. Needs: the number in `brand.json`, in the same terms.

**7. A customer's words.** A real line from a real customer, given as a quote.
- "A customer wrote: 'I threw out 5 bottles after this.' *Here's* what she meant."
- "1 review just says: 'Why did nobody tell me a knife could do this.' *This* knife."
- Reads it as a quote, glances down as if at a phone, then up. Needs: the quote and its source from the `Customer language bank`.

**8. Objection first.** Names the doubt before the viewer forms it.
- "You're going to think this is just another greens powder. *Fair*."
- "Yes, it is a 90 dollar T-shirt. Look at *this* seam."
- A half shrug, a dry tone, then the product. Needs: the avatar's `What almost stops them`.

**9. This replaces these.** The things it replaces are in the frame from the start.
- "These 3 can go. *This* does all of it."
- "You don't need the 5 pans. You need *this* 1."
- 1 hand slides the old things a little aside, the other lifts the product. Needs: `Replaces`; a claim that it does replace them.

**10. For someone else.** The buyer is not the user: a gift, a household.
- "If the person you're buying for has run the same route every Sunday for 10 years, show them *this*."
- "If someone in your house has killed every plant they have owned, get them *this*."
- Warm, amused. Needs: the avatar that buys for someone else.

**11. A real deadline.** Only when `offers.json` has a real date.
- "Order by the 18th and it's there before the *24th*."
- Brisk, 1 finger tapped on the product on the date. Needs: the date and terms, word for word.

**Wildcards (1 per batch at most).**

**12. Quiet aside.** Close to the lens, almost whispered: "Nobody tells you this when you buy cast iron." For a brand whose voice allows it.

**13. Caught mid-task.** The presenter is doing the thing and speaks without looking up at first: "Hang on. Nearly done. Okay. *This* is the one." The task is the product in use.

## Only from a real person

These need someone who can say them truthfully: a creator who has used the product, or a founder or customer from their own upload. With a real creator, leave the true detail as a placeholder in square brackets.

- **Confession or near miss:** "I almost spent [what you almost bought]. Then I found this."
- **Their own result:** "[Week / day] in. Not dramatic. Just [what changed]."
- **A family story:** "My dad has never [his true habit]. Then he opened this."
- **Someone's reaction, filmed:** the person receiving it, reacting for real.

## Mixing a batch

A batch is 2 or 3 hooks on 1 ad body. Each uses a different lever. If the product has an action a camera can see, 1 of them is lever 5. Hooks that differ only in wording are 1 hook.

## Fitting the lever to the product

Look at the product first, then choose. A product with a visible action: 5, 9, 6. A product bought for someone else: 10, 1. A product in a crowded, doubted category: 8, 4, 7. A product whose edge is a number: 6. Never draft the line first and force the product to fit it.
