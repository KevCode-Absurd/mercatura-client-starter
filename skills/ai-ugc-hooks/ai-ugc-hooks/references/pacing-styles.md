# Pacing

3 ways of saying a hook. Each row of a line-up has 1. A video model reads what a face and a body do, not a mood word, so each pacing is written as what is seen and heard.

| | Quiet | Fast | Direct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sounds like | Telling 1 friend something true | Can't wait to say it | Stating a fact |
| The face | Still, eyes on the lens, no stop before the turn | Eyebrows moving, a quick grin | Level eyes, a slight knowing smile |
| The body | Almost no gesture; the product lifted gently | 1 free hand moves with the words; a small lean in | Shoulders square; 1 clear lift |
| The voice | Low, warm, steady; weight on the cue word | Bright, quick, no gaps between sentences | Even, no rise at the end |
| Words | 10 to 14 | 14 to 18 | 12 to 16 |
| Seconds | What its words need at the speaker's pace, which follows their age | The same | The same |
| Fits levers | 7, 10, 12 | 2, 9, 11, 13 | 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 |

## Choosing

- The brand's voice decides first: `Tone by context` and `Motion and sound` (Pace) in the brain. A calm, premium brand has no Fast hook. A loud brand has no Quiet one unless it is the wildcard.
- Then the ad body: the hook is said at the pace the body opens at, so the join is not a change of speaker.
- In a batch of 2 or 3, at most 1 Quiet. Quiet hooks tire quickest when several run together.

## What every pacing shares

- It starts mid-thought, mouth already opening, with no greeting.
- No word is said while the mouth is hidden by the product.
- After the last word the mouth closes and the pose holds for half a second. A cut on an open mouth reads as a clipped word.
