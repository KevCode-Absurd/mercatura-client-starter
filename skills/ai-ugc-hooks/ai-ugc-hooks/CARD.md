# Talking hook: the card

**What it is.** The first shots of a UGC ad, joined with hard cuts: 1 person says 1 line to their phone and the product arrives on a cue word.

**What it is for and judged on.** It keeps people watching so the rest of the ad is seen. The first frame reads as a person, not an advert. The line makes the viewer need the next line. The product arrives on the cue word and is the real product. The last frame leads into what follows.

## Structure: the order of things (the brief gives the clip's length and its shots)

- The first frame: the presenter is mid-thought, mouth just opening. Where they look is the shot's own: the lens is 1 option, as are their own hands, the thing they doubt, or somewhere off to one side. The product is not in this shot. The opening frame is described that way.
- Most of the hook: the line, with a jump cut closer on its key phrase.
- Near the end of the line: the cue word. A cut to a shot that opens with the product already low in 1 hand; the hand brings it into use or up into view. Beside the face at chest height is 1 way to show it, never the way of every hook.
- The end: the product is in view, its front readable, as the last word lands. Nothing is held: the next shot cuts on from there.

The hook is the first shots of the first clip. What follows in that clip cuts on from it.

When the script's first line does not point at the product (a problem, a story), nothing is lifted: the product stays out of the frame, and the script decides when it arrives.

Approved handling overrides every lift/use example below. With `handling=display`, reveal the verified sealed front on a support at the cue cut; nobody holds, opens or uses it. Keep it there at the end. Do not infer scale from a front-only photo or choose an action hook without use evidence.

## The line

- It is the script's approved first line. Do not rewrite it into another hook. If it cannot fit, propose a shorter line retaining its first words and cue, and obtain approval before generation.
- It is said at the speaker's pace, which the brief gives; never slow it to fill time or silently cut approved words.
- The cue word is the word on which the product comes up: usually "this", a number, or the last word. Name it in the scene's description.
- It starts mid-thought. No greeting, no "so", no brand name in the first 3 words.
- The body must pay off the opening question/expectation with its actual explanation or use step. An unrelated stunt or unanswered promise is not a stronger talking hook.

## Levers: what kind of line it is, and what the picture does for each

1. **Recognition** (a habit the buyer has): eyebrows up, the look of someone caught doing it themselves (a knowing smile is 1 way to play it); the product lifted on "this".
2. **Stop and swap** (put the usual thing down): a flat hand raised on "stop"; the usual thing is in the frame; then the lift.
3. **Open question:** head tilted; the stress on the word that asks; the product lifted on the last word, with no stop.
4. **Myth flip:** a small head shake on the myth, level eyes on the turn.
5. **Watch this** (the product's action is the hook): the eyes go from the lens to the product on the cue word and stay on it; the action gets most of the seconds and 8 words or fewer.
6. **Number first:** deadpan; the stress on the number, with no stop; the lift straight after it.
7. **A customer's words:** said as a quote; a glance down as if reading, then up.
8. **Objection first:** a half shrug, a dry tone, then the product.
9. **This replaces these:** the replaced things are in the opening frame; 1 hand slides them a little aside, the other lifts the product.
10. **For someone else** (a gift, a household): warm, amused.
11. **A real deadline:** brisk; 1 finger taps the product on the date.
12. **Quiet aside** (rare): closer to the lens, a lower voice.
13. **Caught mid-task** (rare): eyes on the task at first; the task is the product in use.

## Pacing: 1 per hook, written as what is seen and heard

- **Quiet:** still face, the stress on the cue word with no stop before it, almost no gesture, a low steady voice.
- **Fast:** eyebrows moving, a quick grin, 1 free hand moving with the words, a small lean in, a bright quick voice.
- **Direct:** level eyes, mouth relaxed, shoulders square, 1 clear lift, an even voice with no rise at the end.

The brand's voice decides: a calm brand has no Fast hook.

## The camera and the presenter

- A reveal is a cut to the product and the product coming up, never the camera hunting for it. The phone may push in, pull back or follow a hand, 1 move a shot.
- The hand that is not holding the phone holds and lifts the product.

## The product on screen

- It is in the frame when the shot of the cue word opens, low in 1 hand. On the cue word it comes into use or up into view, its front readable, never over the mouth.
- A rule about how it is held or stood (from the lessons) is written into the scene that lifts it.

## Sound

The voice, the room, and the product's own sound on the lift if it has one (a lid, a rustle). No music.

## The first and the last frame

- First: a face in the upper middle of the frame, wearing the face its shot was given, a gesture already begun, a real room behind. Nothing that matters in the top or bottom sixth.
- Last: the product in view, its front readable, as the last word lands. The next shot cuts on from here.

## Avoid

- A hook that the next line repeats, or that the next line has nothing to do with.
- A product that appears from behind the camera, or a phone tilted down to "reveal" it.
- A greeting. A second speaker. Words said while the product is being opened.
- A number, date or quote that is not in the brand's facts.

## After the render, look at

1. Is the whole line said, word for word, at the speaker's pace, not cut off?
2. Do the lips match?
3. Does the product come up on the cue word, and does it match its photo?
4. Is the product up in the last frame, with no hold before the next shot?
5. Has a person listened: the voice fits the person, no music?
