# Plan a static visual argument
Choose the image's persuasive job before its layout. Useful archetypes include problem moment, identity mirror, grounded myth correction, real founder portrait, sourced testimonial, verified proof grid, fair comparison, feature annotation, price/value, genuine deadline, real bundle and objection-resolving review. A missing proof or offer asset rules out that execution. Synthetic people or staged scenes never authenticate customer results.

Build a glanceable reading order: dominant idea, relevant support, action when useful. Assign zones for the image, exact text, attribution/qualification and product; reflow to the requested aspect rather than stretching a square. Essential qualifications remain with their claim. Honour text-free and product-free choices. A selected layout or format is a constraint, not a reason to default to a selfie.

Choose style by its job: studio for product clarity; flat lay for contents; macro for material; documentary for use context; editorial/fashion for identity; food/interior for category setting; graphic, illustration or comparison for explanation. UGC is one option.

Keep product identity and explicit brand constraints fixed. Vary relevant mood, lighting, composition, setting, product prominence, style or camera angle deliberately. Distinct ads change argument or scene as well as surface styling. Describe only the selected execution using the caller's schema.
