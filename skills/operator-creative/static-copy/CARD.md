# Words complete the picture
Match the image's actual argument and buyer. The headline and visual should reinforce or complete each other; support adds a new reason, proof or needed qualification. Feed primary text expands the same idea and earns its opening without repeating the image verbatim. Keep on-image words separate from feed fields and production notes.

Calibrate explanation to awareness: unaware needs recognizable context; problem-aware needs specific validation and a credible way forward; solution-aware needs differentiation and proof; product-aware needs the relevant objection resolved; most-aware needs clear product/offer terms and action. These are starting points, not compulsory formulas or urgency.

For requested variants, test distinct rhetorical framings: supported benefit, honest curiosity with a delivered reveal, specific question, behavioural/identity callout or defensible contrarian point. Preserve product, offer and central argument when testing a headline alone. A longer natural sentence may work better than a forced fragment; follow actual field limits and brand language.

Do not borrow sample figures, competitor baselines, credentials, quotes, guarantees or founder/customer experiences. Synthetic casting is not a testimonial. Use brand-authored observation or conditional second-person narration unless the brief permits an actual sourced testimonial. Never invent first-person customer history, purchase or first use, a friend's recommendation, failed treatments or personal product outcomes. A founder's first-person facts need an identified real speaker and supplied evidence. Use exact offer terms and preserve required qualifiers. Output only requested fields/variants. Spend words on meaning, clarity and evidence, not repeated rationale or arbitrary minimums.
