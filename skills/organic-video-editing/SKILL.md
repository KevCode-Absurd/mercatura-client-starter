# Organic video editing for D2C brands

Start from the brand's supplied footage and the purpose of this post: teach a use/care step, answer a real question, tell a founder moment, show the making, or help choose between actual products. Keep one idea clear. An organic post does not need to inherit an ad's offer or purchase CTA.

## Read the material before cutting

Inventory source clips, duration, orientation, speech, baked-in words/music and any supplied transcript. Watch the opening and the demonstrated action. Confirm product names, quantities, negations and care instructions against the source; an automatic transcript can mishear the first word. Retain the speaker's meaning, conditions and honest experience when removing pauses or repeated takes.

Choose the strongest complete thought for the opening. Cut setup that delays it, but leave enough context to understand the result. Use the recorded voice as the timing authority. Retain a laugh, a breath or a human pause when it carries personality. Do not speed the whole performance simply to hit an arbitrary runtime.

For generated or externally edited source clips, review the middle and final spoken beat before selecting the take. Check mouth timing, teeth/jaw, exact words and voice changes separately from the image's polish. Compare a generative edit to its source for unrelated changes in people, wardrobe, setting or motion. Select a clean take or name the needed replacement; a softer image, music or a faster cut cannot repair a broken performance.

## Match the picture to the words

Use the brand's real packing, process, product or usage footage for B-roll. Show the detail when it is discussed, with enough time to see it. A cutaway can conceal a jump cut, but cannot imply a different product, a customer result or a sourcing fact the material does not support. Generated illustration is an explanation, not documentary proof.

Full-frame, a speaker with B-roll, or an optional split layout all work. Choose the layout because it explains the idea; do not impose a fixed presenter crop, a logo conveyor or a meme quota. Keep the face and the product visible. Use the brand's colors and typography. Sparse labels, a demonstration step or a small diagram usually do more than constant zooms and flashing transitions.

## Captions, sound and endings

Time captions to the actual kept speech after cuts. Use short complete phrases and correct names. Keep words away from the mouth, hands and label, and inspect at phone size. Caption, cover and post description should promise the same thing the video delivers. A cover can use a real frame with one specific benefit or question; it need not repeat a comment keyword.

Keep clear voice first. Add only owned or appropriately cleared music at a level that leaves every word audible. Sounds baked into the source are one mixed track unless separate stems exist. A trending sound is not evidence of better distribution and may have different permissions for commercial use.

End with the next useful step: a real question, save the instructions, or watch the next part. Preserve a gifted/paid creator disclosure where applicable. Do not invent a testimonial, milestone or offer.

## Repurpose a longer recording

Select self-contained passages; record source in/out times, the opening, what the viewer learns and a soft ending for each. A clip that begins with “this” must show or name its referent. Keep a needed instruction or caveat attached. Do not use a virality score as a guarantee. A useful short, a story teaser, a carousel and a text post can share one source while each has its own clear job.

## Deliver and verify

Work from copies. Keep a cut list with source file, in/out seconds, order, caption windows, audio choices and output shape. Re-time captions after any cut. Preserve aspect ratio; keeping the whole frame is the safe initial fit, and a crop needs a visual check throughout the motion. Inspect first frame, each join and ending; listen on a phone and watch muted. Verify actual dimensions, duration, codec and audio, then deliver the requested master and captioned copy.

Supplied voice takes priority for transcription and starts at timeline zero. Retiming the opening can require an updated voice file.
