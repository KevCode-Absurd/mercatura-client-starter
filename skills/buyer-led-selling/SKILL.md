# Buyer-led selling

Read the brief and the brand's actual evidence before deciding what should persuade this buyer. Personal taste and historical success stories cannot establish an appeal's effectiveness. This method works in any assistant or model harness; it requires no vendor-specific tools.

## Choose the part for the job

Read [the core](CARD.md), then only the relevant reference:

- [Planning](references/planning.md): select the buyer, appeal and the fact that earns belief. Keep missing evidence visible.
- [Words](references/words.md): scripts, hooks, headlines, feed copy and page copy, within their existing limits.
- [Visuals](references/visuals.md): image ads, demonstrations, shot direction and image prompts that carry the approved argument.
- [Editing](references/editing.md): diagnose a weak selling line without replanning an approved ad.

The buyer's awareness stage governs when the product and offer appear. A cold story can open on a recognisable situation; a ready buyer may need only the last doubt answered and the actual deal. There is no new beat order, compulsory demo, word count or aggressive tone.

Use the brief's existing notes, rationale, sources or test-plan fields to explain an appeal and its evidence gaps. Do not add a scorecard, predicted uplift, invented offer or new output field. When proposing alternatives, distinguish the reason to buy rather than simply changing adjectives. A controlled comparison keeps the other decisions stable.

The source's absolute claims about prevention, length, founder writing, samples and perpetual winners are not universal rules. Prevention can be the real value of a product. A founder can speak honestly. Useful copy can be short or long. Trials and guarantees must already exist. Creative decisions need current evidence and the user's request.
