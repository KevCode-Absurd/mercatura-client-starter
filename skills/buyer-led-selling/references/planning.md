### Find the fact that earns the appeal

Read specifications, approved claims, usage instructions, process details and customer research for one buyer-relevant fact that generic adjectives hide. Select it because it answers this buyer's desire or doubt, not because it is an impressive number. Trace it to the supplied source and preserve its qualifications in the existing rationale or notes.

A verified ordinary process can make a vague promise concrete. Explain what it means to the buyer without claiming it is exclusive, patented or superior. Never turn an industry practice into a unique mechanism without evidence. Prefer a present, observable benefit where it fits; do not invent a result or suppress a legitimate protection or prevention benefit.

Different concepts test different appeals. Keep hypotheses distinct from measured results. Identify the missing evidence if an appeal cannot be supported.
