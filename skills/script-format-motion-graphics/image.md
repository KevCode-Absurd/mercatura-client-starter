# Motion graphics as a still image: 1 designed graphic, type and product

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees the product beside 1 diagram, chart or row of icons, and reads the headline once.

## The frame

- **1 message, 1 supporting visual.** Choose 1: a diagram (how it works, in 2 or 3 links), a chart (1 comparison in figures the brand holds), or a row of 3 or 4 icons (what is in it, or the steps of use). Never 2 of them.
- **The product is a photographic cutout,** large, front to the viewer, at its true proportions and colours. The visual is tied to it: a line from the diagram to the part it explains, the chart beside it on 1 baseline, the icons in a row beneath.
- **A grid with wide margins.** 1 focal point where the eye lands first; everything else lines up to it. A third of the frame is plain ground.
- **By stage:** a solution-aware buyer gets the diagram, or the comparison with the old way; a product-aware buyer the icons or the figure; a most-aware buyer the offer, with the cutout and 1 line of terms.
- **A chart is a measurement:** its shapes are in the proportions of the brand's figures. No figures on file, no chart.

## Words on the picture

This format is words and figures, so its limit is higher than other looks: 30 words in all.

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer, the largest thing after the product, in the top third. It says what the buyer gets, or the 1 figure that proves it.
- 3 type sizes only: headline, label, small print. Labels are 1 to 4 words each, set at the part they name: 2 or 3 on a diagram, 1 under each icon, 1 on each bar of a chart.
- 1 figure may be set large. Every number, unit and term is from the brand's facts, written exactly as the brand writes it. A number that is not on file is not set.
- The offer, when there is one, is 1 line with a button, in a strip at the foot.
- Every word is given exactly, in quotes, in the brand's own typefaces. A comparison names the old way or the category, never a competitor.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: an original flat graphic design, a designed advertisement and not a photograph of a scene. A plain ground in 1 brand colour; flat geometric shapes in 2 or 3 brand colours and 1 accent, on an even grid with wide margins.
- 1 shape language (all square corners or all rounded) and 1 icon style (1 line weight, 1 corner radius). Name each shape, its colour and where it sits.
- Even, shadowless light: no gradients, no gloss, no texture, no depth but 1 soft shadow under the product. Seen flat and square on.
- The product is the 1 photographic thing: cut out cleanly from its photo, its true shape, colours and printing, no letter redrawn.
- Type is the brand's typefaces, crisp and level, in the named sizes and colours. Give each piece of copy in quotes with its place.
- A chart: name each shape and its proportion ("the left bar twice the height of the right").
- Not a 3D render, not an illustrated scene, no people. No words, numbers, logos or marks beyond those given, no watermark, no interface.

## What fails in a still

- An infographic: 5 steps, 3 charts and a paragraph. Nobody reads it in the feed.
- Type too small for a phone, or 4 sizes of it.
- A bar chart with no figures behind it, drawn because it looked like proof.
- The product redrawn as an icon, or its label reset in the brand's typeface.
- Shapes that decorate and point at nothing.
