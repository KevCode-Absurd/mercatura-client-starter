# Format: motion graphics

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it motion graphics

- **An information system built round the product.** Flat shapes, icons, diagrams, charts and type on a grid, with the real product as a photographic cutout at the centre. Nothing is filmed, and nothing is a rendered 3D world.
- **The brand's colours and typefaces are the system.** A compact design system is fixed before a line is written: the grid, 3 type sizes, the palette, 1 shape language, 1 icon style, how the cutout is treated, 1 family of transitions. Every shot uses it and nothing else.
- **1 message and 1 supporting visual to a shot,** with 1 focal point. The visual is 1 of: the cutout, a diagram, a chart, a row of icons.
- **Movement explains.** A thing moves to show 1 of 4 things: what matters most (hierarchy), what leads to what (cause), this against that (comparison), how it works (mechanism). Movement that shows none of the 4 is cut.
- **It reads on a phone:** copy is large, short and held long enough to read.

## What it is good for

It holds figures, parts and terms more clearly than a filmed ad: what is in it, how the steps go, what the offer is. It has no face, and it looks like an ad from the first frame. So it is weak for a problem the buyer has to feel and for proof that rests on a person.

By stage: strongest for product-aware and most-aware buyers (the figures, the contents, the offer in plain terms) and for solution-aware buyers (the mechanism, or a comparison with the old way). An unaware or problem-aware buyer scrolls past a graphic: another format does more.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

- **A chart is a measurement.** Every bar, ring and line is drawn in the proportions of figures the brand's facts hold, and carries only those figures. No data, no chart.
- **An illustrative shape is visibly not a measurement:** no axis, no scale, no bars, no figure beside it. A circle growing is a picture of "more"; 2 bars are a claim of how much more.
- **A diagram** draws the mechanism only as `How it works` gives it, link by link. None is invented. No body before and after.
- **A comparison** is with the old way or the category, never a named competitor.
- **The product** keeps its true proportions, colours and printing. Numbers, study results and medical results come only from the brand's facts.

## What to avoid

- 2 messages in 1 shot, or 2 visuals competing for the eye.
- Movement as decoration: shapes that drift, spin or pulse and explain nothing.
- The brand's colours swapped for prettier ones, or the product redrawn as an icon.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette` and `Typography` (they are the design system), `Motion and sound` (pace, music or none), `Proof on file` (the figures a chart may use), `Brand guardrails`. Palette or typefaces blank: take the colours from the pack and a plain sans-serif, and mark the system "inferred".
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Looks like` and `Pack` (the cutout), `How it works` (the diagram), `Made of` (the icons), `How it is used`, `Replaces` (the comparison), `Core benefit`, `Bundles and offers`. `How it works` blank: no diagram, or ask.
- `customer-avatars.md`: `What almost stops them` (the 1 message worth a shot), `Customer language bank` (the headline's words).
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer, the guarantee. A figure that is not there is not set and not charted.
