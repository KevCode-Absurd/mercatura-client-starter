# Motion graphics as a video: narrated plates, type laid over

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, at phone size, a stranger reads every line once and can say what the product does and what to do next.

## How to write for it

- **2 layers, written apart.** The video model makes wordless moving plates: the product cutout turning, shapes, icons, a diagram drawing itself. Exact type, numbers and the logo are laid over afterward. So a line's on-screen text is what is laid over, exactly as it should read, and its visual note describes the plate under it, with no word, number or logo in it.
- **Narration, 1 voice, off screen.** Nobody appears and nobody speaks to camera. The on-screen text is the same message in fewer words, never a second message.
- **1 line is 1 shot: 1 message, 1 supporting visual.** Name the visual (the cutout, a diagram, a chart, a row of icons) and which of the 4 things its movement shows: hierarchy, cause, comparison or mechanism.
- **One shape that works:** the claim or the figure first; then how it works, or this against the old way; then what is in it or how it is used; then the offer.
- **The product** is in the first shot as a photographic cutout, and stays the fixed point the shapes arrange round.
- **One close that works:** the cutout centred and still, the offer and the call to action as on-screen text, the narration saying the same.

## Length

15 to 30 seconds, 45 at most. The brief gives the shots and their length. Past 45 seconds a graphic has stopped explaining and started listing.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- Describe the system the same way in every visual note: the ground colour, the 2 or 3 brand colours and the accent, the shape language, the icon style, the cutout.
- The cutout and the shapes the ad uses are in the first shot. A new diagram or chart arrives only by the system's 1 transition family (a slide along the grid, a colour block wiping across, a scale from the focal point, or a line drawing itself): say which, and use it every time.
- 1 move to a shot. The plate is still while its copy is read; only the thing the copy names may move.
- On-screen text: 6 words or fewer a shot, 1 figure at most, on screen long enough to be read once. Say where the plate leaves a plain area for it (the top third, the left column).
- A chart's shapes are in the proportions of the brand's figures: write them in the visual note ("the second bar half the height of the first"). The figures themselves go in the on-screen text. No data: an illustrative shape with no axis and no bars.
- The camera is locked and square to the ground in every shot; a cut changes how large the cutout or the diagram is. Flat shapes slide, scale, turn and wipe at real speed, ease to a stop and hold. Nothing morphs.
- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip.
- Only the last shot of the ad stays held past the time its copy needs.
- The product's own printing is the only lettering in a plate.

## What to avoid in a video

- A visual note with a headline, a number or a logo in it: the model will draw them, wrongly.
- Copy that changes before it can be read, or 3 things moving at once.
- A different transition in every shot.
- Narration that says 1 thing while the type says another.
- A person, a hand or a filmed scene: that is another format.
