# Cinematic 3D: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

Every frame is a still from a premium 3D animated feature film that renders light and materials like a photograph. It is a way of rendering: the story is the script's. Judged on:

1. Believable stylised adult characters: adult proportions, refined faces, slightly enlarged expressive eyes.
2. Photographic light and materials around designed, animated forms. Never live action.
3. Each character is the same in every clip.
4. The product is accurate, label copy included; the picture claims only what the script's lines and the brand's claims state.

## The look

1 look, preset `default`. `visual_direction` opens with the style block, word for word:

"A frame from a premium 3D animated feature with photographic realism in its light and materials: real-world light behaviour, ray-traced reflections, physically accurate textures (fabric weave, wood grain, glass, liquid), filmic colour grading and anamorphic depth of field, all with the designed, stylised forms of an animated feature."

It closes with the camera block:

"Composed like a cinematic film still, with the camera inside the scene and the scene filling the entire frame. A high-end 3D animated feature render."

The blocks set defaults; a scene overrides them by saying its own light ("soft grey light from a window with rain-speckled glass, muted and cool") or framing ("a close-up of a hand holding up...", "a zoomed-in macro view").

## The character, so it stays the same

- A person or an animal is named with the style every time ("the stylised 3D animated man"): "a man" alone renders a photograph of a real one (seen on a still, 2026-10-06).
- Only when people are in frame, `visual_direction` carries the character block:

"Characters are stylised 3D animated humans with believable adult proportions about six heads tall: slightly enlarged expressive eyes with detailed realistic irises, a refined stylised face structure with defined cheekbones, jaw and brow and a simplified nose, skin with realistic subsurface scattering and very fine texture, individually groomed hair strands with a natural sheen, and clothing with real fabric texture and natural drape. Unmistakably animated characters, rendered at feature-film quality."

- A video with no people (the product alone, a mechanism) leaves it out: it summons people into the scene.
- `character` describes the person in full: age band, build, hair colour and style, clothing, 1 accessory. What is left unsaid drifts. Every `visual` names them by the same 2 or 3 words.
- An original, fictional adult. Never a child, a real person or a known character; no studio's name or logo. Never a customer, a founder or an expert.
- An animal lead is described in the scene in this look, without the character block.
- A feeling is named, then written as face mechanics: "her inner brows pushed up and her mouth a flat tired line". An adjective alone gives a pleasant blank face.

## What the camera, the set and the material do

- `camera`: inside the scene, composed like a film still; the world runs past every edge of the frame. Each cut changes how close the camera is and how it is held. Close views for hands, the product and a mechanism.
- `setting`: the main place, lit as the scene says. A shot that leaves it names its own place and light. No signs, screens or labels with words.
- 1 clear subject and 1 action a shot.
- A mechanism is shown arriving and acting, mid-action, as "a zoomed-in macro view, no people in the scene", and only as the brand's facts give it. A body is described in plain visual words (layers, strands, a droplet), never clinical terms, and never before and after.

## The product on screen

- First of `props`. Its `look` gives its true shape and colours and states every line of label copy exactly, with its colour ("a white pouch with a clean label reading SALMON DINNER in navy letters").
- In the scene from the shot where the words reach it, in the character's hand or in use, label to the lens.
- Alone in frame it reads as a product photograph: keep a character, a hand or a stylised prop with it when it should read as animation.

## Sound and speech

- `dialogue` is narration or 1 character speaking, 1 speaker at a time. `voice_direction` says which: "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" or the character by name, with age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice.
- A speaking character's face is in view; nobody else's mouth moves. It is fiction and gives no testimonial: it states no result of its own.
- Under the voice: the room's own sounds.

## Movement

- Every `visual` opens with the motion line: "Smooth, cinematic 3D animation at feature-film quality, with natural weight and subtle, believable performance."
- 1 motion action a shot. Stacked actions multiply drift: the second action is the next shot.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The character in the room, wide, like an establishing film still.
- The face, close, as the feeling changes.
- Hands on the product, very close, label to the lens.
- Over the shoulder at what the character looks at.
- The mechanism as a macro view with no people.
- From low or from high, on the turn of the story.

## Transitions

A hard cut may change the place. A change of time, or the turn of the story, may use 1 of these instead, named as 1 concrete visible action:

- Camera-bridge (the anchor holds, the camera moves): a slow push-in from the scene to the product label; a rack focus from a foreground object to the character behind it; a push into macro to reveal the mechanism.
- World-bridge (the camera holds, the world changes around the anchor): the light shifts from grey morning to golden evening around the character; a dark, cold room warms as the curtains open.
- Match cut: a gesture continues across the cut into the next scene, or a round shape becomes the next scene's round shape.

## First and last frame

- First: the character mid-action, the feeling already on the face.
- Last: the character with the product, its label clear and sharp, held, the top third soft and plain for the offer line.

## Avoid

- 2 actions in 1 shot. A crowd. A second speaker in the same clip.
- A character who says what the product did for them.
- A result, a body change or a mechanism the script does not state.

## After the render

- Still animation: no live-action skin, no rounded toy-like cartoon.
- The character's face, hair and clothes match the first clip.
- The feeling shows on the face.
- The label copy is exact; no other letters anywhere.
- Only the speaker's mouth moved.
