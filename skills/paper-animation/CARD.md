# Paper world: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narrated story inside a paper world. Judged on: every frame reads as real photographed paper (grain, edge thickness, cast shadows), never flat vector art; every frame is shot from inside the paper world; 1 look throughout; the product's shape, colours and label copy are true; the picture claims only what the script and the brand's claims state.

## The looks

1 look to a video, from the job's preset. Its wording opens `visual_direction` word for word.

- `papercraft` (default): "Layered papercraft world: everything built from stacked cardstock with visible paper thickness, deep layered depth between foreground, midground and background paper planes, layered cast shadows, paper quilling details, the camera inside the scene and the paper world extending past every edge of the frame."
- `cutout`: "Flat construction-paper cutout scene: simple layered flat card shapes, snipped edges, visible paper grain, jointed paper puppet characters, soft drop shadows between the layers, bold flat colours, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the paper world."
- `handmade`: "Handmade paper stop-motion scene: torn paper edges, visible glue seams, construction-paper grain, hand-placed imperfection, soft practical lighting with visible falloff, the scene filling the entire frame."
- `collage`: "Mixed-media paper collage world, the entire scene built from cutouts composed into one deep coherent scene: black-and-white photographic cutout faces on hand-drawn paper bodies, clothing collaged from colourful patterned paper, structures built from cardboard and newspaper fragments, vintage magazine clippings, washi tape strips and vintage stamps tucked through the scene, hand-drawn ink details over the paper, rich saturated collage colours against newsprint neutrals, the camera inside the scene."
- `origami`: "Origami world scene, everything folded from paper with clean geometric creases: delicate origami figures, crisp angular folds on every object, pleated paper details, soft pastel-coloured papers, soft diffused lighting casting gentle shadows that reveal the dimensional paper folds, the handcrafted feel of a stop-motion paper world, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the paper world."

## The character, so it stays the same

- `character` is 1 sentence: a papercraft man or woman, adult, heads tall, paper colour, hair as 1 card shape, 2 clothing colours, its joints, dot eyes, a snipped mouth. Every `visual` names it by the same 2 or 3 words.
- A fictional adult by its proportions and dress. Never a child, a real person or a known character. In `collage` its cutout face is an invented adult's. Its mouth forms no words.

## What the camera, the set and the material do

- `camera` states a position inside the world: at the paper character's eye level, a worm's-eye view up at the cardstock buildings, or straight down on the paper street.
- Never write "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop" or "model": they show the craft from outside. If needed add "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene".
- `setting`: the main paper place, running past every edge of the frame, with no words in it. A shot that leaves it names its own.
- `visual_direction` ends: "Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed: visible paper grain and fibres, cut edges showing paper thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio lighting, the paper shot filling the entire frame." In `origami`: "real physical folded paper", "crisp crease lines, folded edges showing paper thickness".
- Colour is saturated and high in contrast, the palette named (origami: soft pastel).
- Every `visual` names the paper on each main thing ("the paper waves", "the cardstock sun"). Near a face or the product say "papercraft", never "paper cut".

## The product on screen

- First of `props`. Made of paper at its true shape and colours; its `look` states every line of label copy exactly, with its colour.
- In `collage` it is its real photo, a cutout with rough scissor-cut edges.
- In view from the shot where the words reach it, front to the lens.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:". All `dialogue` is narration.
- Under it: small paper sounds.

## Movement

- Every `visual` carries: "stop-motion cadence, 12 fps judder, slight frame-to-frame jitter, no motion blur" (`papercraft` may soften to "subtle stepped motion").
- All motion is planar. 1 move to a shot: a push-in through the layered paper planes with parallax (the strongest); a paper element slides across the frame or pivots at 1 corner; a jointed paper puppet raises 1 arm from the shoulder.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The flat wide tableau, every paper layer in view.
- A close on 1 paper figure, at its eye level.
- Straight down on the paper street or spread.
- A worm's-eye view up at the cardstock world.
- The paper product, close, its label to the lens.
- At the turn of the story, a page turn or a layer peel as the cut.

## Transitions

A hard cut may change the view or the setting. At the turn of the story the cut is made of paper, by 1 of these:

- "the entire paper shot lifts at one edge and turns like a storybook page, revealing the next shot beneath"
- "slow push-in into the cutout opening; the opening fills the frame and the next shot lies inside it"
- "the top paper layer peels back from one corner and lifts away, revealing the next shot beneath it"
- "the paper pieces of the scene slide apart and exit the frame; the pieces of the next shot slide in and settle into place"
- "the scene folds flat and closes; the next shot rises and unfolds like a pop-up book spread"
- "a torn paper edge sweeps across the frame, tearing the scene away to reveal the next shot behind it"
- "the camera pans past a large foreground paper cloud that fills the frame, and emerges into the next shot"

## First and last frame

- First: inside the first setting, the problem the largest shape, 1 paper piece already moving.
- Last: the product's front clear in the changed scene, held.

## Avoid

- 2 looks in 1 video. A paper transition in every clip. Fast action, crowds, pouring liquid.
- A result the script does not state. A body before and after.

## After the render

- Paper is still paper: grain and edge thickness, no vector flatness, no clay.
- No frame, table edge or board in view. Motion stepped and planar.
- The label copy is exact; no other letters. No mouth formed words.
