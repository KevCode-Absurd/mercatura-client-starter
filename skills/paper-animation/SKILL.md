# Paper world ad

A story told inside a world made of paper. The look is a skin: the story comes from the script, and each picture shows what the narration says at that moment. Nobody lip-syncs.

## What it is judged on

1. **Every frame reads as photographed physical paper:** grain, edge thickness, real cast shadows. Without them it drifts into flat digital vector illustration.
2. **Every frame is a scene shot from inside the paper world,** never the craft object seen from outside.
3. **1 look, the same in every still and clip.**
4. **The product has its true shape, colours and label copy,** and the picture claims nothing the brand's facts do not.

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, materials, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, clip lengths, how narration is written, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-paper`: how the story and the narration are written. This skill is the paper.

## What makes anything read as paper

1. Visible material texture on every element: paper grain, fibres, cardstock weight.
2. Physically plausible cast shadows between layers. Shadows are how flat art gains depth.
3. Imperfect edges: torn, scissor-cut or white-bordered, never a clean vector line.
4. Planar motion: elements slide, pivot and parallax as flat planes. Paper never squashes, stretches or moves organically.
5. Stepped, stuttered timing: a handmade cadence, not smooth easing.

## 1. Choose the look

1 look to a video. Its style block opens every still and clip prompt, word for word. Sameness across shots comes from repeating the block and re-attaching the approved pictures, never from "same style as before".

**`papercraft`, layered papercraft world (the default).** For a product as hero, a premium or gift feel, pushes through depth.

```
Layered papercraft world: everything built from stacked cardstock with visible paper thickness, deep layered depth between foreground, midground and background paper planes, layered cast shadows, paper quilling details, the camera inside the scene and the paper world extending past every edge of the frame.
```

**`cutout`, flat construction-paper cutout.** For comedy, character skits, low-fi charm, irony.

```
Flat construction-paper cutout scene: simple layered flat card shapes, snipped edges, visible paper grain, jointed paper puppet characters, soft drop shadows between the layers, bold flat colours, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the paper world.
```

**`handmade`, handmade stop-motion paper.** For warmth and honesty in a short product ad.

```
Handmade paper stop-motion scene: torn paper edges, visible glue seams, construction-paper grain, hand-placed imperfection, soft practical lighting with visible falloff, the scene filling the entire frame.
```

**`collage`, mixed-media collage.** For an argument, and a story with a human subject. The background is part of the scene, never a board behind pasted items. A cutout face is an invented adult's.

```
Mixed-media paper collage world, the entire scene built from cutouts composed into one deep coherent scene: black-and-white photographic cutout faces on hand-drawn paper bodies, clothing collaged from colourful patterned paper, structures built from cardboard and newspaper fragments, vintage magazine clippings, washi tape strips and vintage stamps tucked through the scene, hand-drawn ink details over the paper, rich saturated collage colours against newsprint neutrals, the camera inside the scene.
```

**`origami`, origami world.** For calm, premium, gentle brands, and for a transformation. Soft pastels (blush, sage, cream) let the fold shadows carry the frame.

```
Origami world scene, everything folded from paper with clean geometric creases: delicate origami figures, crisp angular folds on every object, pleated paper details, soft pastel-coloured papers, soft diffused lighting casting gentle shadows that reveal the dimensional paper folds, the handcrafted feel of a stop-motion paper world, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the paper world.
```

## 2. Fix the character and the world

- **The character** is written once, in 1 sentence, and used unchanged: "A papercraft woman, adult, about 4 heads tall, tan cardstock, a black card bob, a teal card coat, jointed at shoulder and elbow, 2 dot eyes, a snipped mouth." A fictional adult by its proportions and dress. Never a child, a real person or a known character. It does not talk.
- **The camera lives inside the paper world.** The world extends past every edge of the frame and the camera sits in it: at eye level with a paper character, a worm's-eye view up at the cardstock buildings, overhead looking down on the paper street. The angle is free per shot; the interior viewpoint is not. State it in every prompt.
- **The exterior-view failure:** the words "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop" and "model" pull the picture outside the scene, to a framed box, a workbench or a flat-lay board. Never use them. If a frame, a table edge or a board still shows, re-anchor as in the table below.
- **The product** is made of paper, at its true shape and colours, with every line of its label copy stated exactly and its photo attached. In `collage` alone it stays photographic: a photo cutout with rough scissor-cut edges, so the label stays accurate.

## 3. Write the stills

Order: the style block, the scene, then the physicality block, which guards against vector drift:

```
Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed: visible paper grain and fibres, cut edges showing paper thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio lighting, the paper shot filling the entire frame.
```

In `origami` say "real physical folded paper" and "crisp crease lines, folded edges showing paper thickness" in place of the cut-edge wording.

- **Colour is saturated and high in contrast,** the palette stated in every prompt: "saturated greens stepping from lime to deep forest", "a vivid orange sun against a pale sky". Origami alone runs soft pastel.
- **Say "papercraft", not "paper cut", near faces and products.** "Paper cut" blurs fine features; "papercraft" holds them.
- **Weave the material into every element:** "pleated paper towels", "a serum bottle snipped from green card", "shelves layered from vintage magazine clippings".
- **No words in the picture** beyond the product's own label.

## 4. Approve the stills

The character alone, front on. Then the first frame of each setting, with the character picture and the product photo attached and named by their jobs. When a still misses the look, render the same prompt again before rewriting it.

## 5. Write the clips

Left unprompted, a video model smooths everything to organic motion and the paper dies. Every clip prompt carries the motion guard:

```
stop-motion cadence, 12 fps judder, slight frame-to-frame jitter, no motion blur
```

In `papercraft`, soften it to "subtle stepped motion" if full judder is too rough. Repeat the material in the action lines ("the paper waves slide", "the cardstock sun pivots"). 1 move to a clip.

| Move | How to say it | How well it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Slide or rotate flat elements | "the paper [element] slides across the frame", "pivots at one corner" | Good |
| Parallax push through layers | "slow push-in through the layered paper planes, parallax between foreground and background" | Good: the strongest paper move, lead with it |
| Hinge-jointed limbs | "jointed paper puppet raises one arm, pivoting at the shoulder". 1 simple gesture a shot | Medium: over-smoothed into organic motion |
| Pop-in with a hard drop shadow | "a paper [element] pops into frame with its shadow" | Medium: a precise pop is done in the edit |
| Lines that draw themselves | Not asked of the video model: added in the edit | Weak |

## 6. Transitions

Paper itself can carry a change of scene. Use 1 wherever the story moves between settings; inside a setting, a hard cut.

| Transition | How to say it |
|---|---|
| Storybook page turn | "the entire paper shot lifts at one edge and turns like a storybook page, revealing the next shot beneath" |
| Cut-through push | "slow push-in into the cutout opening; the opening fills the frame and the next shot lies inside it" |
| Layer peel | "the top paper layer peels back from one corner and lifts away, revealing the next shot beneath it" |
| Assemble, disassemble | "the paper pieces of the scene slide apart and exit the frame; the pieces of the next shot slide in and settle into place" |
| Pop-up unfold | "the scene folds flat and closes; the next shot rises and unfolds like a pop-up book spread" |
| Torn reveal | "a torn paper edge sweeps across the frame, tearing the scene away to reveal the next shot behind it" |
| Foreground wipe | "the camera pans past a large foreground paper cloud that fills the frame, and emerges into the next shot" |

Made 1 of 3 ways: named at the join inside 1 clip; as a clip of its own, from the last still of setting A to the first of setting B; or with both stills attached, each given its job.

## What fails, and the fix

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Drifts to flat digital vector illustration | The physicality block word for word; add "everything in frame is real photographed paper" |
| Shows the craft from outside: a framed box, a workbench, a flat-lay board | Strike the 4 words; add "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene" and a named interior angle |
| The collage reads as a board of pasted items | Compose every element into 1 deep world; the background is scenery (sky, street, room) |
| Faces or product labels blur | "Papercraft", not "paper cut"; attach the product and character pictures |
| Motion smooths the paper away | Name the cadence ("12 fps judder, no motion blur"); keep all motion planar |
| "Stop motion" alone drifts to clay | Always pair it with "paper": "handmade paper stop-motion" |
| The look drifts across shots | The same style block in every prompt, the approved pictures attached every time |
