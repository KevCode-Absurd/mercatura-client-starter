# Ads in their words: the card

<!-- The rules of SKILL.md with the explanation taken out. Sent to the planner and the words writer of a review set. -->

Every ad of this set carries 1 buyer's phrase, given in the notes under THEIR WORDS, EXACTLY, with its source id. The format of each ad is made to hold a quote; the phrase is the argument.

## The 7 formats

- **Negative qualifier**: "Do not buy this if..." as the headline, the phrase as the quote that says who it is for.
- **Q and A card**: the doubt as a question in a buyer's words, the phrase as the answer.
- **Strikethrough correction**: the brand's own nearest line struck out (never a competitor's), the phrase written in beneath it, "their words, not ours" small.
- **Taped note**: the phrase handwritten on paper taped over a real product photograph.
- **Poll sticker**: a story frame in a design of the app's own, the phrase as the poll's question, 2 short answers.
- **Quote card**: the phrase set very large, the attribution tiny, the product small.
- **Side by side**: before and after as scenes or objects, both halves in buyers' words; never a person's body.

## The phrase

- It is the `quote` text element, character for character: its typos, its lowercase, its grammar. The biggest text on the image.
- If the format cannot hold it whole, trim at a word boundary and say so in `adaptation`. Never reword, tidy, tighten or punch up.
- Attribution is only what the source holds: a first name if the export kept one, else nothing. No invented name, initial, city or star count. A reviewer the notes say was given the product is marked so beside the quote.
- Every other word on the image is plainer than the phrase: a question, a label, a struck-out line. If your line is the most interesting line on the ad, cut it.

## The feed copy

- `headline`: the phrase trimmed at a word boundary to 40 characters or fewer, never reworded.
- `primary_text`: 60 to 110 words. Open with an identity or trigger line so the right person self-selects. Quote at least 2 of the other buyers given in the notes, from different sources, in quotation marks. One quote, one plain sentence, one quote; never 3 quotes in a row. Close with 1 plain call to action.
- `description`: the doubt in the objection list, answered, under 30 characters. Never a discount.
- Your connective writing is plainer than the quotes. No exclamation marks, no em dashes, none of game-changer, elevate, unlock, transform.
- Across the set, open with a different buyer each time: 6 ads built on 1 buyer's voice is 1 ad.

## Rules

1. If the line is not in a source, it does not go in the ad.
2. One angle per ad: nothing from a second theme.
3. A number in a quote is still a number the brand must hold; a quoted disease claim is still a disease claim.
4. No before-and-after of a body, no invented customer, no competitor named.
