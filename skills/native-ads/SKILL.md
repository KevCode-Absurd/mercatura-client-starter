# Native image ads

A native image ad is an image with a long story attached. In the feed it looks like something a person posted, not like an advertisement. The image stops the right person. The story does the selling: it tells the reader's own experience back so exactly that they recognise themselves, then shows the product as the way from where they are to where they want to be.

It is judged on 3 things: the right person stops, they read to the end, and nothing in it would embarrass the brand if the reader knew exactly how it was made.

## When it suits, and when it does not

It suits a product whose buyer has a history with the problem: something they live with, work around or have paid to fix more than once.

- **A product that is used up** (a cream, a supplement, a pet food): the routine that kept failing, the shelf of things half used.
- **A product bought once** (a pan, a jacket, a suitcase): the ones bought before and thrown away, or the day the last one let them down. The proof is the first use and what they no longer buy, carry or work around.

It does not suit an impulse buy, a novelty, or a buyer who has never tried to fix the problem. Nobody reads 1,200 words to buy a novelty. Say so in the notes and write the short length.

## Anatomy

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Image, 1:1 | Stops the scroll for the right person. Looks like a phone photo or a screenshot, never a designed ad. It does not explain the product, and the product is often not in it. |
| First 2 or 3 lines of the story | The hook. Shown in the feed before "See more". Decides whether the story gets opened. |
| The story | The 8 movements in native-ad-copy: opening, the weight of it, the breaking point, the reframe, the bridge, first experience, what changed, the close. |
| Headline (under the image) | Tells the right person to read. It names something they do or a situation they are in: "Read this if you've bought 3 pans in 5 years." Never a condition, a body or a trait they have. |
| Button | Shop now, to the product page. Learn more, when it leads to an article. |

## Tension points

A tension point is a specific, felt problem, said the way people say it: "my face feels a size too small by lunchtime", "I've replaced this pan 4 times and it still sticks". It sits on top of a desire: the desire is what they want, the tension is why they don't have it and what it is costing them.

Each tension comes from the research and is ranked by how often it appears and how strong the language is. A story leads with 1 tension and carries a second underneath, because people rarely feel only one. The research for a tension also gives what they tried before and why each failed, the scenes where it shows, what they feel and don't say aloud, and the phrases they use.

## Two ways to aim

| | Tension-led | Avatar-led |
|---|---|---|
| The opening names | An experience anyone with the problem has | A specific kind of person (their work, their home, their means) |
| Reach | Wide | Narrow, deep |
| Risk | Blends in when rivals say the same thing | The group is too small |

## Honesty

A native ad may look like a person's post. It may not pass something made up as something real.

- **Who is speaking is decided before a word is written:** a real customer telling their own story, the founder, a real named expert, or nobody in particular (a composite, or an observer). A composite is never passed off as 1 real customer: an invented first name at most, no surname, no "verified buyer", no photo offered as them, and the story's last line says it is put together from what customers have told the brand.
- **A borrowed form is a form, not a forgery.** A bulletin look is not a news outlet reporting an event. A review card shows only a real review, word for word, with the stars that customer gave. A note or a chat is the narrator's own, with invented first names, and is never offered as real people's messages. No real app, platform, outlet or person is named or copied.
- **It runs from the brand's own page,** never from a made-up person's profile.
- **Understating sells better than overstating.** This reader has been oversold before.

## Tested in sets

5 stories on 1 tension: a lead story and 4 others, each with a different arc, form and image type, so that what wins can be told apart. How a set is run and read is in `testing.md` beside this file. That file is for the person running the ads and is not sent to a writer.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- **The tension, the things tried, the scenes, the phrases.** `customer-avatars.md`: Ranked insight bank; Customer language bank; per avatar, Grounded in, Why they buy this product, What almost stops them, The moment. Blank: there is no native ad to write. Ask for research, or write an observer piece from the brand's claims alone and mark it "inferred".
- **Whether the buyer has a history with the problem.** `brain.md`: Market awareness level, Market sophistication. Blank: assume they have tried things, and say that was assumed.
- **Who may speak.** A customer's story or review cleared for use; the founder's own words; a named expert who has agreed. The draft contract has no home for these. Proposed: `customer-avatars.md`, `Stories and reviews we may use`, and `brand-guidelines.md`, `People on file`. Blank: a composite with its closing line, or an observer.
