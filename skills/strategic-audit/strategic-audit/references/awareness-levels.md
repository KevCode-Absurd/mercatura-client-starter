# Awareness Levels: What to Lead With at Each Stage

> The 5 awareness levels determine what you say first. Get this wrong and the best ad in the world won't convert, because you're answering a question they're not asking yet.

**Sources:** Eugene Schwartz (Breakthrough Advertising), Harry's Killer Performance Ads, Soar creative testing framework (2025).

---

## The Customer's Voice at Each Level

| Level | What they're thinking |
|---|---|
| Unaware | "I have no idea what you're talking about" |
| Problem Aware | "I know I have a problem, but I don't know how to fix it" |
| Solution Aware | "I know what I need, but I don't know which product to choose" |
| Product Aware | "I love this, but do I really need it?" |
| Most Aware | "I'm almost ready to buy, but I need a final push" |

Your ad must speak to WHERE they are, not where you wish they were.

---

## The 5 Levels

### 1. Problem Unaware (10% of budget)
They don't even know they have a problem yet. You have to surface the pain.

**Lead with:** The problem they don't know they have.
**Hook style:** Pattern interrupt, education, "Did you know..." framing
**Example (gut supplement):** "Why you're always tired (and what your gut has to do with it)"
**Example (blanket):** "You're losing 2 hours of deep sleep every night, and it's not your mattress"

**Mistake to avoid:** Don't pitch the product. They're not ready. Surface the problem first.

### 2. Problem Aware (40% of budget: biggest chunk)
They know the problem. They're feeling it. They don't know solutions exist or which one to pick.

**Lead with:** The problem + hint at a solution category.
**Hook style:** Empathy, "I've been there," agitation of the pain
**Example (gut supplement):** "Is your gut health behind your fatigue?"
**Example (camera bag):** "Tired of bags that make you choose between your gear and your style?"

**This is where most TOF ad spend should live.** Biggest audience, most scalable.

### 3. Solution Aware (30% of budget)
They know solutions exist. They're comparing. They need to know WHY your mechanism is different.

**Lead with:** Your unique mechanism, differentiator, or approach.
**Hook style:** Comparison, "Here's what's different," authority, education
**Example (gut supplement):** "This gut-friendly supplement VS the competitor"
**Example (tablecloth):** "Spill-proof, wipe-clean, looks like real linen, not plastic"

**Key:** At this level, features and mechanism matter. But frame them as outcomes, not specs.

### 4. Product Aware (15% of budget)
They know YOUR product. They've seen it, maybe visited the site. They need proof and nudge.

**Lead with:** Social proof, results, scarcity, authority.
**Hook style:** Testimonials, numbers, "why people love this," urgency
**Example (gut supplement):** "This gut-friendly supplement sold out 5 times. Here's why."
**Example (blanket):** "250,000+ moms chose this blanket for their baby."

### 5. Most Aware (5% of budget)
They know you, they want it, they just need the push, usually price or urgency.

**Lead with:** The offer, the deal, the deadline.
**Hook style:** Direct CTA, discount, free shipping, limited time
**Example (gut supplement):** "Trusted by 100K+ women with gut issues. 20% off RN."
**Example (camera bag):** "Free shipping ends tonight. The Chocolate Drop is almost gone."

## Budget Allocation by Awareness Level

| Level | % of Ad Budget | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Unaware | 10% | Small: hard to convert, but builds top of funnel |
| Problem Aware | 40% | Biggest scalable audience: this is where most TOF spend goes |
| Solution Aware | 30% | High intent, comparing options: strong conversion potential |
| Product Aware | 15% | Retargeting, returning visitors: warm traffic |
| Most Aware | 5% | Bottom funnel, deal-seekers: smallest but highest conversion rate |

These shares are the source's rule of thumb for budget. The app plans by count of new ads: 70 to 90% for unaware and problem-aware buyers. Where the 2 differ, the app's plan wins. In an audit, a level with nothing running is a gap whatever the split.

A product bought once (a pan, a jacket, a bag) keeps its buyer longer at solution-aware and product-aware, comparing price against years of use, fit, materials, and what happens if it is wrong. An account for such a product with nothing at those 2 levels has its biggest gap there, not at the top.

## Awareness Level × Hook Type Matrix

| Awareness Level | Best Hook Types | Worst Hook Types |
|---|---|---|
| Problem Unaware | Pattern interrupt, "Did you know", education | Direct CTA, testimonials, price |
| Problem Aware | Empathy, identity, pain agitation | Hard sell, ingredient lists |
| Solution Aware | Comparison, mechanism, "Here's what's different" | Vague benefits, generic claims |
| Product Aware | Social proof, numbers, authority, reviews | Problem education (they already know) |
| Most Aware | Offer, urgency, scarcity, direct CTA | Education (they don't need convincing) |

---

## How to Diagnose Awareness-Level Misalignment

If an ad has good CTR but low conversion → you might be hooking the right level but losing them in the body. The body is speaking to a different level than the hook.

If an ad has low CTR entirely → the hook might be targeting the wrong awareness level for the audience. Check if you're educating people who already know, or selling to people who aren't ready.

If retargeting ads underperform → you might be running problem-aware copy to product-aware people. They've already visited your site, lead with proof and offer, not problem agitation.
