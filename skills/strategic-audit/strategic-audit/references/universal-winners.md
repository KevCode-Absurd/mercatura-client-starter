# Patterns seen across brands

> 6 patterns that returned well in more than 1 account at the agency this skill came from. They are starting points to test, not results for the brand being audited. The accounts were mostly lower-priced goods (baby, pet, hair, wellness, accessories, home), so each pattern is marked for where it is likely to carry over.

---

## The 6 patterns

### 1. Lead with who the buyer becomes
**Pattern:** The headline names the person, not the feature. "For bold photographers" in place of "Camera bag with padded dividers".
**Why it may work:** People buy to be someone. The ad reads as made for them.
**Where:** Cold audiences. Strongest where the product is seen by others or says something about its owner: clothing, bags, cookware on show, anything given as a gift. Weaker for a product nobody sees (a supplement, a mattress protector) unless the identity is a habit ("the one who never skips breakfast").

### 2. Something really is new
**Pattern:** A new colour, pattern, flavour or batch, announced as news: "The new pattern is here."
**Why it may work:** News is a reason to look again without cutting the price.
**Where:** Brands that do release colours, flavours or seasonal runs. **Only when it is true.** An existing product is never called new, limited or nearly gone to make a reason. That is false urgency, and the app does not write it.

### 3. Doubt first, then belief
**Pattern:** Open with a complaint or disbelief ("I bought this as a joke", "I nearly sent it back"), then turn.
**Why it may work:** It breaks the expected "buy this" opening, and a belief that was earned reads as more honest than praise.
**Where:** A person to camera, cold audiences, categories with low trust. The doubt must be one that a real customer voiced (a review, a ticket). A generated person does not say "this worked for me".

### 4. Bought for 1 person, wanted by another
**Pattern:** A product for a baby, a child or a pet that the adult ends up wanting. "She stole the baby's blanket."
**Why it may work:** A moment people recognise, and 2 buyers for 1 product.
**Where:** Any brand whose range spans ages or family members. For a product bought once it is also the bridge to the second purchase.

### 5. Matching
**Pattern:** 2 people with the same product: sisters, a parent and child, friends.
**Why it may work:** The picture itself asks for 2 units.
**Where:** Clothing, accessories, blankets, anything that comes in sizes or colours. No generated children: this pattern needs real photos the brand has the right to use.

### 6. What it does, before what is in it
**Pattern:** "Less shedding" before "contains biotin".
**Why it may work:** An ingredient or a material raises a question. A result answers the one that matters.
**Where:** Everything. For a product that is used up, the ingredient follows the result. For a product bought once, the material follows what it does ("holds heat when the steak goes in: 3 mm steel").

## Formats in an outside benchmark (Motion, 2026)

An outside figure, not this brand's, and not re-checked here: from $1.3B of spend across 550K+ ads. Hit rate is the share of ads in a format that became large winners. Spend use ratio is the format's share of spend over its share of ads made; above 1.0 it takes more spend than its numbers.

| Format | Hit rate | Spend use ratio | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| An offer-first banner | 8.6% | 1.3 | Takes spend well when the offer is real |
| Unboxing | 9.8% | 1.3 | Used less than its results suggest |
| Demonstration | 8.1% | 1.0 | Reliable |
| Testimonial | 6.5% | 1.0 | Steady |
| A well-known person | 5.9% | 2.1 | Rare to win, large when it does. Only with that person's agreement |

Also strong by hit rate in that data: point of view, behind the scenes, the founder, a transformation, before and after, a list, a split screen, a montage, a review.

By kind of asset: words only, and a product picture with words, won more often than most teams expect. They are quick to make and clear. A highly produced asset plays a credibility role.

By category in that data: health and wellness leaders included a reaction video, unboxing, the founder, a transformation; fashion and apparel leaders included a note-style image, a quiz, a styled product shot, a plain product shot. Other categories were not broken out, so for cookware, luggage or furniture this table says nothing.

## What held across those accounts

Seen in every category the source agency ran. Treat each as a first guess for a new brand.

1. The feeling comes before the features.
2. For cold audiences, an opening about who the buyer is beat an opening about what the product does.
3. A real new release is a reason to advertise that costs no margin.
4. A real expert or creator posting from their own account can return more than the brand's own account. It needs that person and their say.
5. A still image often beat a video for cold audiences.

A format or pattern in this file that the brand has never run is a gap for section 6. It is recommended as "to test", never as "proven".
