# Category playbooks

> What tended to work, and to fail, in 6 categories at the agency this skill came from. They are other brands' patterns: hypotheses for the brand being audited, never its results. Brand names and return figures from the source have been taken out; they were 1 account each and prove nothing about another brand.

---

## How to use this

Before section 6 (formats) and section 7 (competitors):

1. Decide whether the product is used up or bought once (below). That lens applies to every brand.
2. If the brand's category is listed, read its playbook as a list of things to check: has the brand run each angle and format?
3. If it is not listed, use the lens alone, and the nearest category for who buys.

## The lens: used up, or bought once

| | Used up and bought again | Bought once, after longer thought |
|---|---|---|
| What is being bought | A small daily result or pleasure, and then a habit | A thing to live with for years, and often a statement |
| Formats to check first | A person to camera with a real moment of use; a still with 3 short points; a reaction to taste, feel or scent; a new flavour or batch as news | A demonstration (it working, it being cared for, it after a year); a side-by-side with the thing it replaces; a still of it in a real room or on a real person; a customer a year on |
| Angles to check first | The moment in the day; what it replaced; "tried everything"; what is in it and why | The moment the old one failed; cost per year of use; fit and how to choose; what happens if it is wrong (returns, warranty); the gift |
| What tends to fail | A list of ingredients with no result; "feel your best"; a price-led ad for a premium product | A list of features with no moment; a studio picture with no scale; urgency with no reason; "quality" with nothing to show for it |

## Baby and kids (mostly bought once, often as a gift)

**Who buys:** the new parent, buying for their own child, who wants the best and responds to warmth and "first" moments; the gift buyer, anxious to choose well, who responds to proof that others chose it and to a safe, premium feel; the parent who wants things to match (siblings, a room, an outfit), who responds to new colours and patterns.

**Formats to check:** a person at home with the product, not a studio; a warm still of a real room; several products laid out together to show the range. Real photos only: the app makes no generated children.

**Angles that tended to work:** the first touch, the first days; the gift they will remember; matching; "everything else falls apart after 3 washes" (only with a fact behind it); lasts for years, for the higher-priced items.

**Angles that tended to fail:** a minor holiday as the whole idea; "so soft" with nothing specific; price-led ads for premium items.

## Pets (accessories bought once; food used up)

**Who buys:** the owner for whom the animal is family and the product is part of how they present themselves.

**Formats to check:** the animal wearing or using the product, in motion; a casual, funny clip from the owner's point of view; a carousel for a range with a personality per card.

**Angles that tended to work:** the product as the owner's own taste; "everyone at the park will ask"; a low-priced collectible; bold colour against a sea of beige; a starter kit.

**Angles that tended to fail:** features with no personality ("dirt and odour resistant"); "your dog deserves the best".

## Hair care (products used up; tools and accessories bought once)

**Who buys:** the parent who wants mornings to be easier and cares only about the result; the adult with a hair type the market has ignored.

**Formats to check:** a named expert or creator posting from their own account, with their say; a still that names an occupation or a hair type; a named range as news.

**Angles that tended to work:** naming an occupation or identity ("for nurses, teachers, stylists"); naming the hair type ("made for thick hair"); the morning made easy; doubt turned to belief; the real reason behind a common problem, where the brand's claims hold it.

**Angles that tended to fail:** ingredients with no result; "beautiful hair" with nothing specific; a tutorial with no opening.

## Wellness and supplements (used up)

**Who buys:** the tired seeker who has tried many things and half expects to be let down, who responds to specifics, a mechanism and doubt turned to belief; the buyer for whom wellness is identity, who responds to what is new and what is premium.

**Formats to check:** a real expert, with their say; a product still with 3 short points; a new flavour as news; a customer who doubted.

**Angles that tended to work:** taste or experience first; old knowledge with modern evidence (only where the evidence is on file); a named list of symptoms, within what the claims rules allow; "thousands switched", with the real number.

**Angles that tended to fail:** ingredients alone; "feel your best every day".

Claims in this category are restricted. Every angle above is subject to the brand's claims list.

## Accessories: bags, eyewear, jewellery (bought once)

**Who buys:** someone with a particular taste who is tired of what everyone has. The product says who they are.

**Formats to check:** a still that names the buyer; a real new colour as news; the product in a place the buyer knows.

**Angles that tended to work:** who you are when you own it; the new colour; the gift for the person who has everything.

## Home: tableware, prints, textiles (bought once)

**Who buys:** the person who has been meaning to sort it out and needs the right thing to appear; the gift buyer, often buying for someone hard to buy for.

**Formats to check:** the product in a real room; a demonstration that is also the argument (a spill wiped away in 2 seconds); a still for a named occasion and a named kind of person.

**Angles that tended to work:** something specific to the buyer (a print of their own place); lasting, shown by a demonstration; the occasion and the person ("for the dad who coaches and plays").

## Not in the source: use the lens

Cookware, outerwear, luggage, furniture, footwear, electronics, food and drink were not in the source accounts. For these, section 6 rests on the lens above, the brand's own results and what its competitors run. Say so in the audit rather than borrow a neighbouring category's "what works".
