# Desire Map: What People Actually Want + Micro-Personas

> Universal desires mapped to product categories. Plus: the micro-persona framework for reaching buyers the ads have not reached, with creative made for 1 kind of person.

---

## The Micro-Persona Shift

The era of broad creative is over. "Millennials," "wellness lovers," "people who like skincare": too big, too vague, too messy.

Modern creative targets **one person in one moment with one specific problem they feel deeply.**

Not "women who like blankets" → "the chronically tired new mom who just realized her baby's blanket is softer than anything she owns and now she's jealous of an infant."

Not "men who golf" → "the guy who has every golf gadget but nothing on his walls that shows he's a golfer, and his wife decorated the whole house."

Not "pet owners" → "the French bulldog mom who treats her dog's collar like jewelry and wants people to notice."

### Why Micro-Personas Work

- They tell Meta and TikTok the ad is NEW (different persona = different audience cluster)
- When a persona feels seen, relevance skyrockets
- When relevance skyrockets, attention follows
- When attention lands on the right emotion, conversion becomes almost effortless
- Once the algorithm sees the right people engaging, it finds MORE of them for you

**This is how modern creative scales.** Not by reaching more of the same people, by branching into new persona lanes.

## How to FIND Micro-Personas

Brands don't invent personas in a room, they uncover them from real signals:

| Signal Source | What It Reveals |
|---|---|
| **AI research tools** | Patterns in search behavior, emotional language, psychographic clusters |
| **Seeding (sending product to people)** | Who actually loves it and how they naturally talk about it |
| **Affiliate data** | Which tiny communities convert at crazy rates |
| **Ad comments** | Insecurities, frustrations, desires people express publicly |
| **DTC purchase data** | Buying patterns, repeat behavior, bundle preferences |
| **Customer support emails** | The fears people won't announce publicly, the real objections |
| **Reviews (yours + competitors')** | Language patterns, unexpected use cases, emotional payoffs |

Once you find a micro-persona, the creative becomes obvious: **you mirror their world back to them.**

A micro-persona in an audit carries the evidence it came from (the review, the ticket, the survey answer, word for word with its source). One that was reasoned out with no evidence is marked "inferred" and checked before anything is briefed for it.

## Same Product, Different Personas = Different Ads

The product never changes. The offer never changes. What changes is:
1. The persona
2. The emotional trigger
3. The opening visual
4. The story being told

**That's what tells Meta this is a NEW ad.** This is how creative stops circulating in the same bubble and starts reaching new buyers.

### Example: Sunset Lamp (From Soar)

| Persona | Emotional Trigger | Ad Approach |
|---|---|---|
| The aesthetic minimalist | "It needs to feel like me" | Clean, curated room shot: product as decor statement |
| The anxious homebody | "I need calm after a long day" | Warm, cozy bedroom scene: product as self-care ritual |
| The content creator | "My setup needs to look viral" | Studio/desk shot: product as production tool |
| The gift buyer | "I never know what to get her" | Unboxing moment: product as the perfect unexpected gift |
| The wellness seeker | "Blue light is ruining my sleep" | Before/after routine: product as sleep hygiene tool |

Five totally different emotional universes. Five different pieces of creative. Five different paths to conversion. ONE product.

## Universal Desires (The Underlying Motivators)

Every purchase is driven by a deeper desire. When you're stuck on angles, go back to these:

| Desire | What They Really Want | Example Hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Status** | To be seen as successful, tasteful, ahead | "The bag that gets compliments in every airport" |
| **Ease** | Less friction, less thinking, less work | "Four products. Zero guesswork. $68." |
| **Belonging** | To be part of a group, a tribe, a movement | "250,000+ moms chose this blanket" |
| **Control** | To feel in charge of their situation | "Finally: a hair routine that works every time" |
| **Transformation** | To become a better version of themselves | "Her hair, but healthier: starting at the scalp" |
| **Identity** | To express who they are through what they buy | "For Bold Photographers" |
| **Safety** | To protect what matters: kids, health, home | "Safe for kids. No harsh chemicals." |
| **Novelty** | To discover something new and exciting | "The new pattern is here" (when there is one) |
| **Connection** | To bond with someone they love | "She Stole the Baby's Blanket. So We Made Her One." |
| **Relief** | To escape a pain they're tired of carrying | "I was waking up 4-5 times a night. Brain fog was brutal." |

### How to Use This Table

When auditing:
1. Check which desires the brand's ads have led with (`winning-ads.md`, `brain.md`, the ad library)
2. Mark each one led with, partly, not, or not known
3. Map each gap to a buyer who feels that desire most sharply

The same desire reads differently by product. For a product that is used up, Ease is "no measuring, no mess, every morning" and Safety is "nothing in it I would not give my child". For a product bought once, Ease is "it arrives ready and goes back free" and Safety is "the money is safe: it lasts, and it is guaranteed".

## The Horizontal Funnel

Traditional funnel thinking: narrow down from broad → specific.
**Persona thinking: branch OUT from product → multiple persona lanes.**

Each branch has its own:
- Tone
- Hook
- Storyline
- Editing rhythm
- CTA
- Visuals
- Emotional arc

Your job isn't to speak to all of them. Your job is to find the few that matter most, the personas who drive disproportionate revenue, loyalty, and momentum, and build creative that feels like you're speaking directly to them.

**The hardest part is finding your people. The most important part is committing to them once you do.**
