# Wide Face as a still image: an overhead ultra-wide portrait

Judged on 1 thing: the very close overhead viewpoint makes the adult face dominate and taper toward the chin while the shoulders recede coherently, with identity and natural skin preserved.

## The frame

- **A portrait built around perspective.** One adult looks up into a camera directly above the head. The upper face is broad in the frame, the chin narrower and the shoulders receding. The selected character remains recognizable; this is optical perspective, not a redesigned skull.
- **One campaign moment.** Choose the expression, setting, styling and optional product action from the brief. Keep the product accurate and physically held or placed, with its label readable when relevant. Do not copy the camera reference's person or background.
- **Composition:** whole head and chin visible, hairline close to the upper edge, body receding below. Aim for roughly 75 to 85 percent face width and 65 to 75 percent frame height in a vertical portrait; adapt those targets to the chosen aspect ratio without stretching anatomy.
- **Continuity:** use the approved identity or previous portrait when available. A requested single edit preserves all other features, crop and light.

## Words on the picture

The portrait may be wordless. If words are selected, use the exact approved headline and supporting copy, set in the brand's typefaces over a quiet background area away from the eyes, mouth and product label. Preserve the face's dominance rather than shrinking it to create a text panel. With little space, use fewer approved text roles or a different placement; do not rewrite the approved words or invent a testimonial.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: a photoreal Wide Face portrait, camera directly overhead, adult looking up into the lens, optical axis centered on the glabella and upper nose bridge. Extremely close working distance, about 20 to 30 cm as a visual target, rectilinear 14 mm full-frame-equivalent ultra-wide perspective. Broad upper forehead and dominant eye line taper toward a small chin; shoulders and torso recede strongly through perspective. The face stays sharp, with believable anatomy and room lines.
- **References:** the attached canonical image controls camera geometry and face-to-frame scale only. The selected identity reference controls who the person is; product references control the real product. Preserve their distinct roles and disregard the canonical person's features, outfit, room and skin shine.
- **Skin and light:** fine irregular pores and subtle vellus hairs appropriate to the scale, existing natural asymmetry and markings, gentle sheen with texture visible underneath. Default to soft diffused daylight only when the brief has not set light. Keep eyes sharp with plausible catchlights; let the room soften without blurring the face. Do not add freckles or blemishes absent from the selected identity merely because they appear in the canonical image.
- **Editing:** alter only the named element. Keep identity, geometry, expression, crop, light and other styling fixed unless requested otherwise. Render at the selected aspect and supported output size.
- Not a conventional eye-level headshot, circular fisheye, uniformly swollen face, cartoon eyes, waxy smoothing, repetitive pore pattern or harsh sharpening. Do not reproduce the reference's broad shine as a compulsory skin finish.

## What fails in a still

A generic beauty headshot, copied canonical identity, cropped forehead or chin, distorted product, unsupported result claim, or text hiding the face. An ordinary overhead room view with a small face also loses the format. Adjust the camera or composition before adding more skin detail.
