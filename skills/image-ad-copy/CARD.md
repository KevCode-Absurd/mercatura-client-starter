# Image ad copy

An image ad is judged on 3 things: a stranger stops, can say in 1 sentence what it argues, and knows what to do next. The eye decides the order. The step's own rules win over this card. Every word follows the order of priority sent with the step: a plain sentence a person would say, then it sells, then it tells a story, then the brand's language (who it talks to, its words, its tone). A habit of the brand's own ads (a rhythm, a run of short sentences) is an option, never a rule.

**Before writing**

1. Find, in the research and the avatars: the accommodation (what the buyer changed in their life to cope), the failed solution, the last doubt before buying, their word for the problem, the moment they started looking. With no research, write from the avatars and mark the ad `inferred`; never write a quote nobody said.
2. Pick 1 buyer and 1 awareness stage:
   - Unaware: lead with their situation. No headline on the picture, and no product name, price or offer in the feed headline. Native post, long-copy static, advertorial.
   - Problem aware: lead with their words for the problem, then the way out. Accommodation static, native post.
   - Solution aware: lead with the difference and a mechanism. Comparison, mechanism static.
   - Product aware: lead with proof or the last objection answered. Proof static, demo still.
   - Most aware: lead with the offer, exactly as the brand states it, and a true reason for now. Offer static.
3. At market sophistication 3 or above a bare promise is not enough: name the mechanism or take a new angle.
4. Cut what any competitor could say. Replace each adjective with a number, a name, a physical detail or a time, inside a sentence that says what it changes for the buyer.

**Rules**

1. 1 ad, 1 claim, 1 buyer. Picture, words, feed copy and button make the same argument. A second argument is a second ad.
2. The buyer's words, not the pack's. Agitate the consequence, never the symptom.
3. Promise before mechanism. Proof bigger than the claim. Every feature passes "so what?": what it changes in the buyer's day.
4. Each element sells the next: picture, headline, feed copy, button.
5. Facts come only from the brand: no invented review, rating, count, study, award, press logo, expert, deadline, stock level or offer term. An offer or a guarantee is stated in the brand's wording with every condition, or not at all, and never sharpened into a promise of a result. A figure the brand states 2 ways is not used. A worked-out number needs both inputs from the brand. No bracketed placeholder in publishable words: argue something the brand has, and list the gap.
6. A customer's words stay the customer's: word for word, with the source, set as type, no face beside them, never dressed as a screenshot of a review site. What customers felt is never the brand's promise.
7. People: a generated person is a fictional adult, never a customer, founder or expert. No children; an ad for a children's product is written to the adult who buys it and shows the product alone or in an adult's hands. Real people only from their own uploaded photo.
8. Never tell the viewer what they have (a condition, a body, an age, their money). First person, "if you've...", or a scene.
9. Never name or show a competitor. Compare with an unbranded kind of thing, like for like, on 1 argument.
10. The product is the one in its photos. It is shown open, held or in use only as the brand's facts say, with the whole action stated; a visible result only where the brand records one. Otherwise closed and at rest.
11. For a cold buyer a post beats an ad: plain, native, no marketing language. A native format carries no logo and imitates no real platform, publication or person.
12. No cure, treat or prevent language; no guaranteed result or timeline; no before and after of a body; "sustainable", "non-toxic" and "safe for" only with the brand's proof.
13. Technical terms follow the stage. Unaware and problem aware: none; say what the thing does in the buyer's own words. Solution aware: 1 or 2, each followed by what it does for them. Product aware and most aware: the category's terms are welcome where this buyer compares products by them.

**The words on the image** (when the step wants them)

- Every line is a plain sentence, or a phrase a person really says, with its small words left in, and makes sense alone to a stranger. When the space is small, say less: a smaller idea said whole, never a sentence squeezed.
- 20 added words or fewer. The headline is the biggest text and may be a whole sentence of up to about 12 words. A subline of 1 sentence, optional. A badge is a label: a price, an amount, a guarantee term.
- An unaware ad's picture carries no headline. A note, a list or a message in the picture keeps its words, typed the way a person types, and its first line is the hook.
- The headline says what the buyer gets, or names their moment in their own words. Pre-qualify by the problem, not by a label for the person.
- Only added words are written; the product carries its own printing and the logo. No arrow or symbol that carries meaning.
- Words sit in reserved clear space or on a panel, never over the product or a face. In 9:16 nothing in the top 14%, the bottom 35% or 6% at each side.

**When a word slot is off**

- No words on the image: the scene makes the argument alone (the accommodation, the demonstration, the comparison), the layout says where clean space is left, and the hook moves to the first sentence of the feed copy.
- No feed copy: the image carries the claim and a 2 to 4 word call to action.
- A copy without words is wanted: the words can be lifted off and leave a whole picture, so none of them is part of the scene.

**The picture**

- Say what a camera would see: the product, the surface, the background, the light, the angle, 3 to 6 props from the product's real use, any person. The place comes from the brand's world and the buyer's moment.
- 1 subject and 1 entry point. Then the layout: zones in reading order with rough proportions, and where the empty space is.
- Saved winners by stage. Unaware: a lifestyle photo that does the work alone. Problem aware: the problem as headline, about 3 support points. Solution aware: a comparison, or what makes it different beside the product. Product aware: news and proof. Most aware: the offer is the largest thing.
- Support points answer 1 doubt each, as a short plain sentence or a phrase a person says. 1 real proof element, near the headline.
- No image-model prompt language: a later step writes the prompt.

**The feed copy** (when the step wants it)

- How long the primary text runs is given with the request, by the buyer's stage. The brand's own ads show its tone and its words, not how long this one runs. More length is more that happens, never the same point said again.
- The first sentence is 125 characters or fewer, earns the tap on "See more", and does not repeat the words on the image.
- A buyer who does not know the problem, or only feels it, is told the story of it: 1 person, a real moment, what they tried and what each fix cost, the cause said plainly, and the product late, as how the story ends. Paragraphs of 2 to 5 whole sentences, no rows of 1-line paragraphs.
- A buyer who is comparing is told how this differs and what that changes in their day, with 1 proof. A buyer who knows the product is told the deal or has 1 doubt answered: the price framed, the guarantee, 1 action with a true reason for now.
- Headline field: 40 characters or fewer, each option a different way in (promise, offer, proof), each fitting any primary text.
- Description: 30 characters or fewer, or empty. Never the only place the price, the guarantee or an offer term appears.
- Button: Shop now, Learn more, Get offer, Order now or Sign up.
- Price framing uses only the brand's figures: per use, against what the buyer already spends, or the effort as the price. Say the sale price, not only the %; with a subscription, how to cancel; with a first-order price, the price after.

**Carousels**

Card 1 is the hook, works alone and opens a gap the swipe closes. 1 idea per card, said in 1 or 2 plain sentences. If 2 middle cards could swap, it is a list, not an argument. The product arrives late for a cold buyer. The last card says the action in words, with the offer and guarantee, in 20 words or fewer. 1 look throughout.

**From a script.** Hook line: the headline. Problem: the scene. Promise and mechanism: the subline. Objection: the comparison or the middle cards. Proof: the badge. Offer and guarantee: the last card or the close of the feed copy. Keep the script's evidence ids.

**Check before returning**

Every line is one a person would say aloud to a friend, and a stranger would understand it alone. 1 buyer, 1 stage, 1 claim in every slot. Limits counted, not guessed. Every number traced to the brand. The word slots match what was asked. Nothing taken from a reference's words or design.
