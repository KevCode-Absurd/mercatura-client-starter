# Cinematic 3D as a video: a scene from an animated film

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger can say what the character felt, what changed it, and which product was there.

## How to write for it

- **The look brings no story.** The script does: 1 person, their problem, the product, the change.
- **Narration, or 1 character speaking.** A line is a narrator's voice-over or 1 character speaking on camera. 1 speaker at a time: say on each line who speaks. A speaking character is fiction and gives no testimonial: it states no result of its own and is no customer, founder or expert. It may want, doubt, notice and ask.
- **Feeling is acted.** Name the feeling, then write what the face and body do: "her inner brows push up, her mouth a flat tired line". An adjective alone gives a pleasant blank face.
- **1 character,** described once in full (age band, build, hair, clothes, 1 accessory), then by the same 2 or 3 words. An original adult: never a known character, a real person or a child. A second character only as a witness.
- **A mechanism line** is a close view with no people: the thing arriving and acting, mid-action, only as the brand's facts give it.
- **The product's arrival:** in the scene from the shot where the words reach it, in the hand or in use, label to the viewer.
- **One close that works:** offer and call to action as narration over the character with the product, held still.

## Length

15 to 45 seconds, 60 at most. Under 15 seconds: 1 scene, the feeling, the product, the offer.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the speaker talks. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip. 1 action to a line.
- 1 subject and 1 action a shot. Stacked actions make the picture drift: the second action is the next shot.
- The shots cut between the character in the room, the face close, hands on the product, and what the character looks at.
- Movement is smooth, with natural weight and small, believable acting. Not stepped, not rubbery.
- When a character speaks, its face is in view and nobody else's mouth moves.
- Say the light when the scene is not plain daylight ("soft grey light from a rain-speckled window"). Close views suit hands, the product and a mechanism.
- A thing is already in the frame when its shot opens: nothing arrives from out of view inside a shot. A hard cut may change the place. A change of time, or the turn of the story, may use 1 of 3 moves instead, named in the line:
  - **Camera-bridge** (the subject holds, the camera moves): a slow push in to the product's label; a focus pull from a near object to the character behind it; a push in close to show the mechanism.
  - **World-bridge** (the camera holds, the world changes): grey morning light turns to golden evening around the character; a dark, cold room warms as the curtains open.
  - **Match cut:** a gesture carries on into the next scene, or a round shape becomes the next scene's round shape.
- Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.
- No words inside the picture, no signs or labels on the set: the product's own label is the only printing in view. Overlays and captions are added afterward.

## What to avoid in a video

- The product alone for a whole clip: it reads as a product photograph. Keep the character, or a hand, in frame.
- A character telling the viewer how well the product worked for them.
- 2 characters talking over each other, or a line with no named speaker.
- A story that stops so that an ad can start: the product arrives inside it.
