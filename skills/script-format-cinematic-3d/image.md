# Cinematic 3D as a still image: 1 film still, rendered in 3D

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees a frame from an animated film, reads 1 feeling or 1 product in it, and reads the headline once.

## The frame

- **1 subject, 1 action.** For a problem-aware buyer: 1 character caught in the problem, the feeling on the face. For a solution-aware buyer: the mechanism in a close view, mid-action, with no people. For a buyer who knows the product: the character with the product, or the product beside 1 stylised prop.
- **The feeling is in the face:** brows, lids and mouth, not a pose alone. 1 character, 2 at most.
- **Composition:** a film still. The viewer stands inside the scene, the subject sharp, the room soft behind. Close views suit hands, the product and a mechanism.
- **The product:** where the ad shows it, label to the viewer and large enough to read, in the character's hand or reach. Where the ad leaves it out, the frame holds only the problem.

## Words on the picture

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer, as clean type in the brand's own typeface on the soft, out-of-focus part of the frame, in the top third. It says what the picture cannot: whose problem this is, or what just changed.
- 1 smaller line or a row of 2 or 3 chips at most, and a button only where there is an offer. 20 words in all.
- Type is laid flat over the picture, like a film poster's. No sign, screen or object in the scene carries words; the product's own label is the only printing.
- The character says nothing: no speech bubble, no quote marks, no words put in its mouth as a customer's.

## For the image prompt

- Order: the style line, the character line (only when people are in frame), the scene, the camera line.
- Style: "A frame from a premium 3D animated feature, plainly a 3D render and never a photograph. Only its light and materials behave as real ones do: real-world light behaviour, ray-traced reflections, physically accurate textures (fabric weave, wood grain, glass, liquid), filmic colour grading and anamorphic depth of field, all with the designed, stylised forms of an animated feature."
- Characters: "stylised 3D animated humans with believable adult proportions about 6 heads tall: slightly enlarged expressive eyes with detailed realistic irises, a refined face with defined cheekbones, jaw and brow and a simplified nose, skin with subsurface scattering and very fine texture, individually groomed hair strands, clothing with real fabric texture and natural drape. Unmistakably animated."
- Camera: "Composed like a cinematic film still, with the camera inside the scene and the scene filling the entire frame. A high-end 3D animated feature render."
- **The style owns the noun:** "a stylised 3D animated man", "a stylised 3D animated dog", in the scene as well as the character line. "A man" alone renders a photograph of a real one.
- Describe each character in full: age band, build, hair, clothes. Name the feeling, then the brows, lids and mouth.
- The scene's light and framing override the defaults when stated ("cool grey light from a rain-speckled window", "a zoomed-in macro view, no people in the scene").
- The product: its true shape and colours from its photo, every line of label copy given exactly, with its colour.
- Original adult characters only. Not live action, not a rounded toy-like cartoon, no known character, no studio name or logo.

## What fails in a still

- People appearing in a product or mechanism frame: the character line was left in.
- A product alone that reads as a plain product photograph.
- People who read as photographed: the scene named them without the style.
- A feeling named and not shown on the face.
- A change the brand's claims do not state.
