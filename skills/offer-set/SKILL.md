# The offer set

<!-- Run once when a brand is set up, and again whenever a person asks. It writes the short list of offers the Script builder ticks from. The long method (mechanics, margins, the 7 failures) is skills/offer-building; this is the part of it a first list needs. -->

An offer is what the buyer gets, what they pay, and why saying yes today is easy. It is not a discount. A script ends on it, so it must be sayable in 1 breath by a person talking to a friend.

## What a good set holds

- **What the brand runs today, first.** Every deal on its site and in its ads, stated the way the brand states it. These are marked as running.
- **Then proposals the brand does not run yet.** Each is built from things the brand already has: its products, its prices, its guarantee, its shipping, its sizes. Each says what the brand must arrange before it can run.
- **A spread of reasons to say yes**, not 12 versions of money off: a first order that costs less, more for the same money (a longer supply, a pair, a set), a gift that goes with the product, a small way to try it, the guarantee made the headline, a deal for 2 people or a household, a deal for someone leaving a rival kind of product, a deal tied to a moment in the year or in the buyer's life, something for a buyer who already bought.
- **The 5 parts, between them.** An offer has 5 parts: who it is for and why now, the price, what is added, what removes the risk, and a real reason to act now. The app sorts every offer on this list into those parts by its own words and shows a part no offer states as a suggestion. So state each part plainly where the brand has it (the guarantee in its own words, free shipping, a real end date), and let the proposals fill the parts the running deals leave empty.
- **Each one answers a buyer.** Start from a persona's main objection (what holds them back), then their problem, desire or buying trigger, and ask what would make yes easy for that person. The product is said as the answer to that person's problem, in their terms, before any deal is added. Say which personas it suits. An offer for everyone names none.
- **Each one has a place in the journey.** A buyer who does not yet see the problem is not moved by a price: the offers that suit them make trying safe or small. A buyer comparing answers wants the risk gone. A buyer who knows the product wants the deal. Say which stages it suits.

## How each is written

- **Name**: 2 to 4 plain words a buyer could repeat. No pun that hides what it is.
- **Said simply**: the whole offer in 1 sentence, the way a customer would tell a friend, at a reading age of 12. It carries the 1 number that matters and no more. This is the line a script grows its ending from.
- **Terms**: exactly what they get and pay, in 1 or 2 plain sentences. A first-order price is said with what it becomes after, in the same sentence.
- No "unlock", "elevate", "exclusive", "limited time" without a real end date, no exclamation marks.

## Rules

1. Every price is worked out from the brand's own price points. Never a price below what the brand has shown it can charge, unless the offer says what is smaller (fewer units, a trial size).
2. Use only the facts given. Never invent a fact about the product, a result, or a gift the brand does not have: a proposed gift, size or service is named plainly and goes in what the brand must arrange.
3. The guarantee is the brand's, word for word. A longer or wider one is a proposal.
4. Urgency is real or absent.
5. Where the brand's own materials give 2 different figures for the same deal, use the lower claim and say so in the reason.
6. Deliverable by an online store: shipped, downloaded, access granted, or done for the buyer.
