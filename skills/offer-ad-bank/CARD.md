# The offer ad bank: the card

<!-- The rules of SKILL.md with the explanation taken out. Sent to the planner of a set of offer ads. -->

1 offer, argued 3 ways, because the person who needs proof is not the person who needs the deal. Each ad's direction names its argument; the ad makes that argument and no other.

- **The deal** (people who know the brand): the offer and its terms said plainly, the range or the bundle shown.
- **The proof** (people who doubt the quality, not the price): a real review from the research, word for word, is the biggest text. The offer is a small chip. No person in the picture is the reviewer.
- **The product** (colder people): why it is worth it at full price, in 3 numbered callouts on the product, each a fact from the brand's material. The offer appears as a deadline, never as the reason and never as the headline.

## The offer line

- On every ad the offer's own numbers and dates (the price, the saving, what is given, the last day) appear exactly once, as 1 text element with role `offer`, in the offer's own words, 12 words at most, `source` the offer's id.
- No other text element repeats a number or a date of the offer. The headline may say what kind of offer it is without them.
- Every ad of the set states the offer in the same words.
- A deadline is the offer's real date; a limit is its real count. With neither, no urgency is written.

## Told apart at a glance

- At 300 pixels wide the argument of each ad can be named without reading it: a different layout, ground and dominant colour for each argument.
- Each size is composed for its own shape. 9:16 is never a crop of 4:5.
- Many versions of 1 argument are 1 ad.
