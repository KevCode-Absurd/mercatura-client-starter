# Watercolour: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narration over a painted world on cold-press paper. It is a controlled painted world, not a filter. Paint shows what a camera cannot, as 1 pigment move that reveals or explains the selling idea. It is judged on:

1. With the sound off, a stranger can say what the paint revealed and which product it belongs to.
2. Every frame reads as watercolour on paper.
3. The figure, the palette and the product look the same in every clip.
4. The product's silhouette and label are legible.
5. The picture claims nothing the narration could not.

## The look

`visual_direction` opens with the style lock, word for word, then the line of the look the job is given:

> Original watercolor animation painted on cold-press paper. Translucent layered pigment, visible paper tooth, controlled wet-on-wet blooms, softly irregular painted edges, restrained ink accents, preserved negative space, and natural pigment granulation. Painterly motion only; no glossy 3D surfaces and no flat vector gradients.

- `storybook` (default): soft rounded forms, a warm small world, faint pencil underdrawing, gentle blooms in the background only.
- `botanical`: clear single washes, fine observed detail, a specimen on mostly bare paper, no ink.
- `editorial`: broad loose washes behind 1 simplified subject, bold shapes, a lot of air.
- `ink`: a confident dark ink line carries the form, washes laid loosely over and past it.
- `product`: the pack painted precisely with crisp dry edges, loose washes only around it.

Then the locked world, the same in every clip: the paper's tooth, 2 or 3 transparent layers, small blooms, soft edges behind and crisp edges on the subject, 1 colour family named as 3 or 4 colours, the linework, a third of the sheet left bare round the forms and at the edges (never 1 empty band down a side).

## The pigment move

- Before writing scenes, name the 1 move that carries the selling idea: bloom, bleed, dissolve, brush reveal, wash transition, pigment trail or painted transformation. Say in `notes` what it reveals.
- It starts or ends at the product. It happens once, in the scene whose line states the idea, and that scene carries 8 spoken words or fewer.
- A picture claims as much as a sentence. Show only what the script's lines and the brand's claims state. A mechanism is painted only as the brand's facts give it. No body before and after, no skin clearing. A metaphor stays plainly a metaphor.
- If the scene needs the narration to be understood, choose another move and say so in `notes`.

## The figure, so it stays the same

- `character` is 1 sentence: a loosely painted man or woman, adult, build, hair as 1 wash shape and its colour, 2 clothing colours, a simple face of a few brush marks. Every `visual` names it by the same 2 or 3 words.
- An adult by its proportions and dress. Never a child, a real person or a known character.
- It does not talk. It makes 1 slow gesture a shot and is turned toward the thing, not the viewer.
- 1 figure at most. Many ads need none.

## What the view, the paper and the pigment do

- `camera`: flat to the paper, the painting filling the frame. Each shot is held steady or makes 1 slow push in; the cut does the rest. No desk, brush, hand, tape or sheet edge is ever in view.
- `setting`: 1 painted place on 1 sheet, with a third of the sheet bare. Nothing in it carries words.
- "Painted" sits on every noun in every `visual`: a painted mug, painted steam. Light is bare paper; shadows are thin cool washes.
- 1 colour family a scene. A second family enters only by the pigment move.
- Forms hold. Only the secondary pigment edges move: a wet edge creeps, a bloom opens, a wash settles. The paper grain holds still.
- 1 pigment move to a shot at most. Each `visual` with a move says where it starts, where it stops, and that everything else is dry and still.

## Shots this format cuts between

This look is calm on purpose: fewer, longer shots, as the director's rules allow.

- The whole painting, flat to the paper.
- 1 part of it, close: the figure, the steam, a leaf.
- The wet edge or the bloom, very close, as it moves.
- The painted product, close, its edges crisp.

## The product on screen

- It is first in `props`. Its `look` gives its true shape and colours and every line of its label, word for word, with its colour.
- It is painted in the same pigment, its edges the crispest on the sheet. No bloom or wash crosses it.
- It is on the sheet from the shot where the words reach it, already painted when that shot opens, front to the viewer. It does not move and is never painted in or repainted.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" and gives age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice.
- All `dialogue` is narration. No scene has the figure speaking.
- Under it: soft paper and water sounds (a brush stroke, a drop).

## The motion guard

Every clip's `visual_direction` carries it, after the style lock:

> Watercolour animation on cold-press paper throughout. Paper tooth and pigment granulation visible in every frame, and the paper grain holds still. Forms keep their shape; only the secondary pigment edges move. Slow, even, painterly motion, no motion blur, no morphing of the subject. Not CGI and not a filter over live footage. Motion starts at the first frame.

## Transitions

- Hard cuts go between views of the same painting, so the sheet is 1 sheet throughout.
- A wash transition changes the colour family of the same picture: a wash crosses the sheet and the place is left in the second family.
- A brush reveal may paint in a simple shape (a leaf, steam, a window) on bare paper kept for it since frame 1.
- The last shot of the ad is a held frame: the pigment settled and dry.

## First and last frame

- First: the place in its first colour family, 1 wet edge already spreading, bare paper kept where anything will be painted in.
- Last: the settled painting, the product's label clear, the top third bare paper for the offer line.

## Avoid

- A new sheet, a new place or a new object in a later clip.
- Pigment moving in every scene.
- Signs, lettering or brush-written words.
- A figure who speaks to camera or gives a testimonial.
- A painted change in a body.

## After the render

- Paper tooth and translucent pigment in every frame: no photograph, no flat gradient.
- Forms kept their shape; the subject did not morph.
- The pigment move started and stopped; nothing else swirled.
- The colour did not go muddy.
- The product's shape and label are unchanged and no bloom crossed them.
- No lettering appeared on the sheet.
