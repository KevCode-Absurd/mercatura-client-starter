# Watercolour animated ad

A narration over painted pictures. Build a controlled painted world; do not lay a watercolour filter over something else. Paint shows what a camera cannot: a scent, a warmth, where an ingredient comes from, 1 thing soaking into another.

## What it is judged on

1. **With the sound off, a stranger can say what the paint revealed and which product it belongs to.**
2. **Every frame reads as watercolour on paper.**
3. **The figure, the palette and the product look the same in every clip.**
4. **The product's silhouette and label are legible** wherever it appears.
5. **The picture claims nothing the brand's facts do not.**

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, materials, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, clip lengths, how narration is written, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.

## 1. Find the pigment move

Before any scene: the 1 thing paint really does that reveals or explains the selling idea. Pigment motion is never a constant decorative effect.

| Move | What happens on the paper | What it can carry |
|---|---|---|
| Bloom | A drop of wet colour opens outward into a damp wash, its edge feathering | Something spreading or filling: a scent, a warmth |
| Bleed | 1 colour runs into the next along a shared wet edge | 2 things combining; what goes into a base |
| Dissolve | A painted shape loses its edge and thins back into the paper | Something easing, as the claims state it |
| Brush reveal | A wet stroke crosses bare paper and a form stands painted behind it | What is in it; what was there all along |
| Wash transition | A broad wash crosses the sheet and the colour family changes | The turn: 1 mood becoming another |
| Pigment trail | A thin line of colour travels across bare paper from 1 thing to another | A path: from the plant to the bottle |
| Painted transformation | 1 form is repainted into another, stroke by stroke, its outline held between | A raw ingredient becoming the product |

- **It reads without words.** If the picture needs the narration to be understood, find another move.
- **It starts or ends at the product.**
- **A picture claims as much as a sentence.** A mechanism is painted only as `How it works` gives it; an ingredient only if `Made of` lists it. No body before and after. A metaphor (a grey room turning warm) stays plainly one.
- **When paint is the wrong format.** If the result can simply be filmed, or the buyer must see the thing's real texture, film it.

## 2. Choose 1 look

1 watercolour language for the whole ad. Its line follows the style lock in every prompt.

| Look | Suits | Line that follows the style lock |
|---|---|---|
| `storybook` (default) | A story about the buyer's moment: home, sleep, pets | `Loose storybook watercolour: soft rounded forms, a warm small world, faint pencil underdrawing showing through, gentle blooms in the background only.` |
| `botanical` | What it is made of: plants, ingredients | `Transparent botanical watercolour: clear single washes, fine observed detail, a specimen on mostly bare paper, no ink.` |
| `editorial` | An idea or a mood; clothing | `Editorial wash: broad loose washes behind 1 simplified subject, bold shapes, a lot of air.` |
| `ink` | Objects (cookware, bags, tools); how a thing works | `Ink and wash: a confident dark ink line carries the form, washes laid loosely over and past the line.` |
| `product` | A buyer who knows the product; launches | `Detailed product illustration: the pack painted precisely with crisp dry edges and true colours, loose washes only around it.` |

## 3. Lock the painted world

Decide each once and write them as 1 world line, used unchanged in every prompt.

- **Paper tooth:** fine or rough, and whether its white is warm or cool.
- **Pigment transparency:** 2 or 3 layers, the paper showing through; how much granulation.
- **Bloom size:** small. In backgrounds and the pigment move, never on the product or a face.
- **Edge softness:** soft and wet behind; crisp and dry on the subject and the pack.
- **Palette:** 1 colour family a shot, named as 3 or 4 colours, plus the product's own. If the story turns, name the second family too.
- **Linework:** none, pencil, or ink; its colour and weight.
- **Negative space:** a third of the sheet or more stays bare. White is bare paper, never white paint.

> Cold-press paper, medium tooth, warm white. 2 or 3 transparent layers, light granulation in the darks. Small blooms in backgrounds only. Soft wet edges behind, crisp dry edges on the subject and the pack. Warm grey, raw sienna, dull olive. Faint pencil underdrawing, no ink. A third of the sheet left bare.

## 4. The blocks

Every image prompt is assembled in 1 order: style lock, look line, world line, character block (only when a figure is in frame), the scene, camera block. Each block is pasted word for word. Consistency comes from the blocks and the approved references, never from "same style as before".

**Style lock** (opens every image and clip prompt, unchanged):

```text
Original watercolor animation painted on cold-press paper. Translucent layered pigment,
visible paper tooth, controlled wet-on-wet blooms, softly irregular painted edges, restrained
ink accents, preserved negative space, and natural pigment granulation. Painterly motion only;
no glossy 3D surfaces and no flat vector gradients.
```

**Character block** (only when a figure is in frame; the scene then gives its build, hair and 2 clothing colours in full, every time):

```text
The figure is a loosely painted adult with natural adult proportions: a simple face of a few
brush marks, 2 small dark dots for eyes, hair as 1 wash shape, clothing as 2 flat washes with
a dry-brush edge, simplified hands. No photographic skin and no detailed facial rendering.
```

**Camera block** (closes every image prompt):

```text
The painting fills the entire frame, seen flat and square on. No desk, no brushes, no hand, no
tape, no frame and no sheet edge. Light is unpainted paper left white. 1 controlled colour
family. Not a photograph under a filter and not thick opaque paint.
```

**The scene,** written fresh for each shot:

- "Painted" sits on every noun: "a painted ceramic mug", "painted steam". A thing left unnamed drifts toward a photograph.
- Name each thing's colour, and say where the paper stays bare.
- The product is painted from its tagged photo at its true shape and colours, its edges the crispest on the sheet. State every line of its label exactly, with its colour. No bloom crosses it.

## 5. Stills first

1. **The painted product:** alone on bare paper, front on. Approve its label.
2. **The painted figure,** if there is one: alone, front on, plain paper.
3. **The opening frame:** both references attached and tagged with their jobs; the place; the product small and to 1 side; 1 wet edge already spreading. Approve it.
4. By hand, each further shot's still, from the same references and blocks, approved before its clip.

## 6. Animate gently

Left unsaid, a video model moves everything and the painting melts. Every clip prompt, transitions included, carries the style lock and then the **motion guard:**

```text
Watercolour animation on cold-press paper throughout. Paper tooth and pigment granulation
visible in every frame, and the paper grain holds still. Forms keep their shape; only the
secondary pigment edges move: a wet edge creeps, a bloom opens, a wash settles. Slow, even,
painterly motion, no motion blur, no morphing of the subject. Not CGI and not a filter over
live footage. Motion starts at the first frame.
```

- **1 pigment move to a clip.** Name it, say where it starts and stops, and say that everything else is dry and still.
- **A figure makes 1 slow gesture.** The product does not move and is not repainted.
- **The view** is flat to the paper and still, or 1 slow push in.
- **The last shot of the ad is a held frame:** pigment settled and dry.
- **Sound:** 1 narrator off screen, soft paper and water sounds, music only if the brand's `Motion and sound` says so.

## 7. Transitions

By hand, a transition clip runs between 2 approved stills from the same references; its prompt names 1 visible action and the motion guard, nothing else.

- **View moves, painting holds:** a slow push in from the whole sheet to the painted product; a pull back from 1 painted detail to the place.
- **Paint carries it:** a wash crosses the sheet and the next picture is left behind it; the picture dissolves back to bare paper and the next is brushed in; a pigment trail leads the eye off 1 subject and onto the next.

Where clips continue from the last frame, keep 1 sheet: a wash transition changes the colour family of the same picture.

## Failures and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| A photograph under a painted filter | Style lock word for word; "painted" on every noun |
| Flat digital gradients, a vector look | Name paper tooth, granulation and irregular edges |
| Muddy colour | 1 named colour family; 2 or 3 layers; more bare paper |
| A desk, brushes, a hand or a taped sheet in view | Camera block word for word |
| Thick opaque paint | "Translucent", "the paper shows through", "white is bare paper" |
| The label garbled or washed out | State each line of label copy; crisp dry edges; no bloom across it; attach the product reference |
| The subject drifts between shots | Reattach the approved painted reference; describe it in full again |
| Forms melt or morph in motion | Motion guard; 1 move; say what holds still |
| Pigment swirls through the whole clip | Name the 1 move, its start and stop; "everything else is dry and still" |
| A face turns photographic | Character block; step back from the figure |

## What is not done

- No real person, child, celebrity or known character, and no named painter's or studio's style.
- No painted figure who speaks, gives a testimonial, or stands for a customer, founder or expert.
- No words painted into the scene. The product's own label is the only lettering.
