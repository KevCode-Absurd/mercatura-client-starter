# Cinematic film: directing rules

Photoreal live-action-style advertising, with one or two fictional adults. The script supplies the supported product argument; camera, performance and timing make it visible. Comedy, luxury, warmth and suspense are all valid. No mandatory bedroom, threat, weapon, older character or horror genre.

- Define the viewer's emotional change and what supported product action earns the payoff. Open on an event or visual question already underway; avoid a mandatory slow establishing shot.
- `cast.presenter` is Character A; the two-character recipe uses `cast.guest` for Character B. They are distinct adults with fixed face, hair, build, skin, clothes and accessories. Use `Character A:`, `Character B:` and `Narrator:` labels for dialogue; approved words remain exact. `voice_direction` describes the separate voices; a shot's `tone` describes delivery. Only the named speaker's mouth moves; narrator lines are off screen.
- Identity pictures define appearance, never final lighting or set layout. Single-character sheets use the app's six views; duo sheets use three joint views, A on the sheet left and B on its right for identity only. Do not transfer this layout into every shot.
- The opening scene is the master for location, spatial anchors, physical light sources and grade. State lamp/window positions, their reach, shadow direction and actual prop/product state. Product photos control product identity and artwork; the master controls placement.
- Bind each input to a named role, relevant exclusion and shot scope. Track product/prop ownership and transfers; never swap faces/outfits or make all references appear together. Timed events state trigger, contact/path and completion; next shots inherit the visible state. Trim repetition before these specifics.
- Matching coverage changes camera and performance inside that same world. A single, reaction or insert shows only its named subjects; do not force both faces into every shot. Keep the action axis, eyelines, hand/prop states and scale. A light's screen position can change on the reverse while its physical position stays fixed.
- Each shot has a purpose, named visible cast, one event, camera height/size/angle, one dominant move, a gaze target and intentional focus. Put specifics in existing `visual`, `tone`, `place` and camera fields. Describe emotion through observable behavior, not an adjective alone.
- A move reveals named information: start, trigger, path, pace and arrival. An orbit goes only far enough to earn that reveal; face close-ups use short, simple paths. Keep acting at real speed unless the beat explicitly slows it. Use existing camera fields.
- Place the camera at a named physical location/distance, with compatible lens/perspective, crop and focus; describe one visible consequence. Check sightline and product scale. Name the place, not an extra operator. Requested alternatives differ in distance/viewpoint/foreground/focus, not height alone. Matching coverage keeps scene constants; locked shots and repairs keep their setup. No forced four-shot batch or shallow-focus/overhead default.
- Cinematic exceptions to generic montage guidance: staying in one set is valid; a brief live reaction or quiet pause can carry a reveal. Do not add busy action to erase it. A locked reveal can follow handheld shots intentionally. Do not flatten scene shadows into even phone daylight. Name focal planes; "everything sharp" contradicts a blurred foreground.
- Fit action, whole dialogue and recognition time into the actual clip lengths and speaker pace. No assumed 20-second render, 4K output, universal frame rate or unsupported start-frame extension. Segments use the route's available references and clean cuts.
- Use the user's audio choices. Full silence wins; otherwise native audio keeps voices intelligible, source sounds selective and optional music below dialogue. A music drop, pause or cut hit is a choice, not a rule. Combined native audio has no independently removable stems.
- Reuse each speaker's concise voice signature consistently, essential traits first; local delivery belongs in `tone`. Listen to joins: wording is guidance, not a voice guarantee. Keep approved words and punctuation exact.
- A twist, negotiation, misunderstood gesture, silent handoff or visual callback may supply the hook. The product must belong to the payoff through supported use; fiction is not testimonial evidence or a claimed result.
- Check cast, wardrobe, anatomy, set/light geometry, eyelines, focal planes and object states across stills, then actual video joins and sound. Repair the affected shot's controlling instruction with the appropriate identity or scene reference; keep other variables fixed.
- Inspect middle and final spoken beats for lip closures, drifting teeth/jaw, voice changes and an incorrectly speaking listener. A good opening frame does not pass the whole take.

Shared production rules still govern claims, accurate product use and approval. These cinema-specific coverage, lighting and performance rules take precedence over generic phone-UGC styling and continuous-speech defaults.
