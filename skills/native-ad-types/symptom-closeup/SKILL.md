# Native image type: symptom close-up

The thing the buyer checks in the mirror, photographed the way they photograph it to ask a friend "is this normal?".

- **Why it stops the scroll:** A person with the symptom recognises it before they read a word. A person without it scrolls on, which is the right audience leaving.
- **Suits:** A symptom a camera can see: skin, nails, heels, swelling, a scalp parting, a stain, a worn tooth edge. Cold traffic.
- **A product bought once:** The mark the old one leaves: the red line from a strap, the blister from the boot, the dent in the shoulder.
- **Words on the image:** None.
- **Headline under the image:** "Why your [problem] [keeps happening]", or "The reason you [behaviour they are embarrassed by]".

## Briefing the image

- Name the symptom as the research describes it and the routine it shows up in: getting out of the car, putting on sandals, washing hands at the sink.
- Close, but with enough of the room in frame to place it: the bath mat, the car door, the desk. Never a zoomed, isolated body part on a blank background.
- A fictional adult's hand, foot, arm or neck. No face. Real skin: pores, fine hair, uneven tone, a little shine.
- Flat indoor light or a phone flash. An ordinary amount of the symptom, the kind a reader lives with.

## Honesty

- The person is made up and is nobody's likeness. Nothing says they are a customer.
- It is never the "before" of a before-and-after, and no second image shows it gone.
- It names no disease. The copy describes the problem and never says the product treats, cures or reverses it.
- Platforms reject zoomed images of a body part that imply an ideal or a flaw: the routine and the room are what keep it a photograph of a moment.
- No children. Nothing graphic: no blood, no open skin.
- **If the symptom cannot be shown inside those lines:** plan a candid moment of the workaround.

## Avoid

- Retouched skin, studio macro light, a ring light's reflection.
- Circles and arrows drawn by the image model (that is the annotated photo, a different type).
- The product or its texture on the skin.
