# Native image type: don't buy this

A blunt warning in a plain form (a handwritten sign, a typed line over a photo): don't buy this, unless, or until.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** Being told not to buy is the opposite of what an ad does, so it gets read.
- **Suits:** Readers who have tried things and been let down. The warning is about the kind of thing that failed them, or sets a condition the right buyer meets. Good with the quietly angry arc.
- **A product bought once:** Home ground. A considered purchase is exactly where "before you buy another one" lands.
- **Words on the image:** 4 to 12 words, from the story's own warning. "Don't buy another face cream until you read this." "Don't buy another non-stick pan until you read this."
- **Headline under the image:** "Don't buy [the kind of product] until you've read this."

## Briefing the image

- Plain and unpolished: marker on card, or a typed line over a candid photo of the old way.
- The old way in the photo is generic and unbranded.
- Give the words exactly.

## Honesty

- The warning is about a kind of product, never a named rival or a recognisable pack.
- The reason behind the warning is in the story, and is one the brand can support.
- It is one person's warning, not an official one: no hazard triangle, no striped tape, no recall or safety-notice look, no suggestion of danger that isn't real.
- **If the story warns against nothing:** plan a worn object.
- **If words on the image are off:** plan a worn object.

## Avoid

- Scare language about health or safety.
- Anything that could be taken for an official notice.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`, per avatar: proposed `Tried before`, for the kind of product in the buyer's own words; Customer language bank. Blank: the kind the copy names.
- `brand.json`: claims with sources, for the reason. Blank: the warning is told as the narrator's own view in the story, and the image carries only "read this first".
