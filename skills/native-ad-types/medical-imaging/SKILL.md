# Native image type: medical imaging

The test result, photographed the way a patient photographs it to send to family: a phone held up to a monitor or a light box, one area circled in red.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** A scan with a red circle is a question nobody scrolls past: what is wrong there, and is it wrong with me?
- **Suits:** Any pain with a test: joints, heart, liver, blood sugar, sleep, bone, eyes. Only where the brand's facts support the mechanism the copy then explains.
- **A product bought once:** Rarely fits. A gait report, a mattress pressure map, a water test strip, when the brand's facts include one.
- **Words on the image:** None. Any name, date or hospital line on the film is unreadable.
- **Headline under the image:** "Before you [thing they are about to do], read this", or "It wasn't [what they blamed]. It was [the real cause]."

## Briefing the image

- Name the test from the copy's opening and the one place the eye should land. One hand-drawn red circle or one short red arrow, uneven, as a marker pen on film or a finger-drawn mark on a phone screenshot.
- A capture of a screen or a light box: monitor moiré, a reflection of the room, the edge of a desk, a slightly tilted frame.
- An ordinary finding of the kind the copy describes (wear, a narrowed gap, a high reading), not a dramatic one.
- No person. A hand at the edge of the frame at most.

## Honesty

- It is an illustration of a kind of result, not a real patient's file. No readable name, date of birth, hospital or doctor.
- It does not diagnose the reader and it shows no disease the brand cannot speak about. The copy describes a problem; it never says the product treats, cures or reverses what the scan shows.
- No pair of scans, before and after. No doctor, no white coat, no hand holding the product.
- Platforms flag graphic medical imagery: a plain film or a printed sheet, never surgery, never an open wound.
- **If the pain has no test, or the brand's facts do not support the mechanism:** plan an emotional scene.

## Avoid

- A clean render of a skeleton or a glowing 3D joint. It reads as an ad.
- Numbers on a lab sheet that the image model invents and a reader could quote. Keep them out of focus.
- More than one mark.
