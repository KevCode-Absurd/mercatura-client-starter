# Native image type: nightmare scenario

Not the problem today: where the buyer fears it is heading. Shown as one quiet, real object or moment from that future, never as a disaster.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** The reader has already imagined this and pushed it away. Seeing it as a plain photograph, on a table like theirs, is harder to push away.
- **Suits:** A problem with a feared next step the research names: beauty, ageing, mobility, health, safety, money. Only when buyers say the fear themselves.
- **A product bought once:** The cost of the old one failing at the wrong moment: the tent in the bin at the campsite, the receipt for the third replacement.
- **Words on the image:** None, beyond what a real object in the scene already carries (a brochure's title, a sign), kept short and partly out of focus.
- **Headline under the image:** "Before you [thing they are about to do], read this".

## Briefing the image

- Take the feared step from the research in the buyer's own words, and pick the one object that stands for it: a brochure, an appointment card, a consent form, a price list, a walking aid by the door.
- Put it where it would really be: the kitchen table with the post, the car's passenger seat, the hallway.
- Phone photo, slightly tilted, room light, real clutter beside it. Nobody in frame, or a hand at the edge.
- Quiet. The scene is the minute before the decision, not the event.

## Honesty

- The fear is one buyers state in the research, and the step is a real, ordinary one. Never an invented statistic about how many people end up there.
- No accident, no emergency, no hospital bed, no surgery, no body shown as damaged. No children.
- Nothing says the product prevents the outcome. The copy describes the worry; it does not promise the future will not happen.
- No named clinic, procedure brand or professional.
- **If the research holds no such fear, or it is a medical outcome the brand cannot speak about:** plan an emotional scene.

## Avoid

- Shock. A frightening image earns a hide, not a read.
- A face or a body part marked up (surgical lines, circles on skin). The object that stands for the step carries it.
- A countdown, a warning triangle or any device that reads as an ad.
