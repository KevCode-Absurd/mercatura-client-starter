# Native image type: annotated photo

A phone photo someone marked up before sending it: one red circle, or an arrow and a few handwritten words, on an otherwise ordinary picture.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** A circle says "look here" and nothing else. The eye goes to it before the reader has decided to look.
- **Suits:** One flaw a camera can see and a reader recognises: apparel, fit, posture, a room, a surface, a device. Cold traffic.
- **A product bought once:** Exactly where the old one fails: the seam, the hinge, the worn patch.
- **Words on the image:** None, or 1 handwritten note of 4 words at most, in the buyer's own words ("every single meeting", "by 2pm"). Never a claim, never the product's name.
- **Headline under the image:** "Why your [problem] [keeps happening]", or "It wasn't [what they blamed]. It was [the real cause]."

## Briefing the image

- The photo first, described as a clean candid shot of the moment: where, what the person is doing, what the flaw is and where it sits in the frame. No face unless it is a mirror selfie with the phone covering it.
- Then the mark, as a separate instruction: 1 rough red circle around the flaw, drawn as with a finger on a phone screen, slightly open at the top; or 1 short arrow. Uneven, never a perfect ellipse.
- The note, when there is one: given word for word in quotation marks, handwritten in the same red, beside the mark, tilted a little.
- One mark only. Two is a diagram.

## Honesty

- The flaw is ordinary and of the size the research describes. The circle points; it does not exaggerate.
- The person is a fictional adult and is nobody's likeness. Nothing says they are a customer.
- No body is circled as a flaw in itself (a stomach, a face, a thigh): the mark is on the garment, the object or the room, or on what the problem does there (the stain, the gap).
- No second photo with the flaw gone.
- **If the flaw is on the body itself:** plan a symptom close-up with no mark, or a candid moment.

## Avoid

- Typed text, a neat graphic circle or a brand-coloured arrow. It reads as an ad.
- More than 4 words, or any word the image model might misspell: short common words only, checked against the note given.
- The product in frame.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`, per avatar: The moment; the buyer's own phrase for when it happens, for the note. Blank: no note, the circle alone.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: Brand world (Places, Surfaces and props, Never in frame).
