# Native image type: chat thread (group or family)

A group or family chat in a generic chat layout: a group title, sender names, a few messages.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** A group chat is where people actually admit things. It also suits markets where chat apps are the main way people talk.
- **Suits:** Problems people joke about or hide from family. Good for avatar-led stories: the group's name and the way they talk show who the narrator is.
- **A product bought once:** The family chat where the thing comes up: "who took the good pan", "mum's bought ANOTHER one".
- **Words on the image:** A group title of 2 to 4 words, and 4 to 7 messages of 3 to 14 words each from 2 or 3 senders. They show the problem or the joke about it, as the story tells it.
- **Headline under the image:** "Read this if your family chat sounds like this."

## Briefing the image

- A generic chat screen: group title, sender names in colour, times.
- Give every message exactly, with its sender.
- First names only. Initials or plain shapes where a contact picture would be.

## Honesty

- Every name is an invented first name. Never a real person's name, number or photo, and the thread is never offered as a real group's messages.
- The screen is neutral: no real app's name, logo, colours, wallpaper or tick marks.
- No message announces, recommends or names the product, and none states a result.
- **If the story has no family or friends in it:** plan text bubbles or a candid moment.
- **If words on the image are off:** plan a candid moment.

## Avoid

- Emoji walls.
- Faces as contact pictures.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: Customer language bank, for how they talk to each other; per avatar, Who they are, for who is in the group. Blank: the copy's own lines only.
- `brand.json`: the market and its language, for whether a group chat is how this buyer talks. Blank: prefer text bubbles.
