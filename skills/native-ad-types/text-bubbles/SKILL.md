# Native image type: text bubbles

A short text conversation in generic chat bubbles: 3 to 6 messages between the narrator and 1 other person.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** People read message threads without deciding to. It delivers the witness: someone else noticing.
- **Suits:** A story with a second person in it: a partner, a sister or a friend noticing the problem or the workaround. Good with the reluctant believer and searcher arcs.
- **A product bought once:** The friend who asks "is yours peeling too?", the partner who texts "did you get soaked again".
- **Words on the image:** 3 to 6 messages, 4 to 14 words each. The last one leaves a question open. They are the exchange as the story tells it. Best is the before: the other person noticing the problem. A thread about the change is used only when the story has someone noticing it, stays inside what customers report, and names no product.
- **Headline under the image:** "Read this if someone's said this to you."

## Briefing the image

- Generic bubbles in 2 colours, a contact label that is a first name or an initial, a time.
- Give every message exactly, in order, and say who sends which.
- The way real people text: short, lower case, 1 typo at most.

## Honesty

- The thread is the narrator's own, part of the same story, with an invented first name. It is never offered as a real customer's screenshot, or as messages the brand received.
- No message recommends the product, names it, or states a result beyond what customers report.
- The screen is generic: not a copy of any real messaging app's interface.
- **If the story has no second person:** plan a notes app or a candid moment.
- **If words on the image are off:** plan a candid moment.

## Avoid

- A conversation that is a sales pitch in bubbles.
- A contact photo of a face.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: Customer language bank, for how they text; per avatar, Who they are, for who the other person would be. Blank: the copy's own lines only.
