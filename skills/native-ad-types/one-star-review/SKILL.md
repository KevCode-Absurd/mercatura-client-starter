# Native image type: one-star review

A review-style card: stars, a short title, 2 or 3 lines. The complaint is really a compliment.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** A complaint is a pattern break. People read bad reviews first.
- **Suits:** A real, slightly inconvenient effect of the product working, found in real reviews. Good with the quietly angry arc.
- **A product bought once:** Fits well: "bought 1, my husband has taken it, I've had to order another", "now I've given away my other pans".
- **Words on the image:** A title of 3 to 7 words and 15 to 35 words of review. Both are 1 real customer's review, word for word. It may be cut shorter. It is never reworded.
- **Headline under the image:** "Read this customer's only complaint."

## Briefing the image

- A plain review card: stars, the text, and nothing that marks a site.
- The stars are the stars that customer gave. A review that gave 5 stars and a joke complaint is shown with its 5 stars and the complaint as its title ("My only complaint"). The 1-star look is for a review that really gave 1 star.
- The name is shown as the source shows it and the customer allows (a first name and an initial), or left off. The date is the review's own, or left off.
- Give the review text exactly. A slightly cropped screenshot.

## Honesty

- A real review, with its source on record, or nothing. Never an invented review, a paraphrase, or several reviews joined into 1.
- If the brand has not recorded the customer's say, the review is shown with no name, and `compliance_flags` says "real review used: check the customer's say before it runs".
- No rating count, no "verified" mark and no helpful-votes line unless that review carried them.
- Not styled as any real shop's or review site's page.
- The praise inside it stays within the brand's claims.
- **If the brand has no such review:** do not use this type. Plan a worn object or a candid moment.
- **If words on the image are off:** plan a candid moment.

## Avoid

- A made-up reviewer.
- A platform's logo, colours or layout.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- Proposed `customer-avatars.md`: `Stories and reviews we may use` (the review word for word, its stars, its date, where it was posted, how the customer may be named, whether they have agreed to its use in ads). Today the nearest is each avatar's Grounded in (real quotes with source), which has no stars and no say. Blank, or no review that fits: the type is not used.
