# Native image type: hook headline over photo

A candid photo with one headline across it, set like an article's lead image. The headline is the advertorial's own, so the ad and the page it opens say the same thing.

- **Why it stops the scroll:** It reads as a story someone shared, and the headline plants the question the photo alone could not.
- **Suits:** A strong headline and a problem a single photo cannot explain on its own: pets, supplements, sleep, home. The click goes to the advertorial with that headline.
- **A product bought once:** The old one in its failed state, with the headline saying why it keeps failing.
- **Words on the image:** One headline, 12 words at most, in 1 of the 4 shapes below. Nothing else: no button, no badge, no logo, no second line.
- **Headline under the image:** A second hook, not a repeat of the one on the image.

## The 4 shapes

- "Before you [thing they are about to do], read this"
- "Why your [problem] [keeps happening]"
- "It wasn't [what they blamed]. It was [the real cause]."
- "The reason you [behaviour they are embarrassed by]"

## Briefing the image

- The photo is a candid moment: a phone snapshot of the problem, real room, real light, nobody posing. Leave a calm area (a wall, a floor, a sky) for the headline.
- The headline is given word for word, in quotation marks, with its place (across the lower third, or on a plain band). A plain news typeface, heavy weight, white on a dark band or black on a light one. Not the brand's display font: it should read as editorial, not as a campaign.
- No punctuation at the end of the line except a full stop where the shape has one.

## Honesty

- The headline says only what the copy and the page then support. No "doctors hate", no "one weird trick", no invented number, no named expert.
- It is not dressed as a real publication: no masthead, no "as seen in", no journalist's name, no date line.
- No "you" statement about the reader's health ("Do you have arthritis?"). "Why your dog stopped coming upstairs" is about the situation.
- A person in the photo is a fictional adult, never captioned as a customer. No children.
- **If no headline holds without a claim the brand cannot make:** plan a candid moment with no words.

## Avoid

- Two headlines, a subhead or a call to action on the image.
- A styled product shot behind the words.
- Letters the image model has bent or misspelt: the words are checked against the headline given, and a wrong one is redrawn.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- The landing page this ad leads to: its headline, when there is one. Blank: the headline the copy's first hook variant gives.
- `customer-avatars.md`, per avatar: The moment, for the photo. Blank: the copy's opening, marked "inferred".
- `brand-guidelines.md`: Brand world (Places, Never in frame); the words the brand never uses.
