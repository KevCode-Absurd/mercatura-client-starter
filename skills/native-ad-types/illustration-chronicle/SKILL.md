# Native image type: illustration and chronicle

A drawing, not a photo: the problem's history in a few panels, in a style that looks found rather than designed (an old engraving, a newspaper strip, a page from a sketchbook).

- **Why it stops the scroll:** Nothing else in the feed is drawn like it, and a sequence asks to be followed to its last panel, which is the copy.
- **Suits:** Pain that is inside the body, private, or flagged when photographed: digestion, cramps, migraine, nerve pain, hormones, anything a platform will not show as a photo.
- **A product bought once:** The cycle of buying and replacing, panel by panel.
- **Words on the image:** None. The panels carry it; the headline under the image carries the words.
- **Headline under the image:** "Why your [problem] [keeps happening]", or "The reason you [behaviour they are embarrassed by]".

## Briefing the image

- 1 to 4 panels, read left to right or top to bottom, each a moment from the copy's own order: how it started, what they tried, where it left them. The last panel is unresolved.
- Name the style in 1 line and keep it for every panel: black ink cross-hatching on yellowed paper; a 3-colour newspaper strip; pencil on a lined diary page.
- The figure is a simple drawn adult with no likeness: no detailed face, the same clothes in every panel so the reader follows one person.
- Paper texture, a fold line, uneven ink. It should look scanned.

## Honesty

- A drawing claims nothing a photo could not: no panel shows the product working, no last panel shows a cure, no body is drawn before and after.
- No anatomy diagram that implies a diagnosis, and no mechanism drawn that the brand's facts do not support.
- No drawn doctor, expert or named person. No children.
- It is not styled after a living illustrator or a known comic.
- **If the story will not hold in 4 panels:** plan a candid moment.

## Avoid

- Speech bubbles and captions: lettering drawn by an image model is the fastest tell there is.
- Glossy vector art, mascots, emoji.
- A final panel with the product or a smile.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`, per avatar: the history of the problem in the buyer's order (when it started, what they tried). Blank: the order of the copy's first 3 movements, marked "inferred".
- `brand-guidelines.md`: Never in frame; the guardrails on what the brand may show of the body.
