# Talking object: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

1 object from the buyer's home has a face and says, in the first person, what it has watched. The object is the one the script chose. It is not the product: the product stands beside it, real and faceless. It is judged on:

1. Frame 1, sound off, at the size of a thumb: the face and what it feels are read at once.
2. The face is the same face in every clip.
3. The last line lands, and the eye is on the product when it does.
4. The product keeps its real shape, colours and printing.

## Structure in clips

- 1 to 3 clips. 1 clip is a whole ad.
- 1 clip, cut as shots: the grievance, with the feeling already on the face; the detail only this object would know, shown close; the eyes go to the product and the line lands.
- Over 2 or 3 clips: the grievance; what it has had to watch; the product and the line that lands. The turn is in the last clip only.
- 1 shot holds 1 change of expression. The voice runs on as 1 track over the cutaways.
- The offer and the call to action are the `overlay` of the last scene. The object never says them.

## The character, so it stays the same

`character` is 1 sentence, in this order, and every `visual`, `start` and `end` names the object by the same 2 or 3 words from it:

1. the object, its material and its size;
2. its state, as damage that is seen (scratched, dented, half empty, dusty);
3. where the face sits: on its largest clear area, part of the surface, never over a word, a logo or a dial;
4. eyes: shape, size, resting state;
5. eyebrows: shape and resting position;
6. mouth: shape at rest;
7. arms: "no arms", unless a line needs a gesture: then 2 thin arms from its sides ending in mitten hands. No legs.

Example: "A black non-stick frying pan, 24 cm, coating scratched grey in the middle, with a face on its inside surface: large round eyes with heavy lids, thick brows sloping down at the outer ends, a wide flat mouth, no arms."

It keeps the real shape and size of such an object. It carries no words. It is generic (no brand's shape or colours) and can't be taken for the product.

## What the camera and the object do

- `camera`: at the object's own eye level in every shot of its face. Each shot is held steady; the cut does the moving. Looking down from a person's height makes it a thing on a shelf.
- `setting`: 1 place where the object and the product would both really be, 3 or 4 other plain things at most, none with words. The object is kept the way it is kept, face to the lens: a pan hangs on a hook, a tub stands on a shelf, a suitcase stands on its wheels.
- The light carries the feeling (cold and flat for neglected, warm from the side for a confession) and does not change.
- `visual_direction` describes the look and names no studio, film or game: "soft 3D animated film look, rounded forms, expressive face, shallow depth of field".
- Each `visual` says what the features do (the brows drop, the eyes slide left to the product, the mouth presses flat), 1 small movement of the whole object (it sags, leans, tilts), and "its mouth moves with the words".
- It stays in its place. It does not walk, jump, grow or shrink.
- No people, no mirrors, no screens. The person it talks about is never seen.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The face, close, at the object's eye level, mid-word.
- The object whole in its place, wide, with the room it has watched.
- Its damage, very close: the scratch, the dent, the dust.
- What it is talking about, as the object sees it, with no person in view.
- Its eyes sliding to the product, then the product, close.

## The product on screen

- `handling` is display. It is the first of `props`. It comes in at the turn: a shot opens with it already standing beside the object, at its true size against the object, its front to the lens.
- It is not redrawn in the animated look: its shape, colours and printing are those of its photo, under the scene's light.
- It has no face and does not move. Nothing touches it.
- The turn of the monologue is the object's eyes going to it. In the last frame it is whole and in focus.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "The object's own voice, from its mouth:" and gives its age, pace and 2 traits that fit the object ("old, dry, a little hoarse"), in the accent of the brand's buyers.
- Under the voice: the room (a fridge hum, rain on a window) and the object's own material when it moves (a clink, a creak).
- `tone` per scene is the tone the script chose (rant, warning, confession, fact-drop, existential), in 1 or 2 plain words.

## First and last frame

- First: the face already wears the feeling of line 1 (1 brow down, mouth open mid-word), the damage shows. 1 subject, clear edges, nothing important in the top or bottom band.
- Last: the object's settled final expression, eyes on the product; the product's front clear; room near it for the offer line.

## Avoid

- A face on the product, or a second talking object.
- A happy object in frame 1: there is nothing to turn.
- "You" in any line; a line that sells, names a price, explains how the product works, or states a result the object could not have seen.
- A body part, an organ or a symptom as the object.
- Arms that the lines do not need: every limb is something that changes between clips.

## After the render

- The same eyes, brows and mouth, in the same place on the object, as in the clip before.
- The mouth moved with the words and only with the words.
- No limb appeared that `character` does not have. The object did not leave its place.
- No letters appeared on the object. The product's printing is unchanged and it has no face.
- The voice is the same voice as in the clip before.
