# Talking object ad

1 object with a face, 1 place, a monologue of 10 to 30 seconds. The object is not the product. It is something that was in the room, and the product is what it talks about.

## What it is judged on

1. **Frame 1, sound off, at the size of a thumb:** the face and what it feels are read at once.
2. **The object is one the buyer has at home.** They know what it has seen.
3. **The last line lands.** A stranger could repeat it.
4. **The product is the real one,** with its real printing and no face.

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, how speech is written, the tools. No model, tool, studio or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-talking-object`: how the monologue is written. This skill is the character and the clips.

## 1. Choose which object talks

This is the decision that makes or ruins the ad. Make it from the brand brain, not from the product's name.

| Candidate | What it carries | Choose it when | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A witness.** Something in the room when the problem shows | The problem the buyer has stopped noticing | `Market awareness level` is unaware or problem-aware; `Visible result` is "nothing visible" | It can only report what it saw: never a result inside a body |
| **The thing it replaces.** The old way, kept and failing | The failed fix; a comparison with no brand named | The buyer owns the old way (`Tried before`, `Replaces`); the market has heard every claim | Its faults are claims about a category: only what anyone can see, or what the brand's facts state |
| **The problem itself.** A thing outside the body that the product removes or prevents | What the product does, told as a defeat | `Visible result` is something a viewer can watch go | Never a body part, an organ or a symptom. Never anything that would be refused as gross |
| **The product itself** | Nothing the other 3 do not carry better | Not in this app | A face on the product spoils its printing; it can only praise itself, so there is no turn; it is an ad from frame 1 |

The same 3 choices in 3 categories:

| | Witness | Thing it replaces | The problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| A drink powder for the evening | The bedside lamp | The half-used tub at the back of the cupboard | None: the result is not visible |
| A steel frying pan | The wooden spoon in the jar by the hob | The scratched non-stick pan | The burnt-on egg |
| A rain jacket | The hook by the door | The umbrella with 2 bent ribs | The puddle under the coat |

Then the 5 tests in `script-format-talking-object`: it was there; 1 simple shape; something to lose; it stands beside the product at true size and can't be taken for it; nobody's brand and nobody's body. When customers name an object themselves, theirs wins.

**A set for testing.** The same grievance told by 3 or 4 objects from the same room is 3 or 4 separate ads, each with its own character and clip. It is the cheapest way to find which narrator the buyer answers to. Never 2 faces in 1 ad.

## 2. The character, in 1 fixed sentence

Written once and used unchanged in every still and every clip. In this order:

1. **The object and its material:** "a non-stick frying pan, black, 24 cm".
2. **Its state, as damage that is seen:** "its coating scratched grey in the middle, the handle a little loose".
3. **Where the face sits:** on the largest clear area, part of the surface, not a sticker. Never over a word, a logo or a dial.
4. **Eyes:** shape, size and resting state ("large round eyes, heavy upper lids").
5. **Eyebrows:** shape and resting position ("thick brows sloping down at the outer ends").
6. **Mouth:** shape at rest ("a wide flat mouth").
7. **Arms:** none, unless a line needs a gesture. Then 2 thin arms from the sides ending in mitten hands, so there are no fingers to count. No legs. Each extra limb is something that changes between clips.

> A black non-stick frying pan, 24 cm, its coating scratched grey in the middle and the handle a little loose, with a face on its inside surface: large round eyes with heavy upper lids, thick brows sloping down at the outer ends, a wide flat mouth, no arms.

> A half-used white plastic tub with a dented lid and a peeling blank label, with a face on its front below the lid: small tired oval eyes, 1 brow higher than the other, a small crooked mouth, 2 thin arms from its sides.

The object keeps its real shape and size. The feeling of each line is written as what these features do: the brows drop, the eyes slide to the product, the mouth presses flat.

## 3. The place, the light and the look

- **The place** is where the object and the product would both really be, from the avatar's `The moment`: a shelf, a hallway, a hob. 1 place, 3 or 4 other things in it at most, none with words on them.
- **It is kept the way such an object is kept, with its face to the lens:** a pan hangs on a hook with its inside to the room, a tub stands on a shelf, a suitcase stands on its wheels.
- **The light** carries the feeling: cold and flat for neglected, warm from the side for a confession. It is the same in every clip.
- **The look** is described, never named after a studio, a film or a game: "a soft 3D animated film look, rounded forms, an expressive face, shallow depth of field". The same line in every prompt.
- **The camera** is at the object's own eye level, close or medium, on a stand. A camera looking down from a person's height makes it a thing on a shelf; at eye level it is a character.
- **No people, no mirrors, no screens.** The person it talks about is never seen.

## 4. The product in the frame

- From its own tagged photos (`Reference photos`: pack front, or the product out of its pack). It is not redrawn in the animated look: shape, colours and printing stay exactly as in the photo, under the scene's light.
- It stands at the edge of the frame from the first frame, at its true size against the object, its front to the lens. It has no face and does not move.
- The turn of the monologue is the object's eyes going to it.
- In the last frame it is fully in view with clear space near it for the offer line.

## 5. Stills first

1. **The character picture.** The object alone, front on, plain background, from the fixed sentence. Check: 1 face, on a clear area; the damage shows; it reads at the size of a thumb. Approve it. Further views, if needed, are separate pictures: 1 view per picture.
2. **The opening frame.** The character picture and the product photo, each tagged with its job; the place, the light, the camera at eye level; the face already wearing the feeling of line 1, mouth open mid-word. Approve it.
3. Only then, clips.

## 6. The clips

1 to 3 clips of about 10 seconds. The words each holds follow how fast the object's voice talks. Clip 1 starts on the approved opening frame; each later clip is an extension of the one before, which is what holds the framing (`models.md`), and is asked to keep the face and the voice.

Templates and 2 filled examples: `references/prompts.md`. Each prompt holds, in this order: the references and their jobs; the place and the camera in 1 line; the fixed character sentence; the beats, each with what the features do, 1 small movement and the line; the frame it ends on; the product line; the sound.

- **The mouth moves with the words** and closes when the line ends. Say so in every beat: the model does not assume an object can talk.
- **The spoken lines ride in the prompt.** A line with a word the filter refuses (`models.md`) is reworded before the clip is asked for.
- **1 change of expression per beat.** It leans, sags, turns its eyes. It does not walk, jump or change size.
- **The voice** fits the object: its age, pace and 2 traits ("old, dry, a little hoarse"), in the accent of the brand's buyers. The same line in every clip.
- **Sound:** its voice, the room (a fridge hum, rain on a window), and its own material when it moves (a clink, a creak). No music unless the brand's `Motion and sound` says so.
- **A shape for 10 seconds:** 0 to 3 the grievance, with the feeling on the face from frame 1. 3 to 7 the detail only this object would know. 7 to 10 the eyes go to the product, the line that lands.

The offer and the call to action are on-screen text over the last shot, added in editing. The object never says them.

## 7. The check after each clip

On top of the shared check:

1. The face is the same face: same eyes, brows and mouth, in the same place on the object.
2. The mouth moved with the words and only with the words.
3. No limb appeared that the character sentence does not have. The object did not leave its place.
4. No letters appeared on the object. The product's printing is unchanged and it has no face.
5. The voice is the same voice as in the clip before. A person has listened to it.

## What is not done

- The product is not given a face.
- No studio, film, game or known character is named or imitated as the style.
- The object is not a competitor's product by shape, colour or name.
- No body part, organ or symptom talks.
- The object does not address the viewer, sell, name a price, or state a result it could not have seen.
