# Comic page ad

A printed comic page lies on an artist's desk and is filmed from above. The desk, the paper, the artist's hand and the product are a photograph. Everything inside the panels is flat ink that moves a little, like a motion comic. The page is read panel by panel, the hand turns it, and the ad ends on the real product standing on the same desk.

This file is the whole method for a person or an agent working by hand. `CARD.md` beside it is the same craft with the method taken out: it is what a planning step is sent. The rules every picture and clip follows (tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, real speed, sound said out loud, the check after a render) are in `skills/production-principles` and are not repeated here. Anything about a named model or tool is in `skills/production-principles/models.md`. How the words are written is in `skills/script-format-comic`.

## What it is judged on

1. With the sound off, a stranger can say what the problem was, what changed and which product did it.
2. Frame 1 reads as "a comic on a desk" at the size of a thumb.
3. The product at the end is the product in its photo.

## When it fits

It fits a script whose load-bearing beat is a **problem** that can be told as a short story, or a **mechanism** that can be drawn as a diagram, and that dramatises 1 claim.

It is the wrong format for proof that rests on a real person (a drawn person proves nothing), for an offer with terms, and for a product whose appeal is how it looks in use: a pour, a texture, a fit. Those want a demonstration or a person.

## Why it works: 4 things to protect

- **The photographed desk.** It makes the drawing look made by hand and not generated.
- **The panels.** They fix the order of the story in a medium where a video model drifts.
- **The page turn.** A reader waits for it. It is the format's hold on attention.
- **The real product at the end.** It turns "a nice drawing" into "a thing I can buy".

Every choice below serves 1 of these. If an idea puts the drawn person, the product or the split at risk, make the idea simpler.

## Structure

1 page is 1 clip.

| Pages | What they carry |
|---|---|
| 3 | The problem, the mechanism, the turn |
| 2 | The problem; the turn, with the diagram as its first panel |
| 1 | Problem, then product |

- **A page has 2 panels**, 1 above the other, each about half the page. Both read from above without the camera going close, and both read on a phone. A clip is then 3 beats: panel 1, panel 2, the closing move. (By hand, in a clip of 13 seconds or more, a page can carry 3 panels.)
- **The problem page:** the same person in the same place twice, the second panel worse than the first. A third egg welded to the pan. A third night staring at the ceiling.
- **The mechanism page:** panel 1 is drawn differently from every other panel in the ad, as a draughtsman's diagram: a cutaway or cross-section, construction lines, graphite shading, 2 or 3 empty label tags. Against the story panels it reads as "this was engineered". Panel 2: the product arrives in the story, and the accent colour arrives with it.
- **The turn page:** the panels of the problem page again, changed. Same person, same place.
- **The close:** the camera drifts from the last panel to the real product on the desk and holds.

### Keeping the diagram and the turn honest

A drawing is a claim. The viewer takes what the diagram shows as how the product works, and what the turn page shows as what the product does.

- The diagram shows what the product is made of, or what it does to the thing it touches, as the brand's facts state it: the layers of a pan, a fibre taking up vapour, what is in a sachet and how it is taken. Each label tag is a thing the brand can say.
- It shows the inside of a body only when the brand's claim is about the body, and then as a plain diagram and only as far as the claim goes. "Supports" is drawn as the product being taken and the body carrying on, never as damage mended.
- The change on the turn page is the product's `Visible result`, or its core benefit in the brand's own wording, and no larger. If the brand says "helps you wind down", the panel shows a person winding down, not 8 hours of sleep.
- The drawn person is fiction. They are an adult, they are nobody's customer, and they claim nothing as fact.

## The look

- **The split, said every time.** Desk, paper, hand and product are a photograph. Inside the panels is flat 2D ink. A prompt that does not say this mixes the 2.
- **Ink wording that renders:** bold black ink outlines, flat colour, halftone dot shading, cream newsprint paper, 2 muted tones. Wording led by "cross-hatching" drifts toward a scratchy generic comic. Describe the ink; never name an artist, a publisher or a title.
- **The desk, by plain names:** marker pens, graphite pencils, a steel ruler, a mug, loose pencil sketches, a few dried paint marks. Named things render; "a messy desk" does not. No brand of pen. The surface and the light come from the brand's `Brand world` when it is filled in.
- **1 accent colour:** the product's or its pack's own colour, or the brand's main colour when the product is black, white or grey or comes in many colours. Until the product arrives in the story the ink is black and 2 muted tones. The accent enters with the product and stays to the end. A still that shows the accent early is made again: the turn depends on the reserve.
- **The drawn person:** an adult, described by role, look and clothes, in the same words in every prompt. 2 drawn people at most on a page.
- **The product in ink** is its shape and its colour only, with no printing. A model that draws a label draws scribble. The real printing is seen only on the real product on the desk.
- **The real product** stands or lies on the desk beside the page from frame 1, the way its reference photo shows it: a pouch upright, a pan flat with its handle out, a garment folded. It is made from its tagged reference photos like any other product picture.

## Camera and movement

- Straight down from above, with the whole page and the product in frame, drifting slowly toward the panel being read. Kept this wide, nothing leaves the frame and has to be drawn again.
- Only the panel being read moves: 1 small movement inside the ink. Steam rises, a head drops onto a hand, an egg slides. The other panel holds still, which is what leads the eye.
- The artist's hand rests on the lower corner of the page from frame 1. The turn is 1 action: the hand lifts the corner and lays the page over, and the next page lies flat beneath.
- Showier moves exist: a fast low sweep across the desk tools before the page, a push through the last panel until the ink becomes the real product. They are what the format was first seen with. Both ask a video model for things it does badly (a camera path given in words, 1 thing turning into another), so they are tried only by hand, from a first and a last frame that are both approved stills, and only after the plain version has passed.

## Lettering and narration

- Each panel has 1 empty caption box in a top corner. Lettering is 5 words or fewer in capitals (the diagram's labels: 6 words in all), and it is set in editing in the brand's typeface or a comic face the brand has the right to use. A video model writes no words, and an image model asked for hand lettering misspells it.
- Nothing else is lettered: no sound-effect words, no numerals, a clock with hands and no digits.
- Narration is a voice-over, 8 words or fewer to a panel, none over a page turn. It says what the caption does not.

## Sound

Paper is the sound: the room, a pencil tap, the dry snap of the page. Say each sound in the prompt of the clip it belongs to. No music from the video model; if the ad has music it is laid in afterwards and comes in on the turn page, not before. A person listens to every clip.

## The method by hand

1. **Read the brain** (the fields are listed at the end). Choose the 1 claim. If the claim, the mechanism or the visible result is blank, ask; do not draw a guess.
2. **Write the beat sheet as a table**, 1 row per panel: page, panel, what is drawn, the lettering, the narration, what moves. Get it agreed before any picture is made. This is the cheapest place to change course.
3. **Make the stills, and look at each before going on.** The first page on the desk with the product beside it; then each later page on the same desk; then the last frame with the product centre frame. Each is made with the product's reference photos attached and tagged with their jobs, and each later still is made from the first so the desk does not change. Check every still against the list at the end. A page is not animated until its still is right.
4. **Make 1 clip per page.** The clip starts on its page's still and ends on the next page's still (the last clip ends on the product still). Attach the product photos again with their tags. How a model takes a first frame, a last frame or an extension is in `models.md`.
5. **Join on the settled page**, with hard cuts and no dissolves. Add the lettering in the caption boxes, then the narration.
6. **Check the render** against the list below and write down each failure as 1 line.

A worked example for 2 products is in `references/worked-example.md`.

## What to avoid

- A drawn customer giving a result, a drawn expert, a drawn child.
- A drawn pack with letters on it.
- The accent colour before the product.
- A story with no product in it until an advert starts at the end. The product is on the desk from frame 1 and in the story from the mechanism page.
- A turn page that shows more than the brand claims.
- Dialogue between drawn people.

## Check after the render

On top of the list in `production-principles`:

- Ink stayed flat ink and the desk stayed a photograph, in every frame.
- The drawn person is the same in every panel of every page.
- The caption boxes are empty: no scribbled letters anywhere.
- No accent colour before the product arrives in the story.
- Each turn landed on the page that was approved as a still.
- The product on the desk matches its photo and never left the frame.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `What it is`, `Key features`, `Core benefit` (the 1 claim and the diagram labels); `Looks like` and `Pack` (the drawn shape and colour, and the real product on the desk); `How it is used` (the panel where the product arrives); `Visible result` (the turn page); `Never show`; `Reference photos` (pack front and product out of pack, for every still and clip).
- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `How it works` (proposed in this pass: the mechanism as 2 or 3 links, each with its source). It is what the diagram panel draws. Blank: ask, or leave the mechanism page out and use the 2-page form.
- `customer-avatars.md`, the avatar: `Who they are`, `The moment` (the place and the hour of the problem page), `Customer language bank` (the lettering, word for word where it is a quote).
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette` (the accent), `Typography` (the lettering), `Brand world` (the desk surface and light), `Motion and sound`, `Brand guardrails`.
- `brand.json`: claims with sources and forbidden claims. `lessons.json`: how the product is held and used.

A blank field is a question for the brand. Where the answer cannot be had, the output says "inferred" beside what was guessed; a claim is never guessed.
