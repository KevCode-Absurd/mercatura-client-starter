# Worked example: 2 comic page ads

Both brands are made up for this example. Every claim below stands in for a line in the brand's facts; with a real brand, a claim that is not in the facts is not drawn.

## A. A consumable: a magnesium night drink in stick sachets

**From the brain.** Claim: "magnesium glycinate, 300 mg a sachet; supports relaxation before bed". Looks like: a slim matte sachet, deep blue, torn across the top; pale powder. Pack: a deep blue carton, the name in white. How it is used: tear the top, tip into a glass of water, stir. Visible result: nothing visible. The moment: in bed, awake, the house quiet. Accent: the deep blue.

Because nothing is visible and the claim is "supports", the turn page shows an evening that goes differently, not a night's sleep measured.

> A photograph taken straight down onto an artist's wooden desk in soft window light. A printed comic page on cream newsprint lies in the centre, filling most of the frame. Around it: marker pens, graphite pencils, a steel ruler, a mug, loose pencil sketches. An adult's right hand rests on the lower right corner of the page. At the top right edge of the desk stands the deep blue carton from @Image1 with 1 sachet from @Image2 lying beside it, their printing exactly as in the photos. The page has 2 panels, 1 above the other, drawn in flat ink: bold black outlines, flat colour, halftone dot shading, black and 2 muted greys, no blue anywhere in the ink. Top panel: a woman in a grey T-shirt lies on her back in bed with her eyes open, a wall clock with hands and no numbers behind her. Bottom panel: the same woman at a desk by day, chin on her hand, a full mug beside her. Each panel has 1 empty white caption box in its top left corner. No lettering anywhere on the page. 9:16.

**Clip 1.** Attached: the still above as the first frame, the still of page 2 as the last frame, and the 2 product photos with the same tags and jobs.

> 9:16, 10 seconds, one continuous take from straight above, no cuts. The desk, the paper, the hand and the carton are a photograph. Everything inside the panels is flat ink that moves like a motion comic. [0-4s] The camera drifts slowly toward the top panel. In the ink the woman blinks once; nothing else on the page moves. [4-7s] The camera drifts down to the bottom panel. In the ink her head sinks a little onto her hand. [7-10s] The right hand lifts the lower corner and lays the page over to the left; the next page lies flat beneath, as in the last frame. The carton stays where it is, its printing unchanged. Sound: a quiet room, 1 pencil tap, the dry snap of the page turning. No music. No words appear anywhere.

## B. A durable: a carbon steel frying pan

**From the brain.** Claim: "no coating: oil baked onto carbon steel makes the surface, and it gets better with use". Looks like: a dark grey-black pan, 28 cm, a long riveted steel handle, a faint blue sheen. Pack: a brown card sleeve. Visible result: an egg slides across the pan. The moment: a weekday breakfast, an egg welded to an old coated pan. Accent: the brand's rust orange.

| Page | Panel | Drawn in ink | Lettering | What moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | A man at a hob scrapes at an egg stuck to a pale scratched pan | THIRD PAN. SAME EGG. | The spatula scrapes |
| 1 | 2 | The same pan in the sink, flakes of coating in the water | WHERE DOES IT GO? | A flake drifts down |
| 2 | 1 | Diagram: the pan in cross-section, 1 layer of steel, a thin dark layer on top, 3 empty tags | (tags: CARBON STEEL / OIL, BAKED ON / NO COATING) | The thin layer darkens |
| 2 | 2 | The man wipes oil into a dark pan with a cloth; the cloth is the only orange on the page | 1 WIPE OF OIL | The cloth circles once |
| 3 | 1 | The same hob; an egg slides to the pan's edge | SAME EGG. | The egg slides |
| 3 | 2 | The pan hangs on a hook, darker than before | BETTER EVERY TIME | The pan swings slightly, then stills |

The close: the camera drifts to the real pan lying flat on the desk, handle pointing out of frame, exactly as in its photo.

What differs from A: the result can be seen, so the turn page shows it happening. The diagram is of the product itself, so no body is drawn at all. The product on the desk is out of its sleeve, because the pan and not the sleeve is what the buyer is shown.
