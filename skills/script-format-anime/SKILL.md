# Format: anime

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

This is not the Comic format (a printed page filmed on a desk): here the drawing is the whole screen and the hero moves.

## What makes it anime

- **1 original graphic language,** held across the ad: line weight, shadow count, palette and texture, defined once.
- **1 original hero** with a locked silhouette, facial proportions, palette, outfit, line weight and shading method: a fictional adult, plain from its proportions and dress. Silhouette and expression come before fine detail.
- **1 narrative device** turns the selling idea into a scene. Name it first.

| Device | What is seen | Its limit |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation | The hero's day or routine changes at the product | Never a changed body |
| Dramatic reveal | Tension builds, then the product is shown large | The product, not a result |
| Versus comparison | The hero against the old way (a drawer of dull razors) | Never a named competitor or a lookalike pack |
| Power-up mechanism | The product's edge drawn as an aura or a charge | A metaphor, and plainly one |
| Talking hero | The hero speaks to the viewer as a guide | No testimonial |
| Panel-by-panel explainer | 2 or 3 panels, 1 step each | Only steps the brand's facts give |

- **Key poses and geography.** Define readable anticipation, action and reaction, with a clear path and endpoint. Identity, environment, starting composition and action key frames have distinct reference roles. Speed lines, halftone, impact frames and panel borders appear only when they reinforce the shot; a fight is optional.
- **The product** is drawn in the same line and colour at its true geometry, every line of its label stated exactly.
- **Speech:** narration, or the hero speaking, 1 speaker at a time.

**8 looks** (the job is given 1): `cel` (default clean line and cel shadows), `inked` (bold brush line), `manga` (monochrome screentone with product accent), `graphic-novel` (muted ink), `pop` (bright graphic shapes), `retro-ova` (painted cels, hard ink shadows and subtle analog texture), `cyberpunk` (urban depth, mechanical detail and controlled neon), `deadpan-comedy` (simple adult expressions against an exaggerated event). Default to colour; monochrome and analog artifacts are choices. Use original descriptive traits, not required artist-name tags.

## What it is good for

A drawing exaggerates freely: a frustration at full size, the old way as a rival, an invisible edge as a charge of light.

It has no believed human face: weak for proof that rests on a person or a product sold on its real texture. A quiet emotional story is valid when performance and the product role are readable.

By stage: strongest for problem-aware buyers (the old way as the rival) and solution-aware buyers (the versus, the power-up): the genre is a conflict and its answer. Its look alone can stop an unaware buyer; a reveal can carry a launch to a product-aware one. A most-aware buyer needs the offer, and another format.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

An aura, a glow or a burst is a feeling, never a result: the hero's body and face do not change; no body before and after. A mechanism is drawn only as `How it works` gives it. The hero is fiction: it says what the brand states and never "it worked for me".

## What to avoid

- A protected character, costume, franchise symbol or copied composition; a named artist's or studio's style.
- A hero who reads as a teenager or a child.
- A fight with no product in it.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Looks like` and `Pack` (the drawn product, its label word for word), `Replaces` (the old way), `How it works` (the power-up), `How it is used`. Blank `Replaces` or `How it works`: ask, or choose another device. Never invent.
- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are` (the hero), `The moment`, `Tried before`, `Customer language bank`. Blank: mark the hero inferred.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette`, `Brand voice and tone`, `Brand guardrails`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer. A claim that is not there is not drawn.
