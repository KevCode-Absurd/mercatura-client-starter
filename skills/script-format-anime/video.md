# Anime as a video: direct a complete product story

With sound off, the viewer can identify the obstacle, what changes and the product's role. Choose one device: reveal, routine change, old-way comparison, graphic power-up metaphor, talking guide, emotional gesture or deadpan visual comedy. Action and suspense are optional; a fight with a pack shot tacked on is not an ad argument.

## Words and story

- Preserve approved words. Specify narrator off screen or hero speaking, one speaker at a time. The hero is fiction, never a real customer's result or expert endorsement.
- Open on an event already happening or a visible question. Anticipation, action, reaction and payoff need purposeful time; a brief reaction hold can be the joke.
- Product use and mechanisms follow facts. A graphic aura around the hero is a metaphor, never a transformed body or fabricated efficacy. The product arrives when the planned story calls for it.
- End with recognition time and a relevant next action. Exact captions, balloons and offer text go in editing, not generated lettering.

## Timing and references

Use the selected ad length and actual supported clip lengths; 15, 30 or 60 seconds can be a sequence of controlled clips. Do not assume one 30–60 second generation, an exact fps or an extension feature. Dialogue must fit the speaker's pace and clip budget.

- Lock one original adult hero's silhouette, face, hair, outfit and palette. The current app's anime route is single-lead; do not silently create a second speaking cast member.
- Prepare identity, environment, starting composition and difficult action key frames before animation. Each actual reference has a role; an identity sheet does not dictate pose or background.
- Map hero, product and obstacle positions, gaze targets, distances and travel direction. Carry moved objects, clothing and effect states across clips.
- Each shot names one main event, dominant camera move, light and motivated VFX. A steadier camera helps fast action; a complex background needs simpler action.
- Write clear key poses plus the path between them when travel matters. Walking or a short dash is allowed with a start and endpoint. Split elaborate choreography into readable beats.
- Use deliberate 2D timing: anticipation, action, short holds with hair/fabric/blinks, a brief impact or speed lines. Do not force perpetual real-life motion, slow drift or smooth 3D interpolation.
- Keep line weight, cel shadows, palette and the chosen texture stable. Preserve product shape, scale, colours and label artwork; effects must not cover it. No known character, copied costume or franchise scene.
- Sound and BGM follow the job's audio choices, including full silence. Selective contact sounds support events and music supports voices. Native audio generates with the clip; it is not independent stems.

Watch actual clip joins for identity, geography, object states and timing. A difficult grip needs a key frame or spatial repair, not a longer stack of style adjectives.
