# Podcast clip as a still image: 1 frame of the conversation, with its caption

Judged on 1 thing: a stranger takes it for a frame from a real conversation, reads the caption, and wants to know what was said next.

## The frame

- **Freeze the moment of the reaction.** 1 of them has just said the line; the other is caught reacting: eyebrows up, leaning in, a hand half raised, mouth just open to object. Both faces carry it. 2 people looking calmly at each other is not a moment.
- **Composition:** both from the chest up at 1 table, a microphone on an arm in front of each, the Host on the left, the Guest on the right. The speaker's hands are mid-gesture. In a tall frame the pair sit in the middle third, with the caption below them and quiet wall above.
- **The product** stands on the table between them, its front to the lens, untouched. For an unaware or problem-aware buyer it may be small, or left out: then the table holds only 2 cups.
- 1 plain room: a wall, a shelf or a curtain, a warm lamp. Nothing in it carries words.

## Words on the picture

- **1 caption, as a clip's subtitle:** the line that was just said, 10 words or fewer, in quotation marks only if it is a customer's real words. It is a question, a doubt or a claim about the product or the category that the brand's facts hold ("Why does every one of those die in a year?"). Never a result either of them had, never a title or a name for either of them.
- It sits in the lower third as bold subtitle type on a plain bar, with 1 or 2 words in the brand's accent colour.
- The offer and the button, where there is an offer, are a small separate line under the caption or beside the product. 20 words on the whole image. No show name, no episode number, no platform's interface.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: a frame from a video podcast, filmed by 1 fixed camera at seated eye level. A real photograph: real skin texture, slight asymmetry, ordinary clothes with real wrinkles, soft warm room light from 1 named side.
- Preserve both identities, tone/undertone, age, marks and makeup. Skin detail follows zone, coverage, distance and room light; keep it subtle in a two-shot. No smoothing, etched pores, dusty fuzz or sharpening halos; no selfie drift or flash.
- Accessories keep count/design/scale/position, attachment/contact and material response. Use [Skin detail craft](../production-principles/references/skin-detail-craft.md) for skin, makeup, accessory and repair details.
- Describe each person by role, look and clothes (2 adults), who is on which side, and what each face and each pair of hands is doing at this moment.
- Hands keep plausible anatomy, occlusion, nail/accessory styling and contact. Do not force hidden digits into view or add product handling.
- 2 broadcast microphones on arms, with no maker's mark. No headphones with logos, no screens, no signs.
- The product is the real one from its photo, standing on the table, its printing sharp.
- The caption is given exactly, once, with its place and style.
- **Type, the look's own:** heavy, plain, slightly condensed sans-serif subtitles, white on a dark bar. Offer/button use the brand's typefaces.
- **Type, the brand's:** heavy brand body type on the same bar, preserving width and letter shapes; never its small tag face. Offer/button use the brand's typefaces.

## What fails in a still

- For a skin fault, change one local texture/pore/highlight clause. Lock both identities, styling, expression, pose, crop, fixed camera, room light and product/caption. Compare at the same scale; use existing render limits and the shared skin-repair method.
- 2 people smiling at the lens: a stock photo, not a clip.
- A caption that is ad copy ("The best one on the market") or a testimonial ("It fixed mine in a week").
- A lower-third naming the Guest as a doctor or an expert.
- The product held up to the lens.
