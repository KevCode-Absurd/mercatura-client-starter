# Podcast clip as a video: a conversation cut into short clips

Judged on 2 things: the first line leaves a question a stranger waits to hear answered, and 1 belief has moved by the last line.

Every spoken line starts with its speaker, `Host:` or `Guest:`, 1 speaker to a line. Give each a way of talking and keep it.

## The 4 structures. Pick 1.

1. **Problem, then solution.** The Host names the problem the way customers say it. The Guest says why the usual fix fails, then what works.
2. **Discovery.** The Guest came across something in the brand's facts that surprised them. The Host is sceptical, asks the question the viewer would ask, and is answered. A discovery is something read or noticed, never a result the Guest had.
3. **Myth-bust.** The Guest says the opposite of what buyers believe. The Host pushes back with the buyer's own belief. The Guest gives the 1 reason. Only when the research shows buyers hold that belief.
4. **What's on the table.** Part-way through, a shot opens on the product standing on the table, and the Host asks what it is. It is never fetched from a bag or a pocket.

## How to write it

- **Open mid-conversation.** The first line is a reaction or a claim, not a welcome. Write its 6-word version as on-screen text, for a viewer with the sound off.
- **Words per clip.** The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the 2 of them talk. A clip is 2 or 3 turns. More words than it holds and the last line is cut off.
- **Short turns.** 1 or 2 sentences, 2 to 6 seconds. A long explanation is broken by the other speaker ("Wait, so...").
- **Clean cuts.** A clip ends at the end of a turn. No sentence runs across a clip.
- **Spoken, not written.** Contractions. 1 false start in the whole ad, not 1 a line. The reply picks up a word the other just used. 1 fact to a turn: a list of benefits is copy.
- **The doubt is real.** The Host's objection is the avatar's own, in their words, and stands until the reason is given. 2 people agreeing for 30 seconds is not a clip: 1 of them has to doubt.
- **The product** is named once or twice, late, as what the Guest came across or read about. It is never the first thing said. The brand name is said the way a person says it, not the way a label prints it.
- **One close that works.** The last turn is the Host's: what they will do next ("okay, send me that") or a line that lands. The offer and the call to action are not spoken by either of them: write them as on-screen text for the end of the last clip.

## Length

15 seconds is 1 clip, 30 seconds is 2 or 3, 45 seconds is 3 or 4. Under 15 seconds there is no room for a second turn each: use another format.

## Writing shots a model can render

- 1 room, 2 seats, 2 microphones. Say in the first visual note that the Host is on the left and the Guest on the right, and never swap.
- The visual notes cut between the 2 at the table, a single on whoever speaks, the listener reacting, and hands or the product close. A cut never falls inside a word.
- Each visual note gives 1 thing the speaker does (a hand opens on the table) and 1 reaction from the listener (a nod, eyebrows up, leaning in). A reaction is seen, not said under the line.
- The product comes in when the words reach it: a close shot of it standing on the table.
- No captions inside the picture: they are added afterwards.
