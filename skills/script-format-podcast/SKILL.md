# Format: podcast clip

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it a podcast clip

- **It reads as something overheard, not something sold.** 2 people at microphones, mid-conversation: a question a stranger wants answered, a doubt, a reaction.
- **1 argument.** 1 belief has moved by the end. A second argument is a second ad.
- **The product is secondary.** It stands on the table, its front to the lens, and is never held up to it. A garment hangs on the wall between them; something too big for a table stands on the floor at the table's end.
- **Plain room, plain clothes:** no words, logos, signs or screens, and the show has no name.

## Who is speaking, and what each can honestly say

- 2 fictional adults: a **Host** who asks, doubts and reacts, and a **Guest** who explains. The Host is on the left and the Guest on the right, always.
- Neither is the founder, a customer, a doctor or any other expert, and neither says they are. Neither is named.
- **Each can say:** a question, a doubt, a reaction. A fact from the brand's facts, said as a fact about the product ("there's no coating on it"). What customers say, attributed ("the reviews keep saying") and word for word when quoted. The problem as an observation or a question ("why does every one of those die in a year?"). That they read or came across something, when what follows is in the brand's facts.
- **Neither can say:** a result they had ("it fixed my bloating", "mine's lasted 5 years"), how long they have used it, that they tested it, a title or a job, a study or a number that is not in the brand's facts, anything about other products that the brand's facts do not state.
- A customer's first-person words are attributed or turned into a question. They are never given to a speaker as their own story.

## What it is good for

It holds attention through disagreement, surprise and an open question. It shows nothing a camera can't, so a mechanism has to be simple enough to say in 2 lines.

So it carries the **problem** (named out loud by someone who is tired of it) or a **belief to overturn** (a myth, a hot take). It is weak for a mechanism that needs a picture, for proof that rests on a real person, and for an offer with several terms.

By stage: problem-aware buyers (the problem said out loud) and solution-aware buyers in a market that has heard every claim (the myth turned over). The offer is never spoken: it is type beside the product.

## What to avoid

- A Guest introduced as an expert, or 1 who gives a testimonial.
- Lines that sound like ad copy read aloud. If neither of them would say it across a table, cut it.
- A doubt or a myth no customer voiced: that is a straw man.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: `Customer language bank` (the Host's problem line), `What almost stops them` (the Host's doubt), `Ranked insight bank` (the 1 argument); proposed `Believes` (the myth) and `Tried before` (why the usual fix fails). Blank: pick a structure that does not need the field.
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Core benefit`, `Key features`, proposed `How it works` (the 2-line reason). Blank: ask. Never invent a mechanism.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand voice and tone`, `Brand guardrails`, proposed `Said aloud`. Blank name: spell it as said and mark it "inferred".
- `brain.md`: `Market awareness level`, `Market sophistication` (myth-bust suits a market that has heard every claim).
- `brand.json`, `offers.json`: every fact a speaker states and the offer. A fact that is not there is not said.
