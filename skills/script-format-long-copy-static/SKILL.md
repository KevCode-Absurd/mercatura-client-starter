# Script format: long-copy native static

Load this with a script or image-copy task skill. Where the two differ, this wins.

It is judged on 2 things: the first line earns the tap on "See more", and each paragraph earns the next until the reader reaches the offer.

## What this surface carries

A plain photo (a garden, a kitchen bench, a hallway floor, a bathroom shelf) with long copy beneath it. No sound, no believed face, no control over the image's reading order. But the copy is read in order, it holds attention for as long as the story does, and of all formats it reads most like a person and least like an advertisement.

So it suits scripts whose load-bearing beat is the **problem** told as a story, for unaware and problem-aware buyers.

## The image

- Looks like something a person posted. No logo, no headline, no product hero shot, no design.
- Shows the moment or the accommodation: the place the story happens, or the object that stands for it.
- 0 to 6 words on it at most, as a caption a person would add. Often none.
- The product may be in the frame the way it would be in real life, never posed, and always the real product as its photos show it.
- Nobody needs to be in it. A generated person is never shown as the narrator.

## The copy carries every beat

Write the full argument as 1 story, in the order the beats run. The reader should feel they are reading someone's account, and meet the product partway through as part of it. In the script, the lines are the story as it will run in the feed; on-screen text is empty except for a caption, and the first line's visual note is the photo.

- **First line:** the hook, 125 characters or fewer. A confession, the moment, or the accommodation. No brand, no greeting.
- **Shape:** pick 1: confession, failed-solutions journey, discovery, or transformation with a witness.
- **Whose story:** a first-person account is a real customer's or the founder's own, built from their words in the brand's material. With none on file, write in the 3rd person about what customers report, or as the brand speaking plainly. A story that did not happen is not told as testimony.
- **Stakes:** something risked, lost or embarrassing, early.
- **Problem:** long here. It escalates toward 1 consequence.
- **Mechanism:** as what the narrator found out, in plain words. The promise can stay implied until then.
- **Objection:** what they tried before, and why it half worked.
- **The product's arrival:** inside the story, named late, eased in.
- **Proof:** a count, a third party, a quoted word, each from the brand's facts. Not the narrator's result alone.
- **Offer, risk reversal, call to action:** the real terms, the real guarantee in the brand's wording, 1 clear action. The story must bridge into them; it can't stop and become an ad.
- **Close:** 1 identity line.

## Voice

Short paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences, with white space. Varied sentence length. Contractions. No marketing words. No bullet lists or emoji unless that is how this brand writes its own long posts. It should read aloud as a person talking.

## Length

The length the job gives. The story decides inside it. Lower in the funnel it usually runs 150 to 500 words; for a buyer who does not know the problem yet it runs as long as the request's range for that stage, which wins. Each paragraph has to earn the next; a story that needs padding is a shorter story.

## What to avoid

- A designed image. It moves the ad to the pile people skip.
- A story with no stakes.
- A testimony written for someone who hasn't used the product.
- A story that ends, followed by a sales paragraph in a different voice.
