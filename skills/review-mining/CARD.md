# Review mining: the card

<!-- The rules of SKILL.md with the explanation taken out. Sent to writers that build on a mined angle. -->

An angle from the library is a buyer's own phrase, kept exactly as typed, with the id of the source it came from (R-012 a review, C-003 a call, T-007 a ticket, S-004 a survey, M-002 a community post), the job it does, and how many separate sources say the same thing.

The 12 jobs a phrase can do: 01 identity (who they were before), 02 trigger (what made them search), 03 failed fix (what they tried first), 04 objection (the doubt before buying), 05 slow start (when they thought it was not working), 06 mechanism (how it works, by feel), 07 first notice (the moment they knew), 08 real win (the benefit they kept), 09 ritual (how it fits their day), 10 complaint (the honest gripe), 11 villain (what it replaced), 12 repeat signal (why they would buy again or gift it).

## Rules

1. If the line is not in a source, it does not go in the ad. Every headline, hook and subject line traces to a source id.
2. Verbatim means verbatim. Trim at a word boundary; never fix grammar, tighten, punch up or make it "more us". Typos and lowercase stay.
3. Your connective copy must be plainer than the quote. If your sentence is the most interesting one in the ad, you wrote an ad about yourself.
4. One angle per asset.
5. Rank by frequency, not by taste. Build the phrase many sources say before the phrase you like.
6. Never let a model regenerate a quote. Lock it; read it at full size; discard anything reworded.
7. Cite the source id in the brief, the angle and the record of what was made.
8. Do not run an angle twice: respect its status.
9. Quoted material is marked with quotation marks in the output, so what is theirs and what is yours can be told apart. One quote, one plain sentence, one quote; never three quotes in a row.

Unexpected uses stay attached to product and source ID under ritual or real win. Verify official use and compatibility before proposing a demonstration. Frequency describes these sources, not typical outcomes. A PDP handoff includes the question, source IDs and distinct-source count; inspect the actual answer and visibility rather than inferring coverage from shared words.

## Legal flags, before anything goes out

- A quote from an unverified review is read, never put in a paid ad.
- A quote from a reviewer who was given the product or paid says so where it is used.
- Do not change a quote's meaning. Do not present an unusual result as typical. A quoted claim still has to be held by the brand.
- Flag, in a "review before use" list with the reason: any phrase implying a health, medical or disease outcome; a result that could read as typical when it is not; a competitor named; a claim the brand brain cannot support. Never silently drop or soften them.
- Attribution is only what the export holds. No invented names, initials or cities.
