# Motion graphics: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A flat information system built round the real product cutout: shapes, icons, diagrams and charts on a grid, in the brand's colours. The video model makes wordless moving plates; exact type, numbers and the logo are laid over afterward. It is judged on:

1. Every line of copy can be read at phone size in the time it is held.
2. Every movement explains something: hierarchy, cause, comparison or mechanism.
3. Every chart matches the brand's figures; an illustrative shape is visibly not a measurement.
4. The product is the real one, and the system is the same in every clip.

## The look (preset `default`)

`visual_direction` opens with this, filled from the brand's `Color palette`, and every `visual` keeps its words:

"Original flat motion graphics system. A plain [ground colour] ground on an even grid with wide margins. Flat geometric shapes with [rounded or square] corners in [2 or 3 brand colours] and 1 accent, [accent colour]. Icons in 1 even line weight. Even shadowless light, no gradients, no gloss, no texture, no depth except 1 soft shadow beneath the product."

- `setting` is the ground, not a place: its colour, the grid, and where the plain area for copy is (the top third, or 1 column).
- 1 shape language and 1 icon style, named once and used throughout.
- The accent colour is kept for the 1 thing each shot is about.

## Nobody is in it

- There is no person, no hand and no face. `character` says so: the subject is the product cutout.
- A person as an icon is a plain pictogram in the icon style, an adult by its proportions, standing for nobody and claiming nothing.

## Structure

- The brief gives each clip's shots and their length. A shot is 1 scene.
- Each shot: 1 message, 1 supporting visual (the cutout, a diagram, a chart or a row of icons), 1 focal point.
- Each shot: 1 move, then the plate is still while the copy is read once.
- The first shot: the cutout and the visual of the claim, a movement already under way.
- The last shot: the cutout centred and still, a plain area above or beside it for the offer.
- `end` of every clip is its last shot come to rest, every shape named with its place and colour. Only the last shot of the ad stays still past the time its copy needs.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The cutout large on the plain ground, 1 shape moving.
- The whole grid: the cutout small, the diagram or chart beside it.
- 1 diagram drawing itself, filling the frame.
- 2 things on 1 baseline, for a comparison.
- A row of icons, 1 of them in the accent.
- The cutout centred and still, to close.

## What moves, and why

- Each `visual` names its 1 move and which of 4 things it shows:
  - hierarchy: the thing that matters arrives last, largest, in the accent
  - cause: 1 shape travels to another, and the second responds on contact
  - comparison: 2 things on 1 baseline change at the same moment, at the same speed
  - mechanism: a diagram draws itself link by link, in the order the script gives
- A move that shows none of the 4 is cut.
- 1 transition family for the whole ad, named in `visual_direction`: slide (along the grid's lines), wipe (a block of the accent crosses and uncovers the next layout), scale (from the focal point) or draw-on (a line draws itself and shapes fill in after it). A new diagram or chart enters only by it.
- `camera`: locked and square to the ground in every shot. No push, pan, shake or drift: the cut changes how large the cutout or the diagram is.
- The motion guard closes `visual_direction`: "Flat graphic motion at real speed. Shapes slide, scale, turn and wipe along the grid, ease to a stop and hold still. Nothing morphs. No generated words, numbers, logos, watermarks, or UI."

## No words in the plate

- No `visual` contains a word, a number, a logo or an interface, quoted or described. The scene's on-screen text holds the copy, exactly, and is laid over afterward.
- A `visual` says where the plain area for that copy is, and that it stays plain while the copy is read.
- On-screen text: 6 words or fewer a shot, 1 figure at most.
- The product's own printing is the only lettering in any frame.

## Charts and diagrams

- A chart's shapes are in the proportions of the brand's figures, and the `visual` writes the proportion ("the left bar twice the height of the right"). The figures go in the on-screen text.
- No figures in the script's lines: no chart. An illustrative shape has no axis, no scale and no bars.
- A diagram has 2 or 3 links, each 1 shape. No link is added that the script does not state. No body before and after.
- A comparison is with the old way or the category, never a named competitor.

## The product on screen

- It is first in `props`: a photographic cutout from its photo, its true proportions, colours and printing. Its `look` gives its size against the frame and its 1 soft shadow.
- It is in the first frame and stays the fixed point. It may turn slowly on its upright axis, scale, or slide along the grid.
- It is never redrawn as an icon. It opens or pours only as the lessons say.
- The other `props` are the shapes the ad uses, each with its colour and its place, in view in the first frame or entering by the transition family.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" and gives age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice.
- All `dialogue` is narration. It says what the on-screen text says, in more words. The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks.
- Small sounds follow the moves: a soft click as a shape settles, a slide. Music only if the brand's `Motion and sound` says so.

## First and last frame

- First: the cutout sharp on the plain ground, 1 shape already moving, the plain area for copy clear.
- Last: the cutout centred, front to the lens, everything at rest, the top third plain for the offer.

## Avoid

- A headline, a figure or a logo described in a `visual`.
- 2 moves at once, or a new technique in a later clip.
- Gradients, gloss, 3D, particles, a moving camera.
- A hand, a face or a filmed scene.
- A chart drawn for effect.

## After the render

- No letter or numeral was generated anywhere but on the product.
- Flat stayed flat, and the palette is the brand's.
- Each shot made 1 move in the chosen family and came to rest.
- The plain area stayed plain while the copy was read.
- A chart's proportions match the figures.
- The cutout matches its photo in every frame.
