# Form: an expert's voice

A professional explains what they see in people with this problem and what is usually missed.

- **Opens with:** Their standing and the pattern. "I've been a [role] for [N] years. If [the workaround people use], here is what is being missed." A skin therapist on washing; a chef on pans; a guide on jackets.
- **Order:** Opening, the pattern they see in case after case (the weight of it, from the outside), why the usual approach doesn't reach the cause (the reframe), what they look for instead (the bridge), what people report after (what changed, as observations), the close, sign-off with name and title, P.S.
- **Carried by:** Authority and explanation. The mechanism is most of the piece.
- **Lengths it suits:** standard, long
- **A product bought once:** the expert is someone who uses the thing for a living, and the piece is what they look for when they choose one.
- **Closes with:** A sign-off with name and title, then 1 P.S. that quotes what people commonly say after a few weeks.

## Care

- Only with a real, named professional the brand has, who has agreed. Name, title, years and credentials are placeholders unless the brand's material gives them. An invented expert is never written, and the notes say the text needs that person's approval before it runs.
- No diagnosis, no advice to change or stop a treatment, no claim to treat a condition.
- Quotes in a P.S. are real customer quotes from the research, word for word, or placeholders.
