# Form: the mistake i was making

The narrator was doing something they believed was right, and it was part of the problem.

- **Opens with:** The mistake, said with the pride they had in it. "Every night I washed my face until it squeaked, and I was proud of how clean it felt." "Every night I put the pan in the dishwasher, and I was proud of how it came out."
- **Order:** Opening (the habit), why they thought it was right, the first sign something was off, the breaking point, the reframe (why the habit made it worse or missed the point), the bridge, first experience, what changed, the close.
- **Carried by:** Guilt turning into understanding. Suits problems where people try hard in the wrong direction, and care for someone else: a child, a parent, a pet.
- **Lengths it suits:** standard, long
- **A product bought once:** the mistake is how they chose it or looked after it: buying the cheap one again, washing it the wrong way.
- **Closes with:** "If you're doing what I was doing, you didn't do anything wrong. Nobody told us." Then the link.

## Care

- The mistake is a common, well-meant habit found in the research. The reader is never blamed.
- That the habit makes things worse is a claim: it rests on the brand's material, or it is told as what the narrator came to believe.
- No dramatic harm is claimed. "I almost ruined" or "I almost killed" is not written unless the brand's material supports the danger.
