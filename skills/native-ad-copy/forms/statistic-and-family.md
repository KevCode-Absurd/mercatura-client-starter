# Form: a number, then a family story

Opens on a real, sourced fact, then tells what happened to someone the narrator knows well, then what the narrator did differently.

- **Opens with:** The fact, then the turn to the personal. "Not 1 in 20. Not 1 in 10. [the sourced figure]." Then: "My mother was one of them." Or: "[The sourced figure for how long the usual kind lasts]. My grandmother cooked on 1 for 40 years."
- **Order:** Opening (the fact), the other person's story as the weight of it, the narrator's own breaking point (refusing to go the same way, or wanting what they had), the reframe, the bridge, first experience, what changed, the close, sign-off and P.S.
- **Carried by:** Someone else's path: the fear of following it, or the wish to have what they had. The second person's story is most of the length.
- **Lengths it suits:** long
- **A product bought once:** the number is about cost, waste or how long the usual kind lasts. The family story is the one that lasted, or the pile that didn't.
- **Closes with:** A first-name sign-off, the link, and a P.S. that states the result in 1 or 2 sentences.

## Care

- The opening fact must be in the brand's material with its source, and is said exactly as the source says it. If there is none, this form is not used: say so in the notes and write a personal journey instead.
- No illness or outcome is attributed to a medicine, a doctor or a product. The family story is what the narrator saw and felt, not a medical claim.
- Never advise stopping or refusing treatment.
- The family member is part of the composite unless the story is a real customer's: an invented first name or none, and no detail that could identify a real person.
