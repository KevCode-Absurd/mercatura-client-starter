# Form: a letter about someone

The narrator writes about a friend, a partner or a parent they watched struggle. The product arrives through them.

- **Opens with:** The other person, in a scene. "My best friend of 28 years sat in her sunroom that autumn with her sleeves pulled down over her hands." "My dad has cooked on the same pan since before I was born. I've been through 5 in 8 years."
- **Order:** Opening (the scene), who this person is to the narrator, what the narrator watched them try, the breaking point seen from outside, how one of them found the reframe, the bridge, what the narrator saw change, the close.
- **Carried by:** Witness. The narrator can say what the person couldn't see about themselves.
- **Lengths it suits:** standard, long
- **A product bought once:** it is often a gift, or something handed down. The other person is the one who had the good one, or the one the narrator bought it for.
- **Closes with:** "I'm writing this the night before I see her again." Then the link.

## Care

- The narrator reports what they saw, which is the proof. The other person's results stay inside what customers report.
- The other person is part of the composite: an invented first name, and nothing that could identify a real person. A real person is written about only with their say.
- The other person is an adult, unless the product is made for children and the brand's material supports every word said about the child.
