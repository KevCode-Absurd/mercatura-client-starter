# Form: please stop doing this

Opens by telling the reader to stop the thing everyone does, then tells the story of how the narrator learned why.

- **Opens with:** "Please stop trying to fix [the problem] with [the usual fix]. I know that sounds crazy." "Please stop scrubbing a dry face with a hot flannel." "Please stop buying a new non-stick pan every 2 years."
- **Order:** Opening (the instruction and the reason in 1 breath), the scene where the narrator's own version of the problem cost them something (a holiday, a photo, a dinner), what they had been buying, the reframe in a few lines, where they learned it, what changed in short, the close.
- **Carried by:** A pattern break, then a vivid scene. Short and fast.
- **Lengths it suits:** short, standard
- **A product bought once:** the thing to stop is replacing it, or the workaround everyone uses.
- **Closes with:** "If you're [the workaround, as something they do], read this." Often leads to an article, not the product page.

## Care

- "Stop" applies to a kind of product or a habit that doesn't reach the cause, never to medical treatment or a professional's advice.
- The usual fix is described, not named by brand, and the reason to stop it is one the brand can support.
- The thing to stop is never something the brand's own product also asks of the buyer.
