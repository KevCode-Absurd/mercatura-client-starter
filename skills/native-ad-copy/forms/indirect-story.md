# Form: the indirect story

The copy of an indirect ad (skills/indirect-ads). On a direct ad the copy is two lines; here the copy is the ad. The image earned the stop by planting one question; the primary text picks that question up in its first line and holds the reader for a short read (the length is given with the request), then sends them to a page that teaches, never to a product page.

- **Opens with** 1 of 5 leads, and the first line answers the question the image asked:
  - Story: a specific person, a specific moment. "Murphy slept at the foot of our bed for nine years."
  - Warning: stops them on the thing they are about to do. "Before you book another cortisone shot, read this."
  - Authority reversal: an expert who believed the usual answer until something changed. "I told patients the same thing for 12 years." Only a real expert the brand has, on record, with their say.
  - Confession: disarms them before the pitch. "I used to plan meetings around my shirts."
  - Curiosity question: a question the reader cannot answer yet. "Why does a non-stick pan start sticking after a year?"
- **Order, the spine under every lead:** the relatable problem in the buyer's own words; the false belief everyone holds about it ("it's just age", "it's wear and tear"); what they have already tried and why it did not work; the real mechanism, the reason the problem actually happens; a hint of the answer, without a hard sell; one soft call to action to the page: "I put everything I found on one page."
- **Carried by:** one new belief installed in the middle (the coating was always going to wear off, however careful they were); the reader's own phrases from the research ("the egg pan", "nobody else is allowed to touch it"); paragraphs of 2 to 5 whole sentences, plain words, no technical term.
- **Lengths it suits:** brief, short.
- **A product bought once:** the usual fix is the thing they keep replacing; the mechanism is why it keeps failing.
- **Closes with:** "It's all on one page. Tap below and read it before your next appointment." The click goes to an advertorial. Never a price, never a discount, never the product page.
- **The headline under the image** is a second hook, not a product name, in 1 of 4 shapes: "Before you [thing they are about to do], read this"; "Why your [problem] [keeps happening]"; "It wasn't [what they blamed]. It was [the real cause]."; "The reason you [behaviour they are embarrassed by]". Each hook variant's `overlay` is one of these.

## Care

- The narrator is the founder ("My mom had her second cortisone shot in March"), or the brand summarising what customers say ("Almost every couple who writes to us tells the same story"). A first-person story in a customer's voice only retells a real review or a real conversation given in the research, word for word where it quotes. No invented customer.
- No invented expert: "a surgeon" or "Dr. Somebody" narrates only when that person is real, on record and agreed.
- The copy can describe a problem; it cannot say the product cures, treats or reverses a disease.
- No "you" statements about personal health ("Do you have arthritis?"): "Why your partner ended up on the couch", not "Do you snore?".
- The product is hinted at, not pitched; the price is never said. The mechanism is one the brand's facts support.
- "I'm not a doctor and I'm not going to tell you to stop seeing yours": a line like it belongs in any health category.
