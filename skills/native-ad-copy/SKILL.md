# Native ad copy

The job: copy that a person with this problem reads to the end, because it is their own experience told back to them, and that leaves them wanting to see the page. The copy does the selling. The image (a separate skill) only gets it opened.

It is judged on 3 things. Would the buyer take the first 3 lines for their own life? Does every fact trace to the brand's material or the research? Would it still be honest if the reader knew exactly who wrote it?

You are given the concept (the buyer, the angle, the offer), research themes with real quotes, the brand's facts and guardrails, and 4 choices: a **form** (the shape of the piece), an **arc** (how the narrator feels about it), a **narrator** and a **length**. The form file and the arc file are loaded with this skill. The form decides the structure; where it differs from the spine below, the form wins.

## Non-negotiable

1. **Nothing invented that a reader would take as fact.** No statistic, study, expert, credential, review, rating, price, number of buyers or event ("my doctor said", "a lot of you asked") that isn't in the brand's material or the research. A fact the copy needs and you don't have is a square-bracket placeholder, listed in the notes.
2. **The before is the research.** The problem, the scenes, what was tried and the phrases come from the themes and quotes you were given.
3. **The after is what customers report.** What the narrator experiences with the product stays inside what real customers say and what the brand's claims allow. Never a result a guardrail rules out, never a medical outcome the brand can't claim, never a length of use longer than the product has been on sale.
4. **The reframe, the mechanism and the proof rest on the brand's claims.** A criticism of the usual fix is one the brand can support, about a kind of thing, never a named brand.
5. **A customer's words in quotation marks are word for word** from the research. The narrator's own sentences borrow customers' vocabulary without quotation marks.
6. **Understate.** No overnight change, no "all my problems are solved".
7. **No false urgency.** The offer, the price, the guarantee, the returns terms and any deadline are said as the brand's facts give them, or not at all.
8. Forbidden claims and guardrails hold in every field.

## The narrator

| Narrator | Use when | What keeps it honest |
|---|---|---|
| `customer`, a real one | The research holds 1 customer's own story or review | Their details and their words, nothing added to what they reported. What is missing is a placeholder. The notes say whose story it is and whether the brand has recorded their say; if not, no name is used and the notes flag it. |
| `customer`, a composite | There are real customer accounts and no single story to tell (the usual case) | 1st person, built only from what several customers report. An invented first name at most. The last line of the copy says so plainly: "This story is put together from what our customers have told us. It is not 1 person's account." |
| `founder` | The story of why it was made is true and in the brand's material | 1st person as the maker. Only real process facts. The founder's words are never given to a customer. |
| `expert` | The brand has a real, named professional who has agreed to put their name to it | 1st person as that professional. Name, title, years and credentials are placeholders unless the brand's material gives them. The notes say the text needs that person's approval. Never invent an expert. |
| `observer` | No customer accounts yet, the brand's rules forbid testimony, or the form needs a review or a fact the brand does not have | 2nd person, or 3rd person about a described kind of customer. No personal result is claimed. What the product does is said as the brand's claim or as "customers tell us", with real quotes word for word. |

**Whose voice.** The narrator is a person, not the brand. In a customer's story the brand's voice block gives you the buyer's own vocabulary (use it for the narrator), the guardrails on words (always) and what the brand calls its product. The brand's jokes, slogans and emoji are the brand's: a customer telling their story would not use them. A founder's story is in the brand's voice throughout. The voice block measures the brand's short ads: this piece has the length you were given and the shape of its form.

## Decide first

| Decision | How |
|---|---|
| Does a story suit this product | See "When it suits" in native-ads. If the buyer has no history with the problem, say so in the notes. |
| Lead tension, and the one underneath | From the themes: the most frequent and intense as the lead; the one that most often appears with it as the second layer |
| The old way | What this buyer did or bought before, as a kind of thing. For a product bought once: the ones they bought before. |
| Awareness | Problem aware in the usual case: they live with the problem, have tried things and have not found what fixes it. Unaware when they feel it and have not named it as something to solve. A long story is for these 2. A buyer who already knows the kind of answer and is comparing wants a shorter piece: say so in the notes. |
| How the narrator found it | Own research after everything failed; someone told them; stumbled on it. Pick what the arc suggests. |
| Aim | Tension-led unless the concept's persona is specific enough to open on (then the first lines carry 1 or 2 details of who the narrator is). |
| Where it leads | The product page (the copy names the product near the end) or an article (the copy sends them to read more and doesn't sell the product itself). |

Say these in the notes.

## The spine

Every form is built from these 8 movements. A form may reorder them, stretch one, or drop one.

| # | Movement (beat) | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening (hook) | Opens inside a specific moment, fact or admission the reader recognises. No product, no brand, no greeting. The first 2 or 3 lines must work alone above "See more". |
| 2 | The weight of it (problem) | Everything they tried or bought before, in order, with what each cost in money, time and hope. Then the quiet conclusion underneath: that it was their fault, or that this is just how it is. |
| 3 | The breaking point (problem) | 1 scene where carrying on as before stopped being possible. Small and exact: a photo, a receipt, the third one in the bin. |
| 4 | The reframe (mechanism) | Why nothing worked: 1 plain idea, said the way a friend would and with no technical word, that makes every failed attempt make sense. "I hadn't been failing. I'd been doing it out of sequence." "I wasn't cooking it wrong. The coating was always going to wear off." Still no product. |
| 5 | The bridge (bridge) | Finding something built on that idea, and exactly what is different about it. Scepticism stated. |
| 6 | First experience (proof) | The first use, small and specific. Then 1 real test. |
| 7 | What changed (promise) | Ordinary days, over the time customers really report: what they stopped doing, buying or working around, and what they can do again. "It isn't gone, but it's different." |
| 8 | The close (cta) | Speaks to the reader directly for the first time. Names their doubt, restates the reframe as theirs, and points to the link. The offer, the guarantee or the returns terms may be said once, in the narrator's words. |

Optional passages, used when the length and the form allow:

- **"I know what you're thinking."** Between 7 and 8: the reader's objection in their own words, answered by the reframe, not by a claim.
- **What's in it and why.** After 6: 3 to 5 lines, each a real ingredient, material or feature with what it does, from the brand's claims. The only place a short list is allowed.
- **Someone noticed.** After 7: another person remarking on the change, inside what customers report.
- **Sign-off and P.S.** A first name, then a P.S. that restates the result in 1 or 2 sentences, or answers "where do I get it". No P.S. chains.

## Length

How many words the piece runs is given with the request. What each length holds:

| Length | Holds |
|---|---|
| `brief` | The cold-traffic story only (forms/indirect-story.md): the question the image asked, the false belief, what failed, the real cause, a hint, 1 soft call to the page |
| `short` | Opening, 1 thing tried, the reframe in 2 sentences, first experience, result, close |
| `standard` | The full spine, 1 optional passage |
| `long` | The full spine with a second story inside it (a parent, a friend), every optional passage, a sign-off and P.S. |

A long piece is not a standard one padded: it has a second person's story or a longer journey in it. Long because more happens, never because a point is said twice.

## How it reads

- **Paragraphs of 2 to 5 whole sentences,** then a blank line. Each leads into the next, joined by "and", "but", "so" and "because". A short sentence only where the story turns. No rows of 1-line paragraphs, and no fragment on its own line.
- It is told the way a person tells a friend what happened. Never 4 very short sentences in a row. A phrase may be repeated once for weight ("I spent 17 years on creams, and 17 years hiding my hands."); never 3 lines in a row that open on the same word.
- Every sentence is one a person would say, in words a 13 year old knows. No technical term outside "what's in it", where each one comes with what it does.
- The reader's own words for the problem, from the quotes. What was tried is a kind of thing, never a rival's brand.
- **Scene details carry it:** the time on the clock, the colour of the jumper, the shelf it sat on. In a composite they may be composed, must fit the research, and are never a fact about the product, a price, a result, a professional or a number someone could check. In a real customer's story every detail is theirs.
- Numbers as people say them. Specific over round.
- The narrator talks about themselves until the close. Then they turn to "you".
- No headings, no bold, no bullet lists outside "what's in it". An emoji only where the brand's own ads use them, and then 1, before the link.
- The reading level given for this brand. Contractions. No em dashes.

## What to return

- `lines`: the movements in order, 1 line object each, with its beat. Each `text` holds that movement's paragraphs, separated by blank lines. `on_screen` and `visual_note` are empty: the image is made by another skill.
- `hook_variants`: as many as asked for. Each has `text` (an alternative first 2 to 3 lines that leads into the rest of the opening), `overlay` (the headline under the image: "Read this if..." naming something the reader does or a situation they are in, in their words; never a condition, a body or a trait they have) and `first_frame` (1 line on what an image could show: the moment with its place and time, or the object). Variant 1 is the copy's own opening.
- `title`: the tension, the form and the arc in a few words.
- `load_bearing_beat`: problem, unless the reframe carries it (then mechanism).
- `test_plan`: what this copy tests against its siblings.
- `notes`: the decisions; the narrator and, for a customer, whether real or composite; the word count; every placeholder; everything marked "inferred" because the brand's material was blank; and **image material** quoted from the copy: the moment, the object, and any words the narrator wrote or was sent (a note, a list, a reminder, a message), or "no written words in the story".
