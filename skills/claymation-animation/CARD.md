# Clay world: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narrated story played by a cast of clay characters in a whole miniature clay world. The look is the rendering; the story is the script's. Judged on: every frame reads as photographed physical clay, shot from inside the world; cast and product look the same in every clip; the picture claims nothing the narration could not.

## The look: 1 preset per ad

`visual_direction` opens with the preset's style wording, word for word.

- `cinematic` (the default): "Detailed cinematic claymation scene: hand-sculpted plasticine characters with an appealing idealized build and richly sculpted features, matte clay skin carrying thumb-worked smoothing and fine tool striations, chunky rounded clay hands with softly tapered fingertips and no realistic fingernails, real woven fabric clothing with stitched seams, staged on a physical miniature set and photographed with a macro lens at shallow depth of field with strong background bokeh, warm practical lighting with a soft rim light, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the miniature world." It closes: "No photographic human skin, no real human hands, no fingernails, no wrinkled realistic faces."
- `goofy`: "Goofy claymation scene: everything sculpted from plasticine with visible fingerprints and thumb smudges, wonky handmade geometry where nothing is perfectly straight, saturated teal and terracotta palette, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the clay world."
- `clean`: "Polished claymation scene: everything sculpted from smooth matte modeling clay, clean rounded forms with soft even surfaces and subtle tool marks, gently exaggerated cartoon proportions, refined handcrafted stop-motion feel, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the clay world." Simple dot eyes.
- `puppet`: "Stop-motion clay puppet scene: clay characters detailed with tiny craft materials: thread-wrapped hair, fabric details, wire accents, on a fully sculpted clay set, cinematic stop-motion film lighting with gentle shadows, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the puppet world."

## The cast, so it stays the same

- `character` describes the lead, and 1 other at most, each in full: adult age, build, clay skin colour, hair, clothes, then the preset's face wording.
- `cinematic`: "large almond eyes with prominent white sclera clearly visible around a deeply coloured iris, a dark sculpted upper lash line over sculpted upper and lower lids, thick sculpted brows above a defined brow ridge, defined cheekbones and a defined jaw, smooth unlined young clay skin, sculpted hair with deep comb grooves".
- `goofy`: "an oversized head, huge round googly cartoon eyes (white sclera, small dark pupils, thick sculpted lids), bold sculpted eyebrows, round blush-pink clay cheeks, a wide open-mouthed grin, hand-sculpted clay hair, neatly shaped with a few playful strands out of place".
- The full wording rides on every character in every `visual`: shortened, the face goes generic. Fictional adults: never a child, a real or known person, a customer, a founder or an expert. Nobody speaks.
- A feeling is face mechanics, never an adjective: "her inner brows pushed up and her mouth pulled flat".
- A crowd: "each one a distinct sculpted clay face with prominent white sclera and thick sculpted brows". A setting, not proof.

## What the camera, the set and the clay do

- `camera`: inside the miniature world, each shot at its own named angle and distance, most often a tight medium at eye level. Wide views go stiff and doll-like: keep them short. Never a set edge.
- `setting`: a whole clay place from the persona's moment, running past every edge.
- Name the clay on each of `props` and each thing in a `visual`: "a coiled clay towel". Glass, liquid, metal and flame are "sculpted as a professional clay imitation of" the real thing. Outside the fixed wording, never "smooth", "polished" or "glossy".
- Bright warm light and a named palette. No dusk, night or long shadows. A cold source is named, with "her warm clay skin holding its own warm colour against it".
- A mechanism only as the brand's facts give it. No body before and after.

## The product on screen

- First in `props`: sculpted in clay at the true shape and colours of its photo, with a real printed paper label. Its `look` states every line of label copy exactly, or it reads "CLAY".
- In the frame from the shot where the words reach it, in the lead's hands or in use, front to the lens. A shot of it alone keeps everything around it clay.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" with age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice. All `dialogue` is narration, over small set sounds.

## Movement and transitions

- Every clip's `visual_direction` carries the motion guard, word for word: "Handmade stop-motion claymation throughout: matte plasticine, visible fingerprints and tool marks, slightly stuttery 12fps stop-motion cadence, no motion blur. Non CGI. Non cartoon. Animation must start at the first frame." In `cinematic`, "fine tool striations" replaces "visible fingerprints and tool marks".
- 1 motion action to a shot: 1 gesture, or 1 camera move (a push in to a macro on the product, a pull back). Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.
- A hard cut may change the view or the place. World-bridge is the look's own way to a new place, kept for the turn of the story: the scene melts into a swirled plasticine pool while the product stays standing; it kneads and reshapes into the next; it rolls into a clay ball and unfolds as the next; clay coils and pellets roll in and assemble the product; a clay smear wipes the frame. It is a shot of its own; the shot after it describes the new scene in full.

## Shots this format cuts between

- A tight medium on the lead at eye level (the usual one).
- Clay hands handling the product, close.
- A macro on a texture or on the product's label.
- The other figure or the crowd reacting.
- A worm's-eye view from ankle height.

## First and last frame

- First: the lead mid-action in a tight medium.
- Last: the product front to the lens, the lead beside it, a calm top third for the offer line.

## Avoid

- 3 figures each acting: 1 acts, the rest hold.
- Signs, screens with text, letters anywhere but the product's label.

## After the render

- Clay is still clay: making-marks on every surface, no real skin or hands; stepped movement, the texture did not melt.
- Each face matches the first clip; no mouth formed the narrator's words; the label reads exactly as given.
