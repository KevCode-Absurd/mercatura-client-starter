# Clay world ad

A narrated story in a miniature world of clay, with a cast. A look and nothing more: the story is the script's. Not `skills/claymation-ads` (1 character, 1 tabletop set, 1 thing that changes, the real product never remade).

## What it is judged on

1. **Every frame reads as photographed physical clay,** shot from inside the miniature world.
2. **Each character and the product look the same in every shot,** the label words exact.
3. **The picture claims nothing the brand's facts do not.**

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, materials, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, clip lengths, how narration is written, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-clay-world`: how the story and the narration are written. This skill is the clay.

## 1. What makes anything read as clay (every look)

1. **Visible making-marks on every surface:** fingerprints and thumb smudges (`goofy`, `puppet`) or fine tool striations (`cinematic`, `clean`).
2. **Soft clay materiality:** a matte-to-satin plasticine sheen, slight warmth under the surface. Outside the fixed blocks, never write "smooth", "polished" or "glossy": they trigger the CGI drift.
3. **Clay imitations for hard materials.** Glass, liquid, metal and flame: write "sculpted as a professional clay imitation of [an amber glass dropper bottle / pouring water / a brass kettle]". 1 tested exception, in `cinematic` only: with a character in frame, real hard props (translucent plastic, metal, real liquid) look excellent. With no character they turn the picture into product photography.
4. **Handmade geometry:** wonky or refined by look, never factory-perfect.
5. **Real cast shadows,** lit like a real miniature set.
6. **Stop-motion cadence in motion:** stepped 12fps judder, no motion blur.

**The camera lives inside the miniature world:** the clay world runs past every edge of the frame and the camera sits inside it at a named angle ("camera at eye level, inside the scene"). A macro feel and shallow depth of field read as a stop-motion film set. Never show set edges, a workbench or the room around the set.

## 2. Choose 1 look for the whole ad

Its style block opens every still prompt, word for word. A look's eyes, proportions and hair are fixed; a character's face, outfit and colouring are free, held by its reference picture.

**`cinematic`** (the default): premium brands, hero product shots, story shots, crowds. Dense sets; a sage, cream and terracotta palette.

```
Detailed cinematic claymation scene: hand-sculpted plasticine characters with an appealing idealized build and richly sculpted features, matte clay skin carrying thumb-worked smoothing and fine tool striations, chunky rounded clay hands with softly tapered fingertips and no realistic fingernails, real woven fabric clothing with stitched seams, staged on a physical miniature set and photographed with a macro lens at shallow depth of field with strong background bokeh, warm practical lighting with a soft rim light, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the miniature world.
```

Character block, on every character in frame:

```
a hand-sculpted plasticine [adult man or woman] with an appealing idealized build and richly sculpted features: large almond eyes with prominent white sclera clearly visible around a deeply coloured iris, a dark sculpted upper lash line over sculpted upper and lower lids, thick sculpted brows above a defined brow ridge, defined cheekbones and a defined jaw, a neat sculpted nose, softly sculpted lips, smooth unlined young clay skin; matte clay skin showing thumb-worked smoothing and fine tool striations, sculpted [hair] with deep comb grooves, wearing [real woven garment] with stitched seams
```

Physicality block, closing the prompt:

```
Everything in frame is hand-sculpted plasticine and real fabric, photographed on a physical miniature set: visible tool striations and thumb-worked clay surfaces, woven fabric weave, real cast shadows, the miniature scene filling the entire frame. No photographic human skin, no real human hands, no fingernails, no wrinkled realistic faces.
```

**`goofy`**: playful brands, comic shots, high-energy hooks. Cluttered sets.

```
Goofy claymation scene: everything sculpted from plasticine with visible fingerprints and thumb smudges, wonky handmade geometry where nothing is perfectly straight, saturated teal and terracotta palette, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the clay world.
```

```
a goofy plasticine [adult man or woman] with an oversized head, huge round googly cartoon eyes (white sclera, small dark pupils, thick sculpted lids), bold sculpted eyebrows, round blush-pink clay cheeks, a wide open-mouthed grin, and hand-sculpted clay hair, neatly shaped with a few playful strands out of place
```

**`clean`**: minimal brands, calm explainers, a product that out-details the world. Dot eyes.

```
Polished claymation scene: everything sculpted from smooth matte modeling clay, clean rounded forms with soft even surfaces and subtle tool marks, gently exaggerated cartoon proportions, refined handcrafted stop-motion feel, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the clay world.
```

**`puppet`**: story-led ads, darker tones. Deep teal shadows, warm amber light.

```
Stop-motion clay puppet scene: clay characters detailed with tiny craft materials: thread-wrapped hair, fabric details, wire accents, on a fully sculpted clay set, cinematic stop-motion film lighting with gentle shadows, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the puppet world.
```

## 3. Write each still

Style block, the scene, then (in `cinematic`) the physicality block. Never "same style as before".

- **The full character wording rides on every character in frame,** however long the prompt gets: shortened, the faces go generic.
- **Emotion is face mechanics, never an adjective:** "his cheek squashed hard against his palm so it deforms, eyelids heavy and half-lowered".
- **Bright, warm light.** The look dies in low-key, dusk and long-shadow light. A cold source is kept, with the skin pinned against it: "her warm terracotta clay skin holds its own warm colour against it".
- **Weave the clay into every element** ("clay plants with thumb-pressed leaves") and name a palette, saturated unless the look says otherwise.
- **The product:** sculpted in clay, at the true shape and colours of its tagged photo, with a real printed paper label. State every line of label copy exactly, or the material's name leaks into the text ("CLAY").
- **State identity:** age, build and colouring are dice-rolled when unstated. Each character is a fictional adult (never a child, a real or known person, a customer, a founder or an expert), made once as its own picture and attached to every shot.
- **A mechanism only as the brand's `How it works` gives it;** no body before and after.

## 4. Shot types

- **Tight character medium:** native to `cinematic`, strong background blur. Pulled wide it goes stiff and doll-like.
- **Macro texture close-up; fingerprint insert; worm's-eye miniature** (clay-ankle height); **googly-eye extreme close-up** (`goofy`).
- **Clay-hands insert:** chunky rounded clay fingers handling the product, "sculpted like a clay figurine's hands".
- **Crowd scene** (a train carriage, an audience): "each one a distinct sculpted clay face with prominent white sclera and thick sculpted brows", or the background turns to mush.

## 5. Stills, then clips

Approve each still before its clip. Unnamed, the cadence is smoothed and the clay vanishes. Every clip prompt, transitions included, carries the motion guard:

```
Handmade stop-motion claymation throughout: matte plasticine, visible fingerprints and tool marks, slightly stuttery 12fps stop-motion cadence, no motion blur. Non CGI. Non cartoon. Animation must start at the first frame.
```

In `cinematic`, "fine tool striations" replaces "visible fingerprints and tool marks". 1 motion action per clip: 1 gesture, 1 camera move.

## 6. Clay transitions

Build a first and a last frame as a pair: 1 shared anchor from the same references, 1 action, a matching composition, a stated fate for everything that leaves.

- **Camera-bridge** (anchor fixed, camera moves): a push in from a mid shot to a macro on the product; a pull back into a new scene.
- **World-bridge** (camera fixed, clay transforms around the anchor): the scene melts into a swirled plasticine pool while the product stays standing; it kneads and reshapes into the next; it rolls into a clay ball and unfolds as the next; clay coils and pellets roll in and assemble the product; a clay smear wipes the frame; a clay blob travels the whole ad, scene to scene.

The clip prompt names the 1 visible action that carries frame A into frame B (a camera move alone is no bridge when the subject also changes), plus the motion guard, and nothing else.

## 7. What fails, and the fix

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Drifts to a CGI render | No "smooth", "polished", "glossy"; physicality block word for word; rerun before rewriting |
| Characters generic | Write the character wording out per character |
| The emotion does not show | Face mechanics: brows, lids, mouth, where the cheek deforms |
| Clay skin blue or stained | Pin the skin's own colour against the cold source |
| Faces collapse to dot eyes | The character block's eye line, in full |
| Goofy hair goes wild | "neatly shaped with a few playful strands out of place" |
| Real human hands | "Sculpted like a clay figurine's: thick rounded fingers with plain blunt tips and no fingernails at all" |
| Glass or liquid renders real | "Sculpted as a professional clay imitation of ..." |
| Object-only shot stops reading as clay | Everything clay, real printed label only |
| Label reads as nonsense | State the label copy explicitly |
| A character differs between shots | Make it once; attach that picture as a reference |
| Transition cuts instead of morphing | Rebuild the pair: same anchor, 1 action, like composition |
| Texture melts mid-clip | Motion guard in every clip prompt |
