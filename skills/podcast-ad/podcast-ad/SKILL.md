# Podcast clip ad

2 people at microphones, caught mid-conversation. 1 asks and doubts, 1 explains. The product is on the table.

It is an ad in the style of a podcast clip. It names no show, copies no real show's set or hosts, and never passes its 2 speakers off as real people.

## What it is judged on

1. **The first line leaves a question a stranger wants answered.** That is all that holds them past second 3.
2. **The 2 stay 2 different people, on the same sides, from the first frame to the last.** A swapped seat or a changed face ends it.
3. **It sounds like talk across a table.** A line that reads like copy is cut.
4. **Every line is one its speaker can honestly say.** See "What each may say".

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, sound, people, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the video model and the image model take, how speech is written for them, the words a filter refuses, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-podcast`: how the words are written. This skill is how they are made into clips.

## 1. Before anything is made

Read the brand brain (the fields are listed at the end). Then settle 4 things with the person, proposing an answer to each:

1. **The 1 argument.** A problem said out loud, or a belief to overturn. From the avatar's `What almost stops them`, the `Ranked insight bank`, or `Believes`.
2. **The structure.** 1 of the 4 in `script-format-podcast`: problem then solution, discovery, myth-bust, what's on the table.
3. **The length.** 1 clip of 15 seconds, or 2 to 4 clips of 10 to 15 seconds. More than 45 seconds is another format.
4. **The 2 people.** A saved cast from `Generated cast` if the brand has one. If not, 2 new ones, described below.

## 2. What each may say

Both are fictional adults. Neither is the founder, a customer, a doctor or any expert, and nothing in the picture says otherwise (no white coat, no lanyard, no book with a name on it).

A generated speaker may say:
- a question, a doubt, a reaction;
- a fact from the brand's facts, said as a fact about the product ("it's 5 layers of steel", "there's no melatonin in it"), not as their own experience;
- what customers say, when the research holds it: attributed ("the reviews keep saying") and word for word when quoted;
- the problem, as an observation or a question ("why does every one of those die in a year?"). A customer's first-person words are attributed or turned into a question, never given to a speaker as their own story;
- at the very end, what they will do next. An intention is not a result.

Neither may say: a result they had, how long they have used it, that they tested or measured it, a job or title, a study or number that is not in the brand's facts, anything about other products that the brand's facts do not state, a show's name.

## 3. Words per clip

A clip is 1 exchange. The video model speaks 1 person at a time, so a turn is a beat of its own.

| Clip | Turns |
|---|---|
| 10 seconds | 2 or 3 |
| 15 seconds | 3 |

The words a clip holds follow how fast the 2 of them talk, and that follows their age.

- A turn is 1 or 2 sentences and takes 2 to 6 seconds. The answer is the longest turn. A reaction is 3 to 6 words.
- A clip ends at the end of a turn, on the listener's face. No sentence runs across 2 clips.
- More words than a clip holds and the last line is cut off. A beat that needs more is another clip.

How to make the lines sound overheard without adding words: `references/naturalization-guide.md`.

## 4. The cast

2 people a viewer could never mix up. Two alike descriptions come back as twins.

- They differ in at least 4 of: age, build, skin tone, hair (length, colour, shape), facial hair or glasses, the colour of the top.
- Both could be the buyer's peers (age and style from the avatar). Neither looks like a model.
- Plain clothes: no words, logos or prints. Printed clothing comes back as scrambled letters.
- Write each person once, in fixed words (the **cast lines**), and use those words unchanged in every prompt.

For visible hand gestures or permitted object interactions, use [Hands and accessories](../../production-principles/references/hands-and-accessories.md): pose, visible anatomy, nails, object/accessory and contact/light. Name supporting fingers and legitimate occlusion; preserve joint/wrist connectivity, nail beds/cuticles, approved nail shape/color/finish and accessory count/position. A grip need not reveal all five digits. Keep scale, overlap, contact shadows and curved reflections plausible through gestures/cuts. This does not add a manicure, accessory or product-handling action the format/brand did not approve.

```
HOST (left of frame): [age], [build], [skin tone], [hair], [glasses or facial hair or none], [top: garment and colour], [1 mark the Guest does not have].
GUEST (right of frame): the same 7 things, each different from the Host.
```

## 5. The room and the frame

- **1 quiet room in the brand's world** (`Brand world`: places, surfaces, light): 1 table, 1 plain wall, 1 lamp. No sign, no show name, no shelf of book spines, no screen, no window with movement. Every word on a set comes back misspelt.
- **1 frame for the whole ad.** Both behind 1 table, seen from the chest up, turned about 45 degrees toward each other. Host on the left, Guest on the right. Both faces whole.
- **Each has 1 microphone, its head below the chin and to the outer side.** A microphone in front of a mouth hides who is speaking.
- **The camera is on a stand and does not move.** A studio camera does not sway. Punch-ins are done in editing.
- They look at each other, never into the lens.
- In a tall frame, faces sit in the upper middle and the table with the product in the lower middle, above the band where the feed's own buttons sit.

A second angle is possible by hand only: `references/clip-prompt.md`, section F.

## 6. The product

- It stands on the table between them from the first frame to the last, nearer the lens than the microphones, its front to the lens, from its own tagged photo (`Reference photos`: pack front).
- Say its height against something on the table ("as tall as the glass"), from `Looks like`.
- No hand crosses it. Nobody lifts it to the lens.
- **It is picked up only when** it is small enough for 1 hand, `How it is used` and the lessons say how it is held and opened, and the structure needs it. Then that is a beat of its own, with 8 words or fewer spoken over it.
- **A garment** hangs front on from a hook on the wall between them. Neither wears it: wearing it says they own it. **Something too big for a table** (a suitcase, a chair) stands on the floor at the table's end, in view from the first frame, with the frame a little wider.
- Nothing comes out of a bag or a pocket. An object fetched from out of view is invented on the spot.
- Cutaways to the product in use are the brand's own footage or clips made for this ad. Never footage from someone else's channel.

## 7. Stills first

1. **The cast picture.** The 2 together, waist up, in their clothes, against a plain wall. Look at it: 2 different people, plain clothes, nobody a real person would be mistaken for. Approve it or change the cast lines.
2. **The opening frame.** Made from the cast picture and the product's photo, each tagged with its job: the room, both seated, microphones, the product, at the ad's own aspect. The speaker's mouth is already open and a hand already raised; the listener is already reacting. Approve it.
3. Only then, clips.

**A 2-by-2 grid is not given to the video model.** It takes 1 view per picture, each tagged with its job, and a sheet of several views is advised against (`models.md`). What the grid held is still wanted, as separate pictures: the 2 together, the room with both seated, and (only for a second angle) a close view of each. For the image model a sheet of views is fine as the cast picture.

Realism (uneven faces, real cloth, no beauty filter) is asked for in the stills. The clip prompt does not describe skin: several such words are refused by the video model's filter (`models.md`).

For the cast and opening stills, use [Skin detail craft](../../production-principles/references/skin-detail-craft.md): zone → one or two local details → intensity → seated camera distance and room light. Keep forehead/cheek texture soft, pores slightly clearer at the nose, eye/lip lines delicate, brow hairs varied and jaw texture subtle. Detail stays understated in the two-shot and becomes clearer only where a close single can capture it. Preserve each person's approved age, complexion, marks and makeup, plus the fixed-camera setup; do not turn the podcast into a phone selfie or macro study. Motion preserves the approved appearance using compatible wording, with review through speech, blinks and cuts rather than skin-detail keyword lists.

For each cast member, describe complexion depth, undertone, adult age and one or two local variations independently. A complexion or age is not a preset wrinkle/pore pattern. Preserve highlight/shadow detail and each approved person's tonal depth; do not wash out, gray, over-age or rejuvenate either speaker. Diagnose exposure/light response before recasting their complexion. Review speech, reactions and close singles for drifting color, marks or age; actual illumination may change naturally while the person remains consistent.

For any approved makeup, choose coverage, finish and placement independently. Retain subtle skin relief and selected details plausible beneath the base at seated camera distance, not every mark equally. Blend foundation and local concealer/blush/highlight; keep lips dimensional and shine aligned with the room's light. Keep each person's makeup through speech, reaction singles and cuts without smooth masks, powdery patches or frozen reflections. Preserve approved styling; bare skin or reduced coverage is not the default repair. The makeup section of [Skin detail craft](../../production-principles/references/skin-detail-craft.md) supplies refinements without imposing phone-selfie framing.

For a skin fault in a cast/opening still, name the affected person, facial area and symptom, then use [controlled repair](../../production-principles/references/skin-detail-craft.md). Recover subtle relief for waxy skin, lower dark pore contrast or vary repetition separately, or control clipped shine while keeping gentle sheen. Change one clause; keep both approved identities, age, complexion, marks, makeup, gaze/expression, pose, seated crop, fixed camera, room light and product/text intact. Compare that same face/area at matched scale, record the change and revert ineffective edits within render limits. Never turn the two-shot into a selfie/macro or flatten approved dewy styling; a repaired still does not validate speech or motion.

For each approved clip, piercing or tattoo, name position, attachment, overlap, contact and material response using the physical-contact section of [Skin detail craft](../../production-principles/references/skin-detail-craft.md). A worn clip grips a lock with strand overlap, a stud follows its approved lower-lip contour, and matte ink follows skin without a raised edge or object shadow. Keep count/design/scale and body-relative position through dialogue, reactions and occlusion; highlights follow the actual light. Preserve the podcast's framing/camera and each cast member's styling rather than adding tutorial accessories or macro demonstrations.

## 8. The clips

Templates: `references/clip-prompt.md`.

- Clip 1 starts on the approved opening frame. Each later clip is asked for as an extension of the clip before it, which is what holds the framing (`models.md`), and is asked to keep the 2 voices.
- Each prompt holds: the references and their jobs; the room and the frame in 2 lines; the cast lines; the turns in order, each with its speaker, its tone in 1 or 2 words and 1 visible reaction from the listener; the frame it ends on; the sound.
- **Sound:** 2 voices that differ in pitch and pace, close to the microphones, a little room tone. No music, no laughter track, no intro sting. Say so.
- **1 speaker at a time.** No "mm-hm" under a line and no talking over each other. The listener's reaction is seen, not heard.
- Small movement only: a hand opening on the table, a head tilt, leaning in. No laughing fit, no standing up, no turning to the lens.
- Tie the gesture to a phrase and the listener's reaction to what they just learned: a narrowed look at the doubt, a small brow lift at the explanation, a glance at the product when it becomes relevant. Let that reaction settle instead of looping nods, smiling constantly or waving through every line. Keep breathing and blinks independent; normal articulation does not need exaggerated mouth opening. At a cut, preserve the half-finished gesture and the degree of doubt or amusement; a wider shot is not an emotional reset.
- If a turn finishes before its beat, let a brief silent, live reaction carry the remaining moment; do not slow the words, add filler or animate the listener speaking. If there is no useful remaining beat, use supported trimming while preserving whole words and the next turn.
- The spoken lines ride in the prompt. A line with a word the filter refuses (`models.md`) is reworded before the clip is asked for, not after it is refused.
- The offer and the call to action are never spoken. They are on-screen text on the last clip, added in editing.

## 9. The check after each clip

On top of the shared check:

1. Each line came from the right mouth, and only that mouth moved.
2. Sides, clothes, hair and microphones match the clip before. Nobody new is in the room.
3. The 2 voices are still 2 voices, and the same 2 as in the clip before.
4. Every word was said as written. The last line was not rushed or cut off.
5. The product's printing is unchanged and nothing passed in front of it.

A voice that has drifted needs a diagnosed repair: remake the affected clip with a supported continuity route, or mend the smallest coherent turn through supported independent audio controls. Recheck both voices, joins and lip timing in the full edit; an extension request alone does not prove voice preservation. This format does not take a voice from a real person or another ad. [Audio direction](../../production-principles/references/audio-direction.md) covers route selection, phrase repair, editable layers and headphone/phone listening while retaining these cast/voice and music rules.

For appearance stability, read [Portrait realism](../../production-principles/references/portrait-realism.md). Local texture/marks travel with each face through mouth/cheek movement, blinks, turns and occlusion/reappearance; check the whole clip for crawl/flicker, contrast pumping, multiplied marks or sudden smoothing. Allow normal perspective, blur and shape-dependent shading, not screen-fixed texture. Keep fixed podcast cameras and approved dialogue/gaze; the lesson's handheld selfie, five-second duration and three-action studies are not podcast defaults. A still cast/opening frame or separate posed states cannot validate actual moving skin. Correct one diagnosed cause within existing retry limits.

Finishing (joining, captions, punch-ins, music): `references/editing-recipe.md`.
