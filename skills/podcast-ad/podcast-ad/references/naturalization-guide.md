# Making 2 people sound overheard

A clip fails when it sounds like copy read by 2 voices. The fix is in 3 places: the words, what the listener's face does, and the timing. The facts in the lines never change.

## 1. The words

Done when the script is written (`skills/script-format-podcast`), checked again here.

- 1 asks and doubts, 1 explains. If both agree from the first line there is nothing to wait for.
- Contractions. A false start or a restart, once in the ad, not once a line ("It's, okay, it's not the coating").
- The listener's reply picks up a word the speaker just used ("5 layers?").
- A doubt is a real one, from the avatar's `What almost stops them`, in the buyer's own words.
- No line names a benefit list. 1 fact to a turn.
- Filler ("like", "honestly", "you know") costs words from a budget of 24 a clip. At most 1 in the whole ad.

| Reads like copy | Sounds like talk |
|---|---|
| "It contains 300 milligrams of magnesium glycinate and no melatonin." | "There's no melatonin in it. It's magnesium. That's it." |
| "The 5-ply construction distributes heat evenly across the surface." | "It's 5 layers of steel. So the edge is as hot as the middle." |
| "That sounds amazing, I need to try it!" | "Hang on. No coating at all?" |

## 2. The listener

The listener is half the picture. For each turn write 1 thing the listener's face or body does, as something seen:

- eyebrows up, then a slow nod
- head tilts, eyes narrow a little
- leans in 5 cm, forearms on the table
- sits back, mouth pressed shut, half a smile
- glances down at the product and back up

The listener's mouth stays closed while the other speaks. A reaction is never a sound under the line: the video model speaks 1 person at a time, and 2 voices at once come back garbled.

Choose the reaction because of the line's meaning: doubt narrows the eyes, an unexpected explanation lifts a brow, the product mention earns a glance at it. A reaction forms and settles; it does not repeat through the turn. Keep a half-finished gesture and the same degree of amusement or doubt across an angle cut. Avoid synchronized blinks, constant smiling, looping nods and exaggerated articulation.

The speaker's hands stay low: 1 hand opens on the table or lifts a little. No pointing at the lens, no counting on fingers, no hand near the product.

## 3. The timing

- A reply comes straight after the line before it. Thinking is shown by the listener's reaction finishing ("nods once, then answers"), never by a silence.
- The longest turn is 6 seconds. Past that the other person must visibly react.
- A clip ends at the end of a turn, on the listener's face. Only the last shot of the ad has both settled.
- The tone of each turn is 1 or 2 plain words: "doubtful", "dry", "quick, amused". Not a paragraph.
- If a line finishes early, a brief live silent reaction may complete the beat. Do not stretch its words or add filler to fill the duration. Trim an unneeded tail only through available editing controls, preserving whole words and the following turn.

## What is not added

- No "mm-hm", "right", "no way" laid under the other person's line.
- No camera sway, focus pulls or exposure shifts. The camera is on a stand.
- No paragraphs of body language, audio and pacing per clip. The model follows 1 action per beat; a long prompt is cut or ignored.
- No new words that change a fact, add a claim or turn a doubt into a testimonial.

## Worked example, 1 clip of 10 seconds (a pan)

Script lines:

> Host: Why does every non-stick pan die in a year?
> Guest: Because the coating's the weak bit. This one hasn't got one. It's 5 layers of steel.
> Host: Hang on. No coating at all?

23 words, 3 turns. As beats:

> [0-3s] The Host speaks, left hand open on the table. The Guest listens, mouth closed, a small smile starting. Tone: fed up.
> [3-8s] The Guest answers, tilting the head toward the pan on "this one", hands flat on the table. The Host listens, mouth closed, eyebrows going up. Tone: dry, sure.
> [8-10s] The Host leans in and looks down at the pan. The Guest listens, mouth closed. Tone: doubtful.
> Ends on: the Host looking at the pan, the Guest looking at the Host, both still.

The same clip for a drink powder changes only the facts and the object:

> Host: Okay, what's in it? Because I'm not taking melatonin.
> Guest: There's none in it. It's magnesium, 300 milligrams a scoop. That's the whole idea.
> Host: Huh. That's it?

In both, the Host's first line is the buyer's own complaint from the `Customer language bank`, every fact is one the brand's facts hold, and neither speaker says they use the product.
