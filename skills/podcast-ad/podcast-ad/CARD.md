# Podcast clip: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

2 fictional adults at microphones, mid-conversation, in 1 room: a Host who asks and doubts, a Guest who explains. It is an ad in the style of a podcast clip. It names no show and passes for no real one. It is judged on:

1. The first line leaves a question a stranger wants answered.
2. The 2 stay 2 different people, on the same sides, from the first frame to the last.
3. It sounds like talk across a table, not copy read aloud.
4. Every line is one its speaker can honestly say.

## Structure in seconds

- A clip is 1 exchange of 2 or 3 turns, cut as shots. 1 speaker to a line; every `dialogue` starts with `Host:` or `Guest:`. A cut falls between turns or between words, never inside a word.
- Clip 1 opens mid-conversation on the hook, a claim or a question, inside the first 3 seconds. No welcome, no names.
- A turn is 1 or 2 sentences and takes 2 to 6 seconds. The answer is the longest turn; a reaction is 3 to 6 words.
- A clip ends at the end of a turn, on the listener's face, so the next clip opens on the reply.
- Middle clips: the doubt, then the 1 reason. Last clip: the product named, then the Host's closing turn. 1 argument in the whole ad.
- The offer and the call to action are never spoken. They are the `overlay` of the last scene.

## The camera and the 2 people

- Fix approved cast/product/words/sides, action/gaze, room, camera/light and style; name any bounded open choice. Describe visible behavior, not just “natural.” Movement/sound use actual controls. Before rerolling, check missing direction, reference conflicts, feasibility and output compliance; keep this format's rules and retry limits.
- Cameras on stands, level with their eyes. Each shot is held steady; the cut does the moving.
- The wide shot: both behind 1 table, seen from the chest up, turned about 45 degrees toward each other, Host on the left, Guest on the right. In a single the Host still faces right and the Guest left: the sides never swap.
- Each has 1 microphone, its head below the chin and to the outer side. A microphone in front of a mouth hides the speech.
- They look at each other. A glance down at the product is allowed; a look into the lens is not.
- In each shot the speaker's hands stay low (1 hand opens on the table, a small tilt of the head) and the listener does 1 visible thing: a nod, eyebrows up, leaning in. The listener's mouth stays closed. A reaction is seen, never heard: no "mm-hm" under a line, no 2 voices at once.
- Tie gestures to a phrase and reactions to its meaning; let them settle, without constant smiles, nods, waving or over-articulation. Carry the same unfinished gesture/emotion across cuts. Spare time is a brief live silent reaction or supported trim, never stretched speech or added words.
- Visible hands keep connected joints/wrists, plausible occlusion and nail beds/cuticles. Keep approved nail shape/color/finish and accessory count/position. Any permitted object grip names supporting fingers, overlap/contact and true scale; reflections follow the source. Do not force five exposed digits into a grip or add product handling this format does not allow. Check contact through gestures/cuts.
- `start` says who is mid-word and what the other's face is doing. Only the last shot of the ad has both settled, mouths closed.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The 2 at the table, wide.
- A single on whoever speaks, chest up.
- The listener reacting, close, while the other's voice runs on.
- Hands on the table, or the product, close.
- Over 1 shoulder at the other.

## The cast, so it stays the same

- Scene/crop/camera/light first, then local skin and relevant approved details. Inspect full/zoom, recheck the whole after a local fix and approve the still before motion. These are decisions, not required extra renders; changed accessory designs are variants to resolve, not preservation proof.
- 2 people a viewer could never mix up. They differ in at least 4 of: age, build, skin tone, hair (length, colour, shape), facial hair or glasses, the colour of the top.
- The Host is the presenter the brand context gives (the buyer's peer). The Guest is made to contrast. Neither looks like a model.
- In cast/opening stills, skin follows zone/crop/light: subtle forehead/cheeks, slightly clearer nose pores, fine eye/lip lines, varied brow hairs and gentle jaw texture. Two-shot detail stays delicate; close singles reveal more. Keep each approved face, age and marks; no uniform dots, beauty smoothing or added creases. Preserve appearance through speech/blinks with compatible video wording, not a macro texture checklist. Keep the fixed cameras and room lighting.
- Choose each person's complexion, undertone, adult age and local variation separately. Preserve both identities and tonal depth in highlights/shadows; no washed-out/gray skin, exaggerated aging or unwanted rejuvenation. Age/complexion do not set pore or wrinkle quantity. Fix exposure before changing the cast; keep color/marks consistent through reactions and cuts while light behaves naturally.
- Makeup, if approved: choose coverage/finish/placement separately, retaining subtle relief and only details plausible through that base at seated distance. Blend edges and localize shine to the actual light; no smooth mask or powdery patches. Keep each person's styling through speech/cuts; do not impose bare skin or a selfie look.
- Approved contact details: name position/attachment/overlap/contact/material response. A clip grips a lock; a stud follows its contour; matte tattoo lines follow skin with no raised edge. Keep count/design/scale and body-relative location through speech, reactions and occlusion. Reflections move naturally; add no accessory or macro insert solely from an example.
- Plain clothes: no words, logos, prints or badges. Nothing that lends authority: no white coat, scrubs, lanyard, stethoscope, or book with a name on it.
- The plan has 1 `presenter`. Write each of its fields for both people, Host first: "Host: ...; Guest: ...". `features` gives each 1 mark the other does not have.
- `setting`: 1 quiet room in the brand's world: 1 table, 1 plain wall, 1 lamp. No sign, no show name, no shelf of book spines, no screen, no window with movement.

## The product on screen

- `handling` is display. It comes in when the words reach it, in a close shot that it fills: standing on the table, its front to the lens. From then it stands between them in the wide shot, nearer the lens than the microphones.
- Its `look` gives its height against something on the table ("as tall as the glass").
- No hand crosses it, nothing stands in front of it, nobody lifts it to the lens.
- A garment hangs front on from a hook on the wall between them, below the top band; neither wears it. Something too big for a table stands on the floor at the table's end, and the wide shot is a little wider.
- It is picked up only when the brand context says hold or use and the lessons say how. Then it is a scene of its own.

## Sound and speech

- Keep exact words and each voice's identity. Mend a weak turn only through supported controls; listen to joins and recheck lips. Review the full mix on headphones and phone speakers, with room tone below speech. Voice samples do not establish cloning or conversion support.

- `voice_direction` gives 2 voices that differ in pitch and pace: "Host: ...; Guest: ...", in the accent of the brand's buyers.
- A quiet room: voices close to the microphones, a little room tone. No intro sting, no laughter track.
- The brand name is said once or twice, late, the way a person says it.

## What each may say

The script was written to this. Keep to it when cutting, and add nothing that breaks it.

- A question, a doubt, a reaction; a fact from the brand's facts, said about the product; what customers say, attributed; the problem as an observation or a question; at the end, what the Host will do next.
- Never a result they had, how long they have used it, that they tested it, a job or a title, anything about other products that the brand's facts do not state, a show's name. A customer's first-person words are attributed or turned into a question, never given to a speaker as their own story. A line cut for this reason is named in `notes`.

## First and last frame

- First: the speaker's mouth already open and 1 hand already raised, the listener already reacting. It reads as "2 people at microphones" at the size of a thumb. The `overlay` of scene 1 is the hook in 6 words or fewer, for a viewer with the sound off.
- Last: both settled, the Host looking at the product, the product's front clear, room above it for the offer line.

## Avoid

- 2 shots in a row of the same size from the same place, or a cut that swaps their sides.
- 2 people who agree throughout. 1 doubts until the reason is given.
- A Guest who is introduced, named, or given a profession.

## After the render

- Skin fault: name person/area and waxy smoothing, repeated pores or clipped shine. Change 1 local cue, keeping identities, marks, makeup, expression/crop/camera/light and product/text fixed; retain gentle sheen. Compare the same area/scale, record change/result; use existing retry limits.
- Each line came from the right mouth, and only that mouth moved.
- Sides, clothes, hair and microphones match in every shot. Nobody new is in the room.
- Each face keeps its features, complexion and face-attached texture through speech, blinks, turns and occlusion/reappearance; no crawl/flicker, contrast pumping, multiplied marks or sudden smoothing. Allow natural perspective, blur and shading; still states alone prove no motion continuity.
- The 2 voices are still 2 voices, and the same 2 as before.
- Every word was said as written; the last line was not rushed or cut off.
- The product's printing is unchanged and nothing passed in front of it.
