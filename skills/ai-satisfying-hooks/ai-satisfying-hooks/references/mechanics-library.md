# Mechanics: motions that resolve

A satisfying hook is 1 motion with a visible end. The viewer stays to see it get there, and that is the whole job: the longer they stay, the more of the ad body is seen.

So the motion is chosen first and the camera second. A camera move on its own (an orbit, a glide, a pull-back) has no end to wait for, and the model does little with it: an orbit asked for in words came back barely moving (`skills/production-principles/models.md`).

## How to choose

1. **Start from what the product really does.** `Moves and sounds` and `How it is used` in the brain list its real motions. A motion nobody has described is a motion the model makes up.
2. **Choose the object in focus.** In this order: the product itself out of its pack; something it is made of (an ingredient, a material); the thing it replaces (the row of bottles, the stained pan). Never the sealed pack turning on a stand.
3. **Choose the mechanic whose end a stranger would call "done" without being told.** Full to the brim. Shut. Flat. Gone. In a row.
4. **Keep the end in doubt until late.** The glass that is full by second 1 leaves 3 seconds of nothing. The end comes in the last third of the clip.
5. **Check it could be filmed.** 1 take, real speed, the length of the clip, everything in view at the start.

Each entry gives: what moves, what "done" looks like, the sound of the end, and an example for a consumable and a durable. The examples are examples: use the brand's own product and its own words.

## A. What is inside comes out

| # | Mechanic | What moves, and what "done" looks like | Sound of the end | Consumable / durable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pour and settle | Contents leave the product's own opening in 1 stream and come to rest | The last piece landing | Granola into a bowl / screws from a case into a tray |
| 2 | Fill to the brim | A container fills and stops exactly at its rim or line | The stream cutting off | Powder and water rising to the line of a glass / a bottle filled to its neck |
| 3 | Scoop and level | 1 clean scoop from a smooth surface, or a heaped scoop levelled flat | The scrape of the edge | A scoop of collagen levelled with a knife / wax lifted from a tin |
| 4 | Spread | A smooth layer goes edge to edge in 1 pass | Soft drag, then none | A cream across the back of a hand / oil across a board |
| 5 | Pump or squeeze out | 1 push of the pump gives 1 dose that holds its shape | The pump's return | A curl of serum on a glass tile / glue in 1 bead along a seam |
| 6 | Dissolve or bloom | Something added reaches its finished state: clear, frothed, steeped | Fizz dying away | A tablet fizzing to clear / a filter bag colouring the water |
| 7 | Drop and settle | 1 thing falls in and comes to rest | The landing | Ice into a drink / a ring into its box |

## E. When nothing moves

The weakest group: there is no end to wait for. Use 1 only when the product makes no motion at all, and keep it to the shortest clip.

## The camera

The camera serves the motion. It does 1 of these and nothing else, for the whole clip.

- **Locked, close.** The default. The motion does the work.
- **Top down, locked.** For anything that fills, sorts, spreads or fits.
- **A slow push in.** Ends when the motion ends.
- **A small arc.** State the start side and the end side. Expect less movement than asked for.
- **From inside, looking out.** Inside the bowl, the bag, the box. It is set by the opening still and never by words: asked for in words it came back as a side view.
- **Pull back to show the place.** Only when the last frame has to show where the ad body happens.

Wide and aerial moves (a drone lifting, a glide along a path) belong to a product that lives outdoors at that scale: a tent, a bike, a print of a landscape. For a product that sits on a counter they show a room, not the product.

## Variety across a batch

A batch is 2 or 3. They differ by mechanic, and by object in focus where the product allows (the product, what it is made of, what it replaces), not by wording. 2 pours in 2 lights is 1 hook made twice.
