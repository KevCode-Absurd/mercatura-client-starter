# Claymation as a video: a narrated story in clips

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger can say what changed and what changed it.

## How to write for it

- **Narration, not dialogue.** 1 narrator tells the story over the pictures. The character doesn't speak to camera and its mouth doesn't form words. Write every spoken line as narration.
- **Person:** 3rd person about 1 named or unnamed character ("She tried the oil. Then the capsules.") or 2nd person. 1st-person narration works only as a character's voice.
- **Story shapes that work (options, not an order):** a failed-solutions journey, a discovery, a transformation with a witness, or a confession. The character needs stakes: something risked, lost or embarrassing.
- **Problem:** this format can hold a long problem. In a 60-second script give it up to 20 seconds. Escalate toward 1 consequence; don't list symptoms.
- **The thing that changes:** promise first, then show 1 thing acting on another. Write its 3 states in the visual notes.
- **The product's arrival:** inside the story, as what the character found. It comes into the picture when the words reach it, within the character's reach.
- **One close that works:** offer and call to action as narration over the character with the product, then 1 identity line.

## Length

30 to 60 seconds, 90 at most. The extra time goes to the problem and to the thing that changes. Under 30 seconds, drop the story and write problem, mechanism, offer.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- 1 character, 1 small set, 1 product throughout. Describe them the same way every time.
- The thing that changes and any failed fixes are on the set from the first shot. Nothing arrives from out of view inside a shot; things on the set change shape, size and colour instead.
- Something is already moving in the first shot.
- 1 clear action per shot. Clay moves in steps, 1 limb or 1 lean at a time: no falls, avalanches or explosions.
- 1 change of state to a shot, seen close, and 8 words or fewer of narration while it happens, so it can be watched.
- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip.
- Write the cuts into the visual notes: the set wide, the character close, the thing that changes very close, the hands at the product. Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.
- No on-screen words inside the picture, and no signs or labels on the set. Overlays are added afterward.
- No real people, celebrities or third-party characters.

## What to avoid in a video

- A story that runs, stops, and then an ad starts. The product has to arrive inside the story.
