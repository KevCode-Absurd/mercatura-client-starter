# Format: claymation and animated story

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it claymation

- **1 clay character on 1 small handmade set.** The character stands for the buyer: an adult by its proportions and dress, never a child, a real person or a known character. It is fiction: it gives no testimonial, and no personal result is claimed as fact.
- **The thing that changes** is the centrepiece: 1 object on the set that stands for the problem or the mechanism, with 3 states: at its worst, changing, changed. Name it before anything else.

| The product's edge | The thing that changes | Its 3 states |
|---|---|---|
| A routine made simpler (a drink that replaces 4 pots) | A wobbling stack of grey pots on the bedside table | Stack teeters; pots sink flat into the table 1 by 1; 1 pot stands |
| Even heat (a layered steel pan) | An orange clay ripple under the pan | A hot spot in the middle; the ripple spreads; it reaches the rim evenly |
| Rain kept out (a waterproof jacket) | Blue clay raindrops on the jacket's shoulder | Drops land; they sit as round beads; they roll off and the cloth has not darkened |

- **The real product** keeps its shape, colours and printing. It is never remade in clay, and its label is never turned into clay letters.

## What it is good for

Clay shows what a camera can't and is looked at longer than a photograph. It has no believed human face.

So it carries a **mechanism** (something invisible, made visible) or a **problem** told as a story. It is weak for proof that rests on a real person: proof here is counts, third parties and the mechanism made plain.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers (the problem as a small story) and for solution-aware buyers (the mechanism shown). For a buyer who only needs the offer, another format does more.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

A change shown on the set is a claim made. Show only what the brand's claims state, in their terms. No body before and after, no organ, no symptom leaving a body. A metaphor stays plainly a metaphor; a real function (heat spreading, a zip sealing) is shown as it is.

## What to avoid

- A character who gives a testimonial.
- A mechanism explained only in words: if it isn't shown, this format is wasted.
- A picture that needs its words to be understood.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: the avatar the character stands for (`Who they are`), `The moment` (the set), `What almost stops them`, `Customer language bank`. Proposed: `Tried before` (the failed-solutions journey). Blank: ask; a story with invented stakes reads slightly off.
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Visible result` (a real function shown as it is, or "nothing visible", then a routine or a plain metaphor), `How it is used`, `Core benefit`. Proposed: `How it works` (1 thing acting on another, with what it must not be drawn as). Blank: ask; do not invent a mechanism.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Brand guardrails`, `Motion and sound: Sound`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: the claim the picture shows, the forbidden claims, the offer. A claim that is not there is not drawn.
