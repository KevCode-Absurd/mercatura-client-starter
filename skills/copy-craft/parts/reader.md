**Read the reader before you write a word.** None of this appears in the copy.

- **Where the buyer is.** Unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware or most aware. The less they know, the less the opening looks like a pitch and the later the product arrives. A buyer who already knows the product is told the deal straight. Indirect is not the cleverer choice: it is the choice for a buyer who is not ready.
- **How often they have heard this promise.** Rarely: the plain promise is enough. Often: the promise needs how or why it works. From every brand in the category: no promise is believed any more, so the buyer's own situation, named so exactly that they feel known, does the work a claim cannot.
- **The want is found, not made.** Take it from what buyers show: their words, what they tried, what they gave up, what they do instead. Lead with 1 want. When 2 wants compete, the one they are living with today wins.
- **1 piece, 1 idea.** 1 argument, 1 feeling, 1 action. A second idea is a second piece, however good it is.
- **How much change the piece asks for.** Keep everything and add 1 thing; keep most and change 1 thing; or replace the old way. A true line that asks for more change than this buyer wants still fails. Match the size of the promise to the size of change they came for.
- **Say it to yourself first:** for [who buys], who wants [what], after trying [what they tried], the way in is [the lead]. If you cannot finish the sentence from the brand's material and the research, the piece is not ready to write: say what is missing.
