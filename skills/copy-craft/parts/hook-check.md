**Judging a hook.** A hook has 2 jobs: it stops the scroll, and it pulls in the buyer this is for. One that only stops is bait and scores low on relevance however striking it is.

Ask these 3 first, in this order:
1. Would a person say it aloud to a friend, and would a stranger understand it at once? A hook that has to be decoded fails here, however striking it is.
2. Does it give this buyer a reason to stay: what they get, or their own moment named?
3. Does something happen in it, or is about to?

Then:
- Does the first second carry a feeling (worry, surprise, recognition, wanting), or is it a neutral statement?
- Is there something to stay for, or is everything answered in the line?
- Does it matter: is something at risk, or unusual?
A hook with 2 of these 3 missing is weak, unless it leads with proof or the offer for a buyer who already knows the product.

- A plain promise is right when the payoff is interesting by itself. A held-back answer is right when it is not. A mystery made by hiding an interesting payoff, or a question the next line does not take up, is a fault.
- Curious, never confused: 1 idea, in words a stranger knows, with no setup needed.
- Anything odd in it is odd about the product or the problem.
- Curiosity with no benefit showing pulls the wrong people.
- Could it open any ad in the category? Then it is not new. That lowers its mark for novelty and nothing else: a plain hook that makes sense and gives a reason is never marked down for being plain.
