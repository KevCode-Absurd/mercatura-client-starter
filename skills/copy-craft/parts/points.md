**Points: the supporting lines on an image, in a list, or in long copy.** A point is a plain sentence, or a phrase a person really says, and it is not a feature. It is a small sale: 1 benefit with a reason to believe it or a reason to read on. 1 flat point is where the eye starts to skim, and a skimming reader does not buy.

**Mending a flat point, in 4 moves.** Turn the feature into what the buyer gets. Make it specific. Add how or why it works: the form, the amount, the moment of use, what it is compared with. Then add the thing that makes it believed or wanted: a named part, a real source, a time.
- "Pocket springs" can explain independent movement; a claim about what the sleeper feels needs support too.
- "5-ply steel" is a construction fact; retaining heat under a cold steak is a separate performance claim.

**Check the links.** Feature is what it has, mechanism is how it works, benefit is what the buyer gets. Support each separately. An ingredient, material, certification or warranty proves only its own fact or terms. When the next link is unknown, keep the verified fact and ask for evidence; never complete the chain from plausibility.

**Kinds to mix.** A list of 1 kind reads as a list.
- **The plain benefit,** with its number.
- **The bare fact:** a result or a figure with no decoration, and what it changes for them.
- **The turn:** why the opposite of what they expect is true.
- **How it works,** in words the buyer would use.
- **The exact detail** (an amount, a setting, a time). Only one that matters and that the brand can stand behind. A made-up exact number is the most damaging fault there is.
- **The mistake:** what most people get wrong and why it was not obvious. The fault lies with what they were told, never with them. Use it sparingly.
- **The easy way,** with what makes it easy. "Easy" alone is the least believed word in advertising.
- **A customer's words,** real ones only.
- **The proof or the guarantee.** It ends a run: curiosity opens, proof closes.

**Order and shape.**
- The strongest first. The first and the last of a list are the ones read.
- Each point aims at a different live want or a different doubt of the buyer this piece is for. 2 points that make the same argument are 1 point, and a point any buyer could be shown is the weakest in the list.
- A buyer who is ready wants confirmation: facts, amounts, terms, no mystery. A buyer who is comparing wants the difference. A buyer who knows the problem wants their struggle recognised, with no technical term.
- When every rival lists the same benefits, add what removes the first objection ("You don't have to re-season it every month"), or say how it works.
- **On an image** a point answers 1 doubt, as a short plain sentence or a phrase a person says, with its small words left in. When the space is small, say less: pick a smaller thing and say it whole. Never the same thing squeezed.
- **Test:** say each point aloud to a friend. Would they understand it alone? Then read the list fast: where does the eye slide? Rewrite or cut that point.
