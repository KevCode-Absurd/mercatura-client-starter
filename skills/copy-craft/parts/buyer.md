**A buyer a writer can write to.** A label ("women 35 to 55", "busy professionals") is not a buyer. What makes one usable, from the evidence only:

- **What they live through:** what they notice, dread or repeat. The moment it shows up in their day.
- **What they do now:** the workaround, what they buy instead, what they have given up doing.
- **What they tried and why it failed,** in their words. This is what an ad sells against.
- **What they feel about it:** annoyed, embarrassed, doubtful, hopeful, tired of being sold to.
- **What they believe that stops them,** said as they would say it and would defend. And what would have to become believable for them to buy.
- **What they want under the problem:** why it matters, what it costs them, what they would get back.
- **Who they are** (age, work, family) only where it changes the proof, the words or the offer.
- **How much change they came for:** 1 thing added, 1 thing swapped, or the old way replaced.

Never tell a writer what the buyer feels unless the evidence shows it. A gap is named as a gap.
