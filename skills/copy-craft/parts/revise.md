**Changing copy that already exists.** Before the first edit, settle what stays: the idea or image at the centre of the piece, the feeling it was written for, the voice, the offer, the order of the argument, and any wording the person approved or asked to keep.

For every line that changes, compare the old and the new in place:
1. What did the old line do well: attention, curiosity, wanting, recognition, sound, explanation?
2. What does the change fix for this reader?
3. What does the new line lose, and is that worth it?

If both are plain and honest and the new one is only safer or shorter, the old line stays. The 1 exception: a line a person would not say, or a stranger would not understand, is made plain, and that is always worth it. A true detail swapped for a general promise is a worse line. A wrong fact is corrected without rewriting the voice around it. A pattern that was chosen on purpose (a repeat, a long sentence that builds) stays. Change what was asked for and what that change forces, and nothing else.
