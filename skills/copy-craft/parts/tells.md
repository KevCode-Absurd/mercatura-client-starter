**Shapes that read as written by a machine.** Each was found in copy that had passed a careful read. A shape with a name is caught in seconds. A brand that sometimes uses one has a device, not a licence: the device goes only on a line that takes it without bending, and a line nobody would say is rewritten whoever's sound it has.

| Shape | Looks like | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| The squeezed line | A sentence with its small words dropped to sound punchy: "One pan beats whole drawer" | The whole sentence, or a smaller thing said whole |
| The "no" stack | "No springs. No sag. No sore back." | What the buyer gets, as 1 sentence |
| The clipped tag-on | "Yes, really." "Fair." "That's it." after a claim | Cut it. The sentence before it already said it |
| The mirror | "X gets the click. Y gets the sale." "Less mess. More flavour." | Say the 1 true thing directly |
| The matched pair | "Soft like a cloud, firm like a board." "Another morning, another burnt pan." | 1 plain sentence about the 1 that matters |
| The verb chain | "Tracks, sorts, plans and saves your money" | The 1 thing it does that matters here, in a sentence |
| The colon schema | "The problem: X. The fix: Y. The result: Z." | Prose, with the joins a person would use |
| The empty 3 | "Simple. Clean. Effective." | Cut it, or 1 sentence that carries the fact |
| The "it" with no owner | "It's about more than coffee." | Name the subject |
| The wide tail | "...for any kitchen, any cook, any budget" | Stop where a person would stop |
| Growth words with nothing in them | "unlock", "elevate", "take it to the next level" | A verb with an object the buyer can picture |
| The stock opener | "In today's busy world", "Whether you're a ... or a ..." | Start with the fact |
| The quotable last line | A closing line trying to be a saying | End on the clearest concrete sentence |
| The warm-up | "Here's the thing:", "Let's be honest:", "The truth is:" | Say the thing |
| The question that answers itself | "So does it work? It does." | Answer what the buyer was already asking, and never write the question |
| The double negative | "not without", "no lack of" | The positive |
| Excitement in the company's voice | "Exciting news!", "We're thrilled to" | The news itself |
| One breath too long | A sentence that keeps adding clauses after a speaker would have stopped | Stop, and start another. A long sentence that builds to something stays |

**Rhythm.** The ear is part of the reader, even in silence.
- Whole sentences are the rule. The only fragment is a label: a price, an amount, a button. A row of fragments where 1 sentence would carry the idea costs the reader the connection.
- Sentence lengths vary the way speech does. 3 or more in a row of about the same length read as a list.
- A short sentence belongs where the thought turns, and nowhere else. It is never added for effect.
- Between 2 strong lines there is plain connecting prose. 2 or more short balanced sentences in a row, each trying to be quotable, read as generated.
- Where the link between 2 sentences is not obvious, a plain joining word (but, so, because, and) makes it obvious. Unjoined sentences are a list dressed as prose.
- Stress falls where a voice would put it. At most 1 word in a short paragraph is marked, and never a whole sentence in capitals.
