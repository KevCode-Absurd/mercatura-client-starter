**The lead: 6 ways into a sale, from most direct to least.** In an ad the lead is the opening: the part read before "See more" or heard before the thumb moves, and as much after it as the buyer's stage needs. It decides most of the result. Pick 1 for the piece and bring the close back to what it promised. For a buyer who does not know the problem, or only feels it, the lead is the first scene of a long story: how that story is told is in "Long story copy", where it is sent with this.

| Lead | For a buyer who | How it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | knows the product and wants it (most aware) | The best part of the deal first, then its value, the true reason for the deal, the guarantee early |
| Promise | knows the product and has not decided, or is comparing ways out | The biggest benefit, bold and believable, backed in the next breath. Made smaller where the market has heard it too often |
| Problem and way out | has a named problem and is looking | The problem as they live it, in their words: the moment it bites, what they tried, what it costs them. Then the turn to hope. The stage sets how long it is held |
| A fact they do not know | has the problem and doubts the usual answers | The fact is named in the first line and not explained there. The body gives it. Only when there is a real fact |
| A plain bold statement | is unaware, or has heard every claim | Something they have not heard, said like news, proved at once with specifics |
| A scene | is unaware, or knows the problem when the scene carries it | A moment the buyer steps into: 1 person, 1 place, 1 thing happening. It runs as long as the stage needs. Its point is the same as the offer's, and the question it opens is answered by the end |

- A scene written as "you" from what buyers really say is honest ("You set it up in January. By March you'd stopped opening it."). A named customer who does not exist, or a founder's moment that is not on record, is not.
- **2 versions of 1 piece are 2 different leads, never 2 wordings of 1 lead.** 2 wordings teach nothing when they are tested. Pairs that test well: problem and way out against a fact they do not know (problem aware); a bold statement against a scene (unaware, or a crowded market); offer against promise (a buyer who knows the product); promise against problem and way out (comparing).
- When the buyer and the market point to different leads, the buyer's awareness picks the first and the market may pick the second.
