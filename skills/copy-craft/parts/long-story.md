**Long story copy**, for a reader who does not know the problem or only feels it. The copy is the story of the problem. The product is how it ends.

- **Open inside a moment.** A time, a place, 1 person doing 1 thing. No product, no name for the problem, no greeting. The first sentences make sense alone and leave 1 thing unexplained.
- **Stay with 1 person.** The same person from the first line to the last. The reader thinks "that is me" several times before anything is asked of them.
- **Make it worse through what they do about it.** First the small ways they have arranged their life around it. Then each fix they tried: what it cost, how it seemed to work, why it stopped. The fix failed, never the person. Then what goes on happening if nothing changes.
- **Then the real cause, said plainly.** 1 idea, the way a friend would explain it, with no technical word. It makes every failed fix make sense. Where the brand's material holds no cause, stay with what the person notices and do not invent one.
- **The product comes late, as the bridge.** After the cause, never before: how they came across it, their doubt, the first time they used it, 1 ordinary day since. 1 fact at most. No list of what is in it.
- **End with a next step.** Return to the opening moment, changed. Then 1 thing to do, said the way a friend would.

**What keeps someone reading to the end**

- Every paragraph ends leaning into the next: something not yet explained, a "but", a "so then".
- Every paragraph brings something new: an event, a cost, a detail. A point said twice is where they leave.
- Hope rises and falls more than once: a fix seems to work, then does not.
- Small exact details: the time on the clock, the thing in their hand.
- Paragraphs of 2 to 5 whole sentences, joined by "and", "but", "so", "because". A short sentence only where the story turns. No headings, no lists, no rows of 1-line paragraphs.
- Read it aloud. Cut every sentence where nothing happens. Keep every joining word.

Long because more happens, never because it is padded. The roughly usual split is a quarter on life as it is, half on the problem getting worse and the fixes that failed, and a quarter on the cause, the product and the next step.
