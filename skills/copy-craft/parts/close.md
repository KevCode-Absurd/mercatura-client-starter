**The close.** It comes in 2 parts: why to act, then exactly what to do.

- **The deal in plain words:** what they get, what it costs, how soon it arrives. In the brand's own terms, with every condition.
- **The risk taken away, with its real condition.** Only the guarantee the brand has, with its real period and what the buyer does to use it. A guarantee is never sharpened into a promise of a result.
- **The 1 doubt most likely to stop this buyer is answered just before the ask,** not left for a list of questions.
- **1 action.** 2 asks split the reader. The ask names what is on the other side of the tap ("See the 3 sizes", "Start the first month"), never the button.
- **What waiting costs, when it is true.** A real date, a real count, a real price change. Where none exists: the true cost of the problem going on, in the buyer's terms (another month of the same thing, the money already spent on what did not work). Never an invented clock. Leave it out where it would nag: a small impulse buy, or a buyer who has already decided.
- **Lead them by the hand** where the next step is not obvious: what to tap, what happens after.
- **The close returns to what the opening promised.** The buyer should be able to say in 1 sentence what they get.
- A cold buyer for a considered product can take a longer close. A warm buyer for a simple one cannot: 1 line of offer and the ask.
