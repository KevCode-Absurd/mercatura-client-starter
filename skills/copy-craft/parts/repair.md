**Mending 1 passage.** Start from what the reader has to do next: understand, want, believe or act. Find the 1 thing in the passage that stops them, and make the smallest change that removes it. A passage that already does its job stays exactly as it is.

| What is wrong | The repair | Stop before |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody would say it, or a stranger would not follow it | The whole sentence a person would say to a friend, small words in. If it will not fit, say a smaller thing | Making it clever, or squeezing it shorter |
| The problem or the promise is broad | Swap the describing word for a moment the buyer knows, an action, what the product does, or a result that can be seen | Inventing shame, or a kind of person the buyer never said they were |
| The promise has nothing under it | Put the demonstration, the result, the reason or the comparison right beside the promise it supports, from the brand's material | Using a guarantee or a famous name as proof of something else |
| A doubt is left standing | Name the doubt, show the fact that changes it, say what that means for them | Inventing ease, a delivery time, or a way out the brand does not offer |
| Curiosity has become confusion | Keep the idea, make the frame simpler, give the answer | Hiding the product from a buyer who needs to compare it |
| The terms are unclear | The real price, the real commitment, the next step | An invented deadline, a count or a bonus |
| It is dense and still does not convince | Put back the scene, the reason or the rhythm it needs to be understood | Pushing every job into every sentence |

After the change, read the lines on each side again. A bigger promise can lower belief. A tighter line can lose the reason the buyer needed. The join to the next line still has to hold.
