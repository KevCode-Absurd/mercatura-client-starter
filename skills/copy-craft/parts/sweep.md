**The last read: what sinks a piece.** Each is a yes or a no. The first 3 come first and in that order: a no on any of them is a rewrite of that part, whatever else is right.

1. **Natural.** Would a person say every line aloud to a friend, as a whole sentence in plain words, and would a stranger understand each line alone?
2. **It sells.** Does it move the buyer from what they have to what they want, with the product as the way across? Is every count and feature turned into what changes for them?
3. **It tells a story.** Does something happen to someone? For a buyer who does not yet know an answer exists, is it the long story of their problem, with the product late?
4. Does it show the writer knows this buyer: their situation, their words, what they already tried?
5. Is there 1 big idea, or a pile of small points?
6. Do the first lines give this buyer a reason to go on: their own moment, or what they get?
7. Is each claim a doubtful buyer would question backed in the same breath, by something the brand can show?
8. Is the 1 doubt most likely to stop this buyer answered before the ask?
9. Is the ask 1 action, specific and easy, and does it say what the buyer gets?
10. Is every reason to act now true? No reason is better than an invented one.
11. Is the guarantee stated with its real terms?
12. Is it as long as this buyer's stage asks: the whole story for one who does not know the answer, the deal and little else for one who is ready?
13. Does the close come back to what the opening promised?
14. Would the right buyer stop for the first line alone, and would the wrong one skip it?
15. Is the opening about this product or this buyer's situation, or could it open any ad in the category?

A piece is not marked down for being plain, for being bold, for a superlative in the seller's own voice, or for a number that is round. It is marked down for a line nobody would say, for a promise it does not pay, and for anything presented as real that the brand's material does not hold.
