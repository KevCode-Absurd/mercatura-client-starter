**The hook.** It has 2 jobs at once: stop the scroll, and pull in the buyer this is for. A hook that only stops attracts people who will not buy.

**Where a hook comes from.** A buyer is rarely confused. They are stuck, and a belief keeps them there. Answer 4 questions in plain words before writing a line:
1. What are they stuck on, and unhappy about?
2. What belief keeps them there, said as they would say it and would defend? Never "they do not have our product".
3. What would have to be untrue for that belief to fall? Strongest when the viewer can see it break for themselves: a demonstration, a side by side, their own numbers.
4. What becomes possible once it breaks?

The hook leads with the break (3) or with what becomes possible (4), never with the product. Contrast alone wins attention and loses the buyer, because it makes them the target. A change with hope in it keeps them.

**3 things a strong hook carries.** Any of them can sit in the words, the picture or the sound, so judge the first frame and the first line as 1 thing.
- A feeling in the first second: worry, surprise, recognition, disbelief, wanting. Not a neutral statement.
- A gap: something they stay to find out.
- Stakes: why it matters, what is at risk or what is unusual.
A hook with 2 of the 3 missing is rewritten, unless it leads with proof or the offer to a buyer who already knows the product.

**Promise or question.** If the payoff is interesting by itself, say it plainly: clear beats clever. If the body is ordinary, hold the answer back so they stay for it. Never hide an interesting payoff to make a mystery, and never open a question the body does not answer. Self-interest comes first; curiosity alone pulls the wrong people.

**Holds for every hook.**
- It is a sentence a person would say aloud to a friend. Clear beats clever: a clever line has to be decoded before it can be judged, and a stranger scrolling past will not decode it.
- No setup needed. A cold viewer was not planning to watch.
- Start in the action. No warm-up line.
- Curious, never confused: 1 idea, in words a stranger knows. A name the brand made up dies or is explained in the same breath.
- Anything odd is odd about the product or the problem. Random strangeness wins the stop and loses the sale.
- The line that follows deepens the same question. A hook that sounds strong alone and is dropped by line 2 is a failed hook.

**A video hook in 2 steps.** 0 to 2 seconds: something to look at and a tiny ask ("Watch what happens", "Can you see it?"), with no product name and no claim. Say "this", not the name: the vague word is the question. 2 to 5 seconds: who it is for and why to keep watching ("If you [situation] and [what keeps happening], ..."). If they look and nothing is there, the next hook is not believed.

**Words on screen over a hook.** The sentence or the phrase a person would say that carries it, with its small words left in. If it does not fit, say a smaller thing: never the same line squeezed. Big enough to read at a glance. Set like the platform's own text.
