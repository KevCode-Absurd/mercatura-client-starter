# Knitted wool as a video: a narrated story, knitted

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger sees a knitted world, follows what happens in it, and can say what the product did.

## How to write for it

- **Narration, not dialogue.** 1 narrator, in the brand's voice, tells the story over the pictures. No doll speaks or mouths words: the faces are painted. Write every spoken line as narration, in 3rd person about 1 or 2 dolls ("She has tried the sprays.") or in 2nd person.
- **The look brings no story.** Use the script's own shape: a problem deepening to 1 consequence, failed fixes, a discovery. Each line is 1 picture of what the narrator is saying at that moment.
- **3 kinds of shot.** A life shot (1 or 2 dolls in a room), a product shot (the knitted pack, no dolls), a mechanism shot (a close view, no dolls). Say which in the visual note.
- **The mechanism,** only where the brand's facts give 1: promise first, then the ingredient or part arriving and acting as a small glowing knitted element. Plain visual words, never clinical terms.
- **The product's arrival:** inside the story, as what the doll found. It is knitted, and comes into the picture when the words reach it, in the doll's hands.
- **One close that works:** offer and call to action as narration over the knitted product, then 1 identity line.

## Length

30 to 60 seconds. The look holds a viewer through a problem: give it up to a third. Under 30 seconds: 1 room, the problem, the product, the offer.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- **Name every thing as knitted,** with its real colour: "a knitted woman in a green knitted cardigan", "a wood-brown knitted shelf", "a fully knitted glass of water". Describe each doll the same way every time: adult, build, yarn hair, clothes.
- Few things: 1 or 2 dolls, 1 room, 4 or 5 named objects. A thing is already in the frame when its shot opens.
- A hard cut changes the view: a life shot, then hands or the product close. **At the turn a new place may arrive by a yarn transition,** written as a line of its own: the room unravels into loose strands that re-knit as the next; a thread is pulled and the scene unzips stitch by stitch; the world rolls up into a yarn ball and unrolls as the next; loose strands stitch themselves into the product. 1 to a clip at most, with 6 narrated words at most over it.
- 1 action per shot: 1 gesture or 1 camera move. Movement is stop-motion: stepped, small, at real speed. No running, falls or fast action.
- **A feeling is the face and the body:** name it, then the brows, the lids, the mouth, the shoulders. "Tired" alone renders a pleasant blank doll.
- Faces from a step back. Close views only of hands, the product and the mechanism.
- Soft daylight unless the line says otherwise: then name the source and how dark the room is ("lit only by a knitted bedside lamp").
- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip. Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.
- No words inside the picture beyond the pack's own label, and no signs or labels on the set. Overlays are added afterward.

## What to avoid in a video

- A story that runs, stops, and then an ad starts.
- Water, glass, steam or a screen left as itself: each is knitted too.
- A body or health scene in clinical words. Say what is seen, in yarn.
- 3 or more dolls: each is 1 more thing that drifts between clips.
