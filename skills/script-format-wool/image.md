# Knitted wool as a still image: 1 knitted scene, photographed

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees a world knitted from yarn with 1 plain moment in it, and reads the headline once.

## The frame

- **1 moment, of 1 of 3 kinds.** A life scene: 1 or 2 dolls caught mid-action in a room, the problem at its worst in the pose (unaware, problem aware). A mechanism view: the ingredient or part arriving and acting, close, no dolls, only as the brand's facts give it (solution aware). A product scene: the knitted pack on a knitted surface with 3 or 4 knitted things from its use (product aware).
- **The pose is the story.** A still has no narrator: the feeling is in the brows, the mouth and the shoulders. Dolls are fictional adults: no child, no real or known person, no doll as a customer or an expert.
- **Composition:** the knitted world fills the frame to every edge, seen at eye level from a step back, the subject whole. Few things, each named. A calm stretch of wall, sky or table is left for the words.
- **The product:** where the ad shows it, front to the lens and large enough for its label to read. Where the ad leaves it out, the scene holds only the problem.

## Words on the picture

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer, as clean type in the brand's typeface, laid over the calm stretch in the top third. It says what the picture cannot: whose problem this is, or what just changed.
- 1 smaller line, or 2 or 3 chips, and a button only where there is an offer. 20 words in all.
- Type is laid over the photograph. No word is knitted, stitched or embroidered, and nothing on the set carries words except the pack's own label.
- No speech bubble: a doll says nothing. Every number and term is the brand's own.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is, first: a cinematic knitted-yarn scene, a miniature world entirely hand-knitted from yarn, rounded crochet-like stitches with visible stitch rows on every surface, a cosy medium gauge, photographed like a stop-motion film still.
- **The material owns the noun:** "a knitted woman", never "a woman made of yarn" (that renders a real person in a yarn room). **Every element is named as knitted,** with its real colour: "a navy knitted mug", "a fully knitted glass of water", "wood-brown knitted cabinets". A thing left unnamed renders as its real material.
- Dolls, only when people are in frame: knitted dolls with realistic adult proportions, smooth doll faces with a light knit texture, painted eyes, lips and blush, stitch rows across their knitted skin, styled yarn hair, knitted clothing. Give each one's build, hair and clothes. Write a feeling as brows, lids and mouth.
- Light: bright natural daylight, real cast shadows. Textiles, clothing, plants and packaging carry real saturation; wood and counters keep believable tones. Any other light is named with its source.
- Camera: eye level, inside the scene, medium-wide from a step back, subject sharp, background gently soft. Close only on hands, the product or a mechanism.
- The product is knitted at its true shape and colours with a clean label: state every line of label copy exactly, with its colour, or it prints the material's name.
- Not real humans or photographic skin, not chunky toys or bobbleheads, no micro-stitching on faces.

## What fails in a still

- A face in close-up: it turns hyper-detailed and uncanny.
- 1 object left unnamed, rendered real: the world breaks.
- A change the brand's claims do not state.
