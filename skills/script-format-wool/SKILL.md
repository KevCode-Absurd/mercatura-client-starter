# Format: knitted wool

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

Knitted wool is a look, not a story: the script brings the story.

## What makes it knitted wool

- **A whole miniature world, hand-knitted from yarn** and photographed like a still from a stop-motion film: stitch rows on every surface, the dolls' skin included, in real daylight with cast shadows.
- **The material owns the noun.** Every person and thing is written as knitted: "a knitted woman", "a knitted kettle", "a cream knitted fridge". A thing left unnamed turns up as its real material and breaks the world, so a scene names few things.
- **Knitted dolls, not people.** Adults by their proportions and dress, with painted faces. They are fiction: never a customer, a founder or an expert, a child, a real person or a known character. A narrator tells the story; no doll speaks.
- **Seen from a step back,** at eye level inside the world, the subject whole. Close views belong to hands, the product and a mechanism, never a face.
- **The product is knitted too,** at its true shape and colours, with a clean label carrying its real words exactly.

## What it is good for

Wool is soft, warm and unexpected in a feed: a stranger stops to see how it was made. It makes an awkward subject gentle to watch, and it shows what a camera cannot: the inside of a thing, an ingredient arriving and acting.

It is weak where the sale rests on a real face, a real result or the product's true finish (a fabric's drape, food that must look good to eat). A doll proves nothing: proof here is counts, third parties and the mechanism made plain.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers (the look stops a cold viewer and the problem is told kindly) and for solution-aware buyers (the mechanism shown). Product-aware and most-aware buyers want the real product and the terms: another format does more.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

- Show only what the script's lines and the brand's claims state. A change knitted into the picture is a claim made.
- A mechanism is knitted only as `How it works` gives it: the ingredient or part as a small glowing knitted element, arriving and acting, caught mid-action, in plain visual words (layers, strands, a droplet), never clinical terms. It goes no further than the claim: "supports" is the product being taken, never damage mended.
- No body before and after. A metaphor stays plainly a metaphor (a tangle of yarn coming loose).
- Numbers, study results, medical results and named competitors come only from the brand's facts.

## What to avoid

- A doll who talks to camera or reports a result.
- A face in close-up, or a feeling written as 1 adjective: it renders a pleasant blank doll.
- A story that gains nothing from being knitted: film it.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are` (the doll: rough age, hair, dress), `The moment` (the room), `What almost stops them`, `Tried before`, `Customer language bank`. Blank: ask, or mark both inferred.
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Looks like` and `Pack` (the knitted pack: shape, colours, every label line), `How it is used`, `Visible result`, `How it works` (the mechanism shot), `Core benefit`. No label words: ask; a label is never invented. No `How it works`: no mechanism shot.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Brand guardrails`, `Brand world`, `Color palette`, `Typography` (words on a still), `Motion and sound`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer, the guarantee. A claim that is not there is not knitted.
