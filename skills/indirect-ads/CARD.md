# Indirect ads: the card

<!-- The rules of SKILL.md with the explanation taken out. Sent to the writer of an indirect story beside the native copy skill and the form. -->

An indirect ad is a native ad for cold traffic. The image is a moment from the buyer's life, usually the problem, and plants one question; the copy answers it in 150 to 400 words; the click goes to an advertorial, never a product page. It needs a pain the buyer already feels: with none, write a direct ad instead and say so.

## The image asks

- Define subject/action/environment/camera/light/style; name fixed decisions and bounded open choices. Lead with the story's visible question and positive scene cues, keeping exclusions and exact words. Unknown facts are not open choices. Before retrying, check ambiguity, source conflicts, feasibility and output compliance within limits; add no portrait to an object-led type.
- Scene/crop/camera/light first; local skin and relevant approved details follow only when a person belongs. Inspect full/zoom and recheck the whole after a local fix. No compulsory edit series; changed accessory designs are variants, not preservation proof. Keep the final approved anchor for optional motion.
- Picked from the pain: what does this pain look like from the outside? A test becomes the test result; a symptom becomes the symptom; a changed life becomes the room where you can see it changed.
- It plants 1 question and never answers it. Covered product, it still makes sense: the product is not in it.
- A capture, not a render: phone snapshot, mixed room light, real clutter, real skin. No logo. No text in the image except the few words the type allows.
- With a person: subtle forehead/cheeks, local nose pores, fine eye/lip lines, varied brow hairs, gentle jaw texture. Scale to distance/light; keep approved identity/age/marks. No uniform pore dots, dusty fuzz or added blemishes. Unaware messaging never requires harsh skin or a face in an object-led image.
- Complexion, undertone, age and texture are separate choices. Keep approved tone/marks and individual maturity, with highlight/shadow detail; no washed-out/gray skin, exaggerated aging or rejuvenation. Correct exposure before changing complexion. Choose only one or two local variations for a new cast.
- Makeup is optional: separate coverage/finish/placement and retain plausible relief, not every mark equally. Keep approved styling; blend edges, control shine, avoid smooth masks or powdery patches. A candid unaware image need not have bare skin.
- Visible hands: simple pose, plausible digits/joints/wrist, nail beds/cuticles and approved polish. Name supporting grip, true scale/overlap/contact; hidden digits stay hidden. Keep nail/accessory styling and count/position; add no prop/product solely for this study.
- Approved worn details need position/attachment/overlap/contact/material response: clip grips hair, stud follows contour, matte ink follows skin without raised edges. Keep count/design/scale/body-relative location; add no tutorial detail or macro insert that changes the image's job.
- A still proves no moving continuity. For a separate animation task, keep details attached through speech/blinks/turns and occlusion; review the whole clip for crawl/flicker, contrast pumping or sudden smoothing, allowing natural blur/light effects. Keep approved words and format.
- Skin repair: name waxy smoothing, repeated pores or clipped shine at one area; change 1 local cue. Keep identity/marks/makeup, expression/pose/crop/camera/light and lettering fixed; retain gentle sheen. Compare same area/scale, record change/result within retry limits. Keep the story, add no body problem.

## The copy answers

- The first line picks up the question the image asked. 1 of 5 leads: story, warning, authority reversal (a real expert only), confession, curiosity question.
- The spine: the relatable problem in the buyer's own words; the false belief; what they tried and why it failed; the real mechanism; a hint of the answer, no hard sell; one soft call to the page ("I put everything I found on one page").
- 150 to 400 words, short paragraphs, the buyer's own phrases from the research. The mechanism is one the brand's facts support.
- The headline under the image is a second hook in 1 of 4 shapes: "Before you [thing], read this"; "Why your [problem] [keeps happening]"; "It wasn't [what they blamed]. It was [the real cause]."; "The reason you [embarrassing behaviour]". Never a product name.
- No price, no discount, no product page. The close sends them to the advertorial.

## Lines that cannot be crossed

1. No invented customer: a customer's first-person story retells a real review or conversation given in the research; otherwise the founder tells it, or the brand summarises what customers say.
2. No invented expert, no doctor holding the product, no endorsement implied by the image.
3. No disease claim: describe the problem; never cure, treat, reverse.
4. No "you" statement about personal health ("Do you snore?"); "Why your partner ended up on the couch".
5. No before-and-after of a body. A generated adult is a photograph, never a testimonial.
