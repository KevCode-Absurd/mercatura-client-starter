# Skeleton ad: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narrated ladder. A question ("What happens if you ...?") is answered rung by rung (Day 1, Day 30, Day 365), and every rung is lived by the same cartoon skeleton, who stands for "you". It is judged on:

1. With the sound off, a stranger sees each rung go further than the last and knows how it ended.
2. The skeleton is the exact same character in every clip.
3. The product is the real one, and the picture claims nothing the narration could not.

## The looks

The preset names 1 look. Its CHARACTER wording goes into `character` unchanged; its STYLE wording opens `visual_direction`, with the world and the palette filled in once.

- `bare` (the default). CHARACTER: A full anatomical skeleton with natural adult human proportions, tall and lanky, smooth ivory-cream bones with realistic bone detail (NOT toy-smooth, NOT chibi, NOT scary), and large expressive cartoon eyes with white sclera and dark pupils set in the eye sockets, giving an emotive, lovable face. No clothing. STYLE: cinematic 3D animated render, photoreal [THEME] environment, warm [palette] colour grade, soft volumetric light with drifting steam or atmosphere, shallow depth of field.
- `dressed` (something worn, lifestyle, identity). CHARACTER: The same friendly skeleton (ivory bones, large expressive cartoon eyes with white sclera and dark pupils) wearing a complete [THEME-appropriate wardrobe]; skull, hands and any exposed bones still visible. STYLE: as `bare`, without the steam.
- `xray` (what happens inside, only as the script states it). CHARACTER: A translucent glowing anatomical adult human body revealing the full white skeleton PLUS visible internal organs (heart, lungs, intestines) glowing red and orange through a blue-tinted translucent skin outline, with large expressive cartoon eyes. STYLE: clean sci-fi medical 3D render, cool blue translucent body with warm organ glow, a real setting with x-ray glow OR a clean blue gradient backdrop, soft rim light. No organ is shown changing.
- `mascot` (only a brand that is playful on purpose). CHARACTER: A cute chibi cartoon skeleton with an oversized round skull, big adorable eyes, a small rounded body, and smooth toy-like bones; bright, friendly, non-scary. STYLE: a playful 3D animated render, simple clean pastel background, soft even studio lighting, glossy finish. A toy mascot with an adult's job, never a child.

## The ladder in clips

- 1 line of the script is 1 rung. A clip holds 1 or 2 rungs, and a rung is cut as shots: the place wide, then what went further, close. 2 rungs in a clip are joined by the rung turn; otherwise the next clip opens on it. Only the payoff is a held pose.
- Clip 1 opens on the skeleton already doing the habit the question asks about. The last clip is the payoff alone, held for the offer.
- Each rung goes further than the last in 1 visible thing: the pile, the shelf, the onlookers.
- The picture is the proof of the line: every `visual` shows what its line says and no more.
- The marker (DAY 30) is laid over afterwards. It is never in the picture.

## The skeleton, so it stays the same

- `character` is the look's CHARACTER wording, plus for `dressed` the outfit as 3 named pieces with their colours. Every `visual` calls it "the skeleton".
- Its body is not the result: bones do not whiten, thicken or mend.
- It never speaks. Its jaw is closed, or dropped as a feeling. Feelings are eyes and pose: eyes widen, shoulders slump, a fist goes up.
- It is not a customer and claims no result of its own.
- Reaction characters: 3 at most, fictional adults given by role and dress in `setting` ("2 dockers in flat caps at the back"), in the same words in every `visual`. They go from recoil to awe, silent.

## What the camera and the world do

- `setting`: 1 place in the ad's world, photoreal, for the whole video. A rung is a state of that place, not a new place.
- Everything the ladder uses is in the first frame at its smallest. The thing that climbs is second in `props`, after the product, and its `look` is its first state. In a quantity ladder the whole row or stack is in view from the start; the skeleton works along it.
- `camera`: at the skeleton's chest height for the wide shot of a rung, wide enough for the skeleton, the thing that climbs and the onlookers. Each shot is held steady; the cut does the moving.
- No signs, labels, calendars or screens.

## The rung turn, and the 1 move

- The rung turn is this look's own transition, 1 to a clip at most: the skeleton holds its pose, the light sweeps across the place once as time going by, and the place settles 1 rung on.
- In the turn, what is already there may grow, pile up, empty, wear or come nearer. Nothing comes in from out of view.
- Apart from the turn: 1 action a shot at real speed. The skeleton moves 1 limb or leans. Its bones stay joined and rigid.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The skeleton in the place, wide, mid-habit.
- The skull and the eyes, close, for the feeling.
- The thing that climbs, close, 1 rung further on.
- Bone fingers on the thing or on the product, very close.
- The onlookers reacting, from recoil to awe.
- From low, the pile or the skeleton towering.

## The product on screen

- Its shape, colours and printing are those of its photo, lit by the scene. Its `look` gives its size against the skeleton's hand.
- It comes into the frame at the line that names it, at a cut, already within the skeleton's reach or in its hand, front to the lens.
- Bone fingers hold its side, never its front. It opens or pours only as the lessons say.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" and gives a calm, steady voice: age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice.
- All `dialogue` is narration. The clips are silent picture: nobody in them speaks.

## First and last frame

- First: the skeleton mid-habit, the thing that climbs at its smallest.
- Last: the payoff. A triumph: the skeleton upright, the product at its side, the onlookers turned to it. A catastrophe: the place at its worst, the skeleton turned from it to the product. The product's front clear, the top third plain for the offer line.

## Avoid

- A count the picture must show exactly: show more than fits.
- Horror: decay, blood, darkness, empty eye sockets.
- A catastrophe that is an illness or names a competitor.

## After the render

- The same skeleton: white sclera and dark pupils, ivory bones, the same proportions.
- The jaw formed no words, and no letters or numerals appeared.
- Each rung went further, in view. Nothing popped in.
