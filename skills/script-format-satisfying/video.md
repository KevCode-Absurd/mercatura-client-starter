# Satisfying as a video: a wordless hook, then the demonstration

It works with the sound off, and its job is to keep people watching so the rest of the ad is seen.

## The shape: 2 parts

1. **The hook: the first line, 4 to 5 seconds.** 1 shot, 1 motion. Its spoken text is empty; its on-screen text is the overlay; its visual note is the shot.
2. **The demonstration: the rest.** The product doing its 1 action: where it starts, what happens, where it ends. Narrated in 1 voice, plainly.

## The hook shot

**Write the visual note as:** the object in focus in the brand's own words for it; the motion, already part-way when the shot opens; what "done" looks like and that it comes near the end; the place (where the demonstration happens too); the sound of the end; the last frame.

"The lilac cream's foil seal, already a third peeled, comes off in 1 piece; the last corner lets go with a small pop; the open jar holds still on the edge of the bath" is a visual note. "A satisfying shot of the product" is not.

Rules for the shot:
- The motion has an end the viewer can see coming, and the end comes in the last third.
- Real speed. Nothing in slow motion and nothing sped up.
- The last frame has the product in it, still. The demonstration starts from that frame.
- A hook on its own is made to put in front of an ad that already works: write it as a script of 1 line, and write its last frame to match the first frame of that ad.

## The overlay

1 to 4 words in the on-screen text of the first line: "Wait for it", "POV:", or the first words of the buyer's own thought, in their words. It opens a question. Never the offer, a price, a claim or a call to action. It agrees with the picture and with line 2: together they make 1 point.

## How to write the demonstration

- **Bridge in 1 line.** Line 2 names what the viewer just watched and ties it to the buyer: what that motion means for them. It does not introduce the brand. A hook is judged with this line, never alone.
- **Start state, action, end state.** 1 line each. 1 action only: the scoop, the pour, the click, the peel. The product is in the same place and looks the same as in the hook.
- **Say what it asks of the buyer,** in their terms: how long, how many steps, what they stop doing.
- **1 fact** the brand can prove, said once.
- **One close that works.** The offer and a call to action of 2 to 4 words, spoken and on screen.

## Length

15 seconds: the hook and 2 or 3 lines. 30 seconds: the hook and 5 or 6 lines.

## Writing shots a model can render

- The hook is 1 continuous shot with no cut. Everything the motion uses is in view when it starts. It never opens on an empty set.
- The camera does 1 thing. A view from an unusual place (inside the bowl, looking up) is written into the visual note as where the camera is, not as a move. A camera move with nothing resolving (an orbit, a glide) is not a hook.
- After the hook the demonstration is cut as shots, 1 action a shot: the start state wide, the action very close, the end state from above or in a hand. Only the last shot of the ad is a held frame.
- State a count that matters.
- No words inside the picture: the overlay and captions are added afterwards.
