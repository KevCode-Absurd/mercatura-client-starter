# Format: satisfying

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it satisfying

A physical motion that resolves (something fills, peels, clicks shut, slides free) holds the eye before a word is read or said. It needs no face.

**What is in focus.** 1 of 3, in this order of preference: the product itself out of its pack; something it is made of (an ingredient, a material); the thing it replaces (the row of bottles, the stained pan). The pack may be in the frame. It is never the sealed pack on a stand, and never a scene the ad does not come back to.

**The motion. Pick 1,** and take it from what the product really does (`Moves and sounds`, `How it is used`). A motion the brand's facts do not describe is marked "inferred".

- What comes out: pour and settle, fill to the brim, scoop and level, spread, pump out, dissolve or bloom, drop and settle.
- How it opens, shuts or fits: peel, click shut, twist to the stop, perfect fit, zip or fasten, open and reveal.
- How the material behaves: squeeze and spring back, fold or roll, unroll or drape, wipe clean, release (lets go whole), bead and run off, cut through.
- Order from disorder: sort into order, stack or nest, many become 1 (the things it replaces cleared, the product set down).

**It is real.** Materials behave as they do in life: liquid has weight, powder falls, a lid has a thread. Things settle, pour, peel and click; nothing explodes or shatters, and nothing happens that could not (a liquid changing colour for no reason). The product is the real product with its real printing, opened where it really opens, described only in the brand's words. The place is the buyer's own: the kitchen, the bathroom shelf, the hallway.

No person speaks. A hand is fine when the motion needs one. A product that is worn is on a hanger, laid flat, or on an adult cropped below the face.

## What it is good for

Its job is to stop people and keep them looking. It carries no argument on its own, so the words with it have to make 1.

So it carries **how the product is used** or **what it looks like when it works**, for a product with a motion a camera can see. It is weak for a product whose effect is invisible and whose use is nothing to look at (a capsule swallowed with water): use another format, or build it from what the product replaces.

By stage: solution-aware and product-aware buyers (this is what using it is like). For a problem-aware buyer, the thing it replaces is in focus. It is not for an unaware buyer on its own.

## What to avoid

- The product on a turntable, or a scene with no product, ingredient or replaced thing in it.
- Several unrelated satisfying things in a row. 1, then the product.
- Words that describe what the picture already shows.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `Looks like` and `Pack` (the words for the product), `How it is used` (the steps), `Moves and sounds` (the motion and its sound), `Visible result` (whether an end state may be shown). Blank `Moves and sounds`: choose from `How it is used` and mark it "inferred". Blank `How it is used`: ask; do not invent how it opens.
- `customer-avatars.md`: `The moment` (the place), `Customer language bank` (the overlay, the bridge). Blank: leave the overlay as "Wait for it".
- `brand.json`: the 1 fact and the offer. No fact there, no fact in the ad.
- `lessons.json`: how the product is opened, held and used, word for word.
