# UGC as a still image: a phone photo someone posted

Judged on 1 thing: a stranger takes it for a photo a real person took and posted, and stops because of what the person is doing, not because of a design.

## The frame

- **1 caught moment, mid-action.** The product in use or just used, at the place and hour the buyer really uses it: the scoop half way to the glass, the jacket being zipped at the door, the tub open on the bathroom shelf. Not a person smiling and holding a pack up beside their face.
- **How it was taken:** a selfie at arm's length, a mirror photo, or the view down at their own hands. Pick 1. Slightly off-centre, a little tilted, everything in focus.
- **For an unaware or problem-aware buyer** the moment is the problem (what they do to get by), and the product is absent or small at the edge. For later stages the product is in the hand, front readable, never covering the mouth.
- **The room** is 1 ordinary lived-in place with 2 or 3 things that belong there. No studio, no backdrop.
- The person is an adult. Where no real customer photo was uploaded, the person is fiction and is never labelled a customer.

## Words on the picture

- The words look typed on by the person: 1 caption of 12 words or fewer, in a plain story-style text bar or plain white type with a soft shadow, over a quiet area away from the face and the product.
- It is the hook as a thought, a question or an observation in the 2nd person ("you know that 3pm thing"), or a customer's words quoted exactly with who said them. With a generated person, never a result in the 1st person.
- 1 smaller line at most (the 1 number, or what to do next). No button, no logo block, no brand headline type: a designed layer puts it in the ad pile.
- The picture may carry no words at all. The hook is then the first sentence of the feed copy.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: an unretouched phone photo. The room's own light, with where it comes from (a window on the left, a ceiling lamp). Real skin texture with visible pores, slight natural asymmetry, real fabric wrinkles, a slightly wide phone lens.
- Leave out: cinematic, film grain, bokeh, shallow depth of field, dramatic lighting, colour grade, studio, flawless, perfect. No beauty filter, no ring of light in the eyes.
- Hands: name the camera support, free hand and feasible product grip. A direct selfie leaves one hand free and its filming device outside the image; a supported top-down camera frees both hands. Describe naturally occluded digits, not five fingers forced into view. Mirror selfies establish the reflection, rear-camera orientation and correct lettering reversal.
- The product is the real one from its photo: its shape, colours and printing, opened only where it really opens.
- The caption is given exactly, in quotes, with its place and that it appears once.

## What fails in a still

- A posed product shot with a model: it reads as a brand ad.
- A testimonial quote beside a generated face.
- A caption in the brand's headline typeface, or 3 text blocks.
- A scene so tidy and well lit that nobody believes a person took it.
