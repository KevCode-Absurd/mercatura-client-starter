# Script format: static image

Load this with a script or image-copy task skill. Where the two differ, this wins.

A static is judged on 3 things: a stranger stops on it, can say in 1 sentence what it argues, and knows what to do next.

## What this surface carries

A static has no sound, no controlled order and only a moment of attention. The eye decides the sequence. It can show a diagram or a comparison, and it can hold 1 clear frame better than any video.

So it suits scripts whose load-bearing beat is the **offer** (clear terms in 1 frame), a single **proof** point, or 1 **mechanism** contrast. It can't carry a full argument. What doesn't fit goes to the primary text and the landing page.

## The reading order is the script

Decide where the eye lands first, second and third. That order is the ad.

| Read | Job | From the script |
|---|---|---|
| First (largest, highest contrast) | Hook: select the person | The customer's word for the problem, or the accommodation |
| The image itself | Problem or demonstration | The accommodation shown, the product in use, the old way beside the new, a comparison |
| Second | Promise, or the mechanism in 1 contrast | The promise line; "the feature, which means" |
| Third (smallest) | Offer and action | Real terms, the call to action |

Objection, proof detail and risk reversal go in the primary text unless 1 of them is the whole idea of the ad.

In the script, a line's on-screen text is what is set on the image, and the line itself is what the primary text says for that beat. The hook line's visual note is the picture.

## How to write for it

- **1 idea.** A static makes 1 claim. Choose the beat this ad is about and build the image around it.
- **20 words or fewer on the image**, across all text elements. The first read is 8 words or fewer where it can be, never more than 10.
- **Promise before mechanism** still holds: the larger read is what they get, the smaller is why.
- **Specifics, not adjectives.** A number, a measure, a time or a quoted customer word, each from the brand's facts. On a static there is no room for a line any competitor could say.
- **All hooks agree.** The image headline, the first line of the primary text and the platform headline make the same claim at the same awareness stage, in different words.
- **Native or designed.** A native look (a note, a plain photo) reads as a person and suits problem-led ads to cold buyers. A designed look suits offer-led ads to warm buyers. Marketing language on the image puts it in the ad pile either way.
- **The image may carry no words.** When the job says so, the picture does the first read alone (the accommodation, the demonstration or the comparison, plain to see) and the hook becomes the first sentence of the primary text.

## The primary text

The beats the image left out, in script order: problem, promise, mechanism, objection, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action. Short paragraphs. The first sentence is 125 characters or fewer, makes the image's claim, and does not repeat its headline word for word.

## Visual brief

Say what is in the picture, where the product sits, and what each text element says and where. The product is the real one, as its photos and the brand's facts show it; if the facts do not say how it opens or is used, it is closed and at rest. Leave the platform's safe zones clear.

## What to avoid

- Shrinking a video script onto an image.
- 3 claims of equal size.
- The product hero shot with a tagline, for a cold audience.
- A number, a quote or an offer term that isn't the brand's.
- A generated person shown as a customer, a founder or an expert. A child.
