# Knitted wool: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narrated story in a miniature world hand-knitted from yarn, photographed like a still from a stop-motion film. The look is the rendering; the story is the script's. Judged on:

1. Every frame reads as photographed knitted yarn: stitch rows on every surface, the dolls' skin included.
2. Every frame is a self-contained scene shot from inside the knitted world, from a step back.
3. The dolls, the room and the product look the same in every clip, and the picture claims nothing the narration could not.

## The look (preset `default`)

`visual_direction` opens with the style block and closes with the camera block, word for word:

> Cinematic knitted-yarn scene, a miniature world entirely hand-knitted from yarn, rounded crochet-like stitches with visible stitch rows on every surface, a cozy medium gauge, photographed like a stop-motion film still: a medium-wide shot from a step back so the subject is fully visible, the subject sharply in focus and the background gently soft, bright natural daylight. A warm naturalistic palette lifted with real colour: daylight, foliage, textiles, clothing and packaging all carry genuine saturation; wood, cabinetry and counters keep their own believable tones rather than being painted bright. Exteriors and outdoor scenes go fully saturated. The scene fills the entire frame with the camera inside the knitted world.

> Camera at eye level, inside the scene. Everything in frame is knitted yarn, photographed: soft stitch detail visible on every surface, real cast shadows, warm cozy lighting, the knitted scene filling the entire frame. No real humans, no photographic human skin. No chunky chibi toys, no oversized bobblehead, no hyper-detailed micro-stitching on faces.

## The material owns the noun

- Put "knitted" directly on every person and thing, in `character`, `props`, `setting` and every `visual`: "a knitted woman", "a navy knitted mug". Never "a woman made of yarn": that renders a real person in a yarn room.
- Every element is named as knitted, with its real-world colour: "a fully knitted glass of water", "a cream knitted fridge". A thing left unnamed renders as its real material and breaks the world. So few things: 4 or 5 named objects.

## The dolls, so they stay the same

- `character` gives each doll in 1 sentence (a knitted man or woman, adult age, build, yarn hair colour and style, knitted clothes and their colours), then, word for word: "The figures are knitted dolls with realistic adult proportions and idealized builds: smooth doll faces with only a light knit texture, sweet painted features (painted eyes with soft lashes glancing naturally, painted lips, gentle blush, a few tiny stitched freckles); visible stitch rows across their knitted skin; luxurious styled yarn hair in fine strands; knitted clothing fitting the body."
- Every `visual` repeats each doll's build, hair and clothes in the same words. 1 or 2 dolls: fictional adults, never a child, a real or known person, a customer, a founder or an expert.
- No doll speaks. Name the feeling, then write the face: brows, lids, mouth ("her brows pinched together, her mouth pressed flat"). An adjective alone renders a pleasant blank doll.

## What the camera, the set and the yarn do

- `camera`: eye level, inside the scene, medium-wide from a step back, the subject whole. Each cut changes the view. Close views only of hands, the product and a mechanism. A face in close-up turns hyper-detailed and uncanny.
- `setting`: 1 knitted room from the persona's moment, running past every edge, each fixture's colour named.
- Soft daylight is the default. Night, a lamp or a cold screen is said in the `visual`: the source and how dark the room is.
- A mechanism scene, only as the brand's `How it works` gives it: "a zoomed-in macro view, no people in the scene", the ingredient or part as a small glowing knitted element arriving and acting, mid-action. Plain visual words (layers, strands, a droplet), never clinical terms. No body before and after.

## The product on screen

- First in `props`: knitted like everything else, at the true shape and colours of its photo. Its `look` states every line of label copy exactly, with its colour ("a clean label reading HAND BALM in dark green letters"). Unstated, the label prints "YARN" or nonsense.
- In the room from the shot where the words reach it, in a doll's hands or within reach, front to the lens.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" with age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice. All `dialogue` is narration, over soft set sounds.

## Movement and transitions

- Every clip's `visual_direction` carries the motion guard, word for word: "Handmade stop-motion knitted-yarn animation throughout: visible knitted stitch rows and soft yarn fibre on every surface, slightly stuttery 12fps stop-motion cadence, no motion blur. Non CGI. Non cartoon. Animation must start at the first frame."
- 1 motion action to a shot: 1 gesture, or 1 camera move (a push in to the knitted product, a pull back from a knitted detail). Only the last shot of the ad is a held pose.
- A hard cut may change the view or the place; a new place names every thing in it as knitted. World-bridge is the look's own way to a new place, kept for the turn of the story: the scene unravels into loose strands that re-knit as the next; a thread is pulled and the scene unzips stitch by stitch; the world rolls up into a yarn ball and unrolls as the next; loose strands stitch themselves into the product. It is a shot of its own; the shot after it describes the new scene in full.

## Shots this format cuts between

- A life shot: 1 or 2 knitted dolls in the room, medium-wide.
- Knitted hands on the knitted product, close.
- A product shot: the knitted pack alone, its label to the lens.
- A mechanism shot: a macro view, no dolls.
- A knitted detail of the room that the words name, close.

## First and last frame

- First: the doll mid-action, whole in frame.
- Last: the knitted product front to the lens, the doll beside it, a calm area in the top third for the offer line.

## Avoid

- A thing with no "knitted" on it. Water, glass, steam and screens are knitted too.
- A third doll, a crowd, a child.
- Signs, screens with text, or letters anywhere but the pack's label.

## After the render

- Yarn is still yarn: stitch rows on every surface, no real skin, no real liquid.
- Each doll's build, hair and clothes match the first clip.
- Stepped movement; the texture did not melt; no mouth formed the narrator's words.
- The label reads exactly as given.
