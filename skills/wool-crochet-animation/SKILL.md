# Knitted wool ad

1 look: a miniature world hand-knitted from yarn, photographed like a still from a stop-motion film. A narrator tells the story and each picture shows what is being said at that moment. A look and nothing more: the story is the script's. It carries everyday scenes, product shots and mechanism shots equally.

## What it is judged on

1. **Every frame reads as photographed knitted yarn,** stitch rows on every surface, the dolls' skin included.
2. **Every frame is a self-contained scene shot from inside the knitted world, from a step back.**
3. **The dolls, the rooms and the product look the same in every shot,** the label words exact.
4. **The picture claims nothing the brand's facts do not.**

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, materials, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, clip lengths, how narration is written, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-wool`: how the story and the narration are written. This skill is the yarn.

## 1. What makes anything read as knitted yarn

1. **The material owns the noun.** Put the material word directly on every subject: "a knitted red-haired woman", "a green knitted shampoo bottle". When the person comes first and the material second ("a woman made of yarn"), the model drops the material and renders a real person standing in a yarn room.
2. **Every element is named as knitted.** Give each object in the scene its treatment: "a fully knitted glass of water", "wood-brown knitted cabinets". An object left unnamed renders as its real material and breaks the world.
3. **Stitch rows on every surface, skin included,** at a cosy medium gauge.
4. **Photographed, physical light:** real cast shadows and soft daylight, the set lit like a real miniature.
5. **Colour where the world would really have it.** Name each object's real-world colour (navy mugs, a mustard coat, a cream fridge) and let the style block's colour line set the saturation: daylight, foliage, textiles, clothing and packaging carry real saturation, fixtures keep believable tones, exteriors go fully saturated.

## 2. The camera: inside the world, a step back

Every picture is a self-contained scene: the knitted world runs past every edge of the frame and the camera sits inside it at eye level. **Frame medium-wide, from a step back, with the subject fully visible.** Stitch detail grows as the camera nears: from a step back, faces stay smooth painted dolls with a light knit texture; pushed in close, they turn hyper-detailed and uncanny.

**The blocks set defaults; the scene overrides them.** When a shot needs another light or framing, say it in the scene and keep the blocks word for word:

- **Light:** "the bedroom deep in night, lit only by moonlight from the window and the red glow of a knitted alarm clock."
- **Framing:** "A close-up of a knitted woman's hands...", "A zoomed-in macro view...". Close-ups belong to hands, products and mechanism shots; faces stay at a step back.

## 3. The blocks

Every still prompt is built in 1 order: **style block, character block (only when people are in frame), the scene, camera block.** Paste each block word for word. Never "same style as before".

Style block, opening every still prompt:

```
Cinematic knitted-yarn scene, a miniature world entirely hand-knitted from yarn, rounded crochet-like stitches with visible stitch rows on every surface, a cozy medium gauge, photographed like a stop-motion film still: a medium-wide shot from a step back so the subject is fully visible, the subject sharply in focus and the background gently soft, bright natural daylight. A warm naturalistic palette lifted with real colour: daylight, foliage, textiles, clothing and packaging all carry genuine saturation; wood, cabinetry and counters keep their own believable tones rather than being painted bright. Exteriors and outdoor scenes go fully saturated. The scene fills the entire frame with the camera inside the knitted world.
```

Character block. Leave it out of product-only and mechanism shots: its wording summons knitted dolls into scenes that should have none.

```
The figures are knitted dolls with realistic adult proportions and idealized builds: smooth doll faces with only a light knit texture, sweet painted features (painted eyes with soft lashes glancing naturally, painted lips, gentle blush, a few tiny stitched freckles); visible stitch rows across their knitted skin; luxurious styled yarn hair in fine strands; knitted clothing fitting the body.
```

Camera block, closing every still prompt:

```
Camera at eye level, inside the scene. Everything in frame is knitted yarn, photographed: soft stitch detail visible on every surface, real cast shadows, warm cozy lighting, the knitted scene filling the entire frame. No real humans, no photographic human skin. No chunky chibi toys, no oversized bobblehead, no hyper-detailed micro-stitching on faces.
```

## 4. The scene, written fresh for each shot

Who, what, where, the action, the feeling. A pattern, not a template:

```
A knitted broad-shouldered woman in her forties with short curly auburn yarn hair, wearing a mustard knitted raincoat, kneels on a striped knitted hallway rug rubbing a folded blue knitted towel along the back of a knitted golden retriever, her brows lifted and her mouth in a soft tired half-smile; beside her knee stands a tall green knitted bottle with a clean label reading DOG SHAMPOO in white letters, and a fully knitted bowl of water; behind them a softly blurred fully knitted hallway: a wood-brown knitted coat rack, a cream knitted door with a small knitted window, and morning light.
```

- **Describe every doll in full, in every prompt:** age, build, hair colour and style, clothing. Anything unstated is dice-rolled and drifts. Each is a fictional adult: never a child, a real or known person, a customer, a founder or an expert.
- **Name the feeling, then write it as face mechanics:** "her brows pinched together, her mouth pressed into a flat tired frown, her eyes fixed wide on the ceiling." An adjective alone ("sad", "slumped") renders a pleasantly neutral doll face.
- **The product stays accurate.** Attach its tagged photo. Knit the pack like everything else and state every line of label copy exactly, with its colour; unlabelled text picks up the material's name ("YARN").
- **Lock what recurs.** A doll, a product or a room used in more than 1 shot is made once as its own picture, approved, and attached to every shot it is in.
- **A miss:** rerun the identical prompt before rewriting it. With the blocks fixed, chance is almost always the cause.

## 5. Mechanism shots

Only a mechanism the brand's `How it works` gives, no further than the claim. No body before and after.

- **Show the ingredient arriving and acting, mid-action.** Render the active ingredient or part as a small glowing knitted element (a golden knitted droplet, glowing knitted beads) moving into the knitted thing it acts on and changing it, framed as "a zoomed-in macro view, no people in the scene", so the clip can animate the change. Pattern: "A zoomed-in macro view of a knitted cross-section of a pan's base, 3 knitted layers stacked, a glowing orange knitted ripple spreading from the centre toward the rim, no people in the scene."
- **Body shots describe what is visible, in yarn,** in plain visual words: layers, strands, projections, a droplet. Clinical terms (named conditions, hormones, "inflammation") get the picture refused.

## 6. Stills, then clips

Make the reference pictures (each doll, the knitted product, each room), then each shot's still. Approve a still before its clip. Unnamed, the cadence is smoothed and the yarn melts. Every clip prompt, transitions included, carries the motion guard:

```
Handmade stop-motion knitted-yarn animation throughout: visible knitted stitch rows and soft yarn fibre on every surface, slightly stuttery 12fps stop-motion cadence, no motion blur. Non CGI. Non cartoon. Animation must start at the first frame.
```

1 motion action per clip: 1 gesture, 1 camera move. Stacked actions multiply drift.

## 7. Yarn transitions

Build a first and a last frame as a pair: 1 shared anchor from the same references, 1 action, a matching composition, a stated fate for everything that leaves.

- **Camera-bridge** (anchor fixed, camera moves): push in from the medium-wide scene to the knitted product; pull back from a knitted detail to reveal the next scene.
- **World-bridge** (camera fixed, yarn transforms around the anchor): the scene unravels into loose strands that re-knit as the next scene; a thread is pulled and the scene unzips stitch by stitch to reveal the next; the world rolls up into a yarn ball and unrolls as the next; loose strands stitch themselves together into the product.

The clip prompt names the 1 concrete visible action that carries frame A into frame B, plus the motion guard, and nothing else: the frames carry the style.

## 8. What fails, and the fix

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Real humans appear in a yarn room | The material owns the noun ("a knitted woman"); camera block word for word |
| Knitted figures in a product or mechanism shot | Leave the character block out whenever no people are in frame |
| Toy or bobblehead proportions | Character block word for word ("realistic adult proportions and idealized builds") |
| Faces hyper-detailed or uncanny | Step back to medium-wide; close-ups only for hands and products |
| Furniture in random bright colours | Name each fixture's real-world colour; style block's colour line word for word |
| A night or lamp-lit shot comes out in daylight | Describe the light source and how dark the room is |
| The emotion does not show on the face | Name the feeling, then the brows, lids and mouth |
| Refused on a body or health shot | Rewrite as what is visible, in yarn |
| Label garbled, or "YARN" printed on things | State the label copy on every labelled object |
| 1 doll differs between shots | Lock it as a reference picture; attach it to every shot |
| Texture melts mid-clip | Motion guard in every clip prompt, transitions included |
