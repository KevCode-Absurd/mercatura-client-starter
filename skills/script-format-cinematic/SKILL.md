# Format: cinematic film

A short live-action-style brand or product story. The selling idea supplies the story; the film grammar supplies attention and emotion. Distinct from cinematic 3D, phone UGC and podcast staging.

## Write the viewer's change

- Define what the viewer initially expects, what they discover and why the product belongs to that turn. A reveal, quiet kindness, negotiation, visual misunderstanding or callback can serve any suitable tone.
- Open mid-event or on a clear visual question. Earn attention before explaining. Do not spend a fixed 2 to 4 seconds establishing a room.
- Make supported product use part of the payoff. Fictional reactions do not prove efficacy, manufacture a customer result or turn a character into a founder or expert.

## Write filmable beats

- One or two distinct adults. Describe their identity, wardrobe and accessories once. In dialogue use `Character A:` and `Character B:`; use `Narrator:` for voice-over. Label speakers before script approval, since production keeps those approved words exact. Never overlap speakers or move the listener's mouth during another character's line.
- Give each shot one central event, named visible cast, purpose, camera size/height/angle, one dominant move, gaze and focus target. Turn abstract emotion into observable performance.
- Make the move earn new information: a partial arc can reveal context, a pull back can reinterpret a gesture. Name its arrival; keep face-close paths short and simple. Real-speed performance remains the default unless the story needs a deliberate slow-motion beat.
- Cover the same physical world with context, singles, object/hands detail, reaction or over-the-shoulder shots as needed. Two people in the cast does not require two people in every frame. Hold screen direction and coherent eyelines.
- Separate identity reference light from scene light. The scene master fixes location, layout, physical light sources and grade; a reverse angle changes framing without moving the lamp or doorway. Focus follows the shot, not a blanket sharpness instruction.
- Vary rhythm deliberately. A brief live reaction or quiet beat can let the reveal land; no frozen pose, slow motion or busy actions just to fill the clip. Leave enough time for the approved whole line and recognition.
- Plan against actual available clip durations and references, not an assumed 20-second generation. Clip joins are intentional cuts where seamless motion continuation is unsupported.

## Sound and final copy

Voice carries the words, optional music the emotion, selective source sounds the physical contact. Follow the user's sound and music switches, including full silence. Keep exact captions and offer text for post-production overlays rather than generating them inside the scene.
