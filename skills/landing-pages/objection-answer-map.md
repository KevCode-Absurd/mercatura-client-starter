# PDP objection answer map: reviews, visible answers and missing proof

Use when customer doubts may be unanswered by a product page's gallery, copy or buy box. Start with the actual mobile page and this product's reviews, returns, support tickets and non-buyer questions. A screenshot alone does not establish why visitors leave or which objection costs sales.

Build this working table before proposing a section or gallery image:

| Customer question | Source IDs and exact quote | Separate sources mentioning it | Current answer and location | Visibility | Fact or proof needed | Proposed answer and placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The buyer's own question | Keep quote and source together | Count unique records; duplicates count once | Cite the captured section or image | visible / hidden / missing / unknown | Approved fact, policy, test or unresolved gap | One checkable answer at the decision |

**Inspect before marking missing.** Expand the FAQ, check the full gallery and inspect the buy box on a phone. A closed accordion or below-fold answer is present but less visible; record the observation. If it cannot be inspected, mark unknown. Text overlap does not establish that the page answers the question. A missing answer and missing proof are different problems: moving copy cannot establish an unsupported claim.

**Prioritize with context.** Recurrence in the available sources indicates what those sources discuss, not representative prevalence, lost revenue or measured conversion effect. Keep product, source type and sample limitations attached. Returns and complaints may expose failures that positive reviews omit; non-buyers may ask different questions. When evidence is thin, call the priority a hypothesis.

**Place the answer at the decision.** These are starting locations, not a fixed template:

| Question | Useful location | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Will it fit; which size or variant? | Size/variant selector, dimension or scale gallery image | Actual measurements, compatibility limits and fit guidance |
| What do I get; why this price? | Price/contents beside the buy box, contents gallery image | Included items, quantities, real prices and verified comparisons |
| What is it made of; how does that help? | Ingredient/material explanation or gallery close-up | Feature, mechanism and benefit assessed separately |
| How do I use or care for it? | Instructions, use image or demonstration | Official instructions; no invented secondary uses |
| What if it does not work for me? | Risk-reversal terms beside the buying decision | Current return/warranty terms, conditions and exclusions |
| When will it arrive; what does delivery cost? | Buy box or delivery control | Actual destination-dependent terms; never invented arrival dates |
| Can I trust this claim? | Beside the relevant claim | Claim-matched evidence, rather than unrelated social proof |

**Explain without inventing the causal link.** Feature is what it contains or how it is built; mechanism is how it produces an effect; benefit is the buyer's useful outcome. Support each separately. Composition does not establish a clinical result. A warranty establishes a remedy, not a measured lifespan. A generated demonstration establishes neither performance nor compatibility. Keep the verified fact when the next link is unknown; identify the missing evidence. Copy can lead with a supported outcome and explain why naturally; no rigid feature-first formula.

**Turn gallery rows into briefs.** Give each image one question, one supported answer, source IDs and the real product reference. Choose only the images needed for observed gaps; seven slots is a reference structure, not a quota. Use the existing PDP image and product-accuracy workflow. An illustrated use does not prove a test or customer result occurred. Keep facts consistent across the ad, page and gallery.

**Validate the repair.** Walk the same mobile decision path again: can the buyer find and understand the answer, do its terms agree with product and offer, and does the interaction work? Record visible improvement separately from a conversion hypothesis. Follow the app's saved CRO approval contract. This method creates no ad scorecard, sale plan or winning-ad refresh recommendation.
