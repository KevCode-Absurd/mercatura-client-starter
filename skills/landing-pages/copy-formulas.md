# Copy formulas for landing pages

Everywhere: **no em dashes and no en dashes.** Connect ideas with a comma, "and", or "&". No clever wordplay. Direct response: clear, compelling promises.

## Hero headline

Combine 3 elements:

1. **Emotional dream outcome**: the transformed state, identity shift or life change the buyer wants (freedom, status, control, recognition, peace of mind).
2. **Functional benefit with a timeframe**: the specific, measurable result in a concrete period (X days, X weeks).
3. **Unique mechanism or proof element**: what makes it possible or believable (a method, a guarantee, a rate).

Pattern: `[Achieve dream outcome] [functional benefit] in [timeframe] using [unique mechanism] ([proof element])`.

Examples: "Brighter, even skin in [timeframe the brand states] with a vitamin C that does not sting ([the brand's rating])"; "Cook like the restaurant does, on 1 pan that never needs replacing (lifetime warranty)". Lean on the end benefit and the dream outcome. Paint who they become.

A product bought once rarely has a timeframe: its second element is what it does on day 1 and for how long it goes on doing it. Leave the timeframe slot out rather than fill it.

On a page built for an ad, the headline keeps the ad's words (continuity) and adds what the ad left out. The ad's words win over the formula.

## Sub-headline

**Version 1 (benefit):** build on the headline, elaborate the main benefit, lead into the CTA and make them scroll. Open with the pain, then the solution. 1 or 2 sentences.

**Version 2 (mechanism):** `[Pain point] The only [unique mechanism] that [how it works] to [benefit 1], [benefit 2] and [outcome] within [timeframe].` 30 to 40 words at most. Focus on the how: the method or approach that makes it different.

Examples: "Tired of serums that sting? The only vitamin C buffered to skin's own pH, so it brightens and evens without the redness." "Tired of replacing a scratched non-stick pan every 2 years? Carbon steel builds its own non-stick surface as you cook, so it gets better with use and never wears out." "The only" is used when the brand's facts support it; otherwise say what is different.

## Pain point section (problem, agitate, solve)

- **Opening:** "Dear [audience]," then 3 pain points as questions with checkmarks ("Do you...", "Are you...", "Have you..."). Specific, relatable, emotional.
- **Problem:** acknowledge the pain with empathy. "You" language. Make them feel seen.
- **Agitate:** the frustrations, the flaws of the current methods, the failed attempts as bullets, and the cost of doing nothing: what is slipping away.
- **Solve:** turn to hope. Say what they do NOT need (dismantle the objection), then what they DO need. The product as the alternative that fixes the root cause, with proof, results or timeframes where the brand has them.

Formatting for scanning: sentences of 15 words or fewer; 2 sentences a paragraph at most; frequent 1-line paragraphs; bold or capitals on key phrases ("NOTHING worked"); a break every 2 or 3 lines; checkmark bullets; conversational, like talking to a friend.

## Pain point headline (introduces the section)

Bridges the frustration and the solution about to be shown. Under 20 words. Mix the forms, at most 2 or 3 questions in 5:

- A warning or guidance: "Avoid X by doing Y".
- A pain callout with a reframe: "Feeling X? It's not Y, it's Z".
- An empathy question with the fix: "Frustrated by X? Here's the fix".
- A bold claim that challenges an assumption.
- 1 sentence that validates the pain and promises resolution.

Examples: "Still buying a new non-stick pan every 2 years? It's not your cooking, it's the coating"; "Tried every serum and still red by lunchtime? Here's the real reason"; "Avoid a wardrobe of rain jackets you never wear by choosing 1 you would wear dry."

## Value propositions

**Find them:** the top 8 unique value propositions and differentiators, from everything known about the brand. Each must be distinct: no repeats, overlaps or rewordings. Real differentiators only, not benefits every competitor could claim. If fewer than 8 are truly distinct, use fewer and say so.

**Headline each:** `[Action or experience verb] + [specific feature or what they get] + [emotional benefit or dream outcome]`, 12 to 18 words, concrete with numbers, timeframes or guarantees where the brand has them. Transformation, not transaction: freedom and peace of mind, confidence and control, permanence, ease, status or capability.

Examples: "Cook on steel that builds its own non-stick surface and never needs replacing"; "Wake up to calm, even skin with a vitamin C gentle enough for every morning"; "Stay dry on the ride in a jacket that looks at home in the office". A count of customers or a timeframe goes in only when the brand states it.

## Call to action

Value-driven, easy to understand, about what they get. Address the worry, reduce the risk, no pressure. For a product page the button itself is short and active ("Get the routine", "Get my pan"), and the line under it carries the risk reversal in the brand's own terms: the guarantee and "pause or cancel" for a product bought again, the returns window and the warranty for a product bought once. With no terms on file, the line is left out.

## The masterclass rules these sit under

- Benefit first, feature as the justification.
- Every line passes "so what?".
- The FAQ answers the reasons people do not buy.
- No invented numbers: a timeframe, a rate or a count appears only if the brand states it.
- No invented proof: a rating, a review, a press mention or a certification appears only if the brand holds it, as the brand states it.
