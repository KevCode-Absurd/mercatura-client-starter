# Founder as a video: a brief the founder films

This script is a brief for a real person to film. A prompt in square brackets is answered on camera. A script that is half prompts is still a good brief.

## How to write for it

The beats below are one shape that works, not an order: a founder's film may open on the proof or on the process.

- **Person and tense:** past tense for the decisions ("we turned down...", "it took us..."), present for how it works.
- **Hook:** a decision or an admission, not an introduction. "We made the first batch weaker on purpose." "We threw away the handle 4 times." Name and role go in the on-screen text.
- **Problem:** at category level, as what the founder saw customers doing to get by, or what the category gets wrong. Short.
- **Promise, then mechanism:** the promise in the customer's words, then the decision that produces it. "The feature, which means, which is why."
- **Objection:** the founder is the right person to name the failed solution and say plainly why it half works. The category, never a named competitor.
- **Proof:** a demonstration on camera, a test with its source, a count, a customer's words quoted exactly. If the brand has none for a claim, soften the claim or cut it.
- **Offer and risk reversal:** state the real terms.
- **One close that works:** the call to action, then 1 identity line.
- Sentences short enough to say in 1 breath: the founder is not an actor.

## The 5 founder hooks

Open on the 1 that is true for this founder (skills/hook-bank); the others are extra hooks only where each is true too. Seconds are for a video of 40 to 45:

- **Frustration origin:** "I was sick of [problem] so I built [product]." 5, then what they tried 10, the decision 5, what is different 15, the call 5. Needs a founder who was a customer first.
- **Credential:** "I've [credential] and here's what most people get wrong about [category]." 3, what the trade knows 10, what is wrong 10, how theirs differs 15, the call 5. Needs a real credential.
- **Against the grain:** "Everyone in [industry] does [practice]. We do the opposite." 6, why the usual way is wrong 15, their way 15, the call 5. Needs to be true of the brand.
- **The hard moment:** "I almost shut down in [year]. Here's what saved it." 5, what went wrong 10, the turn 10, now 10, the call 5. Needs the real event.
- **The process, shown:** "Here's exactly how we make [product]." 3, behind the scenes 15, why it matters 10, quality 10, the call 5. Needs real footage.

A fact the brand does not hold is a prompt in square brackets, never a number written for the founder. In the notes: film the body once and 10 hooks for it, each hook 5 ways.

## Length

| Length | What fits |
|---|---|
| 30 seconds | The hook, 1 decision, what it means for the buyer, the call to action |
| 60 seconds | 1 decision in full, 1 objection, 1 piece of proof, the offer |
| 2 to 3 minutes | 2 or 3 decisions, each with its proof. The mechanism can be most of it |

Write for the length given. 1 decision told well beats 3 listed.

## Visual track: shots a founder can film

- The founder where the product is made, packed or tested, or at a table with the product if that is all there is. Not an office.
- Cutaways to the thing being described are the demonstration: the product cut open or taken apart, the rejected samples, the raw material in a hand, the machine, the test. Write each cutaway as 1 thing to film, and say what must be in the frame.
- Mark every visual note with 1 of: `to film`, `on file` (footage or photos the brand already holds), or `product photo`. Never describe a picture that would have to be generated.
- Show the label, the part or the test result at the moment it is mentioned.

Nobody on file who may appear: say so; the video can't be made.
