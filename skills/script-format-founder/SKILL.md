# Format: founder or expert

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

A founder ad is judged on 1 thing: a stranger comes away knowing 1 decision behind the product that nobody else could have told them, and believes it.

## The person is real

A founder, a maker or an expert appears only as themselves: in footage they film, or in footage and photos of them the brand already holds, with their say. They are never generated, never voiced by a model, and nobody generated is ever called the founder or an expert. An expert is named with their real role and only speaks to what that role covers.

## What the founder is for

A founder's word is the weakest rung of the proof ladder unless they have real standing in the field. So the founder is not there to say the product is good. The founder is there for the thing only they can say: **why it is made this way**. The material is decisions and their cost: what was rejected and why, a constraint the product imposed, the step in the making that seems boring. For something taken or applied: the amount and why that amount, what was left out. For something worn or used: the cloth, the steel, the stitch, the part that costs more and why it stayed.

## Where the lines come from

Every decision, number and story is something the founder has actually said or the brand's facts hold: the founder's own answers and words, the brand story, the product's notes on how it is made, a claim with a source. Use the founder's own phrases where they exist.

Where the ad needs a fact that is not there, do not write one. Write the line as a prompt in square brackets for the founder to answer, and list each prompt in the notes:

- `[Say what you changed after the first batch, and why.]`
- `[Say how many samples you turned down before this one.]`

A half-finished piece with honest prompts is usable. One with an invented anecdote is not.

## Register

Plain and unhurried, like explaining to a customer across a counter. No pitch voice. 1st person. Technical words only where the founder would really use them, each followed by what it means for the buyer.

## What it is good for

A believed face, with the standing of the person who decided. So it carries the **mechanism**, told as decisions, and a guarantee said by the person who pays for it.

By stage: solution-aware and product-aware buyers (why this one is made differently). For a problem-aware buyer it opens on what the founder saw customers doing to get by. Don't rest proof on "trust me": pair every founder claim with a higher rung the brand really has (a demonstration, a test with its source, a count, a customer's words quoted exactly), or soften the claim.

## What to avoid

- A founder story with no bearing on the product. Standing matters only if it relates to the claim.
- Adjectives about quality. Replace each with the fact behind it.
- Claims the founder can't back with something outside themselves.
- A health or performance result said as the founder's own experience, unless it is true and the founder asked for it to be said.
- An invented decision, count, date or anecdote.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- The founder's own words: the answers to the founder questions and anything held as the founder's source. `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand story`, `Mission`.
- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `Key features`, `Core benefit`, `How it is used`, and `Made this way because` (proposed: 2 to 5 decisions, what was chosen, what was turned down and why, in the founder's words). `brain.md`: `Key decisions`, `Product notes`.
- `brand-guidelines.md`, `Brand world`: `People on file` (proposed: who may appear as themselves, their real role, the footage and photos held, what they have agreed to).
- `brand.json`: claims with sources, proof assets, guarantee, offers, forbidden claims. `customer-avatars.md`: the avatar's `What almost stops them` (the objection), `Customer language bank` (the promise).

No founder words and no decisions on file: write prompts, and say in the notes that the founder questions come first.
