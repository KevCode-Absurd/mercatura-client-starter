# Same ad, new format

The job: a transposition. The ad already exists and already says what it says. You carry it into another format without changing what it says or the order it says it in.

You have no freedom over the words. Your work is in the picture: how each shot is rebuilt in the new format.

It is judged on 2 things: someone who knows the original finds every word of it, in order; and the beat that did the persuading still does.

## Non-negotiable

1. Every spoken word and every on-screen line stays, verbatim, in order. Nothing is cut, added, reworded, improved or reordered.
2. No new claim, number or offer term enters, in any field, including visual descriptions and platform copy.
3. Checks report; they don't rewrite. If a line breaks a claim rule or a guardrail, flag it and leave it. The owner decides.
4. Never recreate a real person, a celebrity, a meme of a real person or a third party's character. Replace it with a generic equivalent that does the same job and say so. A child in the original is not redrawn: the shot is rebuilt around an adult's hands or the product alone, and the notes say so. A founder, customer or expert appears again only from that person's own uploaded photo or footage.
5. The product in the remake is the brand's real product, from its own photos, with its shape, colours and printing kept inside whatever look the new format has. It opens and is used as the original showed and the brand's facts say; where they disagree, follow the facts and note it.
6. If the ad belongs to another advertiser, say so. This mode copies its words and claims as they are; adapting it for a different brand is the job of `script-reference-structure`.

## Steps

### 1. Lay the ad out

From the shot-by-shot breakdown, list each shot with its time span, its picture and the words heard during it. Label each with the beat it does, so production knows its job. A silent shot belongs with the line before it.

Small print the ad holds on screen across several shots (a disclaimer) is 1 overlay, placed once where it first appears.

### 2. Find the load-bearing beat

Decide which beat does most of the persuading in this ad. The new format must be able to carry that beat. If it can't, say so before anything is made:

| The ad rests on | Risk in the new format |
|---|---|
| A believed human face (proof, testimony) | Animation and claymation have no believed face. The words survive; the proof weakens. |
| Showing an invisible mechanism | A talking head or plain static can't show it. |
| A controlled sequence (a reveal, a turn) | A single static has no sequence. A carousel has one only if the turn sits on a swipe. |
| Sound (voiceover carries the argument) | Statics and carousels have no sound: the spoken words must become on-image text or primary text. |

### 3. Translate each shot

For each shot, describe the same setting, framing, action, props and product placement in the new format.

- **Video to another video format:** 1 scene per shot, same order, same relative timing. If the total length has to change to fit the clip lengths available, scale every shot evenly. Put clip boundaries at the cut nearest each mark, and not inside a sentence when a cut within about 1.5 seconds avoids it.
- **Several people on camera:** use 1 consistent character for all of them unless told otherwise. If the ad is a voiceover, keep 1 narrator and don't make the character lip-sync.
- **Video to carousel:** 1 card per beat or per turn in the argument, in order. Each card's text is the ad's own line for that beat. Put the reveal on its own card.
- **Video to static:** the hook line is the headline, the promise or the turn is the second read, the offer and call to action are the last read. The rest of the spoken words go to the primary text, in order.
- **Static to video:** each text element becomes a spoken or on-screen line in reading order; the layout's elements appear in the order the eye took them. On-screen lines are laid over the clip afterwards; a video model writes no words.
- **A slot with a hard limit** (a headline field of 40 characters, a card that holds 12 words): use the ad's own line that fits. If none fits, leave the slot empty and flag it. Never trim a line to fit.
- **No words on the image** (when the job says so): the lines that would sit on the image go to the primary text, in order, and the notes say the image now carries none of them.

How a picture or a clip is made true (tagged product photos, a still approved before a clip, sound said out loud) is in `skills/production-principles`.

### 4. Say what changed

In the notes: how the timing was scaled, anything the new format couldn't show as shot, anything replaced under rules 4 and 5, every slot left empty, and every claim or guardrail flag.

## What you don't do

- Don't fit the ad to the 9 beats, a length preset or the brand's voice rules.
- Don't fix a weak line. A weak line that is the owner's is still the ad.
- Don't add a missing promise, guarantee or call to action.

## What this reads from the brand brain

Almost nothing: the ad is the source. It reads the product's `Reference photos`, `Looks like`, `Pack` and `How it is used` (`products-and-positioning.md`) so the product is rebuilt true, the people on file (real uploads only), and `brand.json` claims and guardrails for the flags. A blank field is never filled from the reference ad's picture alone: note it.
