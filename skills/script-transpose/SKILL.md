# Script transpose

The job: the same script, told from a different seat or at a different time. A winning script in 1st person past can run again as 2nd person present or 3rd person past, and each is a different ad to the viewer and to the platform. It is the cheapest way to get more ads out of something that already works. It is judged on 2 things: a line-by-line comparison finds the same facts in the same order, and no line sounds translated.

You change the voice. You change nothing else.

## What each voice is

| Person | Sounds like | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | "I stopped cooking steak at home." | A story, told by the person it happened to. Usually the strongest. |
| 2nd | "You've stopped cooking steak at home." | Direct address. The viewer is the subject. |
| 3rd | "She stopped cooking steak at home." | Narration about someone. Suits animation and voiceover. |

| Tense | Sounds like | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Past | "I didn't decide to. I just stopped." | Stories. Something that happened. |
| Present | "You don't decide to. You just stop." | Immediate, and right for how something works. |
| Future | "A year from now you'll be buying another one, and you won't remember deciding to." | A warning or a promise of what's coming. Use sparingly. |

## What never changes

1. **Same script.** The same lines, in the same order, each doing the same job. No line added, cut, merged or split.
2. **Same facts.** Every claim, number, price, offer term, guarantee and product name stays exactly as it is. A customer's quoted words stay word for word, in quotation, in any voice.
3. **Same length**, within about 10% per line.
4. **Honest in the new voice.** Converting must not create a claim the original didn't make:
   - To 1st person: only if the speaker could truthfully say it. A line that becomes a personal result ("it worked for me", "mine's lasted 6 years") needs a real customer saying it. A generated presenter, a hired voice or a character has none: keep that line as an observation ("people say...") and note it.
   - To 2nd person: describe what the viewer does or has tried. Never tell the viewer they have a condition or a personal attribute, and never promise them a result the original only reported.
   - To future tense: a result becomes what the product is made to do or what customers report, never a prediction about this viewer.
5. The rules of the app sent with the job hold as they did for the original.

## How to convert

1. **Find the subject.** Who is the script about now? In 1st it is the narrator; in 2nd, the viewer; in 3rd, a person. For 3rd person use "she", "he" or "they" to match the persona, or a first name if the original has one. Don't invent a surname or a biography.
2. **Convert the story lines** (hook, problem, the experience) to the new person and tense.
3. **Leave the mechanism in the present tense** whatever the target: how something works is true now. "Iron holds its heat" and "the dose steps up as skin gets used to it" stay as they are in a past-tense story.
4. **Leave the offer and the call to action addressed to the viewer**, in the present. "Tap below" doesn't become "she tapped below". In a 3rd-person script the narrator turns to the viewer for the close.
5. **Fix what the change breaks.** A confession ("I never told anyone") in 2nd person becomes recognition ("you've never told anyone"). A witness line ("my brother asked where I'd got it") in 2nd person becomes "someone will ask where you got it" only if that stays inside rule 4; otherwise keep it as a report about others.
6. **Keep the rhythm.** Same sentence lengths, same fragments, same repeated phrases, and the brand's own words where the original used them.
7. **Convert the on-screen text and the hook variants** the same way, so each hook still leads into line 2.
8. **Update the visual notes** only where the person on screen changes (a presenter speaking to camera becomes a narrator over footage of someone). The new picture is one that can be made: an adult, the product as it really is, no real person who has not been filmed. Otherwise leave them.

## Read it aloud

A converted script often has 1 or 2 lines that sound translated. Find them by reading as if aloud, and rewrite those lines so they sound native to the new voice, without changing what they say.

## What to return

The same number of lines in the same order, each with its converted spoken text, on-screen text and visual note. The hook variants, converted. In the notes: any line you kept in its original voice and why, and any line where honesty limited the conversion.

## What this reads from the brand brain

Almost nothing: the script it is given already holds the facts. It reads the persona's name and pronoun for 3rd person (`customer-avatars.md`, the avatar; `personas.json`), and the brand's voice sent with the job. If the persona is blank, 3rd person uses "they". It needs to know whether the speaker is a real customer: nothing in the brain says so today, so it assumes not and reports no personal result.
