# Skeleton ad as a still image: 1 rung, or a strip of 3

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees a cartoon skeleton in a situation that has plainly gone a long way, and reads the question once.

## The frame

- **Choose the rung by buyer stage.** Unaware or problem aware: 1 late rung of the old way or of doing nothing, at its most absurd, the product left out or small at the edge. Solution aware: a strip of 2 or 3 rungs (the first, a middle one, the payoff), so the climb is seen. Product aware or most aware: the triumph rung alone, the product at the skeleton's side, the offer under it.
- **1 rung:** the skeleton mid-action in 1 place. The 1 thing that has escalated (the pile, the empty shelf, the onlookers, who are fictional adults) is the largest thing in the frame after the skeleton. Its feeling is in its eyes and its pose.
- **A strip:** 2 or 3 panels of equal size with thin plain gutters, read left to right in a wide or square frame and top to bottom in a tall one. The same skeleton, place and camera angle in every panel: only the state of the place climbs. Never more than 3.
- **The product** is the real pack, front to the lens, within the skeleton's reach, in the payoff panel only. A catastrophe strip may end on the product alone in a clean last panel.

## Words on the picture

- The question is the headline: 8 words or fewer, across the top, above the panels. "What if you never descaled the kettle?"
- Each panel carries the marker of its rung as its label, in capitals, in the same corner every time: "DAY 1", "DAY 30", "YEAR 3". 2 words to a marker. A single rung carries its 1 marker.
- 1 smaller line at the foot may answer the question or state the offer, with a button only where there is an offer. 20 words in all, markers counted.
- A marker beside a visible result is a claim: the time or the number is the brand's own, or the panel shows no result. Absurd exaggeration is free.
- The skeleton says nothing: no speech bubble. Nothing on the set carries words. All type is laid over the picture, clean, in the brand's own typefaces.

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: a cinematic 3D animated render of 1 cartoon skeleton in a photoreal place. For a strip, name the panels, their order and what climbs; skeleton and camera are identical in all.
- The default look, `bare`, word for word: a full anatomical skeleton with natural adult human proportions, tall and lanky, smooth ivory-cream bones with realistic bone detail (NOT toy-smooth, NOT chibi, NOT scary), and large expressive cartoon eyes with white sclera and dark pupils set in the eye sockets, giving an emotive, lovable face. No clothing.
- `dressed`: the same skeleton in a complete outfit that fits the world; skull, hands and exposed bones still visible. `xray`: a translucent blue-tinted adult body outline showing the white skeleton and softly glowing organs, a clean sci-fi medical render, nothing in it shown changing. `mascot`: a small toy-like skeleton with an oversized round skull, a playful 3D animated render, plain pastel background, soft even light.
- Light and lens: a warm colour grade in the brand's palette, soft volumetric light, shallow depth of field, the camera at the skeleton's chest height.
- The product is the real one from its photo, lit by the scene, its printing untouched, at its true size against the skeleton's hand.
- The headline and the marker labels are clean type over the picture, each given exactly.
- Not a horror picture and not a flat cartoon: no decay, no blood, no darkness.

## What fails in a still

- A skeleton beside a pack with nothing escalated: a mascot, not an ad.
- 4 or more panels, or panels in different places.
- A marker with a result the brand does not hold.
