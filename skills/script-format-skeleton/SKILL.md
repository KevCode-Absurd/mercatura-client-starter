# Format: skeleton ad

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it a skeleton ad

- **A curiosity-gap question opens it,** and the payoff answers it: "What happens if you ___ every day?", "What would happen if you ___?", "How long can you ___ before ___?", "How many ___ does it take to ___?", "What if you never ___?"
- **An escalating ladder is the spine.** Each rung has a marker and goes further than the last. 3 kinds: time (Day 1, Day 30, Day 365), quantity (1, 5, 25), stage (Stage 1 to 5). 1 line = 1 shot = 1 rung.
- **The skeleton is the 1 constant character:** the same friendly cartoon skeleton on every rung. It stands for "you": it has no name or story, it is nobody's testimonial, and it never speaks.
- **The picture is the proof of the line:** a rung shows what its line says.
- **The voice:** 2nd person, present tense, short sentences, 1 concrete physical image a line. No corporate words.

Diagnose the product first: transformation is not the default. 2 angles may fuse.

| If the product... | Angle | Spine | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| compounds with daily use (a cast-iron pan) | Transformation: "use it for 30 days" | Time | Triumph |
| replaces a status quo that gets worse (a mattress protector) | Cost of doing nothing: "what if you never fix this" | Time | Catastrophe |
| fixes an old way that is overdone (1 concentrate for 9 sprays) | Limit: "how far the old way goes before it breaks" | Quantity | Catastrophe |
| is vivid in an unexpected world or era (a rain jacket in 1850) | Scenario: "what if you had this in ..." | Time or stage | Triumph |

A triumph is you using it. A catastrophe is the old way, or doing nothing.

## What it is good for

It shows time passing and consequences piling up, which no camera can film, and the open question holds a viewer to the end. It has no believed human face, skin, hair or figure: it is weak for proof that rests on a real person, and a result on a body is never its subject.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers, because the question is about their habit, not a product. Good for solution-aware buyers as a transformation ladder, when the brand holds the time points. Weak for a buyer who only needs the offer.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

- A rung that states a number, a time by which a result shows, or a health result is a claim. It comes only from the brand's facts: "Day 14" with a result needs the brand to hold it. A marker with no result is free.
- Absurd exaggeration nobody would read as a promise is free: a laundry pile through the ceiling.
- A catastrophe is about the old way or doing nothing (things, rooms, money), never a named competitor or a medical outcome the brand cannot hold.
- The skeleton is no before and after: its bones do not whiten, thicken or mend. The inside of a body is drawn only as `How it works` gives it.

## What to avoid

- Rungs that do not escalate, or 2 ideas on 1 rung.
- A rung told in the 1st person.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Core benefit` (what the ladder makes felt), `Replaces` (the old way), `How it is used` (the habit in the question), `Visible result`, `How it works`. Blank `Replaces`: ask, or no catastrophe ladder.
- `customer-avatars.md`: `The moment` (the ladder's world), `Customer language bank`. Blank: mark it inferred.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Brand guardrails`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims (every rung with a number, a timed result or a health result), forbidden claims, the offer, the guarantee. A time point not held: the rung keeps its marker and loses its result.
