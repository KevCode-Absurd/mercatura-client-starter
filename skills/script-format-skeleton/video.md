# Skeleton ad as a video: a narrated ladder, rung by rung

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger can say what was asked, sees each rung go further than the last, and knows how it ended.

## How to write for it

- **Every line is narration** by 1 calm narrator, 2nd person, present tense. The skeleton never speaks and its jaw forms no words. Nobody in the picture speaks: the shots are silent under the narration.
- **One shape that works:** the hook question; 5 to 7 rungs (fewer than 3 is not a ladder), each going further; the payoff, which answers the question; a soft call to action tied to the offer.
- **1 line = 1 shot = 1 rung.** A rung line opens on its marker, said aloud ("Day 30."), then 1 concrete physical image. The marker is also that line's on-screen text, in capitals (DAY 30).
- **The voice, by example.** The old way, by quantity (a drawer of single-use kitchen gadgets): "Gadget 1. A garlic press. Reasonable." "Gadget 9. The drawer no longer shuts." "Gadget 23. You move house to store them." Use, by time (a wool jumper): "Day 1. You wear it to the corner shop and stand a little taller." "Year 40. Lawyers argue over who inherits it."
- **Reaction characters** may amplify the arc: fictional adults (a flatmate, coworkers, townsfolk), by role and dress, who go from recoil to awe. They say nothing.
- **The product lands on 1 clean turn:** 1 line where it is named and does its 1 thing. In a triumph ladder that is the question or the first rung, and every later rung is its consequence. In a catastrophe ladder it is the line after the worst rung ("Or you ..."), and the skeleton turns to it.
- **One close that works:** the payoff held, the offer and the call to action as narration over it.

## Length

50 to 70 seconds for a ladder of 5 to 7 rungs. At 30 seconds: the question, 3 or 4 rungs and the payoff. The words it holds are given with the brief.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- **1 place for the whole ladder.** A rung is a state of that place, not a new place: the pile higher, the shelf emptier, the onlookers nearer, the light later.
- Everything the ladder uses is in the first shot, at its smallest: the thing that will pile up, the onlookers at the back. The product comes in at the line that names it. In a quantity ladder the whole row or stack is in view from the start and the skeleton works along it.
- **The rung turn is this look's own transition:** the skeleton holds its pose, the light sweeps across the place once as time going by, and the place settles 1 rung on. In the turn, what is already there may grow, pile up, empty, wear or come nearer. Nothing comes in from out of view.
- 1 or 2 rungs to a clip. A rung cuts between the place wide, the skeleton's eyes close, and what went further, close. 2 rungs in a clip are joined by the turn; otherwise the next clip opens on it. The payoff has the last clip to itself, held.
- The narrator talks at a calm pace: the words a clip holds are given with its length. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip.
- 1 clear action per line, at real speed. The skeleton is already doing the habit in the first shot. Feelings are eyes and pose: eyes widen, shoulders slump, a fist goes up.
- A line's visual note gives the state of the place at that rung and the skeleton's 1 action, in the same words for both every time.
- No words inside the picture, and no signs, labels or calendars on the set. Markers are laid over afterwards.
- No real people or known characters. No horror: no decay, blood or darkness.

## What to avoid in a video

- A new world for every rung: it cannot be carried from clip to clip.
- A count the picture must show exactly ("42 glasses"). Show more than fits; the marker carries the number.
- A ladder that stops and an ad that starts.
