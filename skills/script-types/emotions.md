# Emotion: the feel of a script

The same script type, persona and angle can be written several ways by changing what it makes the viewer feel. A script joins a feeling the buyer already has, so every feel below starts from something the research shows customers feel. A script has a **lead feel** (the hook and the problem) and an **ending feel** (the promise onward): the move from one to the other is the arc of the ad.

## The scale

| Feel | What it sounds like | Where its material comes from | Natural ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fear-of-loss` | What is being lost while they wait. Quiet and exact, never loud. | What waiting really costs: the mechanism's timeline, a season, a thing wearing out | relief, or transformation |
| `frustration` | Fed up with what they've tried. Dry, a little angry on their behalf. "2 winters. Wet shoulders. That's what 90 bought." | Failed solutions; 2 to 4 star reviews | relief |
| `matter-of-fact` | Calm, plain, no heightened feeling. Lets the facts carry it. | Process facts, numbers | matter-of-fact, or pride |
| `relief` | The weight coming off. "Finally." | The word customers use in reviews | joy |
| `transformation` | Who they become and what opens up. Forward-looking, earned. | Identity; what customers do now | pride |
| `joy` | Light, playful, pleased. Fun to watch. | The brand's own humour; delighted reviews; the pleasure of the thing itself (a taste, a feel, a look) | joy |
| `pride` | Quiet self-respect: the kind of person who does this. | Identity tied to the mechanism | pride |

## Rules

1. **True first.** A feel only works on a true fact. Fear with nothing real at stake is manufactured urgency. Joy over a result the product doesn't give is a false claim.
2. **The accommodation, never the affliction.** `fear-of-loss` and `frustration` name what the buyer has done or lost, not what is wrong with them. No pity, no shame, no stacked symptoms, and never an assertion that the viewer has a condition.
3. **The brand's voice wins.** The voice sent with the job says how this brand sounds and what it never says. If it rules a feel out (many brands forbid fear or guilt), don't use it: take the nearest allowed feel and say so in the notes.
4. **Don't stay at 1 pitch.** Lead with one feel, end on another, and let the mechanism be the turn.
5. **The words carry it, not adjectives.** "That's what 90 bought" is frustration. "Incredibly frustrating jackets" is an adjective.
6. **The visual track follows.** Light, pace and performance match the feel of each beat.

## By beat

When a feel is given for a specific beat, it applies to that beat only. Without one:

| Beats | Feel |
|---|---|
| Hook, problem | The lead feel |
| Promise, mechanism | The turn: plain and clear, so it is believed |
| Objection | The buyer's own feeling about what they tried, usually frustration, met with respect |
| Proof, offer, risk reversal | Steady and specific |
| Call to action, identity line | The ending feel |
