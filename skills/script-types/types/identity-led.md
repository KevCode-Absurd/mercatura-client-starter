# Type: identity led

Leads with who the buyer is, and ties it to what the product does. For markets tired of claims and mechanisms.

- **Opens with:** a line about the kind of person. Used up: "Some people make tomorrow's coffee tonight. They're not organised. They're just done queueing." Kept: "There are people who own 1 good jacket and people who own 4 bad ones."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd or 3rd person, present.
- **Beat order:** hook (the identity), what that kind of person does differently (the accommodation turned round: a choice, not a workaround), the mechanism that makes their choice the right one, the promise, proof that others like them chose it (a count, a quoted word), offer in terms that fit them, risk reversal, call to action that repeats the identity.
- **Carried by:** identity, tied to a mechanism. Ask "so what?" of the feature until the answer is a kind of person.
- **The product arrives:** in the middle, as what that person uses.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the identity is a habit kept. Kept, it is a thing bought once and looked after.
- **Not the same as:** a `straight-argument` with an identity line at the end. Here the identity is the first line and every beat is seen through it.
- **Watch for:** identity with no mechanism behind it is a lifestyle ad. Don't flatter or shame; describe. Never assert a personal attribute of the viewer (age, health, weight, money). It turns away buyers who wouldn't stay, which is intended.
