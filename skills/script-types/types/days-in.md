# Type: days in

A diary entry from 1 day: where things stand after a stated number of days of use.

- **Opens with:** the day count and 1 plain fact about that day. Used up: "Day 10. The bottle is about half gone and you've stopped measuring." Kept: "30 days in. It's been rained on 4 times and washed once."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person, present, or 3rd person as narration. 1st person only in a brief a real founder, customer or creator films, with their own days.
- **Beat order:** hook (the day and where things stand), a glance back at day 1 (problem), what the product is doing by now (mechanism), what a person would do or notice (promise, modest), what is not settled yet, the offer sized to the pack or the returns window, call to action.
- **Carried by:** the middle. Most ads show day 1 or the result. This shows a plain day between them: 1 thing that has become habit, and 1 that hasn't.
- **The product arrives:** in the first line, as what the days are counted from.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the count sits inside the first pack and the question is whether to reorder. Kept, the count is days owned or uses (washes, trips), and the question is whether it has earned its place.
- **Not the same as:** `timeline`, which walks every time point and teaches the mechanism at each. This stands on 1 day and stays there.
- **Watch for:** the number. Use a count the brand holds: the days a pack lasts, a stated adjustment or break-in period, the returns window. Say what the product is doing and what a person would notice, modestly. An effect by a date only where the brand states it. A generated presenter is a model, not a customer: tell it to "you" or as narration, with no result of their own. A real person's diary is a brief they film. This type breaks the usual order on purpose.
