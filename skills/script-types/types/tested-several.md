# Type: several, side by side

Several options of the category are put next to each other and judged on stated criteria, with this product as 1 of them.

- **Opens with:** the line-up and what it is judged on. Used up: "3 ways to have cold coffee at home: a carton, a pod, a concentrate. 3 things to check." Kept: "3 rain jackets on a fence. 1 hose. 10 minutes each."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person, present, or narration. 1st person ("I tested") only in a brief a real founder, customer or creator films, who runs the test.
- **Beat order:** hook (the line-up and the criteria), why these criteria (1 line of problem), each option with 1 note against them (mechanism, in parts), where this product comes out (promise and proof), what it does not win on, offer, risk reversal, call to action.
- **Carried by:** the criteria: 2 or 3, named before any result, that the buyer can check alone. The viewer leaves knowing how to judge the category, whatever they buy.
- **The product arrives:** in the line-up from the start, as 1 of several.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the options are kinds of the same thing, judged on what is in them and what a use costs. Kept, they are put through the same thing (water, a wash, a load) and judged on what holds up.
- **Not the same as:** `category-callout`, which names 1 flaw of the whole category and shows nobody else. This puts several side by side, on the same terms.
- **Watch for:** a test nobody ran. A result is said only where the brand holds it: its own test, a third party's, or a fact on each kind's label. With none, give the criteria and what the brand can state about its own product. The others are unbranded stand-ins ("a carton from the chiller"), never a named competitor, and each gets what it does well. A generated presenter is a model, not a customer, and never says "I tested": a real test is a brief a real person films. This type breaks the usual order on purpose.
