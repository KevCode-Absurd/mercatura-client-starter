# Type: timeline

Walks through what happens over time, step by step. Each step is a small loop of problem, promise and mechanism.

- **Opens with:** the first time point, or the reason the timing matters. Used up: "Week 1, your skin argues with it. Here's what each week is doing." Kept: "Wash 1, wash 20, wash 100. Here's what happens to the seams."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person, present or future. 3rd person as narration.
- **Beat order:** hook, the problem in 1 or 2 lines, then for each time point: what is happening (mechanism), what they'd notice or do (promise, modest), the doubt at that point (objection). After the last point: proof, the offer sized to the timeline, a reversal on the same timeline, call to action.
- **Carried by:** the mechanism in loops. It is the natural way to make an ad longer without padding.
- **The product arrives:** at the first time point, as what is being used.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the time points are the buyer's first weeks and the offer is the pack that covers them. Kept, they are the life of the thing (the first use, a season, years; washes, miles, meals) and the offer is the price against that life, with the warranty.
- **Not the same as:** `three-things`: the order here is time, and no point can be moved.
- **Watch for:** only time points and effects the brand can support. Say what the product is doing, not what the buyer will see by a date, unless the brand states it. Honest early effects (an adjustment period, a break-in) belong at their time point.
