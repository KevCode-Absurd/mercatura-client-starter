# Type: confession

A 1st-person story that starts with something the speaker did and never said aloud. The problem carries the script.

- **Opens with:** the accommodation as a confession. Used up: "I'd been buying a coffee on the way in every day and telling myself I liked the walk." Kept: "I own a rain jacket. I still take the car when it looks grey. I've never said why."
- **Person and tense:** 1st person, past.
- **Beat order:** confession (hook and problem), the moment it became undeniable, what they found out (mechanism), what they'd tried (objection), proof from outside themselves, what they bought and why those terms (offer and risk reversal), identity line. The promise is implied until the mechanism is found; state it after.
- **Carried by:** the problem. Stakes early: something risked, lost or embarrassing. More than half the words are spent before the mechanism.
- **The product arrives:** late, as part of what happened.
- **Not the same as:** `failed-solutions`, which is about what they bought; this is about what they did and hid. `witness` opens after the change; this opens before it. If a sibling script already opens on this accommodation, open on another one from the research.
- **Watch for:** who is speaking. A real customer's account is told as theirs. A generated presenter or a character tells the problem as a story and stops short of a personal result: the proof is the brand's. The story has to bridge into the offer, not stop before it.
