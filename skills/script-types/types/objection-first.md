# Type: objection first

Says the buyer's doubt before they think it, and builds the ad on answering it.

- **Opens with:** the doubt, in the buyer's own words, conceded. Used up: "You think concentrate tastes like the stale stuff in a carton. Most of it does." Kept: "You're between a medium and a large. Nearly everyone is."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person, present.
- **Beat order:** hook (the objection, conceded), why the doubt is fair (what happened to them last time), what is different here (mechanism), the promise this makes safe, proof aimed at exactly that doubt, offer, a reversal that matches the objection, call to action. 1 doubt, answered completely.
- **Carried by:** the objection. It shows the brand knows what happened to them last time.
- **The product arrives:** early: the doubt is about it.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the doubt is usually the taste, a reaction, or being locked in. Kept: the price, the size, the weight, whether it lasts.
- **Not the same as:** `how-its-made`, which opens on the maker's choice with no doubt in sight, and `failed-solutions`, which tells the buyer's history. This opens on the buyer's thought about this product, today.
- **Watch for:** the objection must be one the research shows, ideally the nearly-didn't-buy or a reservation from a 2 to 4 star review. Honest disclosures (an adjustment period, who it doesn't suit, what it weighs) are strong material. Don't raise a doubt the product can't answer. Concede the doubt without telling the viewer what they have: "if the last one made you peel", not "your sensitive skin". This type breaks the usual order on purpose.
