# Type: how it's made

A decision the brand made in making the product, told by the brand from inside the workshop. Process becomes the mechanism.

- **Opens with:** the decision, or the thing rejected. Used up: "We steep it for 18 hours. We tried 12 and poured it away." Kept: "We hose every jacket for 10 minutes before it ships. It's a slow way to run a factory."
- **Person and tense:** 1st person plural ("we"), past for the decision, present for how it works.
- **Beat order:** hook (the decision), what it cost the brand (time, money, the batches thrown out), what it does for the buyer (promise), the easy way in 1 line, proof outside the brand's word, who it was for (1 line of problem), offer, risk reversal, call to action.
- **Carried by:** specifics of the making: a dose, a material, a step, a test, a time, a thing rejected. Facts nobody else can say, or nobody else has said.
- **The product arrives:** it is present from the start as the thing being made; named at the offer.
- **Not the same as:** `category-callout`, which spends its words on what others do wrong: here the others get 1 line. `objection-first` opens on the buyer's doubt; this opens on the maker's choice. `demonstration` shows the using; this shows the making.
- **Watch for:** only real process facts from the brand's material. If there are none, use a placeholder and list it; never invent a factory story. The picture is the making (hands, the material, the test), not a founder's face unless the brand has supplied one. A fact every competitor shares still works if nobody has said it, but isn't claimed as unique. The brand's own word needs a higher rung of proof beside it.
