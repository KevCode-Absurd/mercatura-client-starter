# Type: price of the problem

Starts from what the problem has already cost the buyer, in money, time or things bought, and sets the offer against it. Arithmetic, not a story.

- **Opens with:** the cost already paid, as a tally. Used up: "A coffee on the way in, 5 days a week. Add that up for a year." Kept: "3 rain jackets in 6 years. That's what the cheap one costs."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person, present.
- **Beat order:** hook (the tally), what the money or time went on and why it kept being spent (problem and objection together, counted, not narrated), the promise, the mechanism in 1 or 2 sentences, proof, the offer set against the tally in 1 price frame, a reversal that removes the fear of wasting money again, call to action.
- **Carried by:** the offer, reframed. The buyer's question is "is it worth it?", and the answer is what not buying has cost.
- **The product arrives:** after the mechanism, with the price.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the tally is what the workaround costs every week. Kept, it is how many times the cheap one has been replaced.
- **Not the same as:** `failed-solutions` (the same tries told as a story, arguing the cause) and `offer-first` (opens on the brand's terms, for buyers who know the product).
- **Watch for:** the costs are real: from the research (what customers say they spent or tried) or the brand's own numbers. A persona's back-story is not a source. No figure for a competitor's price. Where the sum isn't given, leave the viewer to do it ("add that up") or use a placeholder. A per-day or per-year price only where the brand states it.
