# Type: an honest review

Weighs the product up the way a fair review does. What is not perfect and who it is not for come before what is good.

- **Opens with:** the shortcoming, said flat, before any praise. Used up: "It's strong, and the first glass you make will be too strong. Everyone over-pours." Kept: "It's heavier than the jacket you have now, and it isn't cheap."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person, present, or narration. 1st person only in a brief a real founder, customer or creator films, with an opinion that is theirs.
- **Beat order:** hook (what is not perfect), who should not buy it (1 line), why it is that way (mechanism: the shortcoming is usually the price of something), what that buys (promise), proof from outside the brand, offer, a reversal for the person still unsure, call to action.
- **Carried by:** the concession. A seller who says what is wrong is believed about what is right. 1 real shortcoming, 1 kind of person turned away, then the good in few words.
- **The product arrives:** at once. It is the thing being weighed up.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the shortcoming is usually the taste, a step in preparing it, an adjustment period, or what each use costs. Kept: the weight, the fit, a break-in, the care it needs, the price.
- **Not the same as:** `objection-first`, which answers 1 doubt until it is gone. Here the shortcoming is true and stays true, and the viewer weighs it.
- **Watch for:** the shortcoming is a real property the brand's material holds: a reservation from a 2 to 4 star review, an adjustment period, the price, who the brand says it is not for. Never one invented for effect, or a boast dressed as one. If the brand holds none, this type is not used. A generated presenter is a model, not a customer: no verdict of their own, and nothing about being unpaid. This type breaks the usual order on purpose.
