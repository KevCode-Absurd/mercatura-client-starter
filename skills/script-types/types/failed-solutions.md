# Type: failed-solutions journey

The story of what they tried before, in order, and why each half worked. The objection carries the script.

- **Opens with:** the first failed solution, in the customer's words. Used up: "First it was the pod machine. Every cup tasted burnt and I had a drawer full of plastic." Kept: "My last jacket was half the price. It leaked at the shoulders by its second winter."
- **Person and tense:** 1st or 3rd person, past.
- **Beat order:** failed solution 1, failed solution 2 (each with what it cost), the low point, what all of them had in common (the bridge to the mechanism), the promise, the mechanism as what sits upstream of everything they tried, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action.
- **Carried by:** the objection. Every failed solution is real, from the research. Say why it half worked: that respects the buyer and sets up the mechanism.
- **The product arrives:** as the thing that addresses what the others missed.
- **By how it is bought:** used up, the tries are a run of products started and dropped. Kept, they are the cheap one replaced and the dear one that disappointed.
- **Not the same as:** `price-of-the-problem`, which adds the same tries into a sum and argues the price. This tells them as a story and argues the cause.
- **Watch for:** name kinds of solution, never brands. Don't invent a failed solution the research doesn't show. The reversal matters most here: this buyer's doubt is "I've been sold to before".
