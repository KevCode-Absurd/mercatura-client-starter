# Type: unaware observation

Starts from something in the buyer's life that they haven't connected to a problem. The coldest type, and the one with the most reach.

- **Opens with:** an observation with no problem named and no product in sight. Used up: "There's a cafe on your way to work that knows your order." Kept: "You look at the sky before you look at your calendar."
- **Person and tense:** 2nd person present, or 3rd person narration.
- **Beat order:** hook (the observation), more of the same pattern until they recognise themselves (the accommodation, escalating), the reason behind it (naming the problem, then the mechanism), the promise, what people usually try (objection), proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action, identity line.
- **Carried by:** a long problem: about half the script. It has to make the buyer aware before anything else can land.
- **The product arrives:** late. The viewer shouldn't know there is a product for the first third.
- **Not the same as:** `straight-argument`, which names the problem in its first line. Here the first lines would make sense in a video that sold nothing.
- **Watch for:** the observation comes from research: something customers describe doing before they knew why. Never tell the viewer they have a condition; describe a behaviour and let them recognise it. The turn from observation to problem is the bridge that decides whether the ad works. Ask for little at the end, and give no deadline.
