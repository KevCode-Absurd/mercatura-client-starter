# Editorial collage as a still image: 1 claim, 1 proof, 1 arrow

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees a cut-out photograph with 1 arrow pointing at 1 thing, and reads the headline once.

## The frame

- **1 claim, 1 proof image, 1 directional cue.** Pick the 1 sentence of the argument that a picture can prove alone, and build the frame as its proof.
- **Pick the proof by stage.** Problem-aware: the old way as a photographic cutout, the arrow on the part that fails. Solution-aware: the old way and the product side by side on 1 line, or the product with the part that does the work ringed. Unaware: 1 surprising object from the buyer's own day, the product small or left out.
- **Photograph and paper, plainly apart.** The proof is a photographic cutout with a cut edge; behind and round it are flat paper shapes on 1 paper ground. Each layer casts a short shadow on the one below.
- **Composition:** the proof image is the largest thing, off centre. The arrow runs from the label to the exact part. A third of the paper is left empty for the headline.
- **The product** is its real photograph, cut out, accurate and easy to find: the proof itself, or at the lower corner. Where the ad leaves it out, the frame is the problem and its arrow.
- **A chart** stands in for the proof image only when the brand holds the figures. A comparison is with the old way or the category, never a named competitor, and never a body before and after.

## Words on the picture

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer, in the larger of 2 type sizes, on the empty part of the paper in the top third. It states the claim the picture proves, said straight.
- 1 or 2 short labels, 2 to 4 words each, in the smaller size on a paper strip, each joined by an arrow to the part it names. A label names a thing the brand's facts state.
- 20 words in all. 1 figure at most, only from the brand's facts, written as the brand writes it. A button only where there is an offer.
- Every word is given exactly, in quotes, in the brand's own typefaces. The accent colour marks 1 word or the arrow, not both. No masthead, no picture credit, no page furniture.

## For the image prompt

- Open with: "Original fast-paced editorial collage product explainer." Say what it is: a photographed paper collage seen flat from above, the paper filling the frame.
- The ground is 1 paper, named (off-white textured stock, kraft, squared paper). On it: photographic cutouts with scissor-cut edges, and flat cut-paper shapes in 1 or 2 neutrals and 1 accent colour, named.
- Every layer is separate, with visible paper thickness and a short soft shadow. Even overhead light, no glare.
- Supporting photographs take 1 treatment (black and white with a coarse dot, or full colour with a thin white cut border). The product is always in full true colour: its real shape and printing from its photo, no letter redrawn.
- 1 arrow, cut from paper in the accent colour: say where it starts and what it points at.
- Type is the brand's typefaces in 2 sizes, crisp, on the paper or on label strips. Give each piece of copy in quotes with its place.
- Not a digital vector illustration, not a pinboard or a desk seen from outside. End with: "No publication logos. No copied layouts."

## What fails in a still

- A mood board: 6 cutouts, 4 arrows and no claim.
- The product lost among the paper.
- A chart or a figure the brand does not hold.
- A layout lifted from a magazine or an explainer channel.
- Words on every scrap of paper.
