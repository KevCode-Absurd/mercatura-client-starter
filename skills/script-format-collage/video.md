# Editorial collage as a video: a fast argument, proved in cutouts

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger follows the argument and can say what was proved and which product it was about.

## How to write for it

- **The thesis first.** Write 1 sentence the ad will prove: the customer's problem, the mechanism, a comparison with the old way, or a surprising fact. If it will not go in 1 sentence, it is 2 ads.
- **Narration, written before any picture.** 1 narrator, off screen, in the brand's voice. Nobody in the picture speaks. Then every sentence gets 1 proof shot: 1 sentence is 1 line.
- **The visual note of a line** names 3 things: the proof image (a photographic cutout, a paper diagram, a close view, the old way beside the new), the 1 directional cue (an arrow, a ring, an underline) and the 1 layer move.
- **The on-screen text of a line** is its claim in 5 words or fewer, exactly as it should read. Type, figures and brand marks are laid over afterward, so the picture has none: a label is a blank paper strip.
- **One shape that works:** the thesis as a claim the viewer doubts or did not know; 2 to 4 proofs, each stepping from the last; the product as what follows from them; the offer.
- **The product** comes onto the paper as a photographic cutout on the line that names it, and takes the centre.
- **One close that works:** the cutout large and still, the offer as on-screen text, the narration ending on the thesis, proved.

## Length

20 to 40 seconds, 60 at most. A sentence and its proof are 1 short shot; the brief gives how many shots a clip has. An argument of more than 5 proofs is a longer format.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- Describe the system the same way in every visual note: the paper ground, how the photographs are treated, the accent colour, the kind of arrow.
- The paper ground and the first proof are in the first shot, 1 layer already sliding. A later proof is on the paper when its shot opens, or arrives by the look's own moves: a cutout slides in as a flat plane, or a paper layer wipes across and uncovers it. Say which.
- 1 move to a line: a slide, a scale change, a wipe, a mask opening, or 1 quick camera push toward the proof. Then the cut.
- The move demonstrates: the arrow lands on the part, the old way slides aside, the bar grows to its height. A layer that only drifts is cut.
- Photographs stay photographs and paper stays paper. Layers move as flat planes with short shadows; nothing bends or morphs.
- A chart's shapes are in the proportions of the brand's figures, written in the visual note; the figures go in the on-screen text. No data: prove the line with an object.
- A comparison is with the old way or the category. Before and after is of a thing, never of a body.
- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks. Sentences are short. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip.
- A cut changes how much of the paper is seen: the whole layout, then 1 cutout close. Only the last shot of the ad stays a held layout.
- No words, numerals or logos inside the picture but the product's own printing.

## What to avoid in a video

- A sentence that has no picture of its own, or a picture the narration has to explain.
- 2 arrows, 2 proofs or 2 moves in 1 shot.
- A new paper, a new accent colour or a new kind of arrow part way through.
- A figure with no source, a real or known person, or a publication's look.
