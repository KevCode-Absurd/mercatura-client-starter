# Format: editorial collage

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it an editorial collage

- **1 argument.** The ad proves 1 thesis: the customer's problem, the mechanism, a comparison, or a surprising fact. It is written as 1 sentence before anything else.
- **Every sentence gets 1 proof shot.** The narration is written first. Each sentence is then given the 1 picture that proves it: a cutout, a diagram, a close view, a highlight, the old way beside the new.
- **Real photography and paper, kept apart.** The product is its real photograph, cut out. Round it are plainly separate layers: cut paper shapes, arrows, label strips, charts.
- **1 claim, 1 proof image, 1 directional cue to a shot.** The cue (an arrow, a ring, an underline) tells the eye where the proof is.
- **1 graphic system:** 2 type sizes, 1 accent colour, 1 paper or photographic treatment, 1 family of arrows, charts and labels. It is the brand's own, in the manner of an editorial explainer, and never a publication's identity or layouts.
- **It moves to show:** layers slide, scale and wipe, and the camera pushes in on the proof. Never for decoration.

## What it is good for

It makes an argument faster than a person talking, and a stranger can follow it with the sound off. It puts the old way beside the new and points at the exact part that matters. It is weak for a feeling, for proof that rests on a real person, and for an offer with nothing left to prove.

By stage: strongest for problem-aware buyers (the cause they had not assumed, or a surprising fact) and solution-aware buyers (the mechanism, or the old way beside this one). It can open cold for an unaware buyer on a fact about their own day. A most-aware buyer needs the offer, not an argument.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

- **A statistic is never invented.** Every figure and every chart comes from the brand's facts, in proportion. No data, no chart: prove the sentence with an object.
- **A comparison** is with the old way or the category, never a named competitor. Before and after is of a thing, never of a body.
- **A diagram** shows the mechanism only as `How it works` gives it. A paper shape that stands for an idea stays plainly a paper shape.
- **A person,** where the argument needs one, is 1 fictional adult as a cutout, seen whole. Never a real or known person, a customer, a founder or an expert, and never a child.
- **The product** is accurate and easy to find in every shot it is in.

## What to avoid

- 2 theses, or a list of features dressed as an argument.
- A sentence with no proof shot, or a shot that proves nothing.
- Unrelated collage templates from shot to shot.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: `The moment`, `Tried before` and `What almost stops them` (the thesis and the old way), `Customer language bank` (its words). Blank: write from the product and mark the thesis "inferred".
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `How it works` (the mechanism), `Replaces` (the old way), `Made of`, `Visible result`, `Core benefit`, `Looks like` and `Pack` (the cutout). `How it works` blank: no mechanism is drawn; argue the problem or the comparison, or ask.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette` (the 1 accent), `Typography` (the 2 sizes), `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Proof on file` (every figure), `Brand guardrails`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer, the guarantee. A figure that is not there is not charted.
