# Script format: advertorial static

Load this with a script or image-copy task skill. Where the two differ, this wins.

It is judged on the click, and on whether the first screen of the page pays off what the ad promised.

## What this surface carries

An image styled as an article headline, a list or a comparison, with editorial-sounding primary text. No sound, no believed face. It reads as information, not as an ad, and it hands the reader to a long page where the full argument lives.

So it suits scripts whose load-bearing beat is the **mechanism** told as a discovery, or an honest **comparison**, for problem-aware and solution-aware buyers.

## The ad's job is the click

The image and primary text don't make the whole argument. They open a question the page answers. Decide what is settled in the ad and what is kept for the page:

| In the ad | On the page |
|---|---|
| Hook, the problem named, the promise of an explanation | Mechanism in full, objections, proof, offer, risk reversal |

If the page does not exist yet, say so: this format has no job without it. In the script, the hook line's on-screen text is the headline on the image, the lines are the primary text, and the beats kept for the page are named in the notes, not written.

## The headline is the hook

- Write it as an editor would: what was found, why something happens, how many reasons.
  - "Why vitamin C turns orange on the shelf"
  - "3 reasons a non-stick pan stops being non-stick"
- It selects the person by the problem in their own words, as a statement about the thing, never about the reader's body or circumstances.
- No brand name and no product name in the headline.
- The headline on the image, the first line of the primary text, the platform headline and the top of the landing page say the same thing at the same awareness stage.

## How to write for it

- **Person:** 3rd person. A discovery ("somebody worked out why this happens; here's what they found") or what customers report.
- **Mechanism as news:** promise first, then the mechanism as what was found. Specifics carry it: a measure, a count, a process fact, a named third party, each from the brand's facts.
- **Comparison:** honest only. Compare kinds of solution (a coating against bare steel, a clear bottle against an amber one, a monthly pack against a quarterly one), with facts the brand can support. Never name or show a competitor.
- **List form:** each item is 1 reason, each a different beat (problem, mechanism, objection, proof). The order escalates; if 2 items could swap, it is a list of features.
- **Proof:** third-party and counted proof suit the editorial voice best, and only what the brand holds.
- **Close of the primary text:** what they'll learn or get by tapping, and the effort it takes, if that is known.

## Honesty of the form

It may look like editorial. It must not claim to be: no real or invented publication names, no invented journalists, experts or study results, no fake dates, no real platform's design. Platform rules may require it to be marked as advertising.

## Image

Article layout, a list card or a 2-column comparison. Headline large; 1 supporting line; the product small or absent, and the real product where it appears. 20 words or fewer on the image. If the job asks for no words on the image, this format does not fit: say so and use a plain photo with the headline as the first line of the primary text.

## What to avoid

- A headline that is really a tagline.
- A promise in the ad that the page doesn't pay off in its first screen.
- Borrowed authority: a white coat, a news logo, a statistic with no source.
