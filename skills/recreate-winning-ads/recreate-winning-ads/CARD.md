# New ads from the brand's own winners: rules

A winner is an ad of this brand's that was measured and did well. It already holds what has worked for this brand in market: how it sets type, its colours, its layouts, how it shows the product, how it opens. The new ad is built in that same system for the product in hand. It is judged on 2 things: someone who knows the brand's ads would take it for one of theirs, and it tests 1 thing the winner did not.

## Read the winner in 6 dimensions

1. **Type:** weight, width, case, how the lines are ranked, what container the words sit in.
2. **Colour:** the dominant tone, the accent, how the background is treated.
3. **Format:** the layout family and its grammar: where the product sits and how large, where the words sit, the reading order.
4. **Look:** photograph or drawn; the light; the background; how the product is staged (in a hand, on a surface, in use, alone); people (none, hands, a face).
5. **Copy shape:** how the headline opens, what the second line does, the button, the length, any small print.
6. **Devices:** what recurs: a badge, a ribbon, labelled lines, a quote card, a sticker, a tick list.

Then the 2 things the look does not tell you: the 1 argument the winner made, and what was measured. If the brand's record does not say why it won, say "not recorded". Do not guess a reason and build on it.

## What carries and what does not

- **Carries:** the type system, the colours, the layout grammar, the device, the light, the shape of the copy.
- **Does not carry:** the winner's words, its claims, its numbers, its reviews, its offer, its deadline, its people, and the product it showed. Each of these is true of that ad, at that time, for that product.
- A claim, a number, a price or an offer appears in the new ad only if the brand's facts hold it for this product, now. A review is a real customer's words about this product, word for word.
- A person in the winner is not recast as a lookalike. A new fictional adult, or nobody.

## Keep and change

- Say what is kept from the winner and what is changed, and why.
- Change 1 thing that matters, so the result teaches something: a new product in a proven format, a new buyer for a proven argument, a new argument in a proven format, or 1 new device. An ad that changes nothing is a copy; an ad that changes everything owes nothing to the winner.
- Where the brand's record lists combinations not yet tried (a format with this product, a format with this buyer), prefer one of them.
- In a set, no 2 ads use the same format unless the person asked for that.

## Fit the format to this product

- The layout was built round another product's shape. Re-proportion it for this one: a slot made for a tall bottle is redrawn for a wide pan or a folded jacket. Do not force the product into a shape it does not have.
- The product is shown from its own photos, as it is sold: the same view the format needs (the pack front on, the product out of its pack, in a hand, in use). If no photo shows that view, use a format that needs a view the photos do show.
- If the winner showed the product open, held or in use, this product is shown the way it really works, or closed and at rest.
- If the format needs evidence this product does not have (a number, a quote, a comparison, a visible change), say so and use the nearest format it can fill honestly.

## The words

- Write new words in the winner's shape: its length, its rhythm, where the turn comes. Never its sentences.
- Whether words are set on the image is the person's choice. With none, the picture and the layout carry the argument, and clean space is left where the winner set its type.
