# Every word a customer reads, in this order

A lower rule never buys back a higher one.

1. **Natural.** A whole, correct sentence a person would say to a friend, in words a 13 year old knows, clear on its own to a stranger. Yes: "I used to wake up at 3 with a sore back." No: "Your spine has opinions."
2. **It sells.** It moves the reader from what they have to what they want, with the product as the way across. A count or a spec is a reason only once it says what changes for them. Yes: "Our old dog finishes his dinner now." No: "Dog food, reimagined."
3. **It tells a story.** Something happens to someone. Before the reader knows an answer exists, the copy is the long story of their problem and the product comes late. Yes: "On Friday I borrowed rent money from my sister again." No: "Budgeting is hard. We make it easy."
4. **The brand's language:** who it talks to, its words for things, its tone. Yes, for a warm, plain kettle brand: "It boils 2 cups before you've found the mugs." No: "Elevate your tea ritual."
5. **The brand's devices, last and optional.** A rhythm or a turn of phrase the brand sometimes uses, only on a line that takes it without bending. Yes: "They're light, they grip in the rain, and they last." No: "Lace up. Head out. Feel alive."

A plain word is still the right word. Where no everyday word is exact, say what the thing is in a few words ("a pill with nothing in it"), never a vaguer word that reads wrong ("a fake"). In copy that is read, a price, a percentage and a count are figures ($24.99, 40%, 28 packs); they are spelled out only in a line someone speaks.

A shorter line is made by saying a smaller thing, never by squeezing a sentence until its small words fall out. A line is never made stranger or more clever to sound more like the brand: plain is never off voice.
