# Script format: carousel

Load this with a script or image-copy task skill. Where the two differ, this wins.

It is judged on 2 things: card 1 works alone, and each card makes the next swipe happen.

## What this surface carries

A carousel is the only image format with a sequence: the reader meets the cards in order, if they swipe. No sound. Each card gets a moment of attention. Many people see only card 1.

So it suits scripts with a clear progression: a **mechanism** in steps, a failed-solutions journey, a list of reasons, the old way and the new, or an **offer** broken into its parts.

## 1 beat per card

Split the script so each card does 1 job, in beat order. For 5 cards:

| Card | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook, with the problem | Has to work alone. Selects the person and gives a reason to swipe. |
| 2 | Promise | What they get, in their words |
| 3 | Mechanism | 1 contrast or 1 picture of how it works |
| 4 | Objection or proof | The failed solution answered, or the strongest proof the brand has |
| 5 | Offer, risk reversal, call to action | Real terms, the guarantee, 1 action |

With fewer cards, merge from the middle: keep card 1 and the last card whole. With 6, give proof its own card.

In the script, 1 line is 1 card: its on-screen text is the words on the card, its visual note is the card's picture, and the line itself is that beat's sentence in the primary text.

## Each swipe is a bridge

The job of card 1 is to get card 2 seen. The job of card 2 is to get card 3 seen.

- End a card on an open question or an unfinished thought the next card completes ("3 things wear it out. The first is the one nobody checks.").
- Number the steps when the content is a sequence.
- A card that could be removed without the next one losing sense isn't doing a job.
- If any 2 middle cards could swap places, the carousel is a list, not an argument.

## How to write for it

- **Card 1 is the hook.** The customer's word or the accommodation. No brand name, no product hero for a cold audience.
- **12 words or fewer per card** on the image. 1 headline, at most 1 supporting line.
- **Promise before mechanism:** the promise card comes before the mechanism card.
- **The product arrives late.** Seen from the mechanism card on, named on the offer card. It is the real product on every card it appears in, shown open or in use only as the brand's facts say.
- **A reveal or a turn** gets its own card, so the swipe delivers it.
- **Consistent look.** Same type, colours, framing and product across cards so it reads as 1 thing.
- **The last card** says the action in words, what happens next and the real reason for now, with the offer and guarantee in the brand's wording, in 20 words or fewer.
- **A quote card** is a customer's words, word for word, with the name the source gives, and no face beside it.

## The primary text

Short. It supports card 1 (the same claim, the same awareness stage) and carries what the cards left out, usually the risk reversal and a proof detail. Its first sentence is 125 characters or fewer.

## Common structures

| Structure | Cards |
|---|---|
| Failed solutions | What you tried, why it half worked, what's upstream of it, the fix, the offer |
| Mechanism in steps | The problem, step 1, step 2, step 3, the product that does all 3 |
| Reasons | Hook, reason 1 to 3 (each a different beat), offer |
| What to expect | The first use, then 2 or 3 later points, then the offer. Only points in time the brand can support: weeks for something used up, years for something kept. |

## What to avoid

Before delivery, review the rendered cards in order: the cover's promise, each bridge and the ending must agree with the primary text. Check each product/logo against the actual SKU reference, every quote/number against its source, and any person's identity and hands across cards. Repair a visible defect on the affected card while retaining the approved copy, composition and series look; a repair is not a reason to strengthen a claim. Platform-specific card limits are delivery settings, not a mandatory slide count. These QA and promise-alignment techniques are adapted from the MIT-licensed Creator OS Starter carousel skills; its automated publishing and regeneration commands are not part of this workflow.

- A product photo on every card with a different adjective.
- All the argument on card 1 and filler after it.
- A call to action on every card.
- A person who changes from card to card, or a generated person shown as a customer.
