# The CGI explainer: the study behind the format

What was measured, on 2026-08-05, off 1 ad: a 40.5 second rendered explainer for a drink mix, 720x1280, 30 frames a second, made in the manner of the creator Zack D Films. Every number in sections 1 to 3 was measured off that file. 1 ad shows that something worked once. It does not show it is the only thing that works, so each section says what this app takes and what it leaves.

Taken: the structure, the pacing, the kind of picture, the kind of caption. Left: the narrator's voice (it is a person's), the creator's name and look, and any rule that came from 1 sample and from nothing else.

## 1. Structure: 7 beats

| # | Beat | Share of the time | In the ad studied |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The scale fact | 0 to 18% | A count of people, over a crowd standing to the horizon |
| 2 | The reframe | 18 to 30% | The cause is not the one everybody assumes |
| 3 | The mechanism | 30 to 43% | A torso opens into a glowing cutaway; the camera pushes in |
| 4 | How it worked before | 43 to 63% | A figure drinking from a spring 10,000 years ago |
| 5 | What got in the way | 63 to 78% | A modern kitchen appliance takes something out |
| 6 | The product, as a return | 78 to 88% | The brand is named here for the first time |
| 7 | What the buyer can do again | 88 to 100% | 2 fast pictures of people being active |

Taken: the order, the shares, the brand not named before beat 6, an ending on something a body does and not on a feeling.

Changed for any category: beat 4 is "how it works when nothing is in the way", which can be the past, nature, or a thing made the old way (a coat that lasted 30 years; a pan with no coating). Beat 5 is a habit, a material or a shortcut, never a person or a named competitor.

Changed for this app: the reframe was spoken as "not because X, it's because Y". The app's script check marks that turn of phrase, so the reframe is written as 2 plain statements (see `script-format-explainer`).

## 2. Pictures and edit

| Measured | Value |
|---|---|
| Shots | 13 in 40.5 seconds |
| Average shot | 3.1 seconds; from 1.5 (a close insert) to 5.0 (the mechanism) |
| Camera | A slow push forward or a slow orbit in almost every shot. Never still, never handheld |
| Joins | Mostly continuous: the next world grows inside the shot, or a dissolve of about 0.75 seconds. 1 or 2 hard cuts in the whole film |
| Light | Bright and neutral; dark with a warm inner glow for the mechanism; golden and hazy for the past |
| Focus | Shallow on close inserts and the product, deep on the scale pictures |

A first pass of this study said "all hard cuts". That was wrong: it came from an automatic cut detector that only fires on sudden change and so missed every gradual join. Looked at frame by frame, the film behaves like 1 long camera move. That is the rule taken: the world changes inside a shot, not at a cut.

The kinds of picture, in the order they came:

1. The impossible wide: thousands of rendered figures to the horizon, a slow move forward.
2. 1 figure picked out of the crowd.
3. The everyday object at the centre of the problem, held up, shallow focus.
4. The cutaway: the mechanism lit from inside, the camera travelling into it.
5. The world before: mist, low sun.
6. The close hero: the 1 thing the film turns on, alone and large.
7. The modern place where it goes wrong.
8. The pack set down in a bright place, then filling the frame.
9. The product in use.
10. 2 or 3 fast pictures of what the buyer can do.

From beat 7 to the end a small picture of the pack sat in the lower left, still, about a sixth of the frame high. Taken as an option: it is laid in when editing, from the real pack photo.

## 3. Captions

| Measured | Value |
|---|---|
| Words on screen | 1 to 3 at a time |
| Time on screen | 0.5 to 1.5 seconds; changes on the spoken word, not on a beat |
| Position | Centred, a little below the middle (centre at 60% of the height) |
| Size | Capital letters about 2.5% of the frame height |
| Type | Capitals, a heavy sans, a hard dark outline and a tight shadow |
| Emphasis | 1 word per card in an accent colour: the word that carries the meaning |
| Motion | A hard cut in. No fade, no pop |

Taken: the count, the timing, the position, the size, 1 accented word. Changed: the typeface is the brand's heaviest face and the accent is the brand's accent colour, provided it holds contrast on dark and on light pictures; the film studied used a gold that belongs to that channel's look. Punctuation is kept.

## 4. The narration

What was measured about the voice in the ad studied (its pitch, its accent, its tone) is left out on purpose: it describes a person, and the format does not copy a person. What is taken is how the read is built: very little silence (15% of the running time), no music under it, gaps only where a beat ends. Its pace is not taken: in the app the pace follows the speaker's age. Each sentence sets something up, turns on 1 word ("but", "so"), and lands.

## 5. 6 ways to open

The ad studied opened on a challenge with a number. 1 sample makes that the control, not a rule: 2 ads that share their first words are scrolled past by anyone who saw the first.

What every opening does: it challenges something the viewer believes; it carries a hard number where the brand's facts have one; it plays over a picture nobody could film; it lands in under 3 seconds; it states the problem and leaves the cause for the reframe; it does not name the brand.

| # | Opening | Use when | First picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The challenge: a surprising number put as a dare | The number is large, true and sourced. Cold audiences | The whole affected crowd from above |
| 2 | The flat number: "[Number] of [group] [have this]. Almost none know why." | The category punishes hype | The same crowd, a slower move |
| 3 | The open question: "There's a reason [familiar frustration]." | The problem is familiar and there is no clean number | 1 figure mid-frustration, then out to the scale |
| 4 | The cold open in the past: "[N] years ago, almost nobody had this problem." | What gets in the way is a modern process | Inside the old world, then forward to now |
| 5 | The hidden part: "Your [body, home, kit] has a [part]. Most people are missing what makes it work." | The mechanism is the most interesting thing the brand owns | The cutaway itself |
| 6 | The failed fix: "You've tried [the obvious thing]." | Buyers have already bought from competitors | The failed remedy held up, the scale behind it |

Examples, to show the openings carry across categories. The numbers are placeholders: a real script uses only a number in the brand's facts.

| Opening | Something taken | Something worn | Something for the home |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | "[N] in 5 adults wake at least once a night. Almost none know why." | "[N] in 5 runners are hurt this year. Almost none of them by running." | "[N] in 10 homes fail an air test. Almost nobody can smell it." |
| 3 | "There's a reason you can lie in bed tired and still not sleep." | "There's a reason your leggings go see-through after a month." | "There's a reason your candle stops smelling after an hour." |
| 4 | "10,000 years ago, almost nobody had trouble sleeping." | "100 years ago, a coat lasted 30 years." | "100 years ago, a pan was handed down." |
| 5 | "Your body has a way of winding down. Most people are missing what it runs on." | "Your feet have 26 bones. Most shoes are built as if they have 1." | "Your skin has a barrier. Most people wash theirs off every morning." |
| 6 | "You've bought the blackout curtains and the white noise." | "You've sized up. You've sized down." | "You've tried every detergent on the shelf." |

Choosing: with a sourced number, 1 or 2; without, 3 to 6. Cold and new to the category leans 1, 2, 4; aware of competitors leans 3, 5, 6. In a set of ads no 2 share an opening. Opening 4 moves beat 4 to the front, so the film starts warm and goes cold.

## 6. Where the format and a brand's look pull apart

This is not a brand-look format. It borrows the manner of a documentary, and it will sit oddly beside most brand books. The same answer each time: keep the format's mechanics, and spend the brand's look on the product and the payoff.

- "Our pictures are bright and airy, and this goes dark." Allow the dark middle; it is why the product beat lands bright. Beats 6 and 7 are fully in the brand's light and palette.
- "Our type is a serif." Captions use the brand's heaviest face. If the brand has no face that reads at caption size, a heavy sans is used for the captions and the brand's face for the end card, and that choice is the brand's to make.
- "We can't say that." Then it is not said and not drawn. The words are "supports" and "helps"; a regulated category has its script read by the brand before any picture is made.
