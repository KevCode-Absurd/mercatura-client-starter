# CGI explainer: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

A narrated mini-documentary in rendered 3D. It teaches 1 thing the viewer did not know about their own body, home or kit, at a scale no camera reaches, then shows the product as what puts it right. No presenter. It reads as a lesson until the product arrives.

Judged on 4 things: a viewer could retell the mechanism to a friend in 1 sentence; every picture shows only what the script says; frame 1 is a picture nobody could film; the product, when it arrives, is the product in its photo.

## Structure

1 beat is 1 scene; the brief gives its length. Shares of the running time:

| Beat | Share | The picture |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The scale fact | 0 to 18% | Very large or very small: a crowd to the horizon, 1 fibre the size of a tree |
| 2. The reframe | 18 to 30% | 1 figure or 1 everyday object picked out of it |
| 3. The mechanism | 30 to 43% | A cutaway: 1 thing acting on another |
| 4. How it works when nothing is in the way | 43 to 63% | The same thing in the past, in nature, or made the old way |
| 5. What gets in the way | 63 to 78% | A modern habit, material or shortcut. Never a person or a named brand |
| 6. The product | 78 to 88% | The real product, standing where it is used |
| 7. What the buyer can do again | 88 to 100% | The product in use, then the opening picture again, changed, the product still in frame |

With 3 clips: clip 1 is beats 1 and 2, clip 2 is beats 3 to 5, clip 3 is beats 6 and 7. With more, the last clip is still beats 6 and 7. The script's order is kept: if it opens on beat 4, so does the plan.

## Camera

- `cast.camera`: every shot glides forward. The camera travels; the lens does not zoom. Never still, never handheld.
- A beat is a shot, and the beats are joined by hard cuts. Each cut changes the scale (the crowd, 1 figure, inside the cutaway) and the angle. Where a line is about going inside, the dive through a surface may stay 1 move.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The scale picture: very wide, or 1 small thing very large.
- 1 figure or 1 everyday object picked out, medium.
- The cutaway: inside the thing, 1 part acting on another, very close.
- The same thing made or working the old way.
- The product standing where it is used, then in use, close.
- The opening picture again, changed.

## How the look is held

- `visual_direction`, in the same words throughout: semi-realistic rendered 3D, matte surfaces, clearly computer-made and not a photograph.
- `cast.character` is the 1 recurring figure: a stylised rendered adult, matte skin, fully clothed, seen whole or from behind. Crowds are the same kind of figure, fully clothed. No real person, no child.
- The thing the mechanism turns on (the script's 1 technical noun) is a prop with 1 name and 1 look, a shape and a colour, the same every time it appears.
- Light: neutral and bright; dark with 1 warm glow for the mechanism; the brand's own light and colours from beat 6 on.

## What must be true on screen

- The product (in its pack, where it has one) is the only photographic thing in the film: its real shape, colours and printing, the label never covered. A hand that touches it is a rendered hand.
- A product that is worn is shown real only on its own, folded, hung or laid flat as in its photo. On a figure it is rendered, in its true colour and cut, with no printing.
- The product is not seen before beat 6. It arrives at a cut: the shot of beat 6 opens with it already standing in the place it is used. It is used the way the lessons say.
- A picture is a claim. Show the product doing exactly as much as the script's verb. For "supports" or "helps", show the product taken or used and the process carrying on; never damage vanishing, never a before and after of a body.
- A body is drawn only as far as the script names it. No organ, cell or structure the script does not name; when unsure, a plain diagram shape and not anatomy. No illness, no clinic, no white coat.
- For a made thing (a pan, a jacket, a case) the cutaway matches what the product is made of: its layers, weave or fibre as the brand context describes them. The usual kind it is set against is plain and unbranded. No invented test, gauge or readout.
- A figure of speech (a switch, a gate, a shield) looks like a model or a diagram, never like real anatomy or real structure.

## Narration and sound

- Voice-over only. Nobody on screen speaks or mouths words. `cast.voice_direction` begins "Voice-over narrator:" and then says how the brand's narrator sounds; with nothing given, an adult voice, brisk, dry and certain, no hype. Never the voice, the name or the catchphrase of a real narrator, creator or channel.
- The 1 technical noun is the same word every time.
- Sound is the rendered world's own low room tone. A sound that matters (a drop, a click) is written into its scene's `visual`. No music.

## Words on screen

- Nothing is written in the rendered world: no labels, signs, dials or numbers.
- `overlay` is the 1 to 3 words of that scene's line that carry it, exactly as spoken. A footnote mark only when the script has one.

## First and last frame

- Frame 1: the scale picture, the glide already under way, 1 subject, no product.
- Last frame: the product in the brand's own light, the label readable, held still.

## What to avoid

- A second mechanism, or a list of ingredients or features.
- Fear pictures: decay, wounds, disease.
- A competitor's product, drawn or named.

## What to look at after the render

- Everything is rendered except the product, and it matches its photo at the start, middle and end of its scenes.
- The same figure throughout, and the mechanism's thing has 1 look.
- No letters, logos or numbers in the world.
- Each cut changed the scale, and each shot kept its glide.
- No picture claims more than its line.
- 1 voice across the clips; the technical noun said right.
