# CGI explainer

A narrated mini-documentary in rendered 3D. It teaches the viewer 1 thing they did not know about their own body, home or kit, at a scale no camera reaches, then shows the product as what puts it right. No presenter. It reads as a lesson until the product arrives, about three quarters of the way through.

This file is the whole method for work by hand. `CARD.md` beside it is the craft alone and is what the shot planner is sent. The rules every picture and clip follows are in `skills/production-principles`; model and tool facts are in its `models.md`; the words are written with `skills/script-format-explainer`; `reference/FORMAT.md` is the study the format came from.

**About the name.** The format was studied from 1 ad made in the manner of the creator Zack D Films, whose name the folder still carries. What is taken is the structure: a lesson first, 1 mechanism, rendered pictures, short captions. Nothing that belongs to a person is taken: not a narrator's voice, not a channel's name, look, catchphrases or footage. The output is the brand's own film.

## What it is judged on

1. A viewer could retell the mechanism to a friend in 1 sentence.
2. Every picture and every line is something the brand can support.
3. Frame 1 is a picture nobody could film.
4. The product, when it arrives, is the product in its photo.

## When it fits: the mechanism test

An explainer earns its place when what the product does can't be seen. All 4 must be true:

- **Invisible.** A camera can't show it. If the result can be watched (an egg sliding, a stain lifting), a demonstration is stronger and cheaper.
- **1 thing acting on another.** It can be drawn as a cutaway with 2 parts and 1 action.
- **Supported.** The brand has a source for every link it will show.
- **News.** A regular buyer of the category would not say "obviously".

## Finding the 1 mechanism, in any category

1. **List what the product does that a camera can't see,** from the product's `How it works`, `Key features` and the claims in `brand.json`. Where to look, by what the product is:
   - eaten or drunk: what 1 ingredient does in the body, as far as the claim goes and no further;
   - applied: what it does at the surface of skin or hair;
   - worn: what the fibre or the weave does with heat, water or movement;
   - cooked with or used at home: what the material does with heat, water, grease or dirt;
   - carried or built: how a joint, a bearing, a seam or a shell takes load.
2. **Write each as a chain of 3 links:** A meets B, B does C, so the buyer gets D. "Oil meets hot steel, the oil bakes into a hard film, so food lets go." What can't be written this way is a feature, not a mechanism.
3. **Mark every link:** a brand claim with its source; common knowledge (a school book states it); or no support. A chain with an unsupported link is cut back to where the support ends, or dropped.
4. **Ask 5 questions** of what is left, and drop a chain that fails 2:
   - Does the buyer already know this?
   - Is there 1 sentence in it that makes someone say "wait, what"?
   - Is the thing in the way something they trust or never think about? (A known villain teaches nothing.)
   - Could they retell it at dinner?
   - Would it be interesting if the product did not exist? This is the sharpest test. A lesson that only exists to set up the sale is a pitch in costume, and viewers feel it.
5. **Choose the chain whose missing piece is the product,** and name its 1 technical noun: the ingredient, material or part the film turns on.
6. **Name what gets in the way:** a modern habit, a material or a shortcut in how the category is made. Never a person and never a named competitor.

If nothing survives, say so and use another format. Never bend a claim to fit.

## Keeping it honest

A picture is a claim. The viewer believes what the cutaway shows.

- Words: "supports", "helps", "is made of". Never "treats", "cures", "prevents" or "fixes".
- The product is shown doing as much as its verb. "Supports" is the product taken or used and the process carrying on. It is never damage vanishing, and never a before and after of a body.
- A body is drawn only as far as the claim names it. No organ, cell or structure that is not in the brand's facts or in a school book. When unsure, a plain diagram shape and not anatomy. No illness, clinic or white coat.
- For a made thing the cutaway matches what the product is really made of (`Looks like`, `Key features`). The usual kind is plain and unbranded. No invented test, gauge or readout.
- A number is said only if `brand.json` has it with a source. With no number, open another way (the 6 openings are in `reference/FORMAT.md`).
- A figure of speech (a switch, a gate, a shield) looks like a model, never like real anatomy or structure.
- A footnote mark and line appear only where the brand's guardrails ask for one.
- A category with rules on claims (supplements, skincare, anything for a condition) has its script read by the brand before any picture is made.

## The 7 beats

| Beat | Share of the time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The scale fact | 0 to 18% | A number or a scale that is hard to believe, over a picture as big or as small as the fact |
| 2. The reframe | 18 to 30% | The cause is not the one the viewer assumes |
| 3. The mechanism | 30 to 43% | What happens, as 1 thing acting on another |
| 4. How it works when nothing is in the way | 43 to 63% | In the past, in nature, or made the old way |
| 5. What gets in the way | 63 to 78% | The habit, material or shortcut |
| 6. The product | 78 to 88% | Named here for the first time, as a return to how it works |
| 7. What the buyer can do again | 88 to 100% | Something physical, then the opening picture again, changed |

About 3 seconds a picture, so 9 to 13 pictures. Under 30 seconds beats 4 and 5 become 1.

## The look, the camera and the sound

- **Rendered, and plainly so:** semi-realistic 3D, matte surfaces, clearly computer-made. This is what frames the film as a lesson; a glossy photographic render reads as an advert. The same style line goes in every prompt.
- **People** are stylised rendered adults, fully clothed, whole or from behind. A figure that recurs is made once as a still and attached to every clip it is in. A hand that touches the pack is a rendered hand.
- **The product is the only photographic thing,** from its tagged reference photos, and is not seen before beat 6. A product that is worn is shown real only on its own (folded, hung, laid flat); on a figure it is rendered in its true colour and cut.
- **The camera** glides forward, never still and never handheld. The film should feel like 1 long move: the world changes inside a shot (down from a crowd to 1 figure, through a surface into the cutaway), not at a cut.
- **Light:** bright and neutral, then dark with 1 warm glow for the mechanism, then the brand's own light and palette from the product on.
- **Captions:** 1 to 3 words at a time, cut in on the spoken word, 1 word in the brand's accent colour, in the brand's heaviest typeface with a dark outline, a little below centre. They are added in editing from the narration as written. Nothing is written inside the rendered world.
- **Sound:** the narrator, and the low room tone of the rendered world. No music.

## The method by hand

1. **Read the brain** (fields at the end). Confirm 1 product and find its clean pack photo.
2. **Offer 3 to 5 angles and stop for a choice.** An angle is a whole film: its opening, its mechanism, what gets in the way, the payoff, who it is strongest for, in under 120 words. No 2 share an opening or a mechanism. Say which links rest on which source, and flag anything a regulator would look at, before the choice and not after.
3. **Write the script** with `script-format-explainer`. The pace follows the speaker's age. Measure a first take against it, then fit the words: when the script runs long, words are cut, and a read is never slowed to fill time.
4. **The voice.** Use a voice the brand has the right to use: a person the brand has recorded or hired, or a voice from a voice tool under that tool's licence. Never a voice cloned or copied from another ad, a creator, a channel or a celebrity. Words the voice may get wrong (the brand's name, an ingredient) are respelled by sound in the text the voice reads, and spelled correctly in the captions. **A person listens to the whole read and says yes before any picture is paid for.** Meters can't hear a flat or mangled read.
5. **Stills first.** The opening picture, the cutaway and the product's arrival are each made and looked at as stills, the product still with the product's photos attached and tagged with their jobs.
6. **Clips.** Each starts on its approved still. Join 2 clips as 1 move only where the move does work in the story (into the cutaway, out to the closing picture); elsewhere a short dissolve is safer, because 1 bad clip then spoils only itself. Lengths, modes and prices are in `models.md`.
7. **Captions and join.** `scripts/` holds helpers for work by hand: timing a read, checking it says what the script says, drawing the caption layer, joining clips on exact frames. Each prints its usage. They still carry the first build's name and its voice targets: use them for pace, gaps and loudness, never to match anyone's voice.
8. **Check** against the list below, then ask the sharpest question once more of the finished film: would this be interesting if the product did not exist?

## Faults seen, and what caused them

| What came back | Why |
|---|---|
| Glossy, photographic people | The style line was left out of that prompt |
| A crowd without clothes | Background figures were not described. Say "fully clothed" in every crowd |
| A refusal on a see-through body | The filter reads it as bare skin. Frame only the region the camera travels into |
| A join that jumps | The next clip did not start on the frame of the cut |
| The brand's name said wrong | No respelling by sound, or the check was told the answer |

## Check after the render

On top of the list in `production-principles`:

- Everything is rendered except the pack, and the pack matches its photo at the start, middle and end of each of its shots.
- The same figure throughout; the mechanism's thing has 1 look.
- No letters, logos or numbers in the rendered world.
- No picture claims more than its line.
- The last picture answers the first.
- 1 voice, heard by a person; the technical noun said right; the captions match the narration word for word.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `What it is`, `Key features`, `Core benefit`; `Looks like`, `Pack`, `How it is used`, `Never show`, `Reference photos` (the pack and its use in beats 6 and 7); `Visible result` (if there is one, think again about the format).
- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product: `How it works` (proposed in this pass: the mechanism as 2 or 3 links, each marked with its source, and the usual way and why it falls short). Blank: ask. The mechanism is never made up.
- `brand.json`: claims with sources (every link, every number), forbidden claims.
- `customer-avatars.md`: the avatar's `What almost stops them` and `Customer language bank` (the assumption the reframe turns over), `The moment` (beat 7).
- `competitors.md`: `Gaps worth owning`, `Differentiation summary` (what gets in the way, without a name). `brain.md`: `Market sophistication` (which opening).
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette` and `Typography` (captions, the product beats), `Brand guardrails`, `Motion and sound`.
- `brand-guidelines.md`, `Motion and sound`: `Voice` and `Said aloud` (both proposed in this pass: the voice the brand has the right to use and how it sounds; how the brand's name and hard words are pronounced). Blank voice: ask; do not pick a likeness.

A blank field is a question for the brand, or the line is marked "inferred". A claim, a number or a mechanism is never filled in.
