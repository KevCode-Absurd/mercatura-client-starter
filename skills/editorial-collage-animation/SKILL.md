# Editorial collage ad

1 argument, proved quickly: narration over photographic cutouts and paper layers that slide, scale and are uncovered to show the viewer where the proof is. It is in the manner of an editorial explainer, used only as broad shorthand. The graphic system is original: never a publication's identity, colours, type or layouts.

## What it is judged on

1. **With the sound off, a stranger can follow the argument.**
2. **Every chart and every figure uses real supplied data.**
3. **The product is accurate and easy to find.**
4. **It is 1 graphic system,** not unrelated collage templates.

## What this rests on

- `skills/production-principles/SKILL.md`: tagged reference photos, a still before a clip, the first frame, real speed, materials, sound, words in the picture, the check after a render, spending. Not repeated here.
- `skills/production-principles/models.md`: what the models take, clip lengths, how narration is written, the tools. No model, tool or price is named in this skill.
- `skills/script-format-collage`: how the thesis, the narration and the on-screen text are written. This skill is the paper, the system and the motion.

## 1. Write the thesis

1 sentence the ad will prove. 4 kinds:

| Kind | A thesis | Its proof shots |
|---|---|---|
| The customer's problem | "A shelf full of bottles, and 1 that gets used." (skincare) | The shelf as cutouts; a ring round the 1 bottle; the rest slide aside |
| The mechanism | "A lunch box leaks at the lid, never at the box." (food storage) | The lid as a cutout; a paper diagram of its seal, as `How it works` gives it; an arrow along the seal |
| A comparison | "1 bar does the work of the bottle it replaces." (hair care) | The bottle and the bar on 1 line; the bottle slides off; the bar stays |
| A surprising fact | "A dog spends half its day asleep." (pet) | A paper clock face, half of it in the accent; the bed as a cutout. A chart only if the brand holds the figure |

A comparison is with the old way or the category, never a named competitor. Before and after is of a thing, never of a body. A fact is the brand's, or common knowledge.

## 2. Write the voice-over, then give every sentence 1 proof shot

- The narration is written first and is finished before a picture is chosen. 1 narrator, off screen.
- Each sentence gets 1 proof shot of 3 to 4 seconds. A sentence nothing can prove is cut or rewritten.
- A proof shot is 1 of: a photographic cutout, a paper diagram, a close view (a quick push in), a highlight (a ring or an underline on the part), the old way beside the new, a chart from supplied data.
- Hierarchy in every shot: 1 claim, 1 proof image, 1 directional cue.

## 3. Fix the graphic system

Decided once, written down, and the same in every prompt:

| Part | What is fixed | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Type | 2 sizes in the brand's typefaces: claim and label | A heavy claim size; a label a third of it |
| Accent | 1 colour from the brand's `Color palette`, for the cue and the 1 thing that matters | A warm red on off-white and grey |
| Treatment | 1 paper ground, and 1 way supporting photographs are treated | Off-white textured stock; photographs in black and white with a coarse dot |
| Arrows, charts, labels | 1 family: how an arrow is cut, how a bar is cut, what a label strip looks like | Hand-cut arrows with a blunt head; label strips with 1 torn end |

Colours, typography, icons and layouts are original to the brand.

## 4. Gather the photographs and the proof

- The product photo, tagged with its job. In the collage it is a photographic cutout in full true colour, its shape and printing unchanged, plainly separate from the paper round it.
- Supporting photographs the brand owns, and any verified data. A statistic is never invented. No data, no chart.
- A person, where the argument needs one, is 1 fictional adult as a cutout, seen whole. Never a real or known person, never a child; they claim nothing.

## 5. The storyboard

1 row to a sentence. An example for a candle:

| Sentence (narration) | Claim (laid over) | Proof image | Cue | Layer move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Most candles burn a hole down the middle." | "A hole down the middle" | A used candle seen from above, cut out, black and white | A ring round the hollow | A quick push in to the ring |
| "This one melts to the edge." | "Melts to the edge" | The product cutout from above, full colour | An arrow from a blank label strip to its rim | The old candle slides off left; the product slides to the centre |

Each clip of about 10 seconds holds 3 rows and ends with every layer at rest.

## 6. Stills first

Background plates and collage pieces are made clean, with no words, because exact type and charts are added in editing. The opening still is approved before any clip. Every prompt has 5 parts, in this order:

1. The opening line and the style block, unchanged in every prompt.
2. The references, each tagged with its job.
3. The current claim and its visual proof.
4. The layer motion (clips only).
5. The closing line.

```
Original fast-paced editorial collage product explainer. A photographed paper collage seen flat from above, the paper filling the entire frame. Ground: [1 paper]. Photographic cutouts with scissor-cut edges; supporting photographs [1 treatment]; the product in full true colour. Flat cut-paper shapes in [1 or 2 neutrals] and 1 accent, [accent colour]. [1 kind of arrow]. Blank paper label strips. Every layer is separate, shows its paper thickness and casts a short soft shadow on the layer beneath. Even overhead light.
```

```
Label strips are blank: no words or numerals anywhere but the product's own printing. No publication logos. No copied layouts.
```

## 7. The clips, with the motion guard

Each clip continues from the last frame of the one before. Every clip prompt carries the 5 parts and the motion guard:

```
Layers move as flat planes at real speed: they slide, pivot, scale and are uncovered by wipes and masks, with quick starts and clean stops, then hold. 1 quick camera push toward the proof at most, then still. Paper does not bend, stretch or morph. Photographs stay photographs. Shadows travel with their layers. The product's shape, colours and printing do not change.
```

The moves, and what each demonstrates:

| Move | It demonstrates | Prompt wording |
|---|---|---|
| Planar slide | 1 thing replacing another; the old way put aside | "the [cutout] slides off to the left as a flat plane; the [cutout] slides in from the right and settles" |
| Scale change | The part that matters, seen close | "the [cutout] scales up until its [part] sits at the centre" |
| Wipe | The argument moving to its next step | "a flat sheet of [accent] paper wipes across the frame, uncovering the next layout" |
| Mask | What is inside or behind | "a round opening widens over the [thing], showing [what is beneath] inside the circle" |
| Quick camera push | Where the proof is | "a quick push in toward the [part], stopping on it" |

1 move to a shot. A move that demonstrates nothing is cut.

## 8. Type, data and brand marks

Exact typography, captions, data and brand marks are added in editing, in the 2 sizes, on the blank label strips and the empty paper. Every figure is checked against the supplied data.

## 9. The check after each clip

On top of the shared check:

1. With the sound off, the argument can still be followed.
2. Paper is paper and photographs are photographs, in every frame.
3. Label strips are blank: no scribbled letters.
4. 1 cue to a shot, and it points at the proof.
5. 1 paper, 1 accent, 1 kind of arrow throughout.
6. The product matches its photo and is easy to find.
7. A chart's shapes match the figures.

## When it fails

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| It drifts to a flat digital vector illustration | "A photographed paper collage", "shows its paper thickness", "a short soft shadow on the layer beneath" |
| It shows a pinboard, a desk or a frame from outside | "The paper filling the entire frame, seen flat from above"; take out "board", "desk" and "scrapbook" |
| Scribbled letters appear on the labels | The closing line; take every quoted word and figure out of the prompt |
| The product turns into a paper drawing, or its label blurs | "A photographic cutout in full true colour"; attach its photo again; make it larger in the frame |
| Layers bend, wobble or melt into one another | The motion guard; name the 1 move with its start and its end |
| Everything moves at once | 1 move to a shot; cut the shot in 2 |
| Each shot looks like a different template | The same style block in every prompt, never "same style as before"; continue from the approved last frame |
| It resembles a known publication | Change the accent, the type and the family of arrows; keep the closing line |

## What is not done

- No invented statistic, and no chart without supplied data.
- No named competitor, no body before and after.
- No real or known person, no child, nobody shown as a customer, a founder or an expert.
- No publication's logo, colours, type or layouts.
