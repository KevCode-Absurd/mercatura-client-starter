# Editorial collage: craft rules

## What it is, and what it is judged on

1 argument turned into a fast visual explanation: narration over photographic cutouts and separate paper layers, with arrows, blank label strips and charts. Each sentence of the narration has 1 proof shot. Exact type, data and brand marks are laid over afterward. It is judged on:

1. With the sound off, a stranger can follow the argument.
2. Every chart uses the brand's real figures.
3. The product is accurate and easy to find.
4. It is 1 graphic system from the first clip to the last.

## The look (preset `default`)

`visual_direction` opens with this, filled once, and every `visual` keeps its words:

"Original fast-paced editorial collage product explainer. A photographed paper collage seen flat from above, the paper filling the entire frame. Ground: [1 paper]. Photographic cutouts with scissor-cut edges; supporting photographs [1 treatment]; the product in full true colour. Flat cut-paper shapes in [1 or 2 neutrals] and 1 accent, [accent colour]. [1 kind of arrow]. Blank paper label strips. Every layer is separate, shows its paper thickness and casts a short soft shadow on the layer beneath. Even overhead light."

It closes with: "Label strips are blank: no words or numerals anywhere but the product's own printing. No publication logos. No copied layouts."

- `setting` is the paper ground, not a place: 1 paper, named (off-white textured stock, kraft, squared paper).
- The accent is 1 colour from the brand's `Color palette`, kept for the cue and the 1 thing that matters.
- Supporting photographs take 1 treatment throughout: black and white with a coarse dot, or full colour with a thin white cut border.
- 1 family of arrows, bars and label strips. The system is the brand's own, never a publication's.

## Structure

- A sentence of the script is 1 shot, and a shot is 1 scene. The brief gives each clip's shots and their length.
- Each shot: 1 claim, 1 proof image, 1 directional cue. The cue is an arrow, a ring or an underline in the accent, and it lands on the proof.
- The proof image is 1 of: a photographic cutout, a paper diagram, a close view, a highlight on a part, the old way beside the new, a chart.
- `end` of every clip is its last shot come to rest, each layer named with its place. It is not held: only the last shot of the ad stays still.

## Shots this format cuts between

- The whole layout from above, the cue landing on the proof.
- 1 photographic cutout, close, its cut edge and shadow in view.
- A paper diagram or chart filling the frame.
- The old way beside the new.
- 1 part of the product, very close, ringed in the accent.
- The product cutout large at the centre, to close.

## What moves, and why

- Each `visual` names 1 move and what it demonstrates:
  - a planar slide: 1 thing replacing another, the old way put aside
  - a scale change: the part that matters, seen close
  - a wipe by a flat sheet of the accent paper: the argument moving to its next step
  - a mask opening: what is inside or behind
  - a quick camera push: where the proof is
- A move that demonstrates nothing is cut. 1 move to a shot, then the cut.
- A new proof is on the paper when its shot opens, or enters by a slide as a flat plane or by a wipe that uncovers it. Nothing pops in or fades up.
- `camera`: straight down on the paper, the paper filling the frame. A cut changes how much of the paper is seen. Inside a shot: still, but for 1 quick push toward the proof, starting and ending still.
- The motion guard closes `visual_direction`: "Layers move as flat planes at real speed: they slide, pivot, scale and are uncovered by wipes and masks, with quick starts and clean stops. Paper does not bend, stretch or morph. Photographs stay photographs. Shadows travel with their layers."

## No words in the picture

- No `visual` contains a word, a numeral or a logo, quoted or described. A label is "a blank paper strip". The scene's on-screen text holds the claim in 5 words or fewer, exactly, and is laid over afterward.
- The product's own printing is the only lettering in any frame.

## Charts, diagrams and comparisons

- A chart's bars are cut paper in the proportions of the brand's figures, and the `visual` writes the proportion. The figures go in the on-screen text. No figures in the script's lines: no chart; the proof is an object.
- A diagram shows only the links the script states.
- A comparison is with the old way or the category, never a named competitor. Before and after is of a thing, never of a body.

## People

- Usually nobody is in it, and `character` says so: the subject is the product cutout.
- Where the argument needs a person, `character` is 1 sentence: 1 fictional adult as a photographic cutout, by role, look and clothes, seen whole, in the same words every time. Never a real or known person, never a child. They do not speak and claim nothing: not a customer, a founder or an expert.

## The product on screen

- It is first in `props`: a photographic cutout from its photo, in full true colour, its shape and printing unchanged. Its `look` gives its size against the frame and its cut edge.
- It is on the paper from the shot where the script names it, and takes the centre.
- It slides, scales or turns flat on the paper. It is never redrawn in paper. It opens or pours only as the lessons say.
- The other `props` are the proof cutouts and paper shapes, each with its treatment and its place.

## Sound and speech

- `voice_direction` starts "Voice-over narrator, off screen:" and gives age, pace and 2 traits from the brand's voice.
- All `dialogue` is narration: 1 sentence to a shot. The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the narrator talks.
- Paper is the sound: a slide, a snip, a soft tap as a layer lands. Music only if the brand's `Motion and sound` says so.

## First and last frame

- First: the proof image large and off centre, the cue on it, 1 layer already sliding, a third of the paper empty.
- Last: the product cutout at the centre, sharp, every layer at rest, the top third empty for the offer.

## Avoid

- 2 arrows, 2 proofs or 2 moves in 1 shot.
- A new paper, accent or kind of arrow in a later clip.
- A masthead, a page number or any page furniture.

## After the render

- Paper stayed paper and photographs stayed photographs.
- Label strips are blank: no scribbled letters anywhere.
- Each shot made 1 move and came to rest.
- The system did not change between clips.
- The product matches its photo in every shot it is in.
