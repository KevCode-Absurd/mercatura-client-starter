## 0. How to use this brain (operating instructions for the AI)

When you do CRO work with this file loaded, behave like a senior DTC CRO strategist:

1. **Diagnose before you prescribe.** Identify the page and the job-to-be-done (acquire, convert, raise AOV, retain). Map the request to the relevant section(s) below.
2. **Always tie a recommendation to a principle in this file**, and where possible to a specific tactic, number, or named example. Generic advice is failure. Cite the rule ("per the free-shipping-threshold mode-vs-mean method...").
3. **Big levers before micro-tweaks.** Offer, hero, price/AOV structure, and mobile speed move the needle far more than button colors. Lead with those.
4. **Speak in benefits and to the customer about themselves.** Every section must pass the "So what?" test.
5. **Quantify the opportunity** when you can (impact x ease), and say what to A/B test vs. what to just ship.
6. **Pull the closest brand example** from the swipe library (Section 15) for the brand's niche when illustrating a pattern.
7. **Respect compliance and the brand's voice** (load the brand brain's guidelines). Never invent offers, never fake urgency, never invent proof: every rating, review, customer or sales count, press mention and badge you recommend showing is one the brand really has.
8. **Judge every page on a phone first, in the order a buyer needs things.** Walk it from the top and stop at the first need it does not meet: (1) is this what the ad showed me (the same promise, words, offer and picture); (2) what is it and what does it do for me; (3) why this one; (4) do I believe it; (5) what exactly do I get and what does it cost in full; (6) will it work for me; (7) what if it does not, and when does it arrive. That first unmet need is the first recommendation.
9. **Fit the kind of product (Section 3h).** A product bought again (a serum, a snack, coffee) and a product bought once after longer thought (a pan, a jacket, luggage) need different sections, a different offer block and different proof. Ask of every tactic: does it hold for a pan, a jacket and a serum? If not, say which it holds for.
10. **You can work without analytics.** From the page alone you can find the first unmet need, missing facts, weak proof and friction, and rank them by impact x ease. What analytics add is size (which leak costs most, phone against desktop, which funnel step) and proof that a change worked. Without the store's numbers, give a ranking and no money figure or predicted lift.

**How numbers in this file are marked.** A figure with a named source is that source's (check the current edition before quoting it). Every other figure, including the examples in quotation marks ("10,000+ 5-star reviews", "Only $0.99 per day"), is a **rule of thumb** or an **illustration of form**: use it to rank work or to show the shape of a line, never quote it to a brand as a benchmark, and never put an example's number on a page. Tools are named by what they do, not by brand; pick whatever the store already runs.

This file is the source of truth. If asked for a CRO opinion, ground it here first.

## 1. First principles

- **Mobile first, always.** The majority of paid traffic is mobile. Write copy, design creative, and build layouts for a phone first, then scale up to desktop.
- **Speed is conversion.** A 1–2 second delay costs sales (rule of thumb). Compress images, minimize heavy scripts, remove unused apps, lazy-load. Target under 3s on mobile. Core Web Vitals (Google's "good" thresholds): LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms (INP replaced FID in 2024), CLS under 0.1. Test with Google's PageSpeed Insights.
- **The page is a continuation of the ad.** Ad → landing page → email must carry the same angle and promise. Framework: **TRACE — Tech, Reporting, Creative, Experience.** Make sure the experience from ad → LP → email is consistent and the tracking/reporting is wired. If the ad says "X," the page says "X" above the fold. The first screen takes 4 things from the ad: the hook line (in the headline), the buyer it spoke to, the offer it named (as the first buying option, at the same price) and the picture (the same product, pack and variant, the same image where the brand has it).
- **Social proof is never more than one thumb-scroll away** at any point on the site, especially in the hero. Show number of products sold, number of reviews, "as seen in," UGC, and testimonials everywhere, each only where the brand really has it: a count as the brand states it, press that really ran, reviews word for word.
- **Reduce clicks to purchase.** Every extra click is a chance to drop off. Ad → PDP → cart → checkout, as short as possible.
- **Benefits over features.** Not "aluminum-free" but "stay fresh all day without harmful metals." Translate every spec into a real-world outcome.
- **Talk to the customer about themselves** and how their life is better after using the product, not about the product's features.
- **The "So what?" test.** Each section must answer why the customer should care. If it doesn't, rework or cut it.
- **The grandmother test.** If a third-grader or your grandmother can't navigate and buy, it's too complicated. Simplify and streamline.
- **Big levers first; measure them with A/B testing tools.** Ship obvious wins without waiting; A/B test the big swings (headline, offer, hero, price).

## 2. The CRO Must-Haves (non-negotiables on every store)

1. Mobile-first, fast-loading site and pages.
2. Social proof everywhere — especially in the hero (review count, number sold, "as seen in," UGC, testimonials).
3. Risk reversal displayed directly under the ATC button (money-back / 30-day satisfaction / no-hassle returns). Display it right under Add To Cart.
4. Best offer in the hero image — lead with it, don't bury it.
5. Cart drawer only (slide-out, never a redirect), optimized with social proof, risk reversal, and upsells.
6. An ATC button that stands out — often an off-brand, contrasting color.
7. Visible, auto-applied discounts — site banner, crossed-out old price (a real former price only), new price in a bold color; make codes easy to copy/apply.
8. Iconography for value props; best-seller and review badges on PDPs (where true).
9. Benefit-led copy + comparison chart on the PDP, and aggressive A/B testing of the big levers.

## 3. Page-by-page playbook

The 44-tips framework organizes the store by page type: **Home Page → Navigation/Menu → Collection Pages → Product/Landing Pages → Cart Drawer → Checkout → Thank-You.** Optimize each in turn.

### 3a. Homepage — the 3-Second Test
Look at the homepage for 3 seconds, then look away. You must be able to answer: Can you tell what the brand sells? Who it's for? Is there a clear next action? If "no" to any, the hero needs work.

**Hero section:**
- Headline states what you sell (not just a clever tagline), includes who it's for OR the main benefit, under 10 words, no jargon. Bad: "Welcome to Our Store" / "Redefining the Future of Wellness." Good: "Skincare That Actually Works for Sensitive Skin."
- Only ONE primary CTA visible; action-oriented (not "Learn More"); contrasting standout color; above the fold on mobile.
- Hero image/video shows the product in use OR the transformation; high quality; doesn't distract from the headline; loads in under 2s. Static hero beats a carousel — nobody waits for slide 3.

**Trust section (within the first scroll):** review count ("10,000+ 5-star reviews"), star rating, press logos ("As seen in..."), customer/order count. Trust badges: secure checkout, money-back guarantee, free-shipping threshold, expected delivery time.

**Navigation:** under 7 main menu items, most important categories first, Shop/Products prominent, search bar accessible, cart icon visible with item count.

**Mobile experience:** loads under 3s, text readable without zooming, tap targets 44px minimum, images properly sized, no horizontal scroll, pop-ups don't cover the whole screen.

**Speed:** LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1.

**Common conversion killers (remove these):** slideshow heroes; "Welcome to our store"; too many equal CTAs (decision paralysis); missing trust signals; giant menus (15+ categories); autoplay video with sound; pop-up on landing (let them see the page first).

**Homepage scoring (rule of thumb, from the source checklist):** count 1 for each point above that the homepage meets. Most met = strong, focus on testing; about two thirds = good foundation, fix gaps; about half = significant issues, prioritize hero + trust; fewer = rebuild. The source's bands (35+, 25–34, 15–24, under 15) belong to its own longer checklist and are not a benchmark.

**Quick wins today:** rewrite headline to state what you sell + who it's for; add review count to the hero; remove 2 unneeded menu items; run PageSpeed and compress your largest images; add shipping info above the fold.

### 3b. Collection / category pages
Lead with best-sellers and "Best Seller" / "Most Popular" badges to guide first-timers to trusted choices. Keep filtering simple, imagery bright and consistent, and show price + star rating on each card. Don't make the shopper think.

### 3c. Product Detail Page (PDP)
- Buy button above the fold on mobile; dynamic checkout buttons enabled. In the first screen show what the product cannot be bought without: pack or bundle rows for a product bought again (not a bare quantity box), the size or colour picker with a size guide 1 tap away for a product bought once.
- **Risk reversal directly under the ATC button.**
- Comparison chart (you vs. alternatives) so shoppers don't leave to Google it.
- Best-seller / review badges; verified-purchase labels and what the reviewer bought.
- FAQ on the PDP answering objections before they leave (shipping, sizing, safety, guarantee).
- "What's inside the box" shown clearly in the images.
- Product videos (use cases, styling, unboxing) — they out-engage static images.
- Cost-per-use framing for consumables ("only $0.99/day"), from the brand's real price and pack size. For a product bought once, cost per year of use only if the brand states how long it lasts.
- State delivery time, the returns window and (for a product bought once) the warranty on the page, by the button. These are the facts a brand most often has not written down; if they are missing, that is the first finding.
- Live chat / instant FAQ for real-time questions.
- **The Product Image Hack (see Section 11)** — the image gallery is a mini landing page; pack it with selling info.

### 3d. Cart drawer — treat it like a landing page
Slide-out cart only (never redirect to a cart page). Don't let it be a dead end. Reinforce with: reviews (even on an empty cart drawer), USPs/value props, risk reversal, and upsells/cross-sells. Show the free-shipping threshold with a progress nudge and convenient, high-profit add-ons that help hit it.

### 3e. Checkout (Shopify settings)
- Guest checkout enabled (never force account creation).
- The platform's accelerated checkout enabled (Shopify calls Shop Pay its highest-converting method: the platform's own claim); express wallet buttons; address autocomplete.
- Tipping disabled unless intentional.
- 2–3 clicks maximum from product page to payment confirmation.
- Reassurance at checkout: the returns or guarantee line and the delivery time. A live "bought today" count only if it is wired to the store's real orders; never a typed number.
- Theme speed score 50+ (rule of thumb); lazy loading on; remove unnecessary apps (each app slows the site).
- What can be changed in checkout depends on the store's plan: check before recommending a redesign.

### 3f. Landing pages & listicle advertorials

**Landing page must-haves.** Common failures: inconsistent brand experience (overloaded, slow, brand "fluff"); underused sales hooks (urgency, quantity sold, real-time proof); no ongoing upsell/downsell; unclear angle/benefits/CTA/social proof; no interactive/personal elements; missed UGC/influencer/FOMO; insufficient pixels/scripts.

Fixes and structure:
- **Above the fold:** one headline = a single benefit/promise (avoid multiple messages); subheader reinforcing it (short sentence or bullets); social proof (star rating or testimonial snippet); primary CTA in a bright standout color with an action phrase.
- **Answer the 5 core questions, above the fold and throughout:** (1) What are you selling? (2) Why should someone care (benefits, not features)? (3) What benefits will the customer get (features → outcomes)? (4) How does this compare to other options? (5) How soon will it arrive if they order now?
- **Stay consistent with the ad angle** — the first section mirrors the ad's exact hook (e.g., ad "Itchy feet at night?" → hero "Finally, a solution for itchy feet at night").
- **Below the fold:** problem explanation (why existing solutions fail) → unique advantage / brief comparison → fail-safe guarantees (shipping time, refund policy, no-questions-asked returns).
- **Sticky elements:** sticky CTA always in view; persistent offer-code reminder ("VIP20 applied").
- **Proof-of-popularity signals:** "Over 5,000 sold this week," "Join 10,000 happy customers"; for upsells, "Over 40% bought the matching accessory." These show the form. On a page each is the brand's own figure, dated or wired to real orders, or it is not there.
- **Three types of social proof:** customer reviews; established sources (press/influencer quotes); UGC (authentic video/images).
- **Transparent pricing/payment:** show price per day/use for a product bought again ("Under $1/day", from the real price); offer pay-in-instalments over about $100 (rule of thumb).
- **Personal/interactive elements:** a short video of the real founder or a real customer; shipping clarity ("Ships within 24 hours", if true); demo clips for higher-AOV items.
- **Tech:** a fast landing page builder; the ad platforms' pixels and a heatmap and session-recording tool; pass UTMs; test distinct landers per angle/audience.
- **Leverage UGC/influencers beyond the page:** solicit video reviews with small incentives and embed the best; whitelist influencer handles for ads; create YouTube/TikTok content so brand-name searches surface positive reviews.
- **Match email flows to the ad angle**; keep messaging + discount consistent in the first abandoned-cart email.
- **Close with FAQ + detailed testimonials.**
- **An advertorial is advertising and is marked as advertising.** It is told in the brand's own voice. No made-up narrator, founder, customer, doctor or journalist; no publication's name or masthead; no review score, review count or press logo the brand does not have. A review shown is a real one, word for word, with its source.

**Listicle advertorials.** A listicle is a short presell advertorial centered on buying arguments for a product. Purpose: build interest in the offer quickly before the product page. **Your listicle is a movie trailer for your product page (the movie).** Goal: quick, scannable, engaging, straight to the point; sells key features/benefits and overcomes objections. Best for introducing new products, highlighting unique benefits, or solving common problems.
- **Strategy starts with the angle** (a unique POV/spin that captures the ideal customer's attention and differentiates the product). Three frameworks: (1) USP / key features & benefits the ICP cares about; (2) problems your product solves for the ICP; (3) offering an alternative — disrupting the mainstream way of doing things.
- **Headlines: the "X Reasons Why…" framework** (7 is the workhorse). Real examples: "7 Reasons Why Celebrities Prefer This Whitening Routine Over The Dentist," "7 Reasons Dog Parents Are Switching to Jinx Kibble," "7 Reasons Parents Are Choosing the Mockingbird Stroller," "9 Reasons Why People Love This Shake," "7 Ways Prose Is Changing Your Haircare Game." Spin variations off the angle: "7 Reasons Why This Toothpaste Company Is Breaking The Internet / Sells A Tube Every 90 Seconds / Is Going Viral." These are other brands' headlines, shown for their shape: borrow the shape, never the claim. Who uses it ("celebrities prefer"), how fast it sells and "going viral" are claims a brand makes only when true and on file.
- **Hero (spend the most time here):** USP/offer announcement bar; headline + subheadline; lifestyle/UGC product image + social proof.
- **Content / reasons-why blocks:** keep it simple and minimal; focus on specific problems you solve; list it out clearly. Each reason supports the one overarching angle.
- **Social proof + value props throughout;** bonus points for explicitly labeling which customer problem each piece of proof solved.
- **Call-to-action buy box at the end:** key brand value props, product/offer pricing, CTA button.
- **5 last points:** (1) the hero is the most important section — angles, headlines, subheadlines, hero images; (2) keep it as simple/minimal as possible; (3) each reason supports the overarching angle/headline; (4) consider what core customers care about when choosing reasons; (5) when testing, traffic flows **Ad → Listicle → Product Page.**

### 3g. Post-purchase
- **One-click post-checkout upsells** (no re-entering payment) are the highest-leverage AOV move (a one-click upsell app does this).
- **The winning offer in the source's own tests: the SAME product the customer just bought, at a hefty discount.** That result is for products bought again (more of what they will use up). Test it first there. For a product bought once, a second pan or jacket is rarely wanted: test what goes with it (the lid, the care kit, the second colour as a gift).
- Thank-you-page time-sensitive offers (only if the time limit is real), post-purchase email flows, store credit for return visits, order-anniversary offers.
- Best practices: keep upsells relevant to the purchase; make them time-sensitive; test offers/layouts/timing continuously; use customer data to personalize.
- Category-specific upsells: fashion → matching accessories, seasonal; beauty/skincare → bundles/kits, subscription for consumables; electronics → cases/chargers/warranties, upgrade to newer model.

### 3h. What changes by kind of product

| | Bought again (a serum, a snack, coffee, a supplement) | Bought once (a pan, a jacket, luggage, a mattress) |
|---|---|---|
| How it is bought | Quickly; the first order is a trial | After longer thought, over several visits, compared with others |
| "Will it work for me?" | Taste or feel, what is in it, how to use it, how long a pack lasts, when to expect a change | Size and fit, dimensions and weight, materials, what it works with, care, how long it lasts |
| Sections the page needs | Ingredients and what each does; how to use; how long a pack lasts; what to expect, as the brand states it | Size or fit guide; dimensions and materials; what is in the box; why it lasts; care; comparison with the cheaper kind |
| The offer block | One-time against subscribe with the saving in money, price per day, how often it arrives, pause or cancel; a starter pack; a gift with the first order | 1 price, the size and colour picker, what is in the box, delivery time, returns, warranty; the set that completes it; instalments |
| Risk reversal | Money-back guarantee, cancel any time | Returns (window, who pays postage), warranty, repairs |
| Proof that fits | Reviews about the result and about sticking with it | Reviews from long-time owners, wear photos, tests by a named body |
| After the purchase | More of the same at a saving; subscription | What goes with it; a referral; a review request after weeks of use |
| What to measure | First order, reorder rate, subscription churn (Section 10) | First order, return rate and its reasons, time from first visit to purchase |

A kit of a durable and its refills (a razor and blades, a machine and pods) uses both columns.

## 4. The master CRO tip library (40 tips, explained)

1. **Optimize every campaign for mobile first.** Most paid traffic is mobile — write copy, design creative, build layouts for mobile first; keep load times quick and layouts uncluttered.
2. **Improve load time.** A 1–2s delay costs conversions (rule of thumb). Compress images, minimize heavy scripts, use a fast landing-page tool.
3. **Use a heat map tool** to see where people click/hover; reveals which sections capture attention.
4. **Watch session recordings** to see where visitors navigate, hesitate, or drop off — reveals hidden friction.
5. **Reduce clicks to purchase.** Streamline ad → PDP → cart → checkout; avoid multi-step friction.
6. **Showcase social proof everywhere** — testimonials/reviews/quotes near ATC, in checkout, on landing pages.
7. **Highlight risk reversals.** Money-back guarantees and easy returns near ATC and checkout reduce anxiety.
8. **Lead with your best offer first.** Don't bury discounts, best-sellers, or limited bundles below the fold.
9. **Use large, bright product imagery** in the hero to capture interest and highlight key features.
10. **Make discounts immediately visible.** Cross out the old price, highlight the new price in a bold color, auto-apply where possible.
11. **Try an off-brand Add-to-Cart button** — an eye-catching color that contrasts with brand colors.
12. **Declutter the site.** Avoid excessive banners/pop-ups/text; guide users to checkout.
13. **Use iconography for key points** (shipping trucks, money-back shields, cruelty-free) to communicate value quickly.
14. **Incorporate comparison charts** so users see how you stack up without Googling.
15. **Focus on benefits, not just features.** "Stay fresh all day without harmful metals," not "aluminum-free."
16. **Conduct the "So what?" test** on each section — make it customer-focused.
17. **Simplify checkout** — no more than 2–3 clicks from product page to payment confirmation.
18. **Maintain consistent messaging from ad to page.** If the ad says "Aluminum-Free," reinforce it immediately on the PDP.
19. **Treat your cart page like a landing page** — reinforce trust with testimonials, guarantees, shipping/returns reassurance.
20. **Label purchases in reviews** — show what the reviewer bought and whether they're verified.
21. **Use product videos** — use cases, styling, unboxing; they out-engage static images.
22. **Test upsells and cross-sells** pre-purchase, in-cart, at checkout, and post-purchase to boost AOV.
23. **Add reassurance to checkout** — the returns or guarantee line; a sales notification only if it shows the store's real orders.
24. **Highlight limited-time offers only if true.** Don't fake urgency on always-on offers — it can violate FTC guidelines.
25. **Optimize for your most common screen size** — tailor designs to top mobile/desktop resolutions.
26. **Use real lifestyle imagery** — real people using the product, not sterile studio shots.
27. **Put the facts that remove risk near the button** — delivery estimate, returns terms, guarantee or warranty. Generic security seals add little.
28. **Design for accessibility (ADA)** — font contrast, alt text, navigation that works for all users.
29. **Display "what's inside the box"** — a clear breakdown of components in product images.
30. **Include FAQ sections on PDPs** — answer shipping/sizing/guarantee objections before users search elsewhere.
31. **Speed up page load times** — optimize images, reduce scripts, fast hosting.
32. **Personalize post-purchase upsells** — one-click upsells based on past purchases.
33. **Label customer favorites** — "Best Seller" / "Most Popular" tags guide first-time visitors.
34. **Display "cost per use" for consumables** — "Only $0.99 per day."
35. **Ensure discounts auto-apply** — don't force copy/paste of promo codes.
36. **Add live chat or instant FAQ support** — help in real time instead of email-and-wait.
37. **Use scroll-depth analytics** — see how far users actually scroll; move key sections higher.
38. **Make guarantees stand out** — return windows, warranties, risk-free trials near CTAs.
39. **Test variations aggressively** — headlines, CTA text, offers, constantly.
40. **Ensure your site works for non-tech-savvy users** — if grandma can't buy, simplify.

**Plus the "21 to test" nuances:** use a scroll-depth heatmap; apply TRACE (ad→LP→email consistency + reporting); keep social proof one thumb-scroll away at all times (especially the hero); display risk reversal right under ATC; cart-drawer only with proof + risk reversal + upsells; for post-purchase test the SAME product at a lower price; comparison charts on the PDP; **make social proof unique** — instead of "we have X 5-star reviews," try a count only this brand can state ("10,000+ people have posted about BRAND", if it is true and can be shown); test % vs $ off.

## 5. Three Shopify revenue wins to ship now

1. **Rush order processing.** Charge ~20% of AOV (rule of thumb) as a post-purchase upsell; set your shipping software so these orders print first. Only offer it if rush orders really ship first.
2. **Shipping protection.** A small charge the buyer chooses to add. Always opt-in, never pre-ticked: a pre-ticked charge is a dark pattern and is unlawful in some markets (the UK and the EU among them), and the brand still owes the buyer the goods they paid for. "Nearly everyone takes it" is the source's own experience, not a benchmark.
3. **Free-shipping threshold + cart fillers.** Convenient, high-profit in-cart add-ons that help hit the threshold. High take rate; make the fillers very high-margin.

## 6. Offers, pricing & discounts (like a 9-figure brand)

- **Discount to drive sales, but protect margin.**
- **Dynamic discounting.** Adjust discount depth by product type so you land on a psychologically appealing price (e.g., a premium ring at $195 instead of $235 to drive volume).
- **Avoid "no-zone" price points** ($180, $225) — neither premium enough nor a clear deal. Round to appealing figures ($195, $150).
- **Customers don't remember exact prices** (the example: owning 10 pairs of Air Force 1s and only knowing they're "$90–$120"). This gives flexibility to adjust and promote.
- **DTC is less price-sensitive than Amazon** — on your own site you can test higher prices and creative discounting without constant price comparison.
- **Contribution margin can beat conversion rate** — a higher price with a lower CVR can still produce more total profit.
- **A price anchor must be real.** A "was" price is one the product really sold at, for a real period. Pricing high in order to show a permanent "sale" is a made-up reference price: it misleads, it breaks former-price rules in many markets (the FTC's in the US, and the UK's and EU's), and it trains buyers to wait. Anchor instead on the price of the parts bought separately, the one-time price beside the subscription, or the cost of the alternative.
- **Bundle math beats single-unit (the core lesson).** Same $60 phone case, 60% margin ($36 profit).
  - Brand A: sells one at a time. AOV $60, $36 profit. 100 sales = $3,600 profit.
  - Brand B: monthly "Buy 2 Get 1 Free," 3 for $120 (effectively $40/case), $72 profit per bundle. 100 bundles (300 cases) = $7,200 profit.
  - Brand B makes ~2x despite giving a case away, because the bundle raises AOV and profit per transaction. **Raise AOV with bundles.**
- **Pricing-threshold psychology:** A/B test within thresholds ($49.99 vs $59.99); use sub-$20 thresholds for impulse add-ons; build bundles at higher thresholds to lift perceived value; set free shipping just above a natural barrier ($45 average → free over $50); make value clear as price rises (quality, warranties, exclusives); price just under major thresholds ($49.99, $199.99); scarcity or urgency only when it is true.
- **Segregate SKUs by channel** so an Amazon discount doesn't upset Target/Walmart buyers (products aren't identical), giving pricing flexibility without channel friction.
- **Sales beyond Prime Day:** run sales before and after Prime Day to sustain momentum; treat July as a secondary Q4 with heavy discounting.
- **Continuous new product launches** keep the brand fresh and let you discount older products more aggressively to clear inventory.
- **Giveaways as a marketing tool:** one big annual giveaway ($5K+ value); every dollar spent = entries; higher-AOV products earn more entries. Prize promotions are regulated differently in every market (in the US some states require registration and a bond above a prize value; the source put the cost at about $8,000): check the rules where the brand sells before running one, and prefer one large giveaway to many small ones.

### Q4 offer menu
- **Percentage discounts** (X% off site-wide)
- **BOGO** (buy one get one free; buy one get one 50% off)
- **Bundle deals** ("buy 3 for the price of 2"; "Buy X Get Y"; "Buy X Get Y 50% Off")
- **Free gift with purchase** (tiered: $50 AOV = gift; $100 AOV = higher-tier gift)
- **Free shipping**
- **Limited-time offers** ("Flash Sale: extra 15% off for the next 24 hours")
- **Tiered discounts** ("10% off $50+, 20% off $100+")
- **Limited stock alerts** ("Limited stock — shop now"), only when stock really is limited
- **First decide the goal:** conversion rate or AOV? Then manage inventory and watch competitors' offers.

## 7. AOV & the free-shipping threshold (the mode-vs-mean method)

The free-shipping threshold is a strategic decision that influences customer behavior, AOV, and margin — not just "cover shipping." Set it wrong and you get abandoned carts (too high) or eroded margin (too low), plus a "greedy" brand perception.

- **Use the mode, not the mean.** Brands wrongly add a few dollars to AOV. The average is skewed upward by a small group of big spenders. Real example: a client with AOV $137 set the threshold at $150; but the mode (most common cart value) was $115 — the $13 / 15% gap from mode to threshold hurt conversion.
- **Do a distribution analysis.** Chart purchase frequency (y-axis) against cart value (x-axis). Find where the bulk of orders concentrate (the mode), and set test thresholds just above it.
- **Test different thresholds**, monitoring both CVR and AOV; pick the one that maximizes both.
- **Continually optimize** — the threshold should evolve as AOV changes; it's not "set and forget."
- **Promote the offer** everywhere (site, marketing, value prop), and pair it with high-profit in-cart fillers that bridge the gap to the threshold.
- Offer pay-in-instalments on orders over about $100 (rule of thumb).

**Defining the audience.** Know who buys and why before optimizing. Use the brand's customer avatars (pains, desires, objections) and make sure every page answers the 5 core questions for that buyer.

## 8. FOMO, urgency & sustainable growth

Problems: over-reliance on paid acquisition (turn off ads and sales collapse); weak brand foundation / no differentiation; ignoring content and FOMO (under-using waitlists, referrals, drops).

- **Cultivate sustainable growth:** content creation, community-building, genuine brand experiences that drive organic demand; press, earned media, partnerships for more stable acquisition.
- **Leverage FOMO for trial & loyalty:** limited product drops / small batches, membership waitlists, exclusive collaborations; reward early adopters and loyal fans with perks/limited editions.
- **Produce & distribute valuable content** (videos, guides) and use paid to amplify top content rather than always leading with a product pitch.
- **Strong referral & advocacy program:** multi-tier referrals (gifts, discounts, recognition); gamify reviews and social posts.
- **Only-if-true urgency.** Never fake limited-time offers that are always on — it misleads and risks FTC issues.
- Supporting moves: secure flexible financing for inventory/ad cash flow; adopt a strong tech stack (landing pages, email automation, analytics); collect user feedback to refine product and messaging.

## 9. Trust, social proof & imagery

- **Three forms of social proof:** customer reviews (text/embedded), established sources (press/influencer quotes), UGC (authentic video/images).
- **Make proof unique:** a count only this brand can state ("10,000+ people have posted on social media about BRAND") beats "we have X 5-star reviews", when it is true and can be shown.
- **Proof is real or absent.** A review is a customer's own words, word for word, with where it came from. A rating and a count are the brand's own, as stated, never a default. Press logos are of press that ran.
- **Label reviews:** show what the reviewer bought and that they're a verified customer.
- **Real lifestyle imagery** over sterile studio shots; show real people using the product.
- **Trust badges and guarantees near CTAs.** Enable social image sharing on asset/thank-you pages.

## 10. Retention: subscriptions, churn & post-purchase SMS

This section is for products sold on subscription (bought again). A product bought once is retained through care, repairs, referrals and what goes with it.

**Why customers unsubscribe (and how to keep them):**
- **Cadence mismatch** is the #1 cancel reason — fixed monthly intervals don't match real usage (travel, skipped days) so product piles up. Offer multiple intervals (30/45/60 days) and easy date adjustment.
- **Lack of flexibility** drives outright cancellation. Add pause/skip before the cancel button; send pre-shipment reminders so they can modify.
- **Retention offers on cancel attempt:** downsell — discount the next shipment or add a free product; for high-value subs, a brief call/chat to personalize an offer.
- **Gather real feedback:** concise exit surveys focused on top reasons (too much product, didn't like it); flag high-value customers for direct follow-up.
- **Continuously test:** intervals, incentive levels (10% vs 50% off, free sample vs discounted bundle), and the cancellation flow. Segment by usage/behavior.

**Post-purchase SMS collection & upsell:**
- Underused thank-you-page real estate + minimal phone collection (asking at checkout adds friction) = missed SMS.
- **Trigger an SMS opt-in pop-up 3–5s after order completion** on the thank-you page (goodwill is highest, no checkout friction). State the benefit clearly (exclusive offers, first to know about launches).
- **Post-purchase upsell flow:** relevant recommendation or one-click add to the existing order while the payment token is active; order-anniversary offers.
- **Exclude existing SMS subscribers** to avoid redundant pop-ups.
- **Test** the delay (3s vs 8s) and track sign-up rate + subsequent repeat-purchase rate.

## 11. Visual-tactic playbook (the lessons that lived in the videos/screenshots)

These tactics came from screen recordings and screenshots in the source brain. The lesson is captured here so you can apply it without the video.

- **The Product Image Hack.** A lot of shoppers swipe through your product images before scrolling the page, so the gallery is a mini landing page. Put as much selling info in the images as possible: benefits, social proof, before/afters, free gifts, us-vs-them comparisons. The more they consume in the gallery, the higher the likelihood of purchase.
- **Listicle build (walk-through).** Built as Hero (USP/offer bar + headline + subhead + lifestyle/UGC + social proof) → minimal reasons-why blocks (each supporting the one angle) → social proof + value props woven throughout → simplified buy box (value props + price + CTA). Keep it minimal; the hero gets the most effort. Traffic flows Ad → Listicle → Product page.
- **Archies Footwear (full-funnel example).** Cited as an amazing CRO example end to end: a strong collection page and PDP, a beautiful slide-out cart drawer, and a clean, high-converting checkout page. Study it as a model for the cart-drawer-as-landing-page and low-friction checkout principles.
- **Pela Case bundle builder.** A build-your-own-bundle mechanic that raises AOV by letting customers assemble a multi-item order — an applied version of the bundle-math lesson in Section 6.
- **Post-purchase upsell screen.** The thank-you/one-click upsell surface where the "same product at a discount" offer is presented.
- **3 Shopify revenue tips (screens).** Visual examples of rush-processing upsell, shipping protection, and free-shipping-threshold cart fillers (Section 5).

## 12. Prioritization — what to test first

Bias to **impact x ease**:
1. **Highest impact, do now:** offer/hero, mobile speed, one-click post-purchase upsell (same product discounted), shipping protection, risk reversal under ATC, social proof in the hero.
2. **High impact, test:** PDP comparison chart + Product Image Hack, listicle landing pages per ad angle, bundle/AOV structure, free-shipping threshold (mode-based).
3. **Ongoing:** heat maps + session replays to find friction, then A/B test the big levers (headline, CTA, offer, price). Ship obvious wins without waiting for a test.
4. **With no analytics:** rank by the buyer's order (Section 0, point 8): the first unmet need on the page most ads point to comes first. A store without the traffic to power a test (about 1,000 conversions per variant: rule of thumb) ships the obvious fixes and watches revenue per visitor over months.

> Log CRO wins and losses back to the brand brain's learnings file (What Works / What to Avoid) so this knowledge compounds over time.
