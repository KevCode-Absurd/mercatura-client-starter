# Talking object as a video: a short monologue in clips

The face and its feeling are read in the first second with the sound off.

## How to write the monologue

- **Short sentences, each said whole inside 1 clip.** The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the object's voice talks.
- **It ends on a line that lands:** something it has realised, a declaration, or an ironic truth. Not a question to the viewer.

## How the product comes in

The product is not in the picture until the turn. At the turn, in the last clip, a shot opens with it standing beside the object; the object looks at it and says what changed, in its own terms ("Then that one turned up. She hasn't opened me since." "Then the steel one moved in. I haven't left the hook.").

The offer and the call to action come after the monologue, as on-screen text on the last shot with the product. The object never says them.

## Length

10 to 30 seconds: 1 to 3 clips of about 10 seconds. 1 clip is a complete ad of this kind. It is usually 10 to 30 seconds; where the request gives a range for the buyer's stage, that range wins.

## Writing shots a model can render

- 1 object, 1 place, the same light throughout. Describe the object the same way in every line's visual note.
- The visual notes cut between the face close, the object whole in its place, its damage very close, and what it is talking about as the object sees it. The voice runs on over the cuts.
- The face is eyes, eyebrows and a mouth on a clear area of the object, with small arms only where a line needs a gesture. Each visual note gives the expression as what the features do (brows drop, eyes slide to the product), 1 action, and that the mouth moves with the words.
- The object stays where such an object sits. It leans, sags and turns its eyes; it does not walk.
- No words inside the picture. No people in the picture: the person it talks about is never seen.
