# Paper world as a video: a narrated paper storybook

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger can say what was wrong and what changed it, and sees nothing that is not paper.

## How to write for it

- **Narration, not dialogue.** 1 narrator, in the brand's voice, tells the story over the pictures. A paper world illustrates a voice-over: nobody speaks to camera and no mouth forms words.
- **The look brings no story.** The script does: the problem, what makes it worse, the mechanism, the product, the change. Write each line so 1 paper picture shows what it says.
- **Think in settings:** 2 to 4 places (a bathroom shelf, a rainy street, a kitchen), joined by hard cuts. Put 1 paper transition at the turn from problem to product.
- **Name the paper in every visual note:** "a paper rain cloud on a thread", "pleated paper towels", "a cardstock sun". A thing named without its paper comes out real.
- **The product's arrival:** in view from the shot where the words reach it. It is paper at its true shape and colours (in the collage look, its photo as a cutout).
- **One close that works:** offer and call to action as narration over the product in its setting, held still.

## Length

20 to 45 seconds, 60 at most. Under 20 seconds: 1 setting, problem then product, no transition.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. A clip is a run of short shots with hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- The words a clip holds are given with its length and follow how fast the speaker talks. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip. 1 action to a line.
- A thing is already in the frame when its shot opens. Nothing arrives from out of view inside a shot, except by a paper transition.
- **1 paper move to a shot,** strongest first: a push in through the layered paper planes, near layers passing far ones; 1 flat piece slides across the frame or pivots at 1 corner; a jointed paper figure makes 1 simple gesture (an arm raised from the shoulder).
- **Movement is flat and stepped:** pieces move as planes in a handmade stop-motion cadence. Nothing squashes, stretches, pours or blurs, and no line draws itself.
- **A hard cut may change the view or the setting. At the turn the cut is 1 paper transition,** named in the line:
  - the whole scene lifts at 1 edge and turns like a storybook page;
  - the camera pushes into a cutout doorway or window and the next scene lies inside;
  - the top paper layer peels back from 1 corner;
  - the pieces slide apart and the next scene's pieces slide in and settle;
  - the scene folds flat and the next rises like a pop-up spread;
  - a torn edge sweeps across the frame;
  - the camera passes a large paper cloud in the foreground and comes out in the next scene.
- 8 spoken words or fewer over a transition, so it can be watched.
- Say where the camera stands inside the world: at the paper character's eye level, low under the cardstock buildings, or straight down on the paper street.
- The shots cut between the wide tableau, a close on 1 paper figure and a view straight down. Only the last shot of the ad is held.
- No words inside the picture, no signs or labels on the set. Overlays and captions are added afterward.
- A paper person is a fictional adult by its proportions and dress. No child, real person or known character.

## What to avoid in a video

- Fast action, crowds, pouring liquid: paper cannot do them, and they come out real.
- A paper transition in every clip. 1 or 2 are a delight; 5 are a trick.
- A story that stops so that an ad can start: the product arrives inside it.
