# Format: paper world

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

A paper world is a look. The story comes from the script; this says what the look adds and where it is weak.

## What makes it a paper world

- **Real paper, photographed.** Every element shows grain and thickness, casts a real shadow on the layer behind, and has a torn, snipped or folded edge. Without these it is a flat digital drawing.
- **The viewer is inside it.** The paper world runs past every edge of the frame. It is never a craft object seen on a table.
- **Paper moves as paper.** Flat pieces slide, pivot and pass each other in layers, in stepped, handmade timing. Nothing squashes, stretches or flows.
- **The paper changes the scene.** A page turns, a layer peels, a pop-up spread unfolds: a move between 2 settings is something to watch.
- **1 look to an ad,** given as the job's preset:

| Preset | Look | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| `papercraft` (default) | Layered papercraft world: stacked cardstock, deep layers | The product as hero, a premium or gift feel |
| `cutout` | Flat construction-paper cutout: jointed paper puppets | Comedy, a skit, low-fi charm |
| `handmade` | Handmade stop-motion paper: torn edges, glue seams | Warmth and honesty, short product ads |
| `collage` | Mixed-media collage: photo cutouts, newsprint | An argument, a story with a human subject |
| `origami` | Origami world: everything folded, soft pastels | Calm premium brands, a transformation |

## What it is good for

Paper shows what a camera cannot: a feeling as a place, a mechanism as 1 paper thing acting on another. It is looked at longer than a photograph.

It illustrates a narration: nobody in it speaks and it has no believed human face. It is weak for proof that rests on a real person, and for a texture or a demonstration that must be seen as it is.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers (the problem as a small storybook) and for solution-aware buyers (the mechanism made plain). Product-aware and most-aware buyers need the real product, proof and the offer: another format does more.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

- Show only what the script's lines and the brand's claims state. A mechanism is cut from paper only as `How it works` gives it. No body before and after. A metaphor stays plainly a metaphor (a tangled paper ribbon pulled straight); a real function (rain beading on a jacket) is shown as it is.
- A paper person is a fictional adult, plain from its proportions and dress: never a child, a real person, a known character, a customer, a founder or an expert, and it gives no testimonial. A photo-cutout face in `collage` is an invented adult's.
- The product is made of paper too, at its true shape and colours, every line of its label copy given exactly. In `collage` alone it stays a photograph, cut out with scissors.

## What to avoid

- Paper as decoration: if nothing in it changes, the format is wasted.
- A character who speaks to camera. 2 looks in 1 ad.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are` (the paper character), `The moment` (the settings), `Tried before`, `Customer language bank` (the narration). Blank: ask, or mark the story inferred.
- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Looks like` and `Pack` (the paper pack's shape, colours and label copy), `How it is used`, `Visible result`, `How it works`. Blank `How it works`: ask; never invent a mechanism.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Brand world`, `Color palette`, `Typography` (words on a still), `Brand guardrails`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer, the guarantee.
