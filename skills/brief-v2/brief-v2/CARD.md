# Brief V2: the card

A concept is thought out before it is worded. Fill the thinking block first; write headlines last.

For visual inspiration, invent a new premise around one motif, readable surprise and payoff. Explain why this buyer might wait/share, the true brand connection and hardest production beat. A "viral" source label is not measured evidence. Keep the requested format; ordinary UGC, podcast and native need no fantasy. See [Memorable visual ideas](../../production-principles/references/memorable-visual-ideas.md) when relevant.

Broaden the storytelling device, not just the costumes: competing agendas, excessive effort, reveal, reversal or callback can become a trailer, documentary, interview, POV, animation, still or carousel. Use supported formats. Connect tension → contradiction → payoff → true selling point. No default sitcom/cat/laugh track; staged reactions or failures are not proof.

## The thinking block, on every concept

- **Stage**: Trigger (the buyer does not know the problem), Exploration (knows the problem, looking at kinds of fix), Evaluation (comparing), Purchase (ready, needs a push).
- **Awareness**: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware.
- **The belief in the way**: what this buyer believes that stops the sale, in their words, from a quote or a stated objection. If neither exists, write it and mark it "inferred".
- **The belief to install**: what they believe after the ad, and the fact that holds it up (a claim on file, a demonstration, the guarantee, a review). No fact, no concept.
- **The moment**: where they are, the time, what they are doing when the problem shows. A scene, not a description of a person. Mark it "inferred" if the buyer's record has none.
- **Evidence tier** with its basis: 1 to 4 only from the brand's own measured results; 5 from research with quotes, or a result the founder reports without a number; 6 to 8 from what an ad library shows running. Run time and number of versions are never called "winning" or "proven".

## Used up, or bought once

A product that is used up is decided quickly: the work is the daily moment, the first try made safe, and a reason to come back. A product bought once is decided slowly: most of the work is at Evaluation (price against years of use, fit, materials, what happens if it is wrong), and the Purchase concept takes the risk away.

## 3 variants, 3 different ads

Each variant differs from each of the other 2 on at least 2 of 4: the sub-persona, the feeling, the awareness level (1 step up or down), the way it opens (the opener and the first picture). Say which 2. 3 headlines for 1 person with 1 feeling are 1 ad: do not write them.

## Across the 3 concepts

- At least 2 are for buyers who do not know the product (Trigger or Exploration), unless the ask is to scale a winner or to promote an offer. For a product bought once, the third is an Evaluation concept.
- At least 2 awareness levels. No 2 concepts against the same belief.
- None repeats a concept already made.

## Words

- Claims, prices, numbers, offers and deadlines only from the brand's facts, as stated. No urgency that is not real.
- A customer's words word for word with the source, or "quote needed".
- A point in a list says what the buyer gets; a material or an amount appears only with what it does.
- A headline is 10 words or fewer.
