# Hook Library: Scroll-Stopping Patterns

> Categorized hook patterns with examples. Your hook isn't just the words, it's the visual moment that defines whether Meta treats your ad as new.

**How to read the examples:** they show the shape of an opening. The words of a real ad come from the brand's facts and its voice, and what a generated clip may show is decided by `skills/production-principles` (small, continuous actions at real speed; nothing smashed or dropped for effect).

**Critical rule from Andromeda (Meta's ad retrieval system):** The first 3 seconds define whether an ad is "unique." Changing only the words while keeping the same shot no longer counts as a different hook. Visual swings are mandatory.

---

## The 3 Hook Layers

Every high-performing hook is built from 3 independent layers. You can test each layer in isolation:

| Layer | What It Is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Text Hook** | The words on screen (overlay/headline) | "Low testosterone sneaks up on 1 in 4 men over 30" |
| **Visual Hook** | What the viewer SEES: the scene, action, framing | Camera following someone from behind, outdoor setting, sign on their back |
| **Dialogue Hook** | What they HEAR: voiceover, creator speaking | Same line as text, but delivered with tone, pacing, emotion |

These layers compound. A strong text hook with a weak visual hook still fails, because the visual is what stops the scroll and the text is what locks them in.

## Hook's 3 Jobs (Evan Lee, Motion)

A hook must accomplish all three of these, not just one:

1. **Generates an emotional reaction**: cuts through noise in a crowded feed
2. **Targets and disqualifies**: calls out the intended audience, signals to everyone else "this isn't for you"
3. **Naturally connects to the sales story**: avoids "hook slop" (shocking hook that doesn't connect to the product)

Anti-pattern: Hook slop: using a dramatically striking hook (shooting a gun in the air, extreme claim) that has no logical or emotional connection to what you're selling. The viewer stops, watches, then feels deceived when the product appears. Trust destroyed.

"The hook is 80% of the game." (Evan Lee)

"We choose hook formats based on what different audiences psychologically respond to." (Evan Lee)

## The Winning Hook Formula

> **Pattern Interrupt + Angle + Curiosity Loop = Winning Hook**

- **Pattern Interrupt** → Break the feed. Avoid familiar, polished, "ad-like" visuals.
- **Angle** → Hit a pain point or benefit specific to a persona.
- **Curiosity Loop** → Create enough tension that the viewer HAS to keep watching.

## Hook Building System

**From scratch:** Pick a Hook Visual + Pick a Hook Text + Add an Advanced Technique

**To scale a winner:** Different Hook Visual + Different Hook Text + SAME Overall Messaging/Angle
→ This gives you new visual signals to break similarity groupings, language variation to test motivators, and predictable ways to feed Meta with net-new context.

## Hook Component Trio: Don't Duplicate (Wk 1 Thu coaching)

A hook has three simultaneous components. The most common mistake is using all three to say the same thing:

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Visual action** | What the viewer SEES in the first 3 seconds (scene, action, product, creator) |
| **Voiceover (VO)** | What they HEAR the creator saying out loud |
| **Header caption** | The text overlay on screen |

**The rule:** These three components should NOT duplicate each other. If the VO says "I've been hiding something from you," the caption shouldn't repeat "I've been hiding something from you." Each component should add a different dimension, the visual sets the scene, the VO opens the story, the caption adds a layer of intrigue or context.

When all three say the same thing, you've used one channel's worth of hook power across three. When each says something different that compounds, you've tripled the hook's pull.

|---|---|---|

| ⛔️ **Negativity** | Open with skepticism, complaint, or negative reaction | Problem/Solution Aware: pattern interrupt |

## Advanced Hook Techniques

Small, high-impact tweaks across production, scripting, and editing that lift hooks from good to great:

### 🎬 Production Shots
| Technique | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Big Impact | Sudden movement: dropping, smashing, jumping |
| Slow Reveal | Delay the punchline or outcome to build tension |
| Danger | Imply something bad or risky is about to happen |
| Surprise | Set one expectation, then flip it |
| Satisfying | Visually viral shots: gloop, breaking symmetry, ASMR textures |
| Obscure | Bizarre, unexpected, unrecognizable visuals that break the scroll |

### 🗣️ Scripting Text/Dialogue
| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| Superlatives/Extremes | "The most powerful skincare formula ever approved for at-home use" |
| Power Words | "EXPLODE your energy levels with this clean, plant-based boost" |
| Commands | "Stop wasting money on ____" |
| Surprise | "This chocolate bar actually contains no chocolate" |
| Numbers | "Save over £500 a year with this genius coffee hack" |
| Timeline | "In just 10 minutes a day, you can train your brain to crave less sugar" |
| Obscure | "Why are 18,000 people rubbing mushroom extract on their face before bed?" |

### ✂️ Editing
| Technique | What It Does |
|---|---|
| Slow Zoom In/Out | Add focus or tension |
| Split Screen | Compare before vs. after, product vs. alternative |
| Greenscreen Overlay | Layer in unexpected visuals |
| Angle Emphasis | Zoom, highlight, color pop, or circle key elements |
| Sound Stopper | Abrupt audio cut to regrab attention |
| Quick Cut Moment | Speed up pacing at key moments |
| Transitions | Glitch effects or dramatic cuts to reset viewer attention |

## Hook Strategy Framework (From Soar)

6 hook strategies. For any 1 angle, write 1 hook per strategy: that gives up to 6 different openings from 1 concept.

| Hook Strategy | What It Does | Example (Menopause Supplement) |
|---|---|---|
| **Emotional Driver** | Speaks to pain, transformation, or identity | "I didn't feel like myself for months. This changed that." |
| **Curiosity-led** | Creates tension, mystery, or interest | "The real reason your anxiety spikes after 45..." |
| **Myth-busting / Objection Handling** | Challenges what the viewer believes | "Menopause anxiety isn't just in your head. It's hormonal." |
| **Call-out Hook** | Identifies a niche audience by name or trait | "If you're over 50 and can't sleep, this is for you." |
| **Problem/Symptom Listing** | Relatable list of pain points | "Can't sleep. Snapping at your partner. Brain fog all day?" |
| **Testimonial** | "I tried everything else. This was the only thing that worked." | Social proof as the hook itself |

### Hook Ideation Method: One Angle × All Strategies

Take ONE angle (e.g., "Anxiety Relief") and run it through every strategy type:

| Angle | Hook Strategy | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Anxiety Relief | Emotional | "I was crying in my car during lunch breaks. Not anymore." |
| Anxiety Relief | Curiosity | "Why anxiety gets worse after 50, and what you can do about it." |
| Anxiety Relief | Myth-Busting | "Anxiety during menopause isn't mental weakness. It's chemical." |
| Anxiety Relief | Call-Out | "To any woman in her 40s who's had a panic attack, this is for you." |
| Anxiety Relief | Symptom Stack | "Restless sleep, tight chest, constant overthinking? That was me." |

**This is the correct way to generate hook variations**: not rewriting the same line 5 times, but running one angle through 5 different psychological entry points. Each one attracts a different person.

## Hook Categories (Expanded)

Each hook type attracts a DIFFERENT audience segment. When testing hooks, pair each one with a distinct visual approach, different creator, setting, or style.

### 1. Curiosity / Open Loop
**Pattern:** Create an information gap the viewer needs to close.
**Best for:** Problem Unaware, Problem Aware
**Examples:**
- "I didn't believe this until I tried it myself"
- "The one thing dermatologists won't tell you about your scalp"
- "Everyone's been using their hot tub wrong"
**Visual pairing:** Close-up of product with blurred/hidden result, creator looking skeptical

### 2. Negative / Skeptic-to-Convert
**Pattern:** Open with skepticism, complaint, or dismissal, then pivot to genuine enthusiasm.
**Best for:** Problem Aware, Solution Aware (builds trust through authenticity)
**Examples:**
- "I bought this as a joke"
- "I need a refund"
- "I almost sent it back"
- "I thought bath soaks for hot tubs were fake. I was wrong."
**Visual pairing:** Casual, slightly messy, real: not polished. Creator face = skeptical → delighted.
**Why it works:** Pattern interrupt. Breaks the expected "BUY THIS" frame. The pivot to conviction feels earned, not scripted.

### 3. Identity / "For People Who..."
**Pattern:** Lead with who the customer IS or BECOMES, not what the product does.
**Best for:** Solution Aware, Product Aware
**Examples:**
- "For Bold Photographers"
- "She Wants to Match Her Sister"
- "The Golfer Who Has Everything Except This"
**Visual pairing:** Lifestyle shot of the PERSON, not just the product. The viewer sees themselves.
**Proof:** Identity-led hooks consistently outperform feature-based messaging across verticals.

### 4. Social Proof / Authority
**Pattern:** Lead with numbers, reviews, celebrity/expert endorsement.
**Best for:** Product Aware, Most Aware
**Examples:**
- "250,000+ moms chose this blanket"
- "Rated 4.8 stars by 12,000 golfers"
- "The #1 selling camera bag on Shopify"
**Visual pairing:** Review screenshots, star ratings overlay, user-generated unboxing
**When NOT to use:** New products with < 50 reviews. Don't fake it: lead with product-benefit instead.

### 5. Pattern Interrupt / "Wait, What?"
**Pattern:** Something visually or verbally unexpected that breaks the scroll.
**Best for:** Problem Unaware (get their attention when they don't know they need you)
**Examples:**
- "Your tablecloth is embarrassing"
- "POV: You just wrapped yourself in the softest blanket you've ever felt"
- Bold text-only hook with no image first, then product reveal
**Visual pairing:** Unexpected visual: product used in an unusual context, extreme close-up, "wrong" setting

### 6. Urgency / Scarcity
**Pattern:** Time or quantity pressure.
**Best for:** Most Aware, Product Aware (they're ready, just need the push)
**Examples:**
- "Almost Gone: Spring Collection"
- "These Don't Restock"
- "Last chance before Mother's Day"
**Visual pairing:** Badge overlays ("NEW DROP", "ALMOST GONE"), countdown elements
**When NOT to use:** TOF problem-unaware. They don't care about scarcity if they don't want the product yet.

### 7. Empathy / "I See You"
**Pattern:** Acknowledge the viewer's specific situation, they feel understood.
**Best for:** Problem Aware
**Examples:**
- "If your mornings involve wrestling your daughter's hair..."
- "You deserve a spa day. We're giving you three."
- "Getting matching dresses for both girls costs less than you'd think"
**Visual pairing:** Warm lifestyle: the moment the viewer recognizes themselves in the scene.

### 8. Demo / "Watch This"
**Pattern:** Show the product doing the thing. No explanation needed.
**Best for:** Solution Aware (they know they need something, show them yours works)
**Examples:**
- Spill on a tablecloth → wipe clean in 2 seconds
- Hair oil application → instant shine
- Spa crystals dissolving in hot tub water
**Visual pairing:** Close-up product demo, before/after in same frame, satisfying action
**Why it works:** Proof > promise. The demo IS the hook AND the selling point.

## Hook Testing Rules (From Soar Framework)

1. **Record hooks separately from the body.** Keep them short (1-2 lines for video, headline + visual for static).
2. **Shoot with different tone options or different creators**: same hook words + different delivery = different audiences reached.
3. **Visual hooks must differ.** Starting one version with a creator selfie, another with a bold overlay, another with a demo shot, all targeting the same motivator, is the correct way to test hooks in 2025.
4. **Think of the first 3 seconds as a persona trigger.** Curiosity for one group, urgency for another, empathy for a third.

## Hook tactics in an outside benchmark (Motion, 2026)

An outside figure, not this brand's, read in a sale season and not re-checked here: from $1.3B in Meta spend across 550K+ ads. Two metrics: **hit rate** (how often this hook produced a 10x winner) and **spend use ratio** (winner share of spend ÷ usage share, >1.0 means it punches above weight).

**Top hooks by hit rate** (6 to 11% range, BFCM window):
Newness · Sale announcement · Price anchor · Urgency · Announcement · Offer only · FOMO · New product announcement · Confession · Exclusivity · Curiosity · Giveaway · Event announcement · Bold claim · Reverse psychology · Shocking statement · If-then · Warning · Wordplay · Contrarian · Relatability · Contrast · Direct address · Authority

**Top hooks by spend use ratio** (0.9 to 2.2 range):
Giveaway · Price anchor · Announcement · Event announcement · Offer only · Confession · Urgency · Curiosity · FOMO · Wordplay · Contrast · Myth busting · Call to action first · Contrarian · Exclusivity · If-then · Warning · Shocking statement · Authority · Product announcement · Sale announcement · Bold claim · Direct address · Storytelling · Reasons why

**The high-value overlap** (top hit rate AND top spend use ratio, the ones that win AND scale):
- **Confession**: wins often and captures outsized spend when it wins
- **Urgency**: BFCM-boosted but consistently reliable
- **Curiosity**: evergreen performer across both metrics
- **FOMO**: strong in both, especially during promotional periods
- **Price anchor**: high spend use ratio; when it works, it absorbs budget

**BFCM context:** Newness, Sale announcement, and Offer-only hooks are boosted by the holiday/promotional window. Confession, Curiosity, and Contrarian hooks are more evergreen signals.

Map to existing archetypes in this file:
- Confession → Negative/Skeptic-to-Convert hook
- Curiosity → Curiosity/Open Loop hook
- Shocking statement / Bold claim → Shock hook
- Newness / Product announcement → Demo/Product hook or Pattern Interrupt
- Direct address → Identity/"For people who..." hook
- Authority → Social Proof/Authority hook

## Hook Analytics Funnel: Diagnosing Where a Hook Failed

The Hook Analytics Funnel is a sequential diagnostic. Work through it in order, the first metric that fails tells you where the problem is:

| Step | Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spend** | Did Meta give this ad a chance? (Low spend = algorithm didn't find a responsive pocket) |
| 2 | **Thumbstop** | Did the hook stop people scrolling? (Low thumbstop = visual or opener failed) |
| 3 | **Hold Rate** | Did they keep watching past the first seconds? (Low hold = hook worked, body lost them) |
| 4 | **CTR (outbound)** | Did they click to learn more? (High hold + low CTR = CTA problem or persona mismatch) |
| 5 | **Hard Conversion** | Did they buy / sign up / call? (High CTR + low conversion = landing page problem, not ad problem) |

"You don't always have a creative problem, sometimes it's a landing page or offer problem." (Evan Lee)

Motion's minimum spend threshold before evaluating a hook: ~$300 (or ~3× CPA). Don't kill an ad that never had enough spend to produce signal.
