# Research Frameworks: How to Find Insights That Drive Winning Creative

Source: Motion 2026 Creative Strategy Bootcamp, primarily Sarah Levinger (Wk 2 Tue), Dara Denney (Wk 3 Tue), Jake Abrams (Wk 2 Thu), Eric Philippou (Wk 2 Thu)

## The Research Rule

Before opening any research tool, ask: **"What decision will this research help me make?"** If you can't answer that, close the browser. Research without a decision to serve is busy work.

> "A good strategist finds pain points. A great strategist finds out when those pain points show up, and why." (Sarah Levinger)

## The Three Realities Research System (Sarah Levinger)

Every creative strategy brief needs three types of research, in this order:

### Reality 1: Customer Reality

Who are you actually talking to, in the specific moment your ad reaches them?

**The Customer Reality Cake (3 layers):**

| Layer | What to research | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Generational Insight (foundation) | How this generation relates to money, risk, trust, and the world | Pew Research Center, BLS, Census, news archives |
| Micro-Moments (middle) | The real-world situation where the pain becomes salient | Reddit, TikTok comments, Facebook groups, Quora |
| Emotional Mapping (top) | The specific emotional state in that moment | Amazon reviews (1-star, 3-star, 5-star), podcast comments |

**Four Generational Questions to answer:**
1. What was their childhood informational environment? (analog, early internet, always-on)
2. What is their relationship with money? (scarcity, abundance, debt, investing)
3. Where are they in real life right now? (life stage, family, career)
4. How do they evaluate risk and decide who to trust?

**Three Emotional Mapping Questions:**
1. Which emotion is present in this micro-moment?
2. Is this a private or social moment?
3. Is this an acute (sudden) or chronic (ongoing) struggle?

Anti-pattern: Avatars ("Minimalist Melissa, 34, goes to Whole Foods") are a crutch. They describe demographics, not psychological states. What helps you scale is having a distinct point of view for the brand in a specific moment, not a generalized persona.

---

### Reality 2: Brand Reality

Two parts:

**Brand Health:** What can we actually produce? What is the cost (the Cost Iceberg, above water is spend, below water is dev time, labor, bandwidth, ops load)? Never pitch a creative volume your team can't produce at quality.

**Brand Alignment:** Everyone on the team (founder, media buyer, creative, designer) must agree on: what we're optimizing for RIGHT NOW. Get a one-sentence Alignment Statement: "For the next [timeframe] we are optimizing for [specific goal] because [business reason]."

Run this question monthly: "If you could only optimize for one thing right now, what would it be?" Ask every team member individually. Write down their answers verbatim. Disagreement is information, not a problem.

---

### Reality 3: Presentation Reality: The T-E-E-P Framework

Creative format should match the customer's psychological state in the buying journey. T-E-E-P maps which types of ads belong at each stage:

| Stage | What the customer is doing | Best creative approach |
|---|---|---|
| **T: Trigger** | Becoming aware of the problem for the first time | Emotional, story-driven; name the moment they're already living |
| **E: Exploration** | Looking around for solutions generally | Education, comparisons, "here's what exists" framing |
| **E: Evaluation** | Comparing your product to alternatives | Proof, specifics, testimonials, objection handling |
| **P: Purchase** | Ready to buy, just needs the push | Price, urgency, friction removal, social proof |

**Default weighting:** 25% each

| Goal | Recommended split |
|---|---|
| TOF growth | Skew to Trigger (40/20/20/20) |
| CVR improvement | Skew to Evaluation+Purchase (10/40/40/10) |
| Cost reduction | Skew to middle (10/35/35/20) |

Build a tagged ad inspiration library categorized by T-E-E-P phase so you can browse by customer state, not just by format.

The Valence and Intensity Model (James A. Russell, 1980) applies here: match the emotional intensity of your ad to the emotional intensity of the moment. High-intensity moments (acute pain, immediate trigger) need high-intensity hooks. Low-intensity moments (chronic background dissatisfaction) need softer, empathy-led openers.

> "Your job is to name the moment they're already living, so they feel seen, not sold to." (Sarah Levinger)

## Two Ways to Think About Personas (Dara Denney)

The gap between these two is the creative strategist's opportunity:

1. **Who your ads speak to now:** from the ad account's audience breakdown, or, with no account, by reading the brand's own ads in an ad library and noting who each one addresses.
2. **Who your customers actually are:** read the reviews (in the app: the Insights and Customers screens) and, for each buyer:
   - Extract core personas (name/age/1-sentence description)
   - Write a 2 to 3 sentence emotional narrative per persona
   - Pull 2 to 3 verbatim quote signals per persona
   - Rank by volume AND by emotional intensity
   - End with a media-buyer-style demographic summary
   - Every persona cites the word-for-word reviews it rests on. One with no quotes is marked "inferred"

If persona A is in your ads and persona B is in your reviews, persona B is your next creative angle.

## 5-Step Brand Diagnosis (Dara Denney)

> "Without a diagnosis, you don't have a real strategy. You just have aimless week-to-week testing."

1. Conduct your research (customer reality + brand reality)
2. Analyze your personas both ways (who your ads target vs. who actually buys)
3. Understand your awareness and sophistication levels (map current top performers to the 5 awareness levels, see `awareness-levels.md`)
4. Determine your creative diversity level (are you over-indexed on one persona, one format, one angle?)
5. Arrive at a 1 to 2 sentence diagnosis that becomes your creative anchor for the next quarter

Re-diagnose roughly every quarter as new data comes in. The diagnosis changes when: you exhaust a persona, a new top performer reveals an unexpected buyer, or the platform shifts (as with Andromeda).

## 8-tier evidence ranking

When choosing which ideas to pursue, rank each by how sure the evidence is, and write the basis beside the tier. Tiers 1 to 4 exist only where the brand's own results have been measured.

| Tier | The idea is | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A further version of an ad of the brand's own that has been measured as a winner | Measured: the ad account |
| 2 | A new format for a message that is measured as working (the same angle, a different kind of ad) | Measured |
| 3 | The measured message, run from a creator's or partner's own account | Measured |
| 4 | A new opening or insight for a buyer the account shows is buying | Measured buyer, new idea |
| 5 | A new buyer from thorough research (reviews, communities, interviews), with quotes. Also: an ad the founder reports as a winner with no number | From research, or reported |
| 6 | Something several competitors are each running in many versions for a long time | Inferred from an ad library |
| 7 | 1 competitor's longest-running ad | Inferred, weak |
| 8 | "A competitor did it" | Inferred, weakest. Never used alone |

What an ad library can and cannot tell you. It shows what is running, when it started, how many versions of 1 idea there are, the format, and the opening words. It does not show spend, sales or return. "This competitor has run this ad for 60 days" is not a result: it may be their best ad, or it may sit in a small retargeting campaign. Write "running for 60 days in 5 versions", never "a winner", and never brief from tiers 6 to 8 alone when anything better exists.

For a shared reference board, use its rendered overview when the text reader returns only a JavaScript shell. A title and listed ad count verify the board, not its individual ads or results. Before adapting a selected ad, inspect that ad and record its source URL, opening hook, visual device, buyer emotion/desire, claim and product role. Keep presentation format, message job, buyer desire, audience and product category as separate tags: a board called "family" is not a new ad format. Borrow a feasible device for the active brand's supported argument; competitor facts, offers, footage, identities and quoted reviews do not transfer. A reference that cannot be inspected stays a gap, never an invented analysis.

## Three Signal Sources for Research (Jake Abrams)

Every creative strategy research session draws from 3 sources simultaneously:

| Signal Source | What it tells you | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| **Ad account performance** | What's already working, what angles have been exhausted, where the funnel breaks | The brand's ad account: spend, purchases, hook rate and hold rate by ad |
| **Customer/brand signal** | First-person language from real buyers, emotional vocabulary, objections, desires | Amazon reviews, Trustpilot, brand site reviews, sales calls, CRM data |
| **External organic signal** | How the niche talks about the problem in unguarded moments; language people actually use (e.g., "semaglutide" vs. "GLP-1") | TikTok, Reddit, Instagram communities |

Research cadence: a deep pass monthly or quarterly (all 3 realities) and a 20-minute refresh weekly.

Every insight says where it came from. Never accept a persona or a moment without the word-for-word source behind it.

## Common Symptoms of "Nothing Working" (Eric Philippou)

When an account is stuck, diagnose which of these four is the root cause:

| Symptom | Root Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Boring** | The ads look like everything else in the niche | Study how buyers in the niche talk when nobody is selling to them; borrow the structure of what holds attention, never the footage or the words |
| **Complexity** | Too many ideas in one ad, no clear singular message | Strip back to one benefit, one moment, one persona |
| **CPM Hell** | High CPMs signal you're in a saturated audience cluster | New personas, new angles: not just new visuals |
| **No Clear UVP** | The brand can't say why it's different in one sentence | Pull the one benefit that doesn't appear in competitor reviews, that's the UVP |

In "nothing working" mode: less than 10% of creative output should be iterations. Go wide, big swings, net-new concepts. Use the Battleship Mindset: test many different things, only iterate when you get a hit (even a partial metric win, improved hook rate, improved hold time, counts).

Redefine "win" beyond ROAS: a dramatic improvement in a single funnel metric (e.g., CPC drops 40%, hook rate jumps to 40%+) is worth iterating on even if ROAS isn't there yet.

## 4 Ways to Gut-Check AI Research Output (Joanna Wallace)

AI can hallucinate personas and micro-moments. Before acting on AI-generated research:

1. **Receipts**: Did it cite the specific review/post/comment that gave it this insight?
2. **Sample size**: How many data points supported this claim? One review isn't a persona.
3. **Second AI**: Put the same question into a different LLM. Do they agree?
4. **Trigger check**: Does this micro-moment actually trigger a purchase decision, or is it just an interesting fact?
5. **SKU-blending check**: Did the AI accidentally blend insights from multiple products or categories into one persona?
