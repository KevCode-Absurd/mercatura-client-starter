# Creative Scaling: How to Multiply Winners Without Killing Them

**Source:** Motion 2026 Creative Strategy Bootcamp, primarily Sophia Beauvoir / Ready Set (Wk 7 Tue), Viti Videtta / Happy Mammoth (Wk 7 Tue), Janae LeVander / Caraway (Wk 7 Tue), Eric Philippou (Wk 2 Thu)

---

## The Core Distinction: Explore vs. Exploit

Every week is either an Explore week or an Exploit week (or a split of both):

**Explore:** Testing new concepts. No winning angle yet. Looking for new personas. Launching new SKUs. Go wide: big swings, net-new ideas.

**Exploit:** You know what works. Double down. Turn winning angles into new formats. Push winning personas with new creators. Drive winning SKUs with format/offer variations.

The mistake most brands make: staying in Explore mode when they've already found a winner. "There is no tomorrow in digital": the window to scale a winner closes faster than you think.

## The Andromeda Reality (Sophia Beauvoir, Ready Set)

Meta's Andromeda system is a re-engineered AI retrieval system. It no longer matches audiences to campaigns, it matches individual users to specific creative signals. This changes everything about iteration.

**What Andromeda does:**
- Clusters similar ads into a single retrieval entity, so 10 versions of the same image with minor text tweaks are not 10 tests, they're 1 test with diluted spend
- Rewards meaningful diversity over minor variations
- Predicts fatigue and limits delivery if hook rate is weak
- Treats the first 3 seconds as the "unique signal" fingerprint of an ad

**The implication for iteration:** Your iteration strategy must shift from small tweaks to meaningful pivots. Minor variations no longer register as new ads to the algorithm. This is not optional.

> "Creative is no longer what you test, it's the targeting itself." (Sophia Beauvoir)

> "Andromeda rewards meaningful diversity over minor variations. Your iteration strategy has to shift from small tweaks to meaningful pivots." (Sophia Beauvoir)

## Pre vs. Post-Andromeda Iteration

| | Pre-Andromeda | Post-Andromeda |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-hanging fruit** | Hook/headline tweaks, visual color changes, minor copy edits | Format swaps (static→video), length tests, message reorders, talent refreshes |
| **Expansion** | "Higher level tests" on the same concept | New personas, new pain points, entire body rewrites |

If an ad doesn't spend, it's not necessarily bad creative, it may be that Andromeda can't find a responsive pocket for that specific signal. The solution is not more budget; it's a more differentiated signal.

## 7 Levers to Scale Meaningfully Post-Andromeda (Sophia Beauvoir)

In order from smallest-swing to biggest-swing:

1. **Visual refreshes**: New footage, same script. Build a footage library so you can pull B-roll on demand without reshooting from scratch.
2. **Talent refreshes**: Single creator → multi-creator, or swap the creator entirely. Same script, new face.
3. **Mashups**: Combine winning elements from two different top performers. High-thumbstop ad + high-CVR body = new test.
4. **Persona testing**: Same product, different buyer framing. "This is for you if you're a busy mom" vs. "This is for you if you travel 3 weeks a month."
5. **Pain point testing**: Same persona, different entry pain. The person is the same; the micro-moment that triggers them is different.
6. **Entire body changes**: Same hook, completely new mid-section. Rewrite the story around the same opening.
7. **Visual format testing**: Static ↔ GIF ↔ short video ↔ long video ↔ carousel. Format itself is a creative variable.

## Ad Families (Sophia Beauvoir)

When a winner scales, don't let it run alone. Build an Ad Family around it: branches of content rooted in the same winning concept, organized by:
- **Persona variations**: same product, different buyer
- **Desire variations**: same person, different outcome they want
- **Awareness level variations**: problem-unaware → most-aware progression

Anti-pattern: letting a base winner run on its own without building an ad family around it. When it fatigues, you have nothing queued to replace it.

## The Creative Scaling Engine (Viti Videtta, Happy Mammoth)

A cyclical process: not a linear one. Every winner feeds the next iteration:

**Winner → Deconstruct → Multiply → Test → Scale → Fatigue → Refresh → (back to Multiply)**

### Step 1: Deconstruct the winner
Break it into 4 components:
- **Hook**: the first 3 seconds
- **Angle**: the reason to buy
- **Format**: the production style
- **Emotional driver**: what feeling it creates

### Step 2: Multiply
Run 3 to 5 batches of iterations using the Iteration Levers (see table below).

### Step 3: Test, then Scale validated winners

### Step 4: Monitor for Fatigue
Watch for: rising CPA, dropping CTR, high frequency.

### Step 5: Refresh
New script, new hooks, new visuals, but keep the "soul" of what made the original work. You're not starting over; you're building version 2.

> "You don't scale an ad. You scale the mechanism behind the ad." (Viti Videtta)

> "Your winner ad isn't the finish line. It's the blueprint for your next 20 ads." (Viti Videtta)

## Iteration Levers for Multiplying a Winner

Sweet spot per winner: 3 to 5 batches of iteration before you've exhausted the concept.

| Lever | What Changes | What Stays |
|---|---|---|
| Different hooks | First 3 seconds | Everything else |
| Same script, different creator | Who delivers it | The words |
| Different script, same creator | The words | The face |
| Same structure, different script | The architecture | The content |
| Same script, different B-roll | The visuals | The voiceover |
| Same concept, different format | Static→Video, Video→Carousel | The core message |

---

## Green/Yellow Cheat Code (Viti Videtta)

When sizing iterations, use this as a gut check:

- **Green (winner):** Still scaling, still delivering. Give it bigger creative swings, meaningful pivots, new personas, format changes.
- **Yellow (fatiguing):** Metrics declining. Give it smaller, surgical swings, hook changes, B-roll swaps, creator refreshes. Don't invest in full new production on a fading signal.

---

## Static → Video → High-Production Testing Ladder (Viti Videtta)

Use production investment as a validation gate:

1. **Test with statics first**: fastest to make, lowest cost. If the angle wins in static, the insight is real.
2. **Graduate to short video** (voiceover + text overlay): if the static wins, validate the message in video before spending on talent.
3. **Move to high-production** (testimonial, creator, staged moment): only invest at this level when lower-fidelity formats have already validated the concept.

This prevents expensive reshoots based on unproven concepts.

## The 50/25/25 Creative Mix for Aggressive Scaling (Eric Philippou)

When an account has clear winners and you're in Exploit mode:

| Bucket | % of new creative | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Blatant visual copies** | 50% | Visually unique versions of the winning ad, same or similar copy, completely different visual treatment |
| **Dramatized iterations** | 25% | Same angle but more emotional or more humorous, inside what the brand's voice allows |
| **Sub-angle tests** | 25% | Deeper niches inside the winning angle, different micro-moment, different persona subset |

The visual copies (50%) are not lazy, they're Andromeda-necessary. Andromeda needs new visual signals. Running the same visual with different text no longer works.

In 2026 you're persuading two entities: the person watching, and Meta's algorithm. The algorithm now treats minor edits as duplicates and deprioritizes spend distribution to them.

## 4-Step Idea Production Process (Viti Videtta, citing James Webb Young)

Creativity is mechanical. Everyone can do it with practice.

1. **Gather**: Collect both specific knowledge (brand/product/customer) and general knowledge (culture, memes, psychology, history). Both inputs are required. Without general knowledge, you land on generic ads.

2. **Work**: Force connections between the specific and general inputs. Ask: What analogy applies here? What's the opposite approach? What's the emotional reframing? What specificity makes this non-generic? What cultural layer applies?

3. **Eureka**: The idea often arrives when you step away. The combination clicks.

4. **Development**: Submit to your team for criticism. Good ideas have self-expanding qualities, feedback makes them better, not worse.

### 5 Question Categories for Generating Non-Generic Ideas

| Category | What to Ask |
|---|---|
| **Analogy** | What is this like? What metaphor explains how the product works? |
| **Opposite thinking** | What if we led with the negative? What's the anti-ad? |
| **Emotional reframing** | What emotion is really at the center of this problem? |
| **Specificity** | What detail makes this real? What exact moment? What exact number? |
| **Cultural layer** | What's happening in culture right now that makes this relevant? |

> "The difference between a junior and a senior creative isn't talent. It's the size, speed and quality of the combinations they can make." (Viti Videtta)
