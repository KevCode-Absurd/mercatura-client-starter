# Layout Specs: Designer Format Decision Tree

Every variant in brief-v2 declares its layout type explicitly. This file defines each type, when to use it, what the designer delivers, and examples by vertical.

The app's own limits win where they differ: a headline on a layout ad is 10 words or fewer, and a headline with its sub-headline is 90 characters or fewer on a concept ad. Every price, number and quote in an example below stands for the brand's own, taken from its facts.

---

## The 7 Layout Types

### 1. HEADLINE ONLY

**What it is:** Single line of text on the visual. No supporting copy.

**When to use:**
- The visual makes the message self-evident (before/after, demo, comparison)
- The concept is identity-driven and the visual shows the person/lifestyle
- The hook is so precise it needs nothing to explain it
- Product is the hero and the headline just names the emotional claim

**Headline length:** SHORT (3-5 words) or MEDIUM (5-6 words) max. Longer headlines on a visual with no support text look cluttered.

**What the designer delivers:** Image + headline text, positioned per template. Nothing else.

**Examples by vertical:**
- Baby: "She Stole the Baby's Blanket": visual shows mom wrapped in infant blanket
- Accessories: "For Bold Photographers": identity visual, identity headline
- Sunglasses: "See What You've Been Missing": paired with polarized comparison visual

**Misuse:** Don't use HEADLINE ONLY when the claim needs support to be believable. "Healthiest Hair of Your Life" with no proof = skepticism. Add a subheadline or USPs.

---

### 2. HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE

**What it is:** Headline creates tension or curiosity. Subheadline resolves it and installs the belief.

**When to use:**
- Fatal Flaw opener: headline names the problem with all other solutions, subheadline names your mechanism as the fix
- Curiosity-gap opener: headline creates an information gap, subheadline closes it with the relevant product claim
- Price reveal: headline creates premium expectation, subheadline reveals the real price
- Identity hook that needs product grounding, "For The Open-Back Life" → "The adjustable-back bra designed for how you actually work out"

**Headline length:** MEDIUM (5-7 words). The subheadline carries the specificity, so the headline can be punchy.

**Subheadline length:** 8-15 words. One sentence. Installs the belief: does not repeat the headline.

**What the designer delivers:** Image + headline + subheadline. Two text elements, sized and positioned per template (headline dominant, subheadline supporting).

**Examples by vertical:**
- Sunglasses: "Why Most DTC Sunglasses Feel Different After 6 Months" → "Italian acetate. Most brands use injection-molded plastic. Now you know why."
- Sports bra: "The Bra That Actually Fits" → "Adjustable back clip. Made to fit your body."
- Supplements: "The Ancient Herb Western Medicine Ignores" → "3,000 years of Ayurvedic use, now backed by clinical research."

**Subheadline anti-patterns:**
- Don't repeat the headline in different words: "The Best Blanket" → "The Blanket That's Best" is not a subheadline
- Don't pivot to features: "Adjustable back clip" standalone is a feature, not a belief. Make it a benefit: "Adjustable back clip: built for the way you actually move"
- Don't end with the product name alone: "The {Brand} blanket" is not a belief being installed

---

### 3. HEADLINE + USP LIST

**What it is:** Headline makes a direct claim. Below it: 2-3 short benefit bullets.

**When to use:**
- Solution-aware or Product-aware audience: they're comparing, they need rapid proof
- The concept is comparison-driven (vs. competitors, vs. alternatives)
- Price point requires multiple proofs to justify
- High AOV with multiple relevant benefits

**Headline length:** MEDIUM (5-7 words). Bold, benefit or problem claim.

**USP format rules:**
- Max 3 bullets: more is visual noise
- Each bullet = one benefit, max 6 words
- Benefit language only: NOT ingredients, specs, or materials unless directly tied to an outcome
  - ✅ "Less shedding: safe for kids" not "Contains biotin and saw palmetto"
  - ✅ "Clips that hold thick hair" not "High-tension spring mechanism"
  - ✅ "Ships in 2 days, gift-ready" not "Expedited fulfillment available"
- No bullet ends with a period, they're scans, not sentences
- Each bullet adds new proof, never repeat the same claim with different words

**What the designer delivers:** Image + headline + 3 bulleted lines, sized per template.

**Examples by vertical:**
- Camera bags: "The Bag Photographers Actually Choose" → bullets: "Padded dividers for all lens sizes · Waterproof canvas · Converts shoulder to backpack"
- Supplements: "10,000 Canadians Switched Their Protein" → bullets: "40g protein per serving · No artificial sweeteners · Made in Canada"
- Sunglasses: "Italian Acetate Changes How Frames Feel" → bullets: "Lighter, warmer, clearer than plastic · UV400 polarized lenses · 1-year warranty"

**Misuse:** Don't use USP lists for Trigger-stage (Problem-unaware) ads. An unaware prospect doesn't care about specs yet. They need to feel the problem first. Use HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE for Trigger-stage instead.

---

### 4. HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE + USP LIST

**What it is:** The full persuasion stack. Headline opens, subheadline narrows the claim, USPs prove it.

**When to use:**
- High-consideration products ($100+) where credibility is the gap
- Solution Beliefs need to be installed alongside Product Beliefs
- First ad ever run for a brand with low awareness (Credibility Belief is blocking)
- Premium-tier products competing against mass-market alternatives

**Headline length:** MEDIUM (5-7 words): punchy.
**Subheadline:** 8-12 words: installs the primary belief.
**USPs:** 2-3 bullets max.

**What the designer delivers:** Image + 3 text zones (headline / subheadline / bullet list). Template must have space for all three, check template layout before selecting.

**Examples:**
- Premium sunglasses: "You'll Know The Difference Immediately" → "Italian acetate, lighter, warmer, clearer than plastic" → bullets: "UV400 polarized lenses · 1-year warranty included · DTC from $180"
- $100+ baby blanket: "The Blanket That Becomes an Heirloom" → "Ultra-soft Minky that doesn't pill, after 500 washes" → bullets: "Hospital-safe · Every size from newborn to adult · Free shipping"

---

### 5. PRICE REVEAL

**What it is:** Headline creates a premium expectation. The price is shown large on the design, and it's lower than expected.

**When to use:**
- Value-positioning concept (premium-vs-price-for-quality)
- The price itself is the hook
- Audience is Solution-aware or Product-aware and comparing prices
- Price anchoring is the primary belief to install

**Headline length:** SHORT-MEDIUM (4-7 words). Should imply quality/premium BEFORE the price lands.
**Price display:** Large, centered, designed like a reveal, not a footnote.
**Optional one-liner:** A single benefit line below the price to explain why the value exists.

**What the designer delivers:** Image + headline + prominently displayed price + optional one supporting line.

**Examples:**
- Activewear: "You Read That Price Correctly" → $80 displayed large → "Full matching set. Bra + leggings."
- Sunglasses: "Italian Acetate Sunglasses From" → $180 displayed → "Handcut. Polarized. Lifetime-designed."

**Misuse:** Don't use PRICE REVEAL for premium-positioning brands where the value is quality, not affordability. Price reveal is for the VALUE surprise, when the price is lower than expected for the quality shown.

---

### 6. TESTIMONIAL BLOCK

**What it is:** A customer quote is the hero element. Attribution below. Optional headline above.

**When to use:**
- Social Proof is the primary blocking belief (Credibility category)
- The account is RED/PIVOT and trust-building is the first job
- The quote is emotionally specific and un-scripted-sounding
- The reviewer's identity matches the target persona exactly

**Quote format:** First-person, emotionally specific, reads like something real a person would say. NOT "Amazing product, highly recommend." YES: "I thought it was missing a zero, nope, $80 for the whole set."

**What the designer delivers:** Image (lifestyle or product) + large-type quote + attribution line (Name, verified buyer) + optional headline above in smaller type.

**Quote sourcing:** Pull from actual customer reviews in products-and-positioning.md or brain.md. If no strong verbatim quotes exist, note "Quote needed" rather than fabricating one.

**Attribution options:**
- "Sarah M., verified buyer": anonymous/safe
- "Tara, mom of 3": only if the review itself says so. A name or a role is never added to a quote
- "★★★★★" star rating + name, highest trust signal

---

### 7. SOCIAL PROOF STAT

**What it is:** A number + outcome as the hero text. Often the largest element on the design.

**When to use:**
- Large verified numbers (10,000+) that are provably real
- The number validates scale (social proof category of Credibility Belief)
- Product-aware or Most-aware audience who needs the final credibility push
- One strong stat exists and speaks louder than any feature could

**Stat format rules:**
- Number must be real and verifiable, never inflate or guess
- Pair with an outcome, not a feature: "250,000 moms chose this blanket" not "250,000 sold"
- Keep it one line: stat + outcome. No explanation needed.

**What the designer delivers:** Image + large stat text + optional subheadline.

**Examples:**
- "250,000+ moms chose this blanket"
- "90,000 women trust this daily"
- "Sold out 5 times. Back now."

## Layout × Awareness Level Quick Match

| Awareness Level | Best Layout Types | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Problem-unaware (Trigger) | HEADLINE ONLY · HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE | USP LIST · PRICE REVEAL · SOCIAL PROOF STAT |
| Problem-aware | HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE · TESTIMONIAL BLOCK | PRICE REVEAL |
| Solution-aware | HEADLINE + USP LIST · HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE | TESTIMONIAL BLOCK alone |
| Product-aware | HEADLINE + USP LIST · SOCIAL PROOF STAT · TESTIMONIAL BLOCK | HEADLINE ONLY (not enough proof) |
| Most-aware | PRICE REVEAL · SOCIAL PROOF STAT · HEADLINE + USP LIST | Trigger-style openers |

---

## Layout × Tone Quick Match

| Tone | Best Layout Types |
|---|---|
| Direct | HEADLINE ONLY · PRICE REVEAL · SOCIAL PROOF STAT |
| Empathetic | HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE (subheadline acknowledges pain) |
| Skeptic-pivot | TESTIMONIAL BLOCK · HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE |
| Educational | HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE (Fatal Flaw structure) |
| Identity | HEADLINE ONLY · HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE |

## Headline Length × Layout Compatibility

| Layout | SHORT (3-5) | MEDIUM (5-7) | LONG (8-12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| HEADLINE ONLY | ✅ Best | ✅ OK | ❌ Too much text without support |
| HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE | ✅ Strong | ✅ Best | ✅ OK if template has space |
| HEADLINE + USP LIST | ❌ Too thin | ✅ Best | ✅ OK |
| FULL STACK | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ if template has 3 text zones |
| PRICE REVEAL | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ Competes with price display |
| TESTIMONIAL | N/A (quote determines length) | | |
| SOCIAL PROOF STAT | ✅ (stat is the hero) | | |

## Template Selection Against Layout

When selecting an ad template, match the layout type to the template format:

| Layout Type | Template format filter |
|---|---|
| HEADLINE ONLY | "Lifestyle", "Product Feature" |
| HEADLINE + SUBHEADLINE | "Product Feature", "Lifestyle", "Educational" |
| HEADLINE + USP LIST | "Feature Checklist", "Comparison", "Product Feature" |
| FULL STACK | "Comparison Chart", "Feature Checklist" |
| PRICE REVEAL | "Offer/Deal", "Promotional" |
| TESTIMONIAL BLOCK | "Testimonial/Review" |
| SOCIAL PROOF STAT | "Social Proof", "Bold Statement" |
