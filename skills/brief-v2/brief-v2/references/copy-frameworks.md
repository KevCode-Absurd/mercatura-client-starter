# Copy Frameworks: Proven Structures for Ad Copy

> How to structure what you say. Not just the words: the architecture of persuasion.

---

## Offers in a brief

A brief states only an offer the brand has in use (`offers.json`), in its own terms. How an offer is designed, and why a flat percentage off is rarely one, is `skills/offer-building`. What a brief can test is the framing of the same offer: "2 for the price of 1", "the second one free" and "half off when you buy 2" are the same sum and 3 different readings. Test the framing with a different picture each time.

## Headline Messaging Approaches

3 ways to say the same thing:

| Approach | Example | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Benefit-led** | "Your immune system boost" | Default: lead with what it does for them |
| **Fear/urgency-led** | "Keep your immune system fighting fit" | When the pain is acute and the audience is problem-aware |
| **USP-focused** | "All the nutrients your immune system needs" | When competing on mechanism/completeness: solution-aware audience |

**Important:** These three framings on the SAME visual no longer count as different ads to Meta. You must pair each messaging approach with a different visual treatment to get true diversification.

## Ad Copy Structures

### PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solve)
Best for: Problem Aware audience
```
Her hair health starts at the scalp.  ← Problem
No harsh chemicals, no guesswork.     ← Agitate (what's wrong with alternatives)
Just healthier roots for her and you.  ← Solve
```

### Before/After/Bridge
Best for: Solution Aware audience
```
Before: Wrestling her hair every school morning.
After: Styled in 2 minutes flat.
Bridge: The Hairstyling Set: $68, everything she needs.
```

### The Confession / Anti-Ad
Best for: TOF skeptical audience, UGC format
```
I'm going to be honest. I bought it because my friend 
wouldn't stop sending me the link. I thought it was a gimmick.
But then I used it. And my hot tub smelled like a spa in Hawaii.
I've been in my hot tub four nights this week.
```

### The Micro-Story (3 beats)
Best for: Emotional TOF, identity-driven brands
```
Tomorrow is National Siblings Day.               ← Setup
Girls' dresses designed to match, ← The product enters naturally
same dress, different sizes.                      ← Resolution / aspiration
```

### The Listicle / "X Reasons"
Best for: Solution Aware, educational content
```
3 reasons this gut supplement sells out:
1. 40g protein per serving
2. No artificial sweeteners
3. 100k+ women trust it daily
```

## Visual Testing Framework (Same Copy, Different Frame)

The words can stay the same, the visuals are the lever. Three visual treatment categories to test for ANY product:

| Visual Treatment | What it looks like | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Hand + product** | Product held in someone's hand, lifestyle context | Relatability, scale reference, human connection |
| **Product photo** | Clean studio product shot, isolated | Premium positioning, clarity, hero product |
| **Using product** | Product being applied, demonstrated, in action | Proof, demo energy, trust building |

**Rule:** Each visual treatment attracts a different person. Someone who stops for a hand-in-use shot is a different buyer than someone who stops for a clean product hero. Test all three for your best-performing copy.

## Belief Engineering Framework (Josh Gavin, AO Masterclass)

Prospects don't buy because of offer quality, they buy because their **beliefs** aligned with purchase. Marketing's job is to eliminate blocking beliefs and install the ones required for conversion to feel natural, not forced.

"No neutral marketing: every touchpoint either builds or breaks beliefs. Pressure tactics (fake urgency, hype, manufactured scarcity) actively undo the work."

### The Six Critical Belief Categories

Every prospect must have all six resolved before conversion happens naturally. Missing any one collapses the bridge:

| # | Belief | Core question in the prospect's mind |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outcome** | "Is this real, attainable, and worth pursuing?" |
| 2 | **Identity** | "Is this possible for ME? Am I worthy of this?" |
| 3 | **Problem** | "Is this real, serious, and urgent for me?" |
| 4 | **Solution** | "Is this approach valid, smart, and emotionally safe?" |
| 5 | **Product** | "Is this specific product the right delivery method?" |
| 6 | **Credibility** | "Is this person/company trustworthy?" |

**+ Opportunity Cost Belief**: "Will spending money here prevent something better?" Sits closest to the final purchase decision for premium products.

**How to use this for briefs:** Diagnose which belief category is the bottleneck before writing any copy. TOF ads usually fight Outcome + Identity beliefs. MOF ads fight Solution + Product beliefs. BOF ads fight Opportunity Cost + Credibility beliefs.

### Belief Transition States

Prospects must progress through these in sequence, you can't skip:
1. **Closed**: Guarded, skeptical, defensive. Nothing lands here.
2. **Receptive**: Open, curious, non-defensive. The only state where copy works.
3. **Transformed**: Beliefs aligned with purchase. Conversion feels natural.

**Mental rejection:** When a prospect rejects a claim in their head, they snap back to Closed. A claim bigger than the reader believes does this. Say what the brand's facts hold, as they state it. Whether a claim is softened ("often", "can") is the brand's voice rule, not a rule of this skill.

**"It's not your fault"** is one of the most powerful phrases in direct response. It dissolves identity-level blocking beliefs while maintaining receptivity simultaneously.

### The Fatal Flaw Technique

Identify the fundamental reason ALL common solutions fail → position your product as the one that addresses the root cause.

Effect: makes the prospect feel smart for recognizing the real issue. Differentiates without attacking competitors. Creates category-of-one positioning.

Application: "The real reason your hair still isn't growing after trying biotin, collagen, and everything else is that none of them address [root mechanism]."

### The Three-Part Ad Structure

**Opener → Body → CTA**: each earns rights to the next:

- Credibility earns storytelling rights
- Story earns diagnostic rights  
- Diagnosis earns solution-positioning rights
- Solution earns CTA rights

The CTA facilitates the next step only, it does not close. Conversion is the result of belief engineering that happened in the body, not the CTA.

### 12 Opener Formulas (Belief-Engineering Hooks)

All begin with question words. Testable against the same body copy:

| Formula | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| "Why [problem] despite trying [effort]" | Validates struggle, signals hidden knowledge |
| "Why not [outcome] despite [effort]" | Validates identity belief gap |
| "What is the real reason [problem happens]" | Implies hidden knowledge |
| "How to [outcome] via [mechanism]" | Curiosity-based, outcome-driven |
| "When is it your turn" | Identity/worthiness hook |
| "What if there was an easier way" | Removes perceived cost of trying again |
| "The biggest misconception about [topic]" | Challenges assumptions, instant receptivity |
| "The biggest problem with [common solution]" | Dislodges blocking belief, opens to reframe |
| "Why doesn't [common method] work" | Fatal Flaw setup |
| Internal dialogue (emotional outburst) | Immediate relatability: "What is wrong with me?" |
| "What is the right way to [do X]" | Implies a wrong way, curiosity gap |
| Story-based dramatic opening | Emotional entry, fastest to receptive state |

### Where claims are restricted

For health, supplements, money and anything for children, direct claims are limited by law and by the ad platforms:
- Say only what the brand's claims list holds, in its wording.
- Move from the result to the result of the result (sleeping through the night, not a clinical outcome).
- Cite the study and its authors, not a personal promise.
- Required wording (the founder's answer to F3.6) goes on the ad as given.

### The 12-Point Belief Engineering Checklist

Before any ad ships, verify:
1. Receptivity created before any claim is made
2. Focus is belief engineering, not feature dumping
3. Blocking beliefs dissolved (problem, identity, solution)
4. Necessary beliefs installed (outcome, product, credibility)
5. Headline passes the Classified Ad Test (works standalone at phone-call level)
6. Most important words appear first (mobile truncation)
7. No claim beyond the brand's facts; hedges as the brand's voice rules say
8. The look fits the brand's own Brand world and Motion and sound, and the place the ad runs
9. Tested on mobile before launch
10. CTA is clear, friction-free, and only asks for the next step
11. No fake urgency or manufactured scarcity anywhere in the ad
12. Every component passes the standalone "who cares" test
