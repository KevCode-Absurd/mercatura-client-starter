# Format: watercolour

Where this and the task skill differ, this wins.

## What makes it watercolour

- **A painted world, not a filter.** Everything is painted on cold-press paper: translucent layered pigment, paper tooth showing through, soft irregular edges, a few ink accents, bare paper left as the light.
- **Locked before anything moves:** paper, pigment behaviour, edge language, palette and subject treatment. The same in every picture.
- **The pigment move** is the centrepiece: 1 thing paint really does, used once, that reveals or explains the selling idea. Name it first. Pigment that moves all the time is decoration.

| The selling idea | The pigment move | What is seen |
|---|---|---|
| What is in it (a herbal tea) | Brush reveal | A wet stroke crosses bare paper and the 3 plants of the blend stand painted behind it |
| A scent that fills a room (a candle) | Bloom | 1 drop of amber opens from the candle into the grey wash of the room |

The other moves: bleed, dissolve, wash transition, pigment trail, painted transformation.

- **Gentle.** Forms stay recognisable; only secondary pigment edges move. 1 colour family a shot, so it never goes muddy.
- **The product** is painted too, at its true shape and colours, its silhouette crisp and every line of its label stated exactly.
- **A figure,** if any, is 1 loosely painted fictional adult, plain from its proportions and dress: never a child, a real person, a known character or a named painter's style. It gives no testimonial.

**5 looks** (the job is given 1): `storybook` (the default), a warm small world for the buyer's moment; `botanical`, a specimen on bare paper, for what it is made of; `editorial`, loose washes behind 1 subject, for an idea or a mood; `ink`, a pen line carrying the form, for objects and how a thing works; `product`, the pack painted large and exact.

## What it is good for

Paint shows what a camera cannot: a scent, a warmth, a mood, where an ingredient comes from, 1 thing soaking into another.

It is weak at hard proof, urgency, loud offers, a product's real texture, and anything resting on a believed human face.

By stage: strongest for unaware and problem-aware buyers (a moment recognised, with no hard sell to resist) and for solution-aware buyers (what is in it, or how it works, as 1 pigment move). A product-aware buyer is served only by the `product` look. A most-aware buyer needs the offer, and another format.

## A picture claims as much as a sentence

A wash that spreads, a stain that dissolves, a colour that returns: each is a claim made. Show only what the brand's claims state. A mechanism is painted only as `How it works` gives it, an ingredient only if `Made of` lists it. No body before and after, no skin clearing. A grey wash turning warm is a mood and stays plainly a metaphor.

## What to avoid

- Pigment swirling in every shot.
- A bloom or a wash across the product's label.
- A painted figure who speaks to the viewer or reports a result.

## What this reads from the brand brain

- `products-and-positioning.md`: `Looks like` and `Pack` (the painted product, its label word for word), `Made of`, `How it works` (the pigment move), `Core benefit`. Blank `How it works` or `Made of`: ask, or paint the moment and not the mechanism. Never invent.
- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are` (the figure), `The moment` (the painted place and hour), `Customer language bank`. Blank: mark the scene inferred.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Color palette` (the colour family), `Brand world`, `Brand voice and tone` (the narrator), `Brand guardrails`.
- `brand.json` and `offers.json`: claims, forbidden claims, the offer. A claim that is not there is not painted.
