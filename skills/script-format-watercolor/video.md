# Watercolour as a video: a narration over painted pictures

Judged on 1 thing: with the sound off, a stranger can say what the paint revealed and which product it belongs to.

## How to write for it

- **Narration, not dialogue.** 1 narrator, off screen, speaks over the painted pictures. A painted figure never speaks and its mouth forms no words. Write every spoken line as narration.
- **Person:** 2nd person ("You light it at 9.") or 3rd person about 1 unnamed figure. Calm and unhurried, in the brand's voice.
- **Story shapes that work (options, not an order):** a moment (the buyer's hour of the day, painted, then changed). An origin (where what is in it comes from). A mechanism (1 thing acting on another). A ritual (how it is used, step by step).
- **The pigment move:** name the 1 move that carries the selling idea (bloom, bleed, dissolve, brush reveal, wash transition, pigment trail, painted transformation) and write in the visual note what it reveals. Promise first, then the move. It happens once, on the line that states the idea.
- **The product's arrival:** it is already painted on the sheet when the shot the words reach it opens. The pigment move starts at it or ends at it.
- **One close that works:** the offer and the call to action as narration over the settled painting, the product's label clear, then 1 identity line.

## Length

20 to 45 seconds, 60 at most. Paint needs time to spread and to settle: fewer, longer pictures. Under 20 seconds, write 1 picture, 1 pigment move and the product.

## Writing shots a model can render

The video is generated in clips of about 10 seconds. This look is calm on purpose: a clip is a few longer shots joined by hard cuts, not 1 held picture.

- 1 sheet of paper, 1 painted place, 1 product, 1 figure at most. Describe them the same way every time.
- A thing is already on the sheet when its shot opens. Nothing slides in from out of view. Only 2 arrivals are allowed, both the medium's own: a brush reveal (a simple shape such as a leaf, steam or a window is painted onto bare paper in view) and a wash transition (a wash crosses the sheet and the same picture changes colour family). The product and the figure are never painted in or repainted.
- 1 pigment move to a shot at most, and 8 words or fewer of narration while it happens, so it can be watched.
- Forms hold. Only the secondary pigment edges move: a wet edge creeps, a bloom opens, a wash settles. A figure makes 1 slow gesture a shot. No running, no splashes.
- 1 colour family a shot. A second family enters only by the pigment move: that is the turn.
- Something is already moving in the first shot: 1 wet edge still spreading.
- 1 clear action per line. The narrator talks at a calm pace: the words a clip holds are given with its length. A sentence is said whole inside 1 clip.
- The shots cut between the whole painting, 1 part of it close, and the wet edge very close. The last shot of the ad is held: the pigment settled and dry.
- In each shot the view is flat to the paper and steady, or 1 slow push in.
- No words inside the picture but the product's own label: no signs, no lettering, no brush-written words. Overlays are added afterward.
- No real people, celebrities or known characters. No child.

## What to avoid in a video

- A painting that plays, stops, and then an ad starts. The product comes in inside the painting's story.
- Pigment that never rests: the 1 move that sells is lost in it.
- A change the brand's claims do not state, painted because it was pretty.
