# Watercolour as a still image: 1 painted sheet with room for words

Judged on 1 thing: at the size of a thumb, a stranger sees a painting on paper with 1 subject in it, and reads the headline once.

## The frame

- **Choose 1 moment.** For an unaware or problem-aware buyer: the buyer's own moment, painted (the place and the hour), the product small or left out. For a solution-aware buyer: the pigment move caught half way (the bloom mid-spread), at the product. For a buyer who knows the product: the product painted large, what it is made of laid around it.
- **A still freezes the pigment move.** 1 wet edge caught as it spreads tells the eye what the paint is doing. 1 move, never 2.
- **Bare paper is part of the picture.** 1 subject, and a third of the sheet or more left unpainted, round the subject and fading out at the edges, never 1 empty band down a side: it is the light, and it is where the words go.
- **1 colour family,** plus the product's own colours. The product is the most saturated thing on the sheet.
- **The product:** where the ad shows it, it is painted at its true shape and colours, front to the viewer, large enough for its label to read. Where the ad leaves it out, the sheet holds the moment alone.
- **A figure,** if any: 1 fictional adult, loosely painted, turned toward the thing and not toward the viewer.

## Words on the picture

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer, as clean type in the brand's own typefaces, on the bare paper: the top third, or the unpainted side. It says what the painting cannot: whose moment this is, or what the spreading colour stands for.
- 1 smaller line or a row of 2 or 3 chips at most, and a button only where there is an offer. 20 words in all.
- Type is printed over the painting, dark on pale paper. Nothing is brush-lettered or handwritten, and no word is painted into the scene. The words on the product's own label are not counted.
- No type across a wash, a bloom or the product.
- The figure says nothing: no speech bubble.

## For the image prompt

- Open with the style lock, word for word: "Original watercolour painting on cold-press paper. Translucent layered pigment, visible paper tooth, controlled wet-on-wet blooms, softly irregular painted edges, restrained ink accents, preserved negative space, and natural pigment granulation. Painterly motion only; no glossy 3D surfaces and no flat vector gradients."
- Then the look. `storybook` (the default): soft rounded forms, a warm small world, faint pencil underdrawing showing through, gentle blooms in the background only. `botanical`: clear single washes and fine observed detail, a specimen on mostly bare paper, no ink. `editorial`: broad loose washes behind 1 simplified subject, a lot of air. `ink`: a confident dark ink line carries the form, washes laid loosely over and past the line. `product`: the pack painted precisely with crisp dry edges, loose washes only around it.
- Everything is painted, and "painted" sits on every noun: a painted mug, painted steam. Light is unpainted paper, never white paint. Shadows are thin cool washes.
- The painting fills the frame, seen flat. No desk, brushes, hand, tape, frame or sheet edge.
- Name the colour family as 3 or 4 colours, and where the paper stays bare.
- The product is painted from its photo at its true shape and colours, its edges the crispest on the sheet. State every line of its label exactly, in quotes, with its colour. No bloom crosses it.
- Not a photograph under a filter, not thick opaque paint, not a digital illustration.

## What fails in a still

- Mud: 3 colour families, or too many layers.
- Paint to every edge: no light, and nowhere for the words.
- A label lost in a bloom.
- A pretty painting in which nothing sells.
- A change the brand's claims do not state.
