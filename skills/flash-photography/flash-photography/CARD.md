# The flash look: rules for planning the frames

Stills that look as if a friend shot 1 real evening on a small film camera with the flash
on. Used where a picture should not look like an advert: feed ads, social posts, the "people
like you" strip of a page or an email. Not for the first image of a product page or a
catalogue ad: the cast and the glare make it the wrong picture to judge a color from.

A frame is judged on 3 things: a stranger would believe a friend took it; the product is the
real product; the frame does its job.

## Light and camera (every frame)

- Open every scene with: "Candid 35mm film photograph, direct on-camera flash, warm amber color cast, the feel of a small camera at a party, gentle grain."
- A hard-edged flash shadow just behind the subject. Bright foreground, darker background. In daylight the flash still overpowers the ambient light.
- Handheld, close, slightly off level, at the height of someone standing in the room. Wide enough to show the room.
- Never cool, never the even light of a studio, never dim and moody.
- Warm walls (cream, pink, terracotta) and real rooms in the evening or at night, unless the brand's world is the morning or outdoors: then its own time of day, with flash as fill.

## People

- Fictional adults, cast from the brand's buyers: say an age band, hair, clothes and 1 thing that sets each apart. Never a real person's face, never the people in any reference.
- Caught mid-action, laughing or absorbed. Eyes on the task, the product or each other, **never at the camera**. Say so in every scene.
- Warm, joyful, sunny, whoever is cast. Tattoos and dark clothes are casting, not mood.
- No child, and no part of one (a small hand, the back of a head). A product for children is shown in an adult's hands, packed, or where a child would use it.
- Every action is written with its mechanics: which hand, holding what, where the other hand is. No finger over the label. Nobody is a customer, a founder or an expert.
- Of 5 frames with people: about 2 alone, 1 pair, 2 groups. Never all groups.

## The frames, each with its job

With people:
- **Alone, absorbed:** 1 adult mid-use. In use, for a feed ad.
- **Alone, the moment after:** pleased, laughing at something off frame, product still in hand. The payoff, for an ad or a post.
- **A pair:** 1 does it for, passes it to or shows it to the other. For a post or an email.
- **A group:** the product in the middle of an evening. The brand's world, for an email header or a brand ad.

Product only, 5 treatments:
- **Flash glint:** the product where it lives, at night, 1 hard shadow, a glint on its edge. A hero for social.
- **Scattered:** out of its pack, laid or spilled on a surface, hard little shadows. Texture, for a carousel card. For 1 solid object: dropped where it was last used (a pan on the hob, a jacket over a chair).
- **Held up:** an adult hand holding it toward the lens, label facing the camera, fingers on its sides. Scale in a hand.
- **Frozen:** mid-pour, mid-toss, mid-zip, sharp in the flash. In use; the first frame of a clip. Only a motion the product facts describe.
- **Stacked:** several of the same product piled or lined up. For a bundle ad.

## What "mid-use" is, by kind of product

- **Worn** (a jacket, a ring): putting it on, adjusting it or moving in it. It sits and hangs as its in-use photo shows.
- **Applied** (a serum, a lipstick): the real amount leaving the real opening onto where it is applied.
- **Eaten or drunk** (a snack, a can): the pinch of 1 piece, the pour, the pass across a table. 1 serving.
- **Used with the hands** (a pan, a suitcase): the task at its key moment, with a grip a hand can make.
- **Placed in a room** (a candle, a throw): lit, switched on or being arranged, people around it and not holding it.

## What must be true of the product

- Named in exact words, the same every time, never by a general noun ("supplement", "cookware").
- Say what it is not: the lookalike a model would draw ("not pills", "not non-stick, no plastic handle", "not a blazer").
- The label sharp, readable and facing the camera. Never blurred, turned away or hidden.
- The flash's hot spot falls on a cap, a shoulder or an edge, never across the printed words.
- The cast is on the scene: a white pack is warm white, not orange.
- Its real size against the hand. 1 use is 1 real amount.
- A line the product facts do not fill is not guessed: the product is shown closed and at rest.
- What is in or on it (food in a pan, things in a bag) is ordinary, unbranded, and only what it can hold.

## Avoid

- A product laid out as in a catalogue; a room that looks staged; props waved and not used.
- An object that looks like a competitor's product or carries another brand's mark.
- A mirror facing the camera: it doubles the product and reverses its label.
- A dark or edgy tone. Anyone posing.
- Added words, date stamps, borders.

## After the render, look at

- The product beside its real photo: the thing itself, container, closure, colors, label words, size in the hand.
- Glare on the label; an orange tint on the product.
- Eyes: anyone looking at the camera fails the frame.
- Hands: a grip a hand can make, 5 fingers, the label clear.
- The light: a hard shadow behind, warm, bright foreground. Even or cool light fails.
- No child, no real person, no second brand.
