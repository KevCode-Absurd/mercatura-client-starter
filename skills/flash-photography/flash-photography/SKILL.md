# Flash Photography

A set of stills that look as if 1 person shot 1 real evening on a small film camera with the
flash on, where every frame is on brand, the product is the real product, and the people
could be the brand's buyers.

**What it is for.** Pictures that read as a friend's photo and not as an advert: feed ads
that should not look like ads, the brand's own social posts, the "people like you" strip of
a landing page or an email. It is not for the first image of a product page or a catalogue
ad: the warm cast and the flash glare make it the wrong picture to judge a color or read a
label from.

**What a frame is judged on.** A stranger would believe a friend took it; the product in it
is the product a buyer will receive; and the frame does the job it was given.

It rests on `skills/production-principles`; a finished image prompt is laid out as in
`skills/image-prompt-craft`; models, tools and prices are only in
`skills/production-principles/models.md`.

## The look (every frame)

- **Light:** direct on-camera flash. A hard-edged shadow thrown just behind the subject, a
  bright foreground falling quickly to a darker background, a warm amber cast, gentle 35mm
  grain. Never cool, never the even light of a studio, never moody and dim. In daylight the
  flash still overpowers the ambient light and still throws its hard shadow.
- **Subjects:** caught mid-action. People are laughing or absorbed, eyes on the task, the
  product or each other, **never at the camera** (said in every prompt). A product alone is
  caught mid-use: half open, mid-pour, set down a second ago. Never laid out as in a
  catalogue.
- **Mood:** warm, joyful, sunny, whoever is cast. Tattoos, piercings and dark clothes are
  casting, not mood. The tone never goes dark.
- **The product stays readable.** The flash's hot spot falls on a shoulder, a cap or an
  edge, never across the printed words. The cast is on the scene; a white pack reads as warm
  white, not orange. The label is sharp and faces the camera: a label blurred, turned away
  or hidden is a failed frame, the same as a garbled one.
- **No added words.** The only words are those printed on the product.

## What to ask first

1. **How many frames?** 8 is the usual set.
2. **People, product only, or a mix?** And the split (5 with people and 3 product only is a
   good mix).
3. **Which product and which variant?** 1 product per set, from
   `products-and-positioning.md`.
4. **Where will they be used?** That decides the sizes and the jobs.

## The product truth sheet (written before any frame that shows the product)

A short written record of what the product really looks like, made by looking at its
photos. Every frame is planned from it and checked against it. The commonest failure of a
generated photograph is a product that is nearly right: gummies drawn as pills, a
carbon-steel pan drawn as a non-stick pan with a plastic handle, a boxy chore jacket drawn
as a blazer. A beautiful picture of the wrong product is a failed picture.

It records, in the brand's own words where it has them:

- **What it is, exactly.** The thing itself, named so that it could be nothing else:
  "chewy translucent red cubes with a light sugar coat", "a 26 cm carbon-steel pan, dark
  grey, with a flat riveted steel handle", "a cropped, boxy cotton-canvas jacket with 3
  patch pockets". A general noun ("supplement", "cookware", "outerwear") is never used for
  the product.
- **Not this.** The lookalike a model would draw in its place, said as a negative: "not
  pills, not capsules"; "not non-stick, no plastic on the handle"; "not a blazer, no
  lapels".
- **The pack.** What it is sold in, as a packaging designer would say it: the container,
  its material and finish, its closure, its shape, its main colors, and the words printed
  large, in order. A thing sold without a pack (a jacket, a pan) has its label, tag or
  stamp recorded instead.
- **Size.** Against a hand, a body or a piece of furniture, whichever it meets: "fits in a
  closed fist", "the hem reaches the hip", "as tall as a mug". Never a measurement.
- **Looks real because.** The 2 or 3 details that make it read as real: sugar dust,
  condensation on cold glass, the grain of brushed steel, the slub of linen.
- **In use.** What it looks like worn, applied, served, in the hand or in place, taken from
  a photo of it in use: what shows and what is hidden, where it opens or fastens, what comes
  out, and how much is 1 use (2 gummies, 1 pump, a pea of cream).
- **Never show.** The mistakes particular to this product and anything the brand forbids.

Where the brand's product facts and photos do not fill a line, it is not guessed. Ask for a
photo or the fact; until then the product is shown closed and at rest, from an angle a photo
shows, and no frame depends on the missing line.

In every plan and prompt the product is named in the same exact words, and the 2 or 3 lines
that frame could break are said as what it is and what it is not.

## The brand's flash world

Worked out from the brain and shown with the shot list:

1. **Who is in the frame.** From the customer avatars: an age band, a mix of people, how
   they dress. Fictional adults only. A picture the brand wants matched gives its mood and
   styling, never its people.
2. **Things in the room.** 6 to 10 real objects from where the product is used. A serum: a
   crowded bathroom shelf, a towel, hair clips. A pan: a chopping board, a tea towel, plates
   waiting. A jacket: a bicycle, a doorstep, a tote. Nothing that looks like a competitor's
   product or carries another brand's mark.
3. **Where.** Places this buyer really is, from `Brand world`. Warm walls (cream, pink,
   terracotta) read best under flash. Evening and night are the look's home; a brand whose
   world is the morning or outdoors keeps its own time of day, with the flash as fill.
4. **What people do with it.** The 3 to 6 real uses in `How it is used` and the lessons.

## The action is real

Every action is written with its mechanics, not its mood: which hand, holding what, where
the other hand is. This is also what "mid-use" means for each kind of product.

- **Worn:** zipping it to the chest with 1 hand while the other holds the hem; fastening a
  clasp behind a friend's neck.
- **Applied:** 3 drops falling from the dropper onto the fingertips of the other hand; a
  stick drawn once along a cheekbone.
- **Eaten or drunk:** 1 piece pinched from the open pack between thumb and finger; a bowl
  passed across a table with both hands.
- **Used with the hands:** the pan tilted by its handle to slide an egg onto a plate; a
  suitcase lid pressed shut with a knee while a hand runs the zip.
- **Placed in a room:** a match held to the wick; a throw shaken out over a sofa by 2
  people. People are around the thing, not holding it up.

If the physics cannot be pictured, change the verb until it can. An action the brand's facts
do not describe is not shown.

## The frames, by what kind of product it is

Every frame has a job and a place it is used. The list is built from the product's kind;
nothing is fixed.

**Frames with people.** Never all groups: of 5, about 2 alone, 1 pair and 2 groups.

| Frame | What it shows | Its job, and where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Alone, absorbed | 1 adult mid-use, eyes on the task | In use: a feed ad |
| Alone, the moment after | The same kind of person a second later: pleased, laughing at something off frame, product still in hand | The payoff: a feed ad, a social post |
| A pair | 1 does it for, passes it to or shows it to the other | Sharing: a social post, an email |
| A group | The product in the middle of an evening, people around it | The brand's world: an email header, a brand ad |

**Product-only frames.** 5 treatments, each obeying the truth sheet:

| Treatment | What it shows | Its job, and where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Flash glint | The product where it lives, at night, 1 hard shadow, a glint on its edge | A hero for social, a story |
| Scattered | The product out of its pack, laid or spilled on a surface, hard little shadows | Texture: a carousel card |
| Held up | An adult hand holding it toward the lens, label facing the camera, fingers on its sides | Scale in a hand: an ad, a product page's second row |
| Frozen | Mid-pour, mid-toss, mid-zip, sharp in the flash | In use: an ad; the first frame of a clip |
| Stacked | Several of the same product piled or lined up | Range: a bundle or subscription ad |

Scattered suits a thing with pieces (food, capsules, jewellery); for 1 solid object (a pan,
a jacket) it becomes the object dropped where it was last used: the pan on the hob with a
spoon in it, the jacket over a chair back. Frozen needs a real motion from the product
facts; a thing that does not move is caught as a hand reaches for it.

**A product for children** is shown with no child, and no part of one (a small hand
reaching in, the back of a head): in an adult's hands, packed, or in the place a child
would use it. A child appears only in the brand's own uploaded photo.

**What is in or on the product** (the food in a pan or a lunchbox, the things in a bag) is
ordinary, unbranded, and only what it can really hold.

Show the list (frame, who or which treatment, the action, its job, its size) for 1 approval
before anything is made.

## Making the frames

- **Every frame that shows the product attaches the product's own photos, each tagged with
  its job** (production-principles section 1). In this skill's first test, prompts with no
  photo got the product wrong in 7 frames of 8.
- **A photo of real people is never attached:** the model returns those people. People are
  cast in words.
- **1 test frame first,** with the product in a hand: checked against the truth sheet,
  approved by the person, then attached to the later frames beside the real photos.
- **The prompt, 1 paragraph, in this order:**

> Candid 35mm film photograph, direct on-camera flash, warm amber color cast, the feel of a
> small camera at a party, gentle grain[, camera angle]. [WHERE: the place and the wall or
> surface]. [WHO: how many adults, each with hair, clothes and 1 thing that sets them
> apart]. [PRODUCT: "the exact product in Image 1, label sharp and readable", then the
> truth sheet's "what it is, exactly", its size, and "not ..."]. [ACTION: the mechanics, who
> does what with which hand]. [FEELING: laughing hard at something off frame / absorbed in
> the task]. Eyes on the task and each other, never at the camera. [THINGS IN THE ROOM: 4 to
> 6]. Hard flash shadow on the wall behind, warm and joyful. No added words.

## The check

Every frame is looked at, at full size, beside a real photo of the product
(production-principles section 9).

**The product first.** Made again on sight: the wrong thing (pills for gummies, lapels on a
chore jacket); the wrong container, closure or colors; label words garbled; the wrong size
in the hand; more than 1 use in the hand; glare across the label; the product tinted orange.

**Then the look and the people:** anyone looking at the camera; a hand that could not hold
that; a prop waved and not used; a cool, even or dim frame; a staged room; a mirror that
doubles the product; a child or a child's hand.

A failure is fixed by changing the part of the prompt that failed, never by sending the same
words again. Each failure is 1 line for the brand's `lessons.json`.

## Deliver

Frames are named by number, job and what they show (`03_in-use_serum-drops`), kept apart
from the product's real reference photos and marked as generated. The flash world (who,
where, the things in the room) goes back to the brain under `Brand world`, and new lines of
the truth sheet under the product. With the set, a short honest note: the keepers, the small
flaws, what was made again and why.

## A still that moves (on request)

An approved still can become a few seconds of motion, starting on that still
(production-principles sections 2, 4 and 5): 1 small action and 1 reaction at real speed, a
slight handheld shake, no cut, nobody turning to the camera, the sound said and no music.
A person watches and listens to every clip. The form of a video prompt is in `models.md`.

## What this reads from the brand brain

A blank field is a question for the brand. It is never guessed.

- `products-and-positioning.md`, the product's entry: `What it is`, `Variants`, `Looks
  like`, `Pack`, `How it is used`, `Never show`, `Reference photos`. These fill the truth
  sheet. The lines the brain does not hold yet ("not this", the pack's words word for word,
  how much is 1 use, what it looks like in use) are listed in Photoshoot Factory's
  `references/01-product-assets.md`.
- `customer-avatars.md`: `Who they are`, `The moment`.
- `brand-guidelines.md`: `Brand world`; `Color palette`; `Imagery do's and don'ts`; `Brand
  guardrails`. If the brand's world has no evening and no gathering, ask before using this
  look at all.
- `lessons.json`: how the product is opened, held and used.
