# Performance playbook for video scripts

Craft rules for e-commerce video ads on Meta that the 9 beats don't cover: how the feed is watched, how the offer is built, how to read results, what the platform rejects, and what each production format needs from the script.

## 1. How the feed is watched

- **Sound is often off at the start.** The first frame and the overlay must carry the hook alone. Captions run for the whole ad.
- **The first 3 seconds decide delivery.** Meta shows the ad to more people like those who keep watching. A hook that pulls in the wrong viewers buys cheap views and expensive customers.
- **Start mid-action.** No logo sting, no "hey guys", no slow push-in. The first frame should already be the accommodation, the moment, the demo or the odd detail.
- **1 change every 2 to 4 seconds** in ads under 60 seconds. A cut, an angle, a prop, a text change, a zoom. Mark these in the visual notes.
- **The product can be seen early and named late.** In hand, on the bench, in the background. Showing it is not pitching it.
- **Vertical first.** Write for 9:16. Keep faces, product and text clear of roughly the top 14% and bottom 20%.
- **End on a readable card:** the action, the price framing and the guarantee, held long enough to read (about 2 seconds).

## 2. E-commerce offer mechanics

The script can only sell the offer that exists. Check which of these the brand has, and say them in plain terms.

| Mechanic | What to say | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription | The price, how often it ships, how to pause or cancel | If research shows fear of being locked in, say the real cancel steps in the risk reversal. Never say "cancel anytime" unless it is that simple. |
| First-order discount | The price they pay today and the price after | A renewal price that surprises buyers shows up as refunds and 1-star reviews. Say both. |
| Bundle or multi-pack | The per-unit or per-day price, and why that quantity (match it to the product's cycle) | "Save 20%" alone is weak. Tie the quantity to the mechanism. |
| Free gift | What it is and what it's for | Only if it helps them use the product |
| Guarantee | The window, what they do, what they get back | Whether it covers the first order only, opened product, subscriptions. Say the limit if there is one. |
| Returns and exchanges (a product bought once) | How long they have, whether a used or worn one can go back, who pays the postage | The answer to "which size am I?" and "what if I don't like it?". Say it with the offer when there is no room for a risk reversal. |
| Size or version | How they find theirs: the quiz, the guide, the presenter's own height and size | A wrong size is the main return reason for anything worn. Never guess a fit. |
| Warranty and repairs | The period and what it covers | A warranty period is not a lifespan. Don't turn "10-year warranty" into "lasts 10 years". |
| Shipping | When it arrives | Only state a date the brand can keep |
| Reason for now | Stock, batch, season, price change, the problem getting worse | Must be true. Fake deadlines cost trust and can break consumer law. |

Price framing options: against the alternative, per day or per use, against the cost of the problem, or the effort as the price. Use 1. Per day suits what is used up; per use, per year or "against what it replaces" suits what is kept. Every figure is the brand's, or worked out from the brand's figures with the basis stated.

## 3. Read the numbers before rewriting

When an ad has run, the metrics point at the beat that is failing. Compare against the account's own average, not a universal benchmark.

| Signal | What it usually means | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| Low hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) | The first frame, overlay or spoken hook doesn't stop the right person | New hooks and first frames. Keep the body. |
| Good hook rate, low hold (people leave between 3 and 15 seconds) | The hook doesn't flow into line 2, or the problem beat is a symptom list | Fix the bridge. Rewrite the problem around 1 accommodation. |
| Good hold, drop when the product appears | The reveal jumps from story to pitch | Show before naming. Add a bridge line. |
| High watch time, low link click-through | No clear promise, weak offer, or a dead CTA | Promise, offer framing, CTA with a reason for now |
| High click-through, low conversion | The landing page doesn't match the ad's claim, the price surprises, or the hook pre-qualified the wrong people | Match the landing page top to the hook. State the price in the ad. Tighten the hook. |
| Strong for a week, then fades | Creative fatigue | New hooks on the same body first, then a new angle |
| Cheap clicks, high refunds or bad reviews | The ad sets an expectation the product doesn't meet | Check the guardrails. Shrink the promise. |

A variant should change 1 thing. If 3 things change, a win teaches nothing.

## 4. What Meta and regulators reject

These get ads rejected or accounts restricted. Write around them from the start.

- **Personal attributes.** Don't assert or imply the viewer has a condition, a body type, a financial state or similar. "Are you struggling with bloating?" and "your thinning hair" are risky. Safer: 1st person ("I'd stopped..."), conditional ("if you've started..."), a scene, or a statement about the category.
- **Health claims.** No cure, treat, prevent or diagnose language for supplements and cosmetics. No guaranteed results or fixed timelines ("lose 5kg in 2 weeks"). Structure and function wording only where the brand has substantiation.
- **Before and after.** Not allowed for weight loss and many health categories. Elsewhere, only real, consented images.
- **Testimonials.** Real, and typical. A standout result needs a "results vary" style qualifier where the market requires it.
- **Proof.** No invented reviews, counts, ratings, press logos, awards or expert endorsements.
- **Comparisons.** Facts only, supportable, no named competitor unless the brand has cleared it.
- **Negative self-perception.** Don't shame the viewer's body or life. Frame the problem as an accommodation they made, never as a flaw.
- **Urgency.** Only real deadlines and real stock limits.

When in doubt, flag the line in the compliance notes and offer a safer version.

## 5. What each production format needs from the script

The script is turned into briefs and prompts. Write the visual notes so each format has what it needs.

| Format | The script must supply |
|---|---|
| Human UGC creator brief | Lines the creator can honestly say, bracketed slots for their own true details, delivery notes (where, what they hold, energy), b-roll list, the hook variants to record, what not to say |
| AI UGC (avatar or generated person) | Who the person is (age, look, setting, to match the persona), shot per line (selfie, mirror, kitchen bench), the exact spoken line per clip, short clips (1 or 2 sentences each; the length a video model makes at a time is in `skills/production-principles/models.md`), the product's own reference photos to use, no invented label text, an adult |
| Claymation or animation | A character and a world, 1 visual metaphor for the mechanism, a scene per beat, a voiceover line per scene, on-screen text per scene. 3rd person or a narrator suits it. It is fiction, so it can dramatise the accommodation freely but may not invent proof. |
| VSL or faceless educational | A visual per sentence (diagram, screen recording, stock scene, text card), the looped structure for length, on-screen proof items |
| Founder | The real founding story and stakes, what the founder shows on camera (the factory, the rejected samples, the product cut open) |
| Statics and carousels cut from the script | The hook as an overlay, the mechanism as a subline, the proof as a badge, the offer as the final card. Hand these to the image ad skill. |

Keep generated people and scenes honest: an AI avatar must not be presented as a real customer giving a real testimonial. No generated children. A founder, a named customer or an expert appears only in that person's own footage. What a good generated picture or clip is, for every format, is in `skills/production-principles`; the `script-format-*` skills say how the words are written for each.
