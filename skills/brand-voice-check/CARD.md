# Brand voice: the card

<!-- The same rules as SKILL.md, with the explanation taken out. Sent at the "focused" context level; "full" sends SKILL.md. -->

For each line: would this brand say this, to this customer, in these words? The voice pack is what you check against, not general copywriting taste. It sits under 1 rule: a plain line a person would say is never off voice.

Check only these 3 things, and flag a word or phrase when:

- **It is a word the brand never uses,** or the customer wouldn't say. Jargon, marketers' category words, a clinical term outside a proof line, a word that breaks one of the brand's don'ts. If customers call it "gritty", the line must not call it "an unpleasant texture"; if they say a jacket "wets out", not "loses water repellency".
- **It is a tone the brand would not take.** A register the pack rules out: too formal, salesy, earnest, preachy, guilt-driven.
- **It talks past the buyer.** The wrong age or world for the customer in the quotes, or a do rule about how to speak to them that clearly applies and is dodged.

Do not flag: a plain, clear line, in any brand; product, ingredient or material names, numbers, prices, legal wording; a line that only states a term (a price, a guarantee, a disclosure); anything on the grounds of persuasion or structure; a line for not using a rhythm, a run of short sentences, a catchphrase or an emoji the brand sometimes uses.

Never rewrite a line to add a device (a rhythm, short sentences, a catchphrase), and never make a line stranger or more clever to sound more like the brand. A brand's device is an option the writer may take on a line that carries it without bending. Its absence is not a fault.

To fix: change the smallest span. Prefer the customers' own word, then a word the brand uses if it fits naturally; never paste a tagline in. The fixed line is still a whole sentence a person would say. Never add, soften or strengthen a claim, a benefit, a number or a promise. No em dashes. Contractions always.

Most good drafts need 0 to 4 fixes. If you find yourself rewriting most lines, keep only the changes where the customer would notice the brand sounds wrong.
