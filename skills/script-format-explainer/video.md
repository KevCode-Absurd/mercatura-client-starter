# CGI explainer as a video: a narrated mini-documentary, 30 to 40 seconds

1 line to a picture. The brief gives how long each picture runs.

## The 7 beats: one shape that works, not a required order

| Beat | Share of the time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The scale fact | 0 to 18% | A number or a scale that is hard to believe, over a picture as big or as small as the fact |
| 2. The reframe | 18 to 30% | The cause is not the one the viewer assumes. Say what they assume, then the real cause, as 2 plain statements |
| 3. The mechanism | 30 to 43% | What happens, as 1 thing acting on another |
| 4. How it works when nothing is in the way | 43 to 63% | In the past, in nature, or in a thing made the old way |
| 5. What gets in the way | 63 to 78% | A modern habit, material or shortcut. Never a person and never a named competitor |
| 6. The product, as a return to how it works | 78 to 88% | The brand is named here for the first time |
| 7. What the buyer can do again | 88 to 100% | 1 or 2 pictures of something physical, then the opening picture again, changed |

At 30 seconds beats 4 and 5 are 1 line each. Under 30 they go, and what is left is another format.

## How to write it

- **1 narrator, voice-over only.** Nobody in the picture speaks. Every spoken line is narration, in the second person.
- **The first line lands in under 3 seconds.** 6 ways to open: a challenge with a number; a flat number ("... Almost none know why."); an open question ("There's a reason ..."); a cold open in the past ("100 years ago ..."); the hidden part ("Your ... has a ..."); the failed fix ("You've tried ..."). With a sourced number, the first 2. Without, the other 4. For buyers who have tried competitors, the last 3.
- **The reframe is said straight.** Not "it's not X, it's Y": the script check marks that turn of phrase. "You drink plenty of water. What's missing is what used to be in it."
- **The technical noun is said 3 times:** first in beat 3, again in beats 5 and 6.
- **The brand is not named before about three quarters of the way through.** Until then it is a film about the viewer's body, home or kit.
- **Words.** The pace and the words the ad holds are given with the brief. 1 line is 1 picture and 6 to 9 words. If it runs long, cut words.
- **One close that works.** Beat 7 is the close: what the buyer does, within what the brand can say. The offer, when there is one, is on-screen text over the last picture with the product.
- A footnote mark goes in the on-screen text only where the brand's guardrails ask for one.
- No music cues. This format has none.

## Writing pictures a model can render

- 9 to 13 lines, each 1 picture. The visual note of a line is that picture.
- Every picture is a move forward through a rendered world. Pictures are joined by hard cuts, and each cut changes the scale: very wide, 1 figure, inside the cutaway.
- No close-up of hands.
- Very large or very small in the first picture.
- The thing the technical noun names is given its 1 shape and 1 colour in the first visual note that shows it.
- The product appears from beat 6 on, used the way it is really used. Something worn is shown real on its own, not on a figure.
- No words inside the picture. On-screen text is the 1 to 3 words of the line that carry it, added afterwards.
