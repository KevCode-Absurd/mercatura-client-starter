# CGI explainer as a still image: the mechanism in 1 picture

Judged on 1 thing: a stranger looks for 2 seconds and can say what acts on what.

## The frame

- **Freeze the mechanism at the moment 1 thing meets the other.** "A meets B" is the picture; "so you get D" is the headline. Not the whole chain: the 1 link a buyer of the category does not already know.
- **Pick 1 view:** a cutaway (the product or the material sliced open, its layers seen from the side), a scale shot (the viewer standing inside it, the thing the technical noun names huge and close), or a split (the usual way on the left, greyed; this way on the right). The split is for a buyer comparing; the scale shot for a buyer who does not yet know the cause.
- **Composition:** the meeting point sits where the eye lands first, large, with depth behind it. Everything else is quiet and darker. 1 direction of travel, shown by shape and light, with 1 arrow at most.
- **The product** is real and whole, small, at the lower edge or in a corner, tied to the picture by 1 thin line or by sharing its colour with the thing that acts. Where the ad leaves the product out, the picture is the mechanism alone.

## Words on the picture

- 1 headline of 8 words or fewer that says what the buyer gets, or the cause they did not assume, said straight.
- 2 or 3 labels, 2 to 4 words each, on thin lines to the parts they name. The technical noun is 1 of them, and each label is a thing the brand's facts state.
- 1 number, large, only if the brand's facts hold it with a source. A footnote mark only where the guardrails ask for one.
- 20 words in all. Claims are worded "supports" or "helps".

## For the image prompt

- Say what it is: a rendered 3D scientific illustration, the kind a documentary uses. Clean physically lit materials, soft volumetric light, a dark or deep-coloured ground, a little depth haze, fine particles for scale.
- Name the view, and the scale ("as if 1 millimetre tall", "1,000 times life size").
- Give the thing the technical noun names 1 shape and 1 colour, and say what it touches and what that does, as far as the claim's verb goes and no further.
- Label lines are thin and straight, the type small, in the brand's body typeface; give each label's words and what it points at.
- The product is the 1 photographic thing: its real shape and printing from its photo.
- No photographed people, no hands in close-up, no hospital or clinic, no gore; an organ only if the brand's facts name it, stylised and whole.

## What fails in a still

- The whole chain as 5 numbered steps: that is an infographic, and nobody reads it in the feed.
- A handsome render with nothing meeting anything.
- Damage vanishing, a before and after of a body, or a picture that shows more than "supports".
- Labels for every part.
