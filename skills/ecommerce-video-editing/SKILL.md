# Ecommerce video editing

Begin with the selected product, audience/stage, approved script/claims/offer, supplied footage and delivery requirements. Editing makes that buyer argument easier to follow. It does not create stronger claims, invented evidence or new offer terms. If footage is missing, name the required shot instead of presenting an unrelated image as proof.

## Build the cut around the buyer

Map the footage to the approved opening, problem or benefit, product/mechanism, supporting proof and action. This is a useful starting structure, not a rule to force every ad into the same sequence. An indirect opening can reveal the product later; a product demo can show it immediately. Each retained moment should earn its place through attention, explanation, evidence or the next step.

Open on a readable, intentional first frame. Trim dead setup without clipping the first word. For a new hook on an approved body, check the handoff for pronouns, argument, voice, product identity, exposure, ambient sound and music. Different hooks can reuse the body only when that join makes sense. Make only the versions requested; editing is not a winning-ad refresh recommendation system.

## Product and proof are protected

Use actual product shots to preserve packaging, logo, proportions, colors and SKU. Show the use or mechanism when its line is spoken. Hold a useful detail long enough to understand it. A flashy transition must not hide the label or interrupt the demonstration. Do not crop away a necessary instruction, qualification or disclosure.

Keep comparisons consistent in angle, light and scale. Preserve the source and context of a real review. A synthetic presenter or illustrative animation is not a real buyer, testimonial or measured result. Do not transfer another brand's outcomes, badges, counters or before/after imagery into this ad.

An optional split speaker/demo layout can clarify a process. Full-frame UGC, product inserts and restrained callouts are often enough. Choose brand typography, colors and a density that suits the product. Celebrity portraits, borrowed reaction GIFs, countdowns and constant shaking are not default D2C ad ingredients.

## Time against the footage

Record source in/out seconds and the resulting timeline. Use actual speech, movement and reveal timing for captions and overlays. Verify an automatic transcript's opening, product names, numbers and negations. The written script estimates pace; it does not measure the finished take. Re-time text after changing a cut.

Keep the creator's natural delivery and lip sync. Cut an unnecessary take instead of warping the person's voice to match an unrelated edit. If a shot needs retiming, review its effect on speech, body weight and the product action. Keep essential exclusions and qualifications with the claim they qualify.

Inspect generated source takes through their final spoken beat before selecting trims. Mouth timing, teeth/jaw stability, exact words and voice identity are separate from attractive lighting. Review the middle as well as the ends. Select a clean take or name a specific replacement shot when a defect damages the claim or performance; music and shorter cuts do not fix it. When an external generative edit is supplied, compare it against its source for unrequested face, wardrobe, background-person and motion changes.

## Sound and the close

Voice carries the argument; synchronized effects emphasize a real action; music supports emotion. Use separate stems when available. Otherwise a mixed source cannot have just its background music removed while retaining its voice. Paid use of a music track must be cleared for paid use. Keep voice intelligible on phone speakers; inspect cuts for clicks, doubled audio and abrupt music starts.

The ending gives one action with the actual offer, price, destination or next step the brand approved. Do not add a discount, urgency, guarantee or “link in bio” because a source template uses it. Check the closing frame at small size and sound off.

## Export and review

Preserve source files and save the cut list, caption windows, aspect/fit, audio choices and actual export properties. Keep the whole frame initially; center cropping requires checking face, hands and label throughout. Deliver the requested ratio and separate copies with/without newly added captions. Baked-in text remains unless a genuinely clean source is supplied. Clean of added text and silent are different choices.

Watch first frame, every join, reveal, demonstration, proof and CTA with sound on and off. Verify actual dimensions, duration, browser-playable H.264/yuv420p MP4 and audio settings. A rendered export remains a draft for the brand to review.

In Creative Engine: **Create → Video editing → Ecommerce ad** accepts up to 12 local clips, trims and joins at original speed, exports 9:16/4:5/1:1/16:9, supports per-clip focal crop, source gains/audio fades, a separate uploaded voice track and optional uploaded music with ducking, and keeps clean/captioned exports. Captions can be entered, imported/exported as SRT or drafted by the connected speech provider. Review their actual words and timing; supplied voice takes priority over source audio for transcription. The voice track starts at timeline zero, so an opening-length change can require a revised voice file. A hook-only variant replaces the first clip, keeps the body, shifts body captions and removes the old opening captions for review. The editor does not automatically check footage claims, generate new shots, replace people, apply a colour/shake realism pass or publish. Supply approved copy; use the brand's existing claims check when new ad words need review.

This method is portable across GPT, Claude and other harnesses. Use the available editor to render the cut list; never claim a proposed edit is a finished video.
