# Length and format

The format is decided by what the argument needs, after the argument exists. Most format failures are writing failures: a beat was placed on a surface that can't carry it, and no amount of re-editing fixes that.

## The load-bearing beat

In every script 1 beat does most of the persuading. Which one is a property of the proposition, not of the format.

| The product's edge | Load-bearing beat | What it needs | Typical of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invisible: it works where nobody can see, or only over years | Mechanism | A way to show what a camera can't | An active in a serum; what a pan is like after 10 years |
| Visible: a result you can watch | Proof | A demonstration, a believed face | Water beading on a jacket; a texture; a change the brand can film |
| In the terms | Offer | A clear single frame | A trial, a warranty, a price nobody else gives |
| In a shared experience | Problem | A person, a voice, time | A daily compromise people recognise |

## What each surface can carry

- **1 person to camera** (UGC, founder, expert) has a believed face, a controlled order and sound. It cannot show what a camera can't.
- **Drawn, rendered or animated** shows the unshowable and holds attention, as a narrated story. It has no believed face.
- **A long-copy native static** reads as a person and holds a reader, with no face, no sound and no controlled order. **A designed static** is a glance: the eye decides the order.

A believed face carries the problem and the proof. Showing the unshowable carries the mechanism. A proposition resting on proof fails on a surface with no believable face; one resting on an invisible mechanism fails as a talking head. A video can change surface part-way (a person, then a drawing for the mechanism, then the person). No surface outperforms the others in general. What matters is the fit.

A static ad is read in the order the eye lands: the first thing seen is the hook, the image can carry the problem, then the promise and mechanism. Whatever doesn't fit goes to the landing page.

## A line is only as good as its picture

Every line of a script gets made: filmed, or generated by a model. What can be shown is the product as it really looks and is really used, an adult, a hand doing something, a place, a simple drawing of how something works. A line whose picture cannot be made is a wasted line: a celebrity, a real person who has not been filmed, a real news or television clip, someone else's footage, a child, or words that have to be read inside the moving picture (words on screen are added afterwards, as an overlay). The format decides the detail.

## Length

No length is best: 30-second ads and 20-minute ads have both carried millions in spend. Longer ads cost more to make and tend to scale further, because length is what an unaware buyer needs.

The hook and the offer never grow. Length comes from the problem, the mechanism, objections and proof. At about 15 seconds there is room for a hook, 1 line of problem or proof, the offer and the action. A promise and 1 sentence of mechanism arrive at 30 seconds, an objection and a risk reversal at 60, and several of each beyond that.

Long ads loop: problem, promise, mechanism, objection, proof, then a second problem and round again, usually 3 times, with a call to action after each loop. A common vehicle is a timeline, each step with its own problem solved and its own mechanism.

A long script cut down by an editor makes a poor short ad: the bridges were built for the long version. Each length is its own script.
