# Objections, proof and the call to action

## The 6 objections

Objections feel particular to each brand but fall into 6 classes. They arise in this order, which is also the order of the script.

| # | The buyer thinks | What answers it | Bought again | Bought once |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | This isn't for me | Language specific to 1 persona | "That's for skincare people" | "That's for serious cooks" |
| 2 | This won't work | Mechanism and proof | "They all say that" | "It'll go the way the last one did" |
| 3 | It might work, but not for me | Proof from someone like them | "My skin reacts to everything" | "Will it fit me?" "Is it too heavy?" |
| 4 | It's not worth it | The offer, and the cost of not buying | "That's a lot every month" | "That's a lot for a pan" |
| 5 | What if I'm wrong? | The reversal | "Am I locked in?" | "Can I send it back once I've used it?" |
| 6 | I'll do it later | A real reason for now | "I'll start after the holidays" | "The old one still works" |

Something bought again can be tried cheaply and is judged in use: the hard doubts are 2 and 5 before the first order, and "will I keep it up?" after it. Something bought once is judged before it is bought, over a longer time: the hard doubts are 3, 4 and 5, answered with the size guide, the price against what it replaces, and the returns.

## Answered before it is thought

The strongest handling names the objection before the buyer forms it, in their own experience:

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "It's gentle enough for sensitive skin." | "You're going to react to this in week 1. Everyone does. That's why the first tube is 0.05% and the second steps up." |
| "It's lighter than you'd think." | "Yes, it's heavy. That weight is what keeps the heat in when the steak goes on." |

The weak line asks to be trusted. The strong line shows the brand knows what happened to the buyer last time. Which experience to name is a research finding.

## The proof ladder

Strongest first:

| # | Proof | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demonstration | The thing working on camera. Available to a brand on day 1. A visible edge is shown as its result (water beading, the crust). An invisible one is shown as its use (the texture, the 1 pump, the first sip), not as its effect. |
| 2 | Third-party verification | Lab tests, trials, certifications, independent press, a standard it was tested to |
| 3 | Volume of specific social proof | Counts of customers, reviews, reorders, years in use |
| 4 | A named individual's testimony | Their own words. Can outrank volume when the person is exactly like the buyer |
| 5 | Mechanism explanation | Not proof, but makes the claim plausible |
| 6 | Founder assertion | Weakest. Carries weight only when the founder has real standing in the field |

"Customers love it" is not proof. A count and a word customers really use is. Each rung is proof only if it exists: a presenter who is not a real customer shows and explains, and reports no result of their own. A modest claim with strong proof beats a big claim with weak proof every time.

## The call to action

A call to action has 4 parts: the action, named; what happens next; the effort, priced; a reason for now.

- Bought once, the work is choosing. Not "learn more" but "Take the 60-second quiz. You'll get your size and 2 cuts that suit your shape."
- Bought again, the work is starting. Not "subscribe and save" but "Start with 1 box. It ships Monday, and you can pause it from your account."

A stronger ending than a bare ask explains the choice, reverses the risk, and closes on who the buyer is.
