# Offer

What decides whether an ad sells, most important first: the offer, the market it is taken to, the mechanism, the proof, then everything else in the script. The best script cannot rescue a bad offer, and price framing, guarantee and urgency can only say what the offer actually contains. An offer is the product plus how its value looks from the buyer's side.

## The 5 parts, by how the product is bought

| Part | The buyer's question | Bought again (used up, reordered) | Bought once (kept for years) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The thing | What do I get? | The first box and what is in it | The item, the size or version, what comes with it |
| The terms | What does it cost, against what? | Today's price and the price after; how often it ships | 1 price, against what it replaces or how long it lasts |
| The reversal | What if I'm wrong? | Money back on the first order; pausing and cancelling, as the offer states them | Returns and exchanges, the trial period, the warranty, repairs |
| The reason for now | Why this week? | The first-order price, a batch, a season | Stock of a size or colour, a made-to-order run, the season it is needed for |
| The effort | What do I have to do? | The habit: when, how long, what it tastes or feels like | Choosing the right one, the care it needs |

A script can only use a part the offer has. No guarantee in the offer means no risk reversal in the script. A price or a term the brand has not stated is not said.

Often the offer doesn't need changing, only saying. A returns policy becomes a guarantee when it is stated with its specifics. A 3-pack becomes "the 90-day pack" when the product takes 90 days to show. A repair service becomes "the last one you'll buy".

## Framing the price

| Frame | Weak | Strong, bought again | Strong, bought once |
|---|---|---|---|
| Against the alternative | "Was 89, now 69" | "2 cafe coffees cost more than a week of this" | "You'll buy 3 coated pans in the time this one lasts" |
| Down to the unit | "69 for a 90-day supply" | "76 cents a day" | "Worn twice a week, that's under a dollar a wear by next winter" |
| Price the problem | "It's only 68" | "You've spent more than this on 2 that didn't work" | "The last one cost half as much and lasted 1 winter" |
| Effort as the price | "Free shipping over 50" | "1 pump at night. You'll forget you're doing it." | "Rinse, dry, a wipe of oil. That's the care." |

Every number in a price frame is the brand's real number, or is worked out from the brand's numbers with the basis in view. A per-day figure belongs to the price it came from. A warranty period is not a lifespan.

## What makes a guarantee work

"30-day money-back guarantee" is what every brand says. "Cook on it for 100 days. If it isn't the pan you reach for, send it back used" is stronger: it is specific, a little uncomfortable for the seller, and as long as the buyer needs to find out (a cycle of use for something used up; a season or a few weeks of wear for something kept).

## Honest urgency

"Limited time only" is manufactured and carries nothing. Real urgency has a real number and a real date ("we make 400 a month, October's gone, the next batch ships on the 14th"), and is more specific as well as true.
