# The 9 beats

A script exists to move 1 belief: what this buyer thinks before the ad, and what they think after it. Every ad that sells does 9 things on the way. A beat is a job, not a sentence: a beat can take 1 line or a whole scene, and 1 line can do 2 jobs.

| # | Beat | Its job | Where its material comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Buys attention and selects the right person | The word (research) |
| 2 | Problem | Names the consequence they've already accommodated | The accommodation (research) |
| 3 | Promise | States what they get, in their terms | The desire |
| 4 | Mechanism | Gives a reason to believe the promise | The product and how it is made |
| 5 | Objection | Answers what they've tried and why this is different | The failed solution (research) |
| 6 | Proof | Substantiates the claim | Proof the brand really has, with its source |
| 7 | Offer | Presents the product and the terms | The offer |
| 8 | Risk reversal | Removes the fear of being wrong | The nearly-didn't-buy (research), the guarantee |
| 9 | Call to action | Says what to do, what happens next and why now | The moment (research) |

An identity line often closes the ad after the call to action, or sits inside it.

## Why this order

The order is the order of the buyer's doubts: is this for me, will it work, will it work for me, is it worth it, what if I'm wrong, why now. The early beats argue at the level of the problem and the category, where no belief in the brand is needed. Belief in the brand is asked for late, once the mechanism has landed.

## The hook is the audience, not the persuasion

Most people who ever see the ad see only the hook, and nobody is sold in 3 seconds. The selling is in the rest, which is where most scripts are weakest.

## A framework, not a formula

The 9 beats make a script competent. They don't make it wanted: that comes from desire, identity and voice, which sit in no single beat. A writer who knows why each beat exists can leave one out or reorder them on purpose: a 1st-person story can't state a promise before the narrator has found the mechanism, and a demonstration puts its proof first. 2 scripts with the same beats in the same order from the same research are 1 ad told twice.

The common faults: no promise (problem runs straight into mechanism); features listed where a mechanism belongs; an objection nobody holds; proof weaker than the claim; a call to action that only names the action.
