# Desire and research

## Desire is found, not created

Copy cannot create desire for a product. It takes wants that already exist in many people and points them at 1 product (Schwartz). An ad that starts from the product and works backwards to a reason to care reads like a product page with a hook on the front. Most weak scripts are weak for this reason, not because of the hook.

A desire is real when there is evidence of it outside the brand's own marketing: a review, a support ticket, a call, a forum thread. With none, a script is a guess that says it is a guess. The crowd is chosen by the message: each word either sounds like that person's own experience or it doesn't.

## The 5 things research yields, and the beat each feeds

| Finding | What it is | Bought again (eaten, applied, used up) | Bought once (worn, cooked on, kept) | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The accommodation | What they changed in their life to work around the problem, usually without admitting it | "I buy a coffee on the way in because the one at home is bad" | "I check the forecast before I decide to walk" | Problem |
| The failed solution | What they tried or own now, what it cost, why it let them down | The one that stung; the tub that went stale half used | The cheap one, replaced twice | Objection |
| The nearly-didn't-buy | The last thing between them and the purchase | "Will I keep it up?" "Is this a subscription trap?" | "Is it worth this much?" "Which size am I?" "Can I send it back?" | Offer and risk reversal |
| The word | The noun and phrases they use for the problem | "sludge", "my skin freaks out" | "soaked through", "it peeled" | Hook |
| The moment | The day or event that made them start looking | The third bad morning in a row | The trip, the season, the old one breaking | Urgency, and the setting of the script |

5 of the 9 beats come straight from research. A finding the research does not hold is a gap that is named, not filled in.

## Whose words they are

A quote's source says what it is good for. A review gives the words customers use; one of 2 to 4 stars gives a reservation, which is an objection in the customer's words. A return reason or a support ticket gives what not to promise. A call gives the last objection before buying ("I nearly didn't..."). A forum gives the language of people who have not bought. The founder and the website are the brand's words: they supply facts, never quotes.

Customers rarely use the category's word. "Waterproof breathable shell" is packaging language; "I get to work wet either way, from the rain or from the sweat" is the buyer's state. Marketer language asks the buyer to translate. Their own needs no translation.

Anything a model makes up about the customer is a hypothesis, and is labelled as one.
