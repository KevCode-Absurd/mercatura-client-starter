# Identity and emotion

## Believing is not wanting

A script can be logical enough that the buyer agrees with every sentence and still does nothing. It passes every review, because reviewers check whether it is correct, and nobody checks whether anyone wants it.

Nobody buys a pan because they want a pan. They buy a version of themselves: the one who cooks properly, the one who did something about it early, the one who buys a thing once.

The buyer already feels something before the ad arrives. A script that joins that feeling, in the register of someone who belongs there, and only then brings in its subject, is entering a conversation already under way (Collier). A script creates no feeling; it joins one.

## The "so what?" test

For any line, ask "so what?". The answer can't be the mechanism. It has to be why a person cares.

| What it does | Who cares about that |
|---|---|
| Starts at a low dose and steps up | Someone who would rather go slowly than quit again |
| Merino that doesn't hold smell for 4 days | Someone who travels with 1 bag |
| A 100-day trial, returned used | Someone who has been sold to before and burned |
| Repaired free for life | Someone who is tired of buying the same thing twice |

What matters is how they see themselves, not whether they really check every label.

## Identity is tied to a mechanism

Identity on its own is a lifestyle brand with a product attached. "Who our customer is" only earns a place in a script when it connects to something the product actually does. Tied to a mechanism it is selling; untied it is decoration. When a market is tired of claims and mechanisms (Schwartz's stage 5), who the buyer becomes is what still cuts through.

## Where it shows

Identity and emotion run through a script; they are not 1 beat. They show in which accommodation is named, in the promise, in the register. A script often also closes on an identity line:

- "The people who cook on iron aren't better cooks. They just stopped replacing pans."
- "The ones who stay with it are the ones who started slowly."

Such a line speaks to who the buyer wants to be, and turns away the person who would not have stayed a customer anyway.
