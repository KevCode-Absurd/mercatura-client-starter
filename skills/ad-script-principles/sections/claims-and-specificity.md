# Claims and specificity

## Two questions for every claim

1. Is it true, and can the brand stand behind it? A line that isn't true for this product is not said, however well it sells.
2. Can the product next to it on the shelf say the same? If so the words are spent on nothing. "Vegan, cruelty-free, made locally" is true of much of a category, so it moves nobody.

## Three tests for a differentiator

| Differentiator | A proposition ("buy this, get this")? | Unavailable to competitors? | Moves a stranger? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family owned since 1994 | Yes | Mostly | No |
| Free shipping | Yes | No | No |
| "World-class service" | No: a claim, not a thing you get | No | No |
| Third-party tested, results published | Yes | Yes (costly to do) | Yes |
| Repaired free for life | Yes | Yes (costly to do) | Yes |

Only a differentiator that passes all 3 earns its seconds, and only if it speaks to a desire the buyer already has. A line that answers an objection the buyer doesn't hold is wasted: which objections are real is a research finding. A weak line often needs no better version. It needs to go.

## Specifics are evidence, adjectives are opinion

"Premium ingredients, superior quality, unmatched results" can be said by any competitor, so the buyer drops it before the sentence ends (Hopkins). The specificity ladder, strongest first: a measured number, a named third party, a physical detail, a time, a proper noun.

| Adjective | Specific |
|---|---|
| "Gentle on skin" | "0.05% retinol, and the first tube is dosed low on purpose" |
| "Fast absorbing" | "Dry in 40 seconds. We timed it." |
| "Built to last" | "The handle is 1 piece with the pan. There's no rivet to work loose." |
| "Luxury craftsmanship" | "1 person stitches each pair start to finish. It takes her 4 hours." |

Specificity doesn't require an outcome claim. A duration, a dose, a count, a material or a process detail is specific and promises no result.

## Process as mechanism

Schlitz told people its bottles were cleaned with live steam. Every brewer did it; nobody had said it. Such facts hide in 3 places: what the maker does that the brand assumes is boring; what was rejected along the way (a formula, a fabric, a supplier); a constraint the product imposed that has never been mentioned. For something eaten or applied the fact is often a dose, a form or a test. For something worn or used it is a material, a join, a weight, or what it was put through before it shipped.

## What cannot be made up

A testimonial, a review count, a statistic, a study, an award and an expert are proof only because they exist. An invented one is not weak proof: it is a false claim with a name on it. A customer's words are theirs only word for word, with where they were said. A figure the brand itself states 2 different ways is not yet a fact. Where a fact is missing a script has a gap, marked, and a person fills it.
