# Problem

## Accommodation, not symptom

- A **symptom** is what is going wrong: skin that flakes, a pan that sticks, a jacket that leaks.
- An **accommodation** (the consequence) is what the buyer has already changed about their life to work around it, usually without admitting it.

A script agitates the accommodation, never the symptom.

| Symptom (weak) | Accommodation (strong) |
|---|---|
| "Dry, flaky skin can be frustrating." | "You've started doing your makeup at the mirror with the worst light." |
| "Non-stick pans wear out." | "You've stopped cooking steak at home. You didn't decide to. You just stopped." |
| "Getting caught in the rain is miserable." | "You check the forecast before you decide whether to walk." |

The symptom describes a state the buyer already knows they're in, and any competitor can write it. The accommodation is something the buyer has done and never said aloud. Once named, they can't unsee it. It selects the person without a call-out.

Not every category has pain. Where a product is bought for pleasure or for how it looks, the accommodation is the compromise: what they settle for, put up with or keep meaning to replace ("the jacket you only wear when nobody will see it").

## Three ways in

| Entry | Sounds like | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The accommodation | "You've stopped cooking steak at home." | The default |
| The moment | "It was the third morning that week I poured it down the sink." | The day they started looking |
| The failed solution | "The last one was half the price. It lasted 1 winter." | Puts the ad at solution aware |

## What makes it work

- **1 person.** A problem beat written for 2 kinds of people lands on neither. This is the strongest predictor of whether it works.
- **Category level.** The product and its mechanism haven't appeared, so the argument is about the problem and the category. No belief in the brand is being asked for yet.
- **Unresolved.** The beat creates discomfort and leaves the buyer in it. Relief comes later, from the promise and the mechanism.
- **Escalating.** Each line leads to the next and toward 1 consequence. If any 2 lines could swap places, it is a list of symptoms, and a list reads as complaining.
- **Short in a normal ad.** About 10 to 15 seconds. A long story ad can stay in the problem for 2 minutes, as a scene.

## Negative, or pitying?

"You've stopped cooking steak at home" is an observation, not an insult. It works because it is true and nobody has said it. What brands rightly object to is pity: stacked symptoms that cast the buyer as a sufferer. An observation describes what someone does. It does not tell the viewer what they have or what they are (a condition, a body, a money worry). The accommodation, never the affliction.
