# Voice

What to say and how it sounds are different skills. A script is heard, not read.

The order for every word: a whole sentence a person would say to a friend, in plain words, comes first. Then it sells, then it tells a story. Then the brand's language, sent with the job: who it talks to, its words for things, its tone. A brand's devices (a rhythm, a run of short sentences, a turn of phrase) come last and are optional, only on a line that takes one without bending. How long a script runs is given with the job, by where the buyer is. What follows is what tends to hold for speech.

## Written versus spoken

| Reads fine, sounds wrong | Sounds right |
|---|---|
| "The cast-iron construction provides superior heat retention, resulting in consistent searing performance across a range of cooking applications." | "Iron holds its heat, so when you put a cold steak on it the pan stays hot. That's where the crust comes from." |

The second version has plain words, the verb early, the joining words a person uses ("so", "when"), and nothing to trip on at speaking pace.

## Settings at sentence level

| Setting | What tends to hold |
|---|---|
| Person | 1st for stories, 2nd for most scripts, 3rd for narration. Each is a different ad. |
| Tense | Past for stories, present for how something works |
| Sentence length | As speech has it: mostly whole sentences joined by "and", "but", "so", "because", with a short one where the story turns. A run of very short sentences sounds like a machine. |
| Reading level | Plain for a general audience: words a 13 year old knows. A technical term only where the buyer already uses it, and none for a buyer who does not yet know an answer exists |

## Register

The same angle and the same person can be 3 different ads by register alone:

- **UGC:** casual, the customer's own nouns. "I stopped cooking steak at home, and I didn't even notice I'd stopped. That's the weird part."
- **Documentary:** 3rd person, calm, no enthusiasm. "She stopped cooking steak at home about a year before she worked out why."
- **Diary:** 1st person, written to nobody. "I cooked on medium heat again tonight, and I don't know when that became the rule."

## The read-aloud test

Read at the pace it would be delivered, to a friend. A stumble marks a line to fix. Running out of breath marks a sentence that is too long. Embarrassment at saying a line marks a line nobody would say.

Voice identity and performance are separate from the words. An approved voice keeps its recognizable timbre and language while local energy/emphasis follows the actual story. Pauses and reactions need time beside complete words; they are not compulsory delays at each cut. Spoken copy stays clean, with useful performance notes in existing direction fields rather than provider tags in the dialogue.
