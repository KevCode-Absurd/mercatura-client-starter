# Hook and bridges

## What a hook is for

A hook does not try to stop everyone. It pre-qualifies: the right person keeps watching and the wrong one leaves. The platform then finds more people like those who stayed, so the hook also steers delivery.

The hook's material is the customer's own word for the problem. It is also the cheapest thing to vary: a new hook on the same body reaches new people.

## Pile A and pile B

People sort mail into 2 piles: what is obviously advertising, and what looks like a personal letter (Halbert). Only the second gets opened. A feed works the same way.

| Pile A: obviously an ad | Pile B: looks native |
|---|---|
| Polished hero shot, centred logo, tagline | A screenshot, a note to self, a person talking to their phone |
| "Introducing the new summer collection" | "I ordered this dress in 3 sizes because I had no idea which one I was" |

Performance mostly comes from pile B. What puts an ad in pile A is the marketing language more than the picture quality: a polished video with native wording can sit in pile B; a rough one that opens with brand talk sits in pile A.

## All the hooks agree

An ad has several hooks at once: the first frame, the spoken first line, the overlay, the headline, the first line of primary text, the top of the landing page. They make 1 claim together. A luxury first frame, discount overlay and science headline are 3 ads fighting.

## The hook sells line 2

The whole job of the first line is to get the second line watched. A hook is judged with the line that follows it, never alone. Hooks written in a batch and stitched onto a body are the usual cause of a cliff in the retention curve: each reads well by itself and makes no sense in front of the problem line.

## Bridges

A script is written in sections and watched as 1 thing. The joins are where people leave. The 2 that matter most are the hook into the problem, and the product's arrival: the moment the viewer goes from not knowing there is a product to knowing.

Nothing makes a person scroll faster than content that turns into an ad in 1 line. People talking naturally go: a thought, a small complaint, a story, and then maybe the product. A script that arrives the same way keeps the viewer:

| Abrupt | Eased in |
|---|---|
| "...and the meat stews. Which is why we made [the product]." | "The fix isn't a better spray or a hotter hob. It's a pan that stays hot. 2 things do that." |
| "So head to the site and grab yours." | "You can keep replacing the coated ones, and people do. Or you can buy 1." |

The product can be seen before it is named, and named well after the mechanism: at the offer, or as late as the call to action.

A line that opens a question the script answers later ("2 things keep a pan hot, and the first is why most people give up on iron") keeps people watching to the answer.
