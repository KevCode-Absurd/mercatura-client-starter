# Promise and mechanism

## Three different things

| | What it is | Something applied | Something used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feature | What it is | 0.05% retinol in tube 1, 0.1% in tube 2 | Cast iron, 4 mm thick |
| Promise | What you get | You get through the first month without peeling | A crust on the steak, on a home hob |
| Mechanism | The reason the feature produces the promise | Skin needs weeks to build tolerance, so the dose steps up as it does | Thick iron stays hot when cold meat lands on it; a thin pan drops and the meat stews |

They chain: the feature, **which means** the promise, **which is why** the mechanism. A feature alone sells only people who are already convinced. A promise alone is what the whole category says. A mechanism makes the promise believable and sets the product apart, in the same sentence.

## The promise

A promise is 1 sentence that answers "what do I get?" in the buyer's terms. Scripts fail on it in 4 ways:

1. **No promise.** The script goes from problem straight to mechanism and leaves the buyer to work out the outcome. Common in technical categories.
2. **Several promises.** Each one devalues the others.
3. **A feature in the promise's place.** "6 studied ingredients" and "3-layer fabric" are inputs, not outcomes.
4. **A promise bigger than the proof.** A smaller promise that can be proved outsells a bigger one that can't (Bencivenga). "It won't make you a chef. It will brown the meat" is modest, true and believable.

## Promise first, then mechanism

Attention falls through an ad. A mechanism in front of the promise opens on jargon and loses people before they hear what's in it for them.

| Mechanism first (weak) | Promise first (strong) |
|---|---|
| "High thermal mass prevents the temperature drop on contact." | "You get a crust, not a grey steak, because the iron stays hot when the meat goes on." |

## A promise needs a mechanism

"Waterproof and breathable" leaves the question "how can it be both?" unanswered. "The holes in the membrane are smaller than a raindrop and bigger than vapour" answers it. A contradiction is not a mechanism. It opens a question: it belongs in a hook, or it needs the mechanism right behind it.

When a feature has no mechanism, a different feature that has one takes its place, or the script carries the promise with proof instead (promise, proof, promise, proof). That is weaker and is how lower-sophistication markets are sold.

## Every product has a mechanism

Somebody made a decision in making the product and that decision has a consequence. It needs no chemistry: "Merino fibre is crimped, so it traps air and holds odour in the fibre, not on your skin." Where the pleasure is the point (a taste, a look, a feel), the mechanism is what was done to get it: "steeped cold for 18 hours, so the bitter oils never come out".

## The product comes after the mechanism

A product named before its mechanism is understood drops into the pile of every similar product, and the buyer's next thought is price. A pan named in line 1 is a pan. The same product introduced after "why this one works" is a different thing.

Seen is not named. A product whose edge is visible can be on screen, working, from the first frame, and still be named and priced late.
