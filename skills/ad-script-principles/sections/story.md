# Story

## A carrier, not a beat

A story is not a tenth beat and not a 30-second section bolted on. It is a way of delivering all 9 beats: the viewer is carried through a sequence of events happening to someone, and the argument arrives along the way.

It works because it changes the viewer's job. Watching an ad, their job is to evaluate: every sentence gets audited. Inside a story, their job is to find out what happened. Every beat is still there; they just aren't marking them.

## The 4 common shapes

| Shape | Sounds like | The beat it carries |
|---|---|---|
| Failed-solutions journey | "I tried the drugstore one, then the prescription one, then I gave up for 2 years." | Objection |
| Discovery | "I found out why my steak went grey, and it wasn't my cooking." | Mechanism |
| Transformation with a witness | "My brother asked where I'd got it before I'd said a word." | Proof |
| Confession | "I'd been buying a coffee on the way in every day and telling myself I liked the walk." | Problem |

## The narrator

The narrator doesn't have to be a customer or a person on camera: the shapes are structures, not casting. In 3rd person the same story runs under animation, and a brand can tell a discovery ("somebody worked out why this happens").

1st person, past tense is usually the strongest form. In a 1st-person story the promise can't come before the mechanism: the narrator hasn't found it yet. The promise is implied or comes after.

## What breaks a story ad

1. **The bridge.** The story runs, then the ad starts. The move from story to product has to be eased, which is why story ads need length. Under about 30 seconds there is no room for both a story and a bridge.
2. **An obvious actor.** A narrator who is clearly reading lines ends the story.
3. **No stakes.** If nothing was risked, lost or embarrassing, nobody cares.

| No stakes | Stakes |
|---|---|
| "I'd been looking for a better pan for a while." | "I'd stopped having people over, because everything I cooked came out the same." |

Stories without stakes come from too little research: the writer has no real accommodation, failed solution or moment to build from, and invents something slightly off.

## Two more properties

- **The story isn't resolved in the hook.** Resolving early removes the reason to keep watching.
- **If it didn't happen, it's fiction, and it has to read as fiction.** True stories come from customers and from real history. A person who hasn't used the product, or who doesn't exist, can't truthfully give a personal result: a generated presenter or a character tells the problem, and the proof stays the brand's. A claim inside a made-up story is still a claim.
