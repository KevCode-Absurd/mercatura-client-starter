# Start with a new client

Create useful work from this client's verified resources before connecting performance data. A new installation starts with no brands and no saved examples.

## First morning checklist

Run the installation steps in README.md. Open `/onboarding`, enter the client's actual trading name and add the brand. Open Brand and upload only resources you are allowed to use: current product catalogue, packaging photographs, approved claims, offer terms, brand guide, customer research and any owned reference creative. Keep unrelated businesses in different installations during the local pilot.

Create a resource inventory with owner, effective date, usage rights and review status. Ask the client to resolve conflicts before extraction: an old discount deck and a new price sheet should not both become current truth. Mark pending claims as pending. Do not promote a marketing assertion to substantiated solely because it appears repeatedly.

Enter the market, currency, audience and voice deliberately. New planner economics start at zero because there is no evidence for inherited spend, margins or return targets. Configure these only after the client supplies them. Check the claims review settings. New learned preferences require explicit review.

## Build the evidence pack

For each product, record a stable SKU, correct name, variant, pack size, ingredients or specifications, restrictions, price, offer dates, guarantee terms and exact source. Upload clear front and back packaging references where permitted. Separate what the product is from what it might help a buyer do.

Customer research should preserve the actual words, source, date and context. Separate a customer's opinion from a verified product property. A testimonial about one person's experience does not prove a universal result. Remove emails, addresses, order IDs and other personal identifiers before prompting models.

List three useful buyer situations, three objections and three reasons to choose a different solution. If you have no customer research yet, label proposed situations as hypotheses. Have the client review them before making a large batch. The goal is a small, coherent source pack, not the largest possible upload.

## Approve live generation

Mock mode is the default. It checks workflow and must not be used to judge final output quality. Add your own provider keys to your local environment only after approving what can be sent to each provider. Select models that actually exist and are enabled for your account; copied route defaults are not an availability guarantee.

Choose a small text cap, image count and video budget. The text cap is limited by the installation's absolute ceiling. Image count and video dollars are separate controls. Existing local caps are not a transactional multi-tenant spend ledger. For a first paid pilot, keep one operator, avoid simultaneous batches and check the provider's billing dashboard against the app ledger.

Suggested sequence: approve five copy concepts, render two or three images, write one video script, produce one landing page outline and one article outline. Review those before increasing volume. A failed quality check is a hold to inspect, not an instruction to keep retrying indefinitely.

## Example image brief

This is a fictitious brief structure. Replace every fact with approved client evidence before use.

> Goal: help a commuter evaluate our compact insulated bottle. Audience: people carrying a laptop and lunch on a train. Product evidence: use the current specification sheet and the supplied product photographs. Do not claim a cooling duration without a verified test. Offer: use only the current approved price sheet. Create 12 concepts across everyday inconvenience, product demonstration, practical comparison and simple product portrait. Vary scene, argument and layout. Keep the product shape and cap accurate. Use clear, restrained language. Return the source for every factual claim and flag any missing proof.

Approve the argument before rendering. Check the product against the reference at full size: silhouette, lid, logo, colour, material and proportions. Check every word in the image. Review at mobile size as well as full resolution. A beautiful image with the wrong product is rejected.

## Example video brief

> Create three distinct 25-second scripts: an ordinary commuter demonstration, an objection-led comparison and a first-person routine story. Each needs a spoken hook, visual opening, timed beats, on-screen copy, production instructions and a clear CTA. Use only approved facts. Do not invent a customer, testimonial, personal result or competitor weakness. Keep the spoken words within a realistic read time. Provide alternate openings that lead naturally into the same body.

Read the script aloud. Check whether the opening creates a question the body answers. Make sure each shot can be filmed or generated and product handling is plausible. Voice, captions and visuals should reinforce the same argument. A script is a production document, not a video file; a rendered video is a separate paid step.

## Example landing page brief

> Build a mobile-first page for the commuter use case. Lead with the everyday problem, show the product in use, explain the relevant specifications, address carrying and cleaning objections, show the current offer and close with a truthful CTA. Attach sources to factual statements. Use review or comparison sections only if approved evidence exists. Provide desktop and mobile previews plus a list of unresolved facts. Do not invent star ratings, order counts, guarantees or scarcity.

Check the page as a purchase journey: message match, readability, product understanding, proof, price, terms, CTA and next step. Inspect narrow screens, long titles, missing images, keyboard focus and contrast. If a checkout is not connected, say so; a polished preview does not prove a working purchase path. Publishing remains an explicit action after review.

## Example substantial SEO brief

> Topic: how to choose a commuter bottle that fits a work bag. Answer practical questions about dimensions, carrying, materials, cleaning and specification tradeoffs. Start with a useful outline and an evidence map from the approved resources. Write a substantial article at the length the questions require; do not pad it to a word count. Give concrete selection steps, a comparison framework, limitations and clear answers. State where evidence is absent. Include an accurate title, description, relevant internal-link suggestions and a restrained product mention. Do not invent lab tests or expert quotations.

Review the full body, not just its title or score. Each section should add information. Verify numbers and links, check duplicated paragraphs and search intent, and ensure a reader could make a better decision after reading. A long article can still be empty. Word count is a minimum-depth signal; useful explanations, evidence coverage and factual accuracy are the acceptance criteria.

## Move from pilot to volume

Once the pilot passes, approve a diversity matrix before requesting a large batch. For example: four buyer situations by three arguments by two visual treatments yields 24 planned combinations. Reserve some slots for genuinely different concepts, rather than dozens of headline substitutions. Use a product fact pack and approved visual anchors consistently while changing the idea.

Review a contact sheet for similarity, then inspect shortlisted assets at full size. Keep accepted, revise and rejected states separate. Record specific reasons such as wrong cap, unsupported duration, repeated layout or unclear offer. Approve a correction as a client-specific lesson only after it is confirmed. Do not automatically transfer it to another brand.

Measure accepted outputs, distinct approved concepts, factual defects, total spend including retries and time to approval. Do not measure quality by files generated alone. Keep original source versions and artifact revisions so the reviewer can understand exactly what was approved.
