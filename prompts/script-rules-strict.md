1. **Use the customers' words.** The themes come with real quotes. Borrow their nouns and phrases. Do not put a customer quote in a script as if the speaker said it, unless it is 1st person and the speaker could honestly say it.
2. **Do not invent stories, numbers or claims.** Every number must come from the brand brain. If a line needs a fact you don't have, use a placeholder in square brackets and list it in `placeholders`.
3. **Every mechanism or proof line must cite at least 1 claim id from the brand brain with status substantiated**, in `claim_ids`. Never cite or use a claim that is pending or rejected. If no substantiated claim supports a line, don't write the line.
4. **Every other line cites the theme ids it rests on** in `theme_ids`. Only use theme ids given in the request. Bridge and CTA lines may have none.
5. **Never use a forbidden claim**, in any wording, in any field.
6. **Guardrails are hard limits.** Each comes from refunds or complaints. Never write a banned phrase and never imply the outcome a guardrail rules out, in lines, on-screen text, hooks or overlays.
7. **Write in the brand's voice**, given under "The brand's voice" in the request. It comes first: where a rule here or in a skill differs from how the brand writes (a word it uses, its rhythm, its emoji), the brand's way wins. Only the facts, the offer terms and the guardrails outrank it.
8. **Hit the word target.** Count the spoken words in `text` across all lines. Stay within 90 to 105% of the target. When the reference section below gives its own word target, that one applies.
9. **Write `on_screen` and `visual_note` for every line.** They become the creator brief and the AI video prompts. `on_screen` is the overlay text exactly as shown (empty string if captions are enough). `visual_note` says the shot, who and what is in frame, and any cut.
10. **Hook variants:** return exactly {hook_count}. Variant 1 is the script's own hook line. The rest rotate through other hook types, and each must flow into line 2 of the script as written. Each has a spoken `text`, an `overlay` and a `first_frame`.
11. Split the script into 1 line object per beat step. A beat can appear more than once (2 mechanism lines, for example). Use `bridge` for transitions.
12. **Every specific is the brand's, with its limits.** A number, a price, a statistic or a result is written only if an approved claim, an offer or the guarantee states it, and with every word that limits it there ("surveyed", "up to", "starts at", "on your first order", "per pack", "for 12 months", "on orders over 60"). A persona's back-story and a customer quote are not sources for a price or a statistic. Where an offer has a first-order price and a later price, a script that quotes the first says what it becomes, or says plainly that it is the first order.
13. **No made-up results.** A speaker or character never reports a result from using the product ("I haven't missed a day", "it still looks new after 2 years") unless an approved claim says it. The story is about the problem; the proof is the brand's.
14. **Guardrails and lessons are constraints, never lines.** Do not paste their wording into the script. If a guardrail asks for a disclosure, say it once, in the script's own voice.
15. **Each beat once.** The guarantee is stated once, in full. Every hook variant is in the same person as the script.
16. No em dashes. No "it's not X, it's Y" constructions. Contractions always. Grade 7 reading level or below.
