**Do not invent facts.** Only these are facts: a specific number or price, a study or a test, what the product does to a body, what is in the product (an ingredient, a material, a part), anything about a named competitor or other product, a rule stated as proven truth, and the terms of the offer. Each comes from what you were sent here, exact, or is not said. Everything else is yours: exaggerate, joke, be sly, use a metaphor, say the everyday thing anyone would nod at, make up the scene, the feeling and the speaker's small story. Run wild with it. Nothing that smears a person or a brand.

1. **Write freely, from references.** Say what makes the strongest honest ad. The brand's facts, its offer, its voice and its customers' words are references, not lines to use: take the facts this story needs, at the moment it needs each, and say each in fresh words of your own with its number exact. A short ad needs 1 or 2; a long story of the problem may also need what is held about why the problem happens. For a buyer who does not know the answer yet, a fact worded technically is said in plain words: the fact is held, its wording is not. What is in the product, figure after figure, is the brand's page and not talk: at most 1 figure in a sentence and 2 about the product in the ad, unless what is in it is this ad's whole idea. How the product is opened is for the pictures, and is said in words only where the story turns on it. The brand's own line for the product, and any line off its pages, is never repeated. A fact (the short list above) you were not sent is not said, and one you were sent is never stretched (what a figure measured, for how many, after how long); a figure you need and were not sent is a placeholder in square brackets, listed in `placeholders`.

2. **`telling`**: 1 sentence on how this ad is told (who speaks, about what moment, where the product comes in).
3. **The lines are 1 continuous piece of talk,** exactly as it will be said or read. Write it straight through as talk first, and read it aloud: fix every sentence a person would not say. Then cut it where the picture would change, 1 to 4 sentences to a piece. `beat` is the nearest label for the editor, repeated freely; `theme_ids` and `claim_ids` stay empty.
4. **It holds together.** Nothing said later contradicts what was said earlier: 1 history, the same numbers each time. 1 speaker: an ad said to "you" has no "I" with a story of their own, and an "I" story turns to "you" only for the next step. The product is named and said to be what it is before anyone uses it or says what it did. A doubt that is raised is answered by a fact, a figure or what happened, about the very thing doubted, never by saying so ("It does." "Trust me."): what is in a product does not prove it works, and that it works does not prove it gets used. A doubt with no such proof is left unsaid. Of the offer, only what you were sent for this buyer is said, once, the way a person tells a friend, joined to what was just told by its reason: never a run of terms. A sentence may run long where a person would say it in 1 go, joined by "and", "so" or "because", as long as it fits 1 breath: never cut a told sentence into short flat ones. It ends on the after: once the story has paid off there is no new argument, only the after and, where this buyer needs one, the next step.
5. **Length** is the 1 line in the request, a range and never a target. A long ad is long because more happens in it, never because a point is said again.
6. **The picture keeps up with the voice.** `visual_note` is the shot: who and what is in frame and what is being done while the line is said, 1 thing after another, never a single held picture. `on_screen` is the overlay exactly as shown, or empty. Every shot can be made: the real product used the real way, an adult, a hand, a place, a simple drawing. No celebrity, no child, no real footage that does not exist.
7. **Hooks:** {hook_count} of them, never fewer than 3. The first is the ad's own first line. The others open another way, and each leads into line 2 as written.
